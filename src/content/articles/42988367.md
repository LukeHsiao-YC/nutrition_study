### 重點摘要
這項研究旨在探討美國青少年零食特徵（能量密度與食物種類）與個人、社會及實體環境因素之間的關聯。分析2011-2018年NHANES數據顯示，零食的能量密度和食物種類主要與零食來源相關，便利商店、販賣機和社交來源的零食能量密度較高，且水果和乳製品攝取量較低。研究發現個人和社會環境因素的關聯較少，表明公共衛生干預應針對便利商店和販賣機，以促進青少年做出更健康的零食選擇。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究明確指出零食來源對青少年零食選擇的顯著影響，特別是便利商店和販賣機。然而，對於個人與社會環境因素對零食特徵的關聯，研究發現的連結相對較少，這可能為未來研究提供了更深入探討這些因素或改進其測量方法的研究方向。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Individual and environmental factors associated with snack characteristics among US adolescents: NHANES 2011-2018.
**Abstract:** To investigate the individual, social and physical environmental factors associated with snack characteristics. Data collected using a 24-h dietary recall from the 2011-2018 National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (1835 boys and 1796 girls 12-19 years) were analysed. The associations between snack energy density (ED; kJ/g) and food groups consumed at snacks with individual, social and physical environmental factors were examined using a multilevel two-part model (logistic and linear regression) stratified by sex. Adolescents aged 12-19 years. Nationally representative sample of US adolescents. We found associations for snack ED and food groups consumed at snacks with the source of snacks. Snacks obtained from convenience stores, vending machines and social sources (from someone else) were more energy-dense than those from grocery stores. Additionally, adolescents had lower odds of consuming fruit at snacks obtained from convenience stores (OR (95 % CI): boys, 0·20 [0·12, 0·33]; girls, 0·31 [0·16, 0·61]) and vending machines (OR (95 % CI): girls, 0·07 [0·01, 0·56]). There were also lower odds of consumption of dairy foods at snacks outside the home (OR (95 % CI): boys, 0·69 [0·55, 0·88]; girls, 0·70 [0·53, 0·92]). Few associations with snack ED and food groups were observed for individual and social-environmental factors. Among US adolescents, snack ED and food groups consumed at snacks were associated with the source of snacks. However, few associations were found for individual and social-environmental factors. These findings suggest that public health interventions targeting convenient stores and vending machines may encourage healthier food choices.
