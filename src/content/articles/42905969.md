### 重點摘要
孟加拉國雖已制定母乳替代品（BMS）法案以保護嬰兒營養，但其執行與合規性仍存在顯著挑戰。本研究採用混合方法，評估了醫療專業人員、孕婦、母親/照護者及零售商對該法案的知識、執行和遵守情況。結果顯示，法案知識水平普遍偏低，特別是在大眾與零售業者之間，且因醫療專業人員培訓不足、諮詢不一致以及持續的BMS促銷活動，導致法案的執行與合規性不佳。研究結論強調需加強執法、定期監測、強制醫療專業人員培訓，以改善法案的全面遵守。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了孟加拉國在母乳替代品（BMS）法案實施與合規性方面證據有限的研究空白，清晰地揭示了知識普及度、醫療專業人員培訓、法規執行與市場遵守度之間存在的顯著差距。未來的研究應著重於評估本文所建議的強化執法、監測機制及強制培訓等介入措施的有效性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Infant Nutrition Protection in Bangladesh: An Assessment of the Implementation and Compliance of the Breast Milk Substitutes Act and Rules.
**Abstract:** Breastfeeding is critical for infant survival, growth, and development; however, inappropriate marketing of breast-milk substitutes (BMS) continues to undermine optimal feeding practices. Bangladesh has adopted the International Code of Marketing of BMS through the BMS Act 2013 and its Rules 2017, yet evidence on implementation and compliance remains limited. This mixed-methods study assessed knowledge, implementation, and compliance with the BMS Act and its Rules across healthcare and retail settings in Bangladesh. Quantitative data were collected between September 2023 and June 2024 through interviews with 272 healthcare providers (HCPs), 136 pregnant women, 304 mothers/caregivers of children under 5 years, and observations of 150 retail shops and pharmacies. Qualitative data included in-depth interviews with HCPs and focus group discussions with mothers, caregivers, shopkeepers, and journalists. Knowledge of the BMS Act and its Rules was reported by 62.1% of HCPs but was substantially lower among pregnant women (39.4%), mothers/caregivers (38.2%-38.7%), and retail sellers (26.3%). Implementation of the legislation was constrained by limited formal training of HCPs and inconsistent counselling on BMS-related issues. Compliance was further undermined by continued promotional activities and non-prescription sales of BMS products, indicating persistent gaps between the legal framework and its implementation. Fewer than half of HCPs had received formal training, and important knowledge gaps persisted regarding promotional restrictions and penalties. Qualitative findings highlighted weak enforcement, limited counselling on BMS during maternal care, and social and clinical pressures contributing to inappropriate BMS use. Despite favourable breastfeeding practices, implementation and compliance with the BMS Act and its Rules remain suboptimal. Stronger enforcement, including consistent application of Section 18 through the Mobile Court Act, routine monitoring, mandatory HCPs' training, and institutionalized WHO NetCode monitoring are needed to improve compliance.
