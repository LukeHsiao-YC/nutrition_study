### 重點摘要
精準營養學雖已證明個體對飲食反應存在異質性，但其將生物預測轉化為具備臨床意義、成本效益、可擴展性與公平性的介入措施仍面臨挑戰。本文指出精準營養已達關鍵十字路口，並提出「情境式精準營養」框架，旨在整合個體生物反應與行為、心理、社會、文化、經濟及環境等廣泛的情境因素。此框架強調生物資訊應能實際改善飲食策略選擇，並考量其傳遞與維持的可行性。最終，精準營養的成功將取決於其能否有效將生物知識轉化為具臨床效益、可擴展、公平且可持續的介入方案，以促進個體與群體健康。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文章指出精準營養學在「發現其生物異質性」與「證明其臨床介入效益」之間存在鴻溝。過去的研究主要聚焦於生物預測，卻缺乏足夠證據證明其在長期健康成果、成本效益、可擴展性及公平性方面能超越既有的實證照護。本文透過提出「情境式精準營養」框架，填補了如何將生物知識有效轉化為具臨床實用性、可規模化、公平且可持續的介入策略之研究空白，並為未來研究如何整合生物資訊與社會情境因素提供方向。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Perspective. Precision Nutrition at a Crossroads: From Biological Prediction to Contextual Implementation.
**Abstract:** Precision nutrition has substantially reshaped nutritional science by demonstrating that individuals can respond differently to identical dietary exposures. Advances in genomics, metabolomics, proteomics, microbiome research, wearable technologies, digital health, and artificial intelligence have improved our ability to characterize biological heterogeneity and predict individual responses to diet. However, demonstrating such variability does not necessarily result in nutritional strategies that provide clinically meaningful benefits beyond established evidence-based care. A critical distinction should therefore be made between precision nutrition as a discovery science, which has convincingly established interindividual heterogeneity in dietary responses, and precision nutrition as an intervention science, which must demonstrate meaningful incremental value over dietary approaches already supported by clinical evidence. Despite increasingly sophisticated technologies and predictive models, evidence of consistent improvements in long-term health outcomes, cost-effectiveness, scalability, and equity remains limited. In this Perspective, we argue that precision nutrition has reached a critical crossroads. We distinguish prediction of individual responses, which is only one route to precision nutrition, from treatment-effect heterogeneity, treatment selection and clinical utility. We then propose contextual precision nutrition as a translational framework that integrates biological responsiveness with the behavioral, psychological, social, cultural, economic, and environmental determinants of dietary behavior. Rather than claiming novelty for these components, the framework integrates them into a decision logic that asks whether biological information improves the selection of a dietary strategy beyond routine information and established care, and how that strategy can be delivered and sustained. It complements population-based dietary recommendations by refining their implementation and maximizing adherence and real-world effectiveness. Ultimately, the success of precision nutrition will be determined not by the sophistication of its predictions but by its ability to translate biological knowledge into interventions that are clinically effective, scalable, equitable, and sustainable, thereby improving both individual and population health.
