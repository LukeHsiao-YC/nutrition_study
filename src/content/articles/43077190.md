### 重點摘要
本範圍文獻回顧旨在運用美國國家少數民族健康與健康不平等研究所（NIMHD）研究框架，綜合分析美國鄉村地區孕婦吸菸的多層次相關因素及戒菸障礙。研究方法依循PRISMA-ScR指南，檢索並納入2014至2026年間發表的13篇研究，其結果依NIMHD框架歸類為個體、人際、社區層面因素，而社會層面證據有限。儘管本研究揭示了多層次影響因素，但目前證據主要集中於個體層面，對於其他層面的深入研究仍有待加強。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻綜合了美國鄉村地區孕婦吸菸及其戒菸障礙的多層次影響因素，填補了該領域的證據空白。然而，研究同時指出未來研究的限制在於現有證據主要集中於個體層面，而人際、社區和社會層面的影響因素缺乏足夠探討。因此，未來的研究應著重於這些層面，以制定更全面、符合當地情境的多層次戒菸策略。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Factors Associated with Prenatal Tobacco Use and Barriers to Cessation Among Rural Populations in the United States: A Scoping Review.
**Abstract:** This scoping review uses the U.S. National Institute on Minority Health and Health Disparities (NIMHD) Research Framework to synthesize evidence on multilevel factors associated with prenatal tobacco use and barriers to cessation among rural U.S. A protocol was developed a priori and registered with the Open Science Framework. The review followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR). Four databases were searched for English-language articles focused on prenatal tobacco use in rural U.S. populations, published between January 2014 and August 2026. Two reviewers independently screened records. Findings were mapped to NIMHD domains and levels of influence. Methodological quality was appraised. Of 1,098 database records and one additional record, 13 studies met eligibility criteria: ten quantitative, two qualitative, and one mixed-methods study. Eleven studies were rated good; two moderate. Factors at the individual level included stress, coping, tobacco-related beliefs, age, education, and socioeconomic characteristics. Factors at the interpersonal level included household environment, tobacco use within social networks, and patient-clinician communication. Factors at the community level included rural/geographic residence, county socioeconomic context, community tobacco norms and messaging, and access to cessation services. At the societal level, evidence was limited to state Tobacco 21 policy, examined in one national rural-urban study. Evidence on prenatal tobacco use among rural U.S. populations remains limited and concentrated at the individual level of influence. Further research examining interpersonal, community, and societal levels of influence is needed to inform multilevel, contextually appropriate tobacco cessation strategies.
