### 重點摘要
這項研究旨在更新愛爾蘭2023年與疾病相關的營養不良（DRM）的盛行率及其對公共醫療和社會照護支出的影響。研究人員根據2023年的全國營養不良調查數據，並參考英國和歐洲的研究，估計了在急性醫院、長期照護和初級照護環境中的盛行率，並利用官方單位成本數據估算了相關的公共支出。結果顯示，2023年DRM的時點盛行率為成人人口的5.1%（約22萬人），其中59%為65歲以上，每年造成的公共成本估計為27億歐元，佔2023年公共醫療預算的11.5%。自2008年以來，DRM的負擔已增長90%，主要集中在老年人，對愛爾蘭醫療系統構成巨大且日益增長的壓力，因此建議將系統性篩查和營養介入擴展到高風險的日間病房和社區環境。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究更新了愛爾蘭2023年與疾病相關營養不良的最新盛行率和經濟負擔數據，填補了此領域的時效性研究空白。儘管醫院和安養院已常規篩查，但研究指出，高風險的日間病房和社區環境仍缺乏系統性篩查和營養師介入，這是一項未來的研究限制或政策改進方向。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The prevalence and economic burden of disease-related malnutrition in Ireland: an update.
**Abstract:** To update estimated prevalence of disease-related malnutrition (DRM) in Ireland and its impact on public health and social-care expenditure in 2023. Prevalence by care setting was derived from the 2023 IrSPEN/INDI National Malnutrition Survey (NMS23) and age-standardised comparisons with UK and European studies where Irish data were incomplete. Public costs were estimated by applying unit-cost data from official sources to activity attributable to DRM, based on point prevalence, with sensitivity analyses used to reflect uncertainty regarding variation in prevalence and higher healthcare utilisation of patients with DRM not captured in point prevalence estimates. Acute hospitals (excluding maternity), long-term and primary care. Adults aged ≥18 years receiving hospital or specified community health/social-care services in 2023. Point prevalence of DRM in 2023 was 5·1 % of the adult population, affecting approximately 220 000 people, 59 % aged ≥65 years. The annual public cost attributable to DRM was estimated at €2·7 billion, 11·5 % of the 2023 public healthcare budget. Hospital care accounted for 61 % of DRM-related costs. Since 2008, the national burden has increased by 90 %, driven by ageing, higher hospital activity and rising unit costs. Public expenditure on oral and enteral nutrition support represented less than 2 % of DRM-attributable costs. DRM imposes a substantial and growing burden on Ireland's healthcare system, concentrated in older adults. While screening is now routine in hospitals and nursing homes, extending systematic screening and dietetic intervention to high-risk day-ward and community settings is warranted to prevent continued increases in prevalence and cost.
