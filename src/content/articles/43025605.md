### 重點摘要
鑑於美國大學生糧食不安全問題在COVID-19大流行後日益突出，且聯邦立法尚未通過，本研究旨在更新對此現象的評估。這項範圍審查系統地綜合了1995年至2026年間的文獻，分析了美國四年制公立大學學生在疫情前、疫情期間及疫情後的糧食不安全盛行率，並探討了其與各州國會代表團多數黨之間的關係。結果顯示，疫情前、期間和後的平均糧食不安全盛行率分別為39%、36%和41%，其中共和黨佔多數的州（43%）的盛行率高於民主黨或兩黨平分（35%）的州。儘管存在黨派差異，美國大學生糧食不安全問題仍普遍嚴重，凸顯了擴大學生參與聯邦糧食援助計畫等全國性政策行動的必要性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了COVID-19大流行後美國大學生糧食不安全狀況缺乏更新且全面的評估的空白，尤其透過比較疫情前、中、後不同時期的盛行率，並首次將其與各州國會代表團多數黨的政治格局進行關聯分析，為理解政策制定者角色提供了新視角。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** A scoping review of food insecurity in U.S. higher education by state's congressional delegation majority.
**Abstract:** Since the COVID-19 pandemic, the literature on U.S. college student food insecurity has expanded, warranting an updated review. The pandemic represented a unique period in which emergency aid may have influenced students' food security status, highlighting the importance of examining the prevalence of food insecurity before, during, and after the pandemic. Additionally, introduced federal legislation aimed at addressing student food insecurity has yet to be enacted. Because congressional delegates help shape the federal policy landscape, examining food insecurity in relation to states' congressional delegations may offer new insight. This scoping review aimed to critically synthesize peer-reviewed publications and grey literature (i.e., institutional reports) on the prevalence of food insecurity among college students attending U.S. four-year public institutions by pre-pandemic, pandemic, and post-pandemic periods, and to document the prevalence by U.S. states' congressional delegation majorities. MEDLINE-PubMed, Web of Science, ProQuest, and EBSCO Host Academic Search Complete were searched for articles published between September 1995 and July 2026. Search terms included "food security" or "food insecurity", "university" or "college" or "students", and "prevalence" or "rate". A hand search was conducted to identify institutional reports on student food insecurity. A total of 48 studies were eligible for full-text analysis. The mean prevalence of food insecurity was 39% pre-pandemic (n=18), 36% during the pandemic (n=12), and 41% post-pandemic (n=18). Higher proportions of food insecurity were observed in states with Republican-majority congressional delegations (43%) compared to those with Democratic-majority (35%) or split delegations (35%). College student food insecurity remains high across the U.S., regardless of state congressional delegation majority, suggesting federal action is needed, such as expanding student access to federal food assistance programs.
