### 重點摘要
這項前瞻性世代研究旨在探討侵入性機械通氣的重症成人患者中，膳食纖維和液體攝取與便秘風險的關聯性，因便秘在重症患者中很常見且其營養成分的作用不明。研究納入74名接受腸道營養的重症患者，追蹤長達144小時，並使用離散時間存活分析評估纖維和液體攝取（時間變量暴露）與便秘的關係。結果顯示54%的患者出現便秘，但在調整後，纖維攝取量和液體給予量與便秘之間均未觀察到顯著關聯。研究結論為在重症機械通氣患者中，纖維和液體攝取與便秘無關，這可能與重症早期胃腸道功能障礙受疾病嚴重度和藥理因素影響較大有關。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究旨在釐清重症機械通氣患者中，膳食纖維和液體攝取對便秘風險的影響，填補了該領域的知識空白。然而，研究結果指出，由於重症早期胃腸道功能障礙可能更受疾病嚴重程度和藥理因素的影響，因此這些發現可能反映了此階段有限的暴露，限制了結果的普適性，並提示未來研究需考慮這些潛在的混雜因素。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Fiber and fluid administration and the risk of constipation in critically ill adults on mechanical ventilation: A prospective cohort study.
**Abstract:** Constipation is a common complication in critically ill patients and has been associated with adverse clinical outcomes. However, the role of enteral nutrition composition, particularly fiber and fluid administration, remains unclear. The aim of this study was to evaluate the association between dietary fiber and fluid administration and constipation in invasive mechanical ventilated critically ill adults. We conducted a prospective cohort study including 74 critically ill adults requiring invasive mechanical ventilation and enteral nutrition. Patients were followed for up to 144 h from initiation of mechanical ventilation. Fiber and total fluid administration were evaluated as time-varying exposures. Constipation was defined as absence of defecation within 144 h. Discrete-time survival analysis was used to assess associations. Constipation occurred in 54% of patients. Time to first defecation ranged from 48 to 504 h. Most patients (97.3%) received early enteral nutrition, although fiber and fluid delivery were frequently below guideline-recommended targets. No significant association was observed between fiber intake or fluid administration and constipation after adjustment, with results expressed as odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) (fiber: OR = 1.17, 95% CI: 0.41-3.38; fluids: OR = 1.40, 95% CI: 0.29-6.84). In critically ill patients receiving invasive mechanical ventilation, fiber and fluid administration were not associated with constipation. These findings may reflect limited exposure during the early phase of critical illness, when gastrointestinal dysfunction is more strongly influenced by disease severity and pharmacological factors.
