### 重點摘要
病患在疼痛藥物管理決策中常難以權衡利弊，本範圍界定回顧旨在系統性地評估止痛藥物管理病患決策輔助工具（PtDAs）的特性、內容與有效性。研究納入39個PtDAs和13項評估研究，發現PtDAs主要針對骨關節炎和背痛等慢性病症，並常將藥物與非藥物療法比較，但普遍缺乏藥物安全性、有效性與成本的詳細資訊。儘管如此，使用PtDAs仍能提高病患知識並減少決策衝突。為提升未來PtDAs的品質，應加強藥物專屬內容的細節，包含預期使用期間、與保守治療的區分以及治療成本和負擔的考量。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本回顧填補了止痛藥物管理病患決策輔助工具（PtDAs）的性質、內容與品質評估的空白。研究指出當前PtDAs普遍缺乏藥物安全性、有效性與成本的詳細資訊，且未能充分闡明藥物預期使用期間及與保守治療的區別，這些是未來PtDAs開發需重點加強的領域。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Patient decision aids for pain medication management: A scoping review.
**Abstract:** Medications are commonly used to manage pain; however, patients may find it challenging to weigh benefits and harms within the context of their own clinical circumstances, goals, and preferences. Patient Decision Aids (PtDAs) can improve knowledge and engagement in health decisions, yet the nature, content, and quality of PtDAs for pain medication management have not been evaluated. This scoping review aimed to identify, describe, and appraise PtDAs for pain medication management, including their characteristics and content, and synthesise evidence from primary studies evaluating their use, including effects on decision-making and medication-related outcomes. A systematic search was conducted across MEDLINE, Embase, PsycINFO, and CINAHL, as well as eight decision aid repositories, and Google. Screening and data extraction were performed in duplicate, capturing target conditions, decision options, formats, and outcomes. PtDAs were appraised using the International Patient Decision Aid Standards instrument (IPDASi) and evaluation studies using the Standards for UNiversal reporting of patient Decision Aid Evaluations (SUNDAE) checklist. Thirty-nine PtDAs and 13 evaluation studies were included. PtDAs commonly targeted chronic conditions including osteoarthritis (49%) and back pain (21%), with nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (77%), corticosteroid injections (74%), and paracetamol (56%) the most frequently included medications. Most PtDAs compared medications to non-pharmacological strategies (e.g., exercise, surgery) but lacked detailed information on safety, effectiveness, and cost. Despite this, randomised controlled trials and pre-post studies generally reported improved knowledge and reduced decisional conflict following PtDA use. Given the central role of medications in pain management, PtDAs should place greater emphasis on medication-specific content. This review identifies key priorities for PtDA development, including clarifying intended duration of use, distinguishing medications from 'conservative care', and considering treatment costs and burdens. Implementing these recommendations may support the development of tools that better reflect the complexities of pain medication decision-making across the continuum of care.
