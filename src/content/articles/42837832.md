### 重點摘要
這項單臂試驗旨在探討女性對於個人化乳癌風險評估結果的懷疑態度如何影響其資訊尋求行為。研究招募了694名主要為非裔美國女性，讓她們完成Gail模型並評估10年乳癌風險，隨後調查她們對結果的懷疑程度及資訊尋求行為。結果顯示，認為自己乳癌風險低於Gail模型估計值的受試者（低估者），其尋求與風險評估一致資訊的可能性顯著較低。這項發現指出，個人對自身風險的低估可能導致他們尋求與評估不符的健康資訊，進而阻礙知情決策及風險分層篩檢的有效實施。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究揭示了個人對乳癌風險評估的懷疑態度，特別是低估自身風險時，如何影響其資訊尋求行為，進而成為知情決策和風險分層篩檢實施的障礙。然而，作為一項單臂試驗，它未包含對照組，且研究對象主要為特定族群（非裔美國女性），限制了結果的普適性。未來的研究需要探索如何有效應對這種懷疑態度，並引導個體尋求與其風險狀況相符的健康資訊。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The effect of skepticism of personalized breast cancer risk results on information seeking behavior: A single-arm trial.
**Abstract:** To investigate how expressing skepticism in response to receiving a personalized breast cancer risk estimate is associated with seeking information that is consistent vs. inconsistent with the risk estimate received. We recruited women from the Dynata internet research panel to complete the Gail model and receive their personalized 10-year risk of developing invasive breast cancer. Participants then completed a survey assessing their skepticism of the results, information seeking behavior, and socio-demographics. "Risk-concordant" information seeking was operationalized as participants clicking a link to receive breast cancer screening information that matched their Gail model risk estimate (e.g., an average-risk woman choosing information for women at average risk). The analytic sample (N = 694) comprised 98% self-reported Black participants and 56% no college degree. The mean age was 56 years. Overall, 211 (31.0%) of participants agreed with their Gail model results, 346 (50.1%) thought their risk was lower than the Gail model results (i.e., they were underestimators), and 124 (18.2%) thought their risk was higher than the Gail model results (i.e., they were overestimators). Compared to participants who agreed with their estimate, underestimators had lower odds of risk-concordant information seeking, OR= 0.62, 95% CI [0.44, 0.87]. The odds of risk-concordant information seeking did not differ between participants who agreed with their risk estimate and those who were overestimators, OR= 0.85, 95% CI [0.54, 1.32]. Individuals who think their risk is lower than their Gail model estimate may seek health information that is inconsistent with their risk estimate. Seeking health information that differs from an individual's personalized risk estimate may impede informed decision making and successful implementation of risk-stratified screening.
