### 重點摘要
乳糖是嬰幼兒關鍵營養素，但全球約三分之二成人面臨乳糖酶活性降低，導致乳糖消化能力差異。本研究旨在釐清乳糖消化不良（生理狀態）與乳糖不耐症（臨床症狀）的區別，並綜合隨機對照試驗、微生物學研究及流行病學數據進行審視。結果顯示，多數乳糖消化不良者在中等乳製品攝取量下並無顯著症狀，且腸道可透過漸進性乳糖暴露來適應並提高耐受性。鑑於乳製品對預防慢性病的健康益處，全球飲食指南仍建議攝取，強調需正確認識這些差異以制定有效的公共衛生策略。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻主要填補了醫學研究與公共衛生領域中，對「乳糖消化不良」（Lactose Maldigestion）和「乳糖不耐症」（Lactose Intolerance）這兩個概念的定義混淆與誤解。它明確區分了兩者的生理與臨床差異，並揭示儘管乳糖酶缺乏普遍存在，但大多數人在中等乳糖攝取下並無顯著症狀，甚至能透過腸道適應機制改善耐受性，糾正了普遍認為乳糖消化不良必然導致嚴重不耐的錯誤觀念。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Lactose, Lactose Maldigestion and Lactose Intolerance: Definitions, Misunderstandings, Mechanisms, and Public Health Implications.
**Abstract:** Lactose is the principal carbohydrate in milk, serving a critical role in infant energy metabolism as well as contributing to neurodevelopment, and the establishment of a healthy gut microbiota. Later in life, lactose digestion capacity varies widely across human populations owing to the genetically determined decline of intestinal lactase expression after weaning-a condition termed 'lactase non-persistence'. Approximately two-thirds of the global adult population exhibits reduced lactase activity, yet the clinical consequences of this enzymatic reduction are far less uniform than commonly assumed. This review provides a comprehensive examination of lactose functionality, the physiology of lactose digestion, and the consequences of lactase persistence and non-persistence. We present formal definitions for the spectrum of terms used in lactose research-including lactase persistence, lactase non-persistence, lactose maldigestion, and lactose intolerance-and argue that the critical distinction between lactose maldigestion (a physiological state) and lactose intolerance (a clinical syndrome) is frequently overlooked in both research and public health. Drawing on evidence from double-blind randomised controlled trials , microbiome intervention studies, and global epidemiological data, the evidence indicates that the majority of individuals with lactose maldigestion do not develop clinically meaningful symptoms at moderate intake levels of most dairy products. We further discuss the mechanism of colonic adaptation, whereby gradual lactose exposure selectively enriches Bifidobacterium populations capable of fermenting lactose without excessive gas production resulting in greater lactose tolerance. Finally, we examine the public health implications of these findings, noting that food-based dietary guidelines worldwide-including those in populations with high prevalence of lactase non-persistence-consistently recommend dairy consumption owing to its established associations with reduced risk of type 2 diabetes, colorectal cancer, and improved bone health.
