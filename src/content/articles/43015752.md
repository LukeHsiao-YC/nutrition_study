### 重點摘要
本研究旨在評估改良型身體形態指數（ABSI）與中老年人認知功能障礙發生率之間的關聯。透過分析4,353名CHARLS隊列參與者的縱向數據，結果顯示ABSI最高的四分位組相較於最低組，其認知功能障礙的風險顯著增高（HR=1.22），且呈現顯著的劑量反應模式。此關聯在女性和70歲及以上個體中更為明顯。研究結論指出，高ABSI水平與中老年人認知功能障礙的長期風險增加相關，提示ABSI可作為中心型肥胖的有效替代標誌物，有助於早期識別高風險個體。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究彌補了傳統體重指數（BMI）在評估內臟脂肪不足及在老年人群中存在「肥胖悖論」的局限性。它提供改良型ABSI作為一個更有效、更精確的中心型肥胖替代標誌物，以早期識別具有認知功能障礙高風險的個體。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Association between modified a body shape index and incident cognitive impairment in middle-aged and older adults: a prospective cohort study.
**Abstract:** Central obesity contributes significantly to cognitive decline. Traditional indices like BMI inadequately capture visceral adiposity and exhibit the obesity paradox in elderly populations.This study looked into the connection between a modified A Body Shape Index (ABSI) and the development of cognitive impairment in middle-aged and older adults. Longitudinal data from 4,353 eligible participants in the 2011-2020 CHARLS cohort were analyzed. Modified ABSI was calculated based on waist circumference, BMI, and height and categorized into quartiles. Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to evaluate the association between modified ABSI and incident cognitive impairment. The fully adjusted model included age, sex, residence, education, marital status, smoking, drinking, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, dyslipidemia, stroke, heart disease, systolic and diastolic blood pressure, triglycerides, LDL-C, HDL-C, HbA1c, CRP, serum creatinine, and uric acid. Restricted cubic spline, subgroup, and sensitivity analyses were also performed. Over the follow-up period, cognitive impairment occurred in 1,822 participants, corresponding to 41.9% of the study population. In the Cox model that was fully adjusted, individuals in the highest ABSI quartile (Q4) exhibited a markedly elevated hazard of cognitive impairment relative to those in the lowest quartile (Q1) (HR = 1.22, P = 0.024). A pronounced dose-response pattern was evident, which persisted as statistically significant and linear following comprehensive covariate adjustment (P = 0.037). Subgroup analyses indicated more pronounced associations among female participants and individuals aged 70 years or older (P < 0.05). High ABSI levels are significantly associated with an increased long-term risk of cognitive impairment in middle-aged and older adults. The findings suggest that ABSI could serve as an effective surrogate marker for central adiposity, facilitating the early recognition of individuals at heightened risk for cognitive decline.
