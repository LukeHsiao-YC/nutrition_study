### 重點摘要
本探索性研究旨在探討癌症治療共同決策（SDM）諮詢中時間的使用方式與討論，並描述互動空間。研究音頻記錄了84例眼部黑色素瘤患者與其眼科腫瘤醫師的諮詢，並開發量化編碼系統評估時間利用及談論方式，同時使用UCAT工具評估互動空間。結果顯示，患者主要利用時間獲取診斷與治療資訊，但很少主動提供個人觀點；腫瘤醫師在決定治療前也鮮少主動探詢非臨床的病患相關資訊。研究強調，此類非臨床資訊對整合醫學專業與病患個人情況至關重要，醫師應與患者討論治療選項如何符合其個人處境。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究揭示了當前共同決策實踐中的一個關鍵空白：儘管諮詢時間充足，醫師仍未能主動探詢並整合患者的非臨床個人觀點於治療決策中，從而阻礙了真正個人化的共同決策過程。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Use of time in shared decision-making: An explorative study.
**Abstract:** Time constraints are often reported as barrier to shared decision-making (SDM). Both cancer patients and physicians report needing time before, during, and/or after consultations to achieve patient involvement in treatment decisions. Perceived time for involving patients in decision-making may further be related to how much space participants have to contribute to the interaction. This study aimed to 1) investigate how time is used and talked about regarding cancer treatment decision-making in consultations in which SDM is appropriate, and 2) describe interactional space of consultations. 84 consultations between eye melanoma patients and their ocular oncologist in which final diagnosis and treatment options (surgery or radiation) were discussed, were audio recorded. We developed a quantitative coding system to rate how time is used and talked about during the consultations. We used items from the Unhurried Conversations Assessment Tool (UCAT) to assess observed interactional space. All analyses were performed in SPSS Statistics version 29.0. About one-third of patients had heard their (expected) diagnosis in advance. Half or more patients asked questions regarding diagnosis or treatment options. In one-fifth of consultations, ocular oncologists asked questions to get to know the patient, and in most they explicitly stated that the patient had time to think about the decision. Overall, participants' pace during consultations was slow. During consultations, patients use time to obtain information about diagnosis and treatment options. Patients do not routinely offer personal perspectives and ocular oncologists do not seek non-clinical patient-related information before a provisional or final treatment decision is made. Such information is however essential to integrate medical expertise with individual patients' situation. Especially when treatments markedly differ, physicians should discuss with patients how options fit patients' personal situation.
