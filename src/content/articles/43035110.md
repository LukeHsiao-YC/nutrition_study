### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討種族歧視如何影響醫療複雜性黑人兒童照顧者的醫療尋求行為和溝通方式。研究透過對31位黑人照顧者進行半結構式訪談，揭示了他們在醫療照護中經歷的五個歧視主題，包括被忽視、刻板印象、害怕報復、去人性化及資源差異。這些經驗導致照顧者不信任、身心俱疲及認為負面健康結果，並影響他們選擇隱瞞擔憂、積極倡導或選擇特定臨床醫師等應對策略。研究結果強調醫療系統與臨床醫師需建立信任夥伴關係、解決結構性不平等並提供以家庭為中心的公平照護。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻透過深入的定性訪談，填補了關於種族歧視如何具體塑造黑人醫療複雜性兒童照顧者醫療尋求行為與醫病溝通的研究空白。它提供了豐富的第一手經驗敘述，解釋了歧視如何影響照顧者的信任度、心理健康及醫療決策。未來的研究可以進一步探索開發和評估旨在減少醫療系統中歧視並增強信任的干預措施，並擴大研究範圍以包含更多元地理區域的參與者。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** How Discrimination Shapes Care-Seeking for Black Caregivers of Children With Medical Complexity.
**Abstract:** Black children with medical complexity (CMC) experience a disproportionately high number of hospitalizations and emergency department (ED) visits. This disparity may reflect systemic and interpersonal racism and discrimination. Understanding Black caregivers' experiences is essential to designing programs that reduce acute care use. This study described caregivers' experiences of racism and discrimination and explored how these experiences influence care-seeking and communication among Black caregivers of CMC. In partnership with family advocacy organizations, we recruited Black caregivers of CMC via national advocacy groups, social media, and electronic health record messaging. Eligible caregivers had a child aged 0 to 21 years with a hospitalization or ED visit in the previous year and a score of 3 or more on the Children with Special Health Care Needs screener. Purposive sampling ensured that one-third of participants were from rural areas. Semistructured interviews, guided by Critical Race Theory, were recorded, transcribed, and analyzed thematically. Thirty-one caregivers completed interviews. Caregivers described discrimination in health care based on race and other characteristics. Five themes emerged in caregivers' experiences of racism: dismissal, stereotyping, fear of retaliation, dehumanization, and differential access to resources. These experiences contributed to distrust, caregiver burnout, and perceived negative health outcomes. Caregivers' responses to discrimination included withholding concerns, advocating, documenting, selecting specific clinicians, managing perceptions, and staying informed. Perceived racism and discrimination shaped how Black caregivers of CMC communicated with clinicians and navigated health care, underscoring the need for health care systems and clinicians to foster trusting partnerships, address structural inequities, and provide equitable, family-centered care.
