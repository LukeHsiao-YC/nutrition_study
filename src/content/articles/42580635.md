### 重點摘要
次撒哈拉非洲心臟代謝疾病日益加重，永續飲食可能為其預防之道。本研究利用RODAM世代研究的縱向數據，分析1,499名迦納成年人的最佳化永續飲食指數（ODI）與肥胖、第二型糖尿病和高血壓的關聯。研究發現，ODI與一般性肥胖、第二型糖尿病或高血壓無顯著關聯，但ODI每增加10分，腹部肥胖風險反而增加19%，尤其在迦納地區。研究結論認為，該隊列對永續飲食的依從性普遍偏低，且依從性變異小可能解釋了永續飲食與腹部肥胖風險增加的意外發現。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻探討了次撒哈拉非洲轉型期成年人永續飲食與心臟代謝疾病的關聯，填補了該領域在考慮環境、經濟、營養及文化等多重標準下，永續飲食對健康影響的實證研究空白。然而，研究發現永續飲食與腹部肥胖風險增加相關，且受試者對永續飲食的依從性普遍偏低且變化不大，這指出未來研究需更深入探討實施永續飲食的障礙，並重新評估其在特定族群中的健康效益，或改良永續飲食的定義與測量方式，以更好地指導公共衛生策略。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Sustainable diets and cardiometabolic conditions among Ghanaian adults under transition: Findings from the RODAM-Pros Cohort Study.
**Abstract:** In sub-Saharan Africa, the burden of cardiometabolic conditions has rapidly emerged. Sustainable diets that are nutritious, environmentally friendly, economically affordable, and socio-culturally acceptable, may support the prevention of cardiometabolic conditions. This study aimed at identifying associations of optimized sustainable diets with incident general/abdominal obesity, type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2D), and hypertension among Ghanaian adults living in Ghana and Amsterdam. We used longitudinal data from the Research on Obesity and Diabetes among African Migrants study involving Ghanaian adults (age: 20-82 years). Baseline dietary data was collected using a Ghana-specific food propensity questionnaire. To construct an Optimized Diet Index (ODI, 0-90 score points), we used baseline adherence to previously identified site- and sex-specific diets, meeting nutritional adequacy, cultural acceptability, and 50% reduction of greenhouse gas emissions (GHGEs) and food costs. Multivariable-adjusted logistic regression assessed associations between ODI per 10-point increase and risk of general/abdominal obesity, T2D, and hypertension, reported as odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). In the analytical sample (n=1,499, women=66%, mean age=47±11 years, mean follow-up time=7±0.5 years, median ODI=29 with interquartile range=23-37), higher ODI scores were related with residence in Ghana, lower total energy intake, older age, and lower educational level. We observed no associations of baseline ODI with incident general obesity, type 2 diabetes or hypertension. A 10-point increase in ODI was associated with a 19% higher risk of abdominal obesity (95% CI: 1.00, 1.42; p=0.049), significantly in rural and urban Ghana. Overall adherence to the optimized sustainable diets is low among this cohort of Ghanaian adults. Sustainable diets are related to increased risk of abdominal obesity in the Ghana. Low variation in the poor adherence may explain the unexpected results.
