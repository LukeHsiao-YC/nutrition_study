### 重點摘要
這篇系統性回顧和統合分析旨在評估特定益生菌L. reuteri DSM 17938在兒童功能性腹痛症 (FAP) 和功能性便秘 (FC) 的療效。研究方法是分析多個隨機對照試驗 (RCTs)，共納入302名FAP兒童和276名FC兒童的數據。結果顯示，L. reuteri DSM 17938無論在改善FAP的反應率、腹痛頻率與強度，或FC的排便頻率方面，均未比安慰劑組有顯著益處。因此，本研究結論L. reuteri DSM 17938在兒童FAP或FC上並無臨床上顯著的效益。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了L. reuteri DSM 17938益生菌在兒童功能性腹痛症與功能性便秘治療效果不明確的研究空白，明確指出其未展現臨床顯著益處。未來的研究限制是需要更多高質量的、針對特定菌株且具有標準化結果測量的隨機對照試驗，以進一步釐清益生菌在此類疾病中的作用。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Limosilactobacillus reuteri DSM 17938 in pediatric functional abdominal pain disorders and functional constipation: A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials.
**Abstract:** Functional abdominal pain (FAP) and functional constipation (FC), are common in children and affect quality of life. Dysfunction of the microbiota-gut-brain axis may contribute to their pathophysiology, with growing interest in probiotic interventions. Limosilactobacillus reuteri (L. reuteri) DSM 17938 has been widely studied, but its efficacy remains unclear. This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to evaluate the efficacy of L. reuteri DSM 17938 in pediatric FAP and FC. Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) published up to October 2025 were identified from major databases. Eligible studies included children diagnosed by Rome criteria and treated with L. reuteri DSM 17938 as a single strain. Primary outcomes were responder rates for FAP and normalization of stool frequency for FC, with abdominal pain frequency and intensity assessed as secondary outcomes for FAP. Five RCTs, including 302 children with FAP, and five RCTs, including 276 children with FC, were analyzed. In FAP, L. reuteri DSM 17938 did not significantly improve responder rates (risk ratio [RR] 1.11, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.83-1.47, p = 0.49), abdominal pain frequency (p = 0.91; 95% CI -0.42 to 0.38), or pain intensity (p = 0.14, 95% CI -1.07 to 0.15) compared with placebo in meta-analysis. In FC, the pooled analysis showed no significant difference in stool frequency (RR 1.1, 95% CI 0.94-1.27, p = 0.27). In this systematic review and meta-analysis, L. reuteri DSM 17938 did not demonstrate clinically significant benefits in pediatric FAP or FC. Further well-powered, strain-specific RCTs with standardized outcome measures are warranted to clarify its role.
