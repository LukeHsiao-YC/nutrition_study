### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討貝南共和國婦女在家中決策權對五歲以下兒童營養狀況的影響，因為當地兒童營養不良是死亡和發病的主因。研究利用2017-2018年貝南人口與健康調查數據，分析4,093名兒童的複合人體測量失敗指數，並透過婦女自述及夫妻交叉回答評估婦女決策權，採用遞歸雙變量probit模型分析。結果顯示，婦女整體決策權並未顯著降低兒童人體測量失敗的機率。然而，當丈夫「授予」妻子決策權時，反而增加了兒童營養不良的機率，這可能反映了在經濟脆弱背景下，權力轉移伴隨著責任卻缺乏足夠資源，因此強化婦女決策權的政策需配合改善其經濟資源的獲取與控制。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究超越了以往僅依賴婦女自述來衡量決策權的限制，透過納入夫妻雙方交叉回應，揭示了不同形式決策權（尤其是丈夫授予的權力）對兒童營養狀況的複雜影響。它強調了單純賦予決策權若無經濟資源配合的局限性，為未來政策制定提供了更細緻的考量，即需同時關注權力與資源的平衡，而非僅著重於決策權的賦予。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Measuring Women's Decision-Making Power Beyond Self-Reports: Evidence on the Nutritional Status of Children in Benin.
**Abstract:** In Benin, malnutrition is the main risk factor for mortality and morbidity among young children. Women's decision-making power within the household appears to be a factor that promotes adequate nutrition for children, both in terms of quantity and quality. The objective of this study is to analyze the effect of women's decision-making power within the household on the nutritional status of children under 5 in Benin. The data used come from the 2017-2018 Benin Demographic and Health Survey, and a total of 4,093 children under the age of 5 were included in the analysis. We use the composite index of anthropometric failure as a measure of children's nutritional status because of its ability to estimate the full extent of child malnutrition in the population. Women's decision-making power is measured using women's own responses and cross-referenced responses from both spouses on household decision-making. For the empirical modeling of the study, we use the recursive bivariate probit model to account for the endogeneity of women's decision-making power. Overall, women's decision-making power does not significantly reduce the probability of experiencing anthropometric failure among children, but it increases this probability when the husband grants decision-making power to his wife. This type of power reflecting a disagreement between spouses regarding decision-making within the household, undercuts, rather than improve women's ability to influence their children's nutritional status. The authority granted by the husband may involve a transfer of responsibility in a context of economic vulnerability. Thus, a woman may be recognized as a decision-maker but still lack sufficient resources, namely the financial or material means, to ensure adequate food for her children or improve their nutritional status. Policies aimed at strengthening women's decision-making power should be accompanied by mechanisms to improve access to and control over economic resources, so that this power may lead to better child nutritional status.
