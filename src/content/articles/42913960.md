### 重點摘要
該研究旨在量化美國成年人重度憂鬱症 (MDD) 所相關的增量醫療保健支出，鑑於MDD是美國失能的主要原因之一且與醫療利用增加相關。研究團隊分析2021-2023年醫療支出小組調查數據，利用兩部分模型並調整多種共變數後，估計MDD的歸因支出。結果顯示，2021-2023年間總個人醫療保健支出中有5.58%（約2023年的2045.8億美元）與MDD相關，並在不同支付方與服務類型間有顯著差異。這些發現強調了MDD巨大的經濟負擔，並突顯預防、早期識別與整合性行為醫療服務的重要性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究透過提供一份經共變數調整、且細分至不同支付方及服務類型的最新全國性重度憂鬱症 (MDD) 相關醫療支出估計，填補了對MDD經濟負擔精確量化評估的研究空白。這項數據為政策制定者提供了具體的證據，以優化資源配置，並強調了推動預防、早期干預和整合性行為健康服務的迫切性，從而減輕其對醫療系統的財政壓力。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Healthcare spending associated with major depressive disorder among U.S. adults.
**Abstract:** Major depressive disorder (MDD) is a leading contributor to disability in the United States and is associated with increased healthcare utilization. This study estimates covariate-adjusted incremental healthcare spending associated with MDD among U.S. adults. The 2021-2023 Medical Expenditure Panel Survey was used. MDD was identified using International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification (ICD-10-CM) category F32, which denotes a depressive episode. A two-part model was used to estimate healthcare spending, adjusting for sociodemographic characteristics, health- and behavior-related variables, selected chronic physical conditions, census region, and year fixed-effects. Covariate-adjusted incremental spending associated with MDD was defined as difference between predicted spending under observed conditions and a counterfactual scenario in which adults with MDD were assumed not to have MDD. Estimated attributable fractions were applied to 2023 National Health Expenditure Accounts personal healthcare spending, excluding dental services and individuals aged ≤18 years, to generate national spending estimates. An estimated 5.58% of the $3.67 trillion in total personal healthcare spending-excluding dental services and individuals aged ≤18 years-was associated with MDD in the pooled 2021-2023 analysis, corresponding to $204.58 billion in 2023. By payer, MDD-associated spending accounted for 10.00% of Medicaid, 4.90% of Medicare, and 11.77% of other federal government spending; by service type, it accounted for 6.23% of non-inpatient and 4.22% of prescription drug spending. These findings highlight substantial healthcare spending associated with MDD and underscore the importance of prevention, early identification, and access to evidence-based and integrated behavioral healthcare to mitigate this burden.
