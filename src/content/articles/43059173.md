### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討心臟手術患者中，早期增加能量和蛋白質供給與60天院內死亡率的關聯，以及營養風險（mNUTRIC評分）是否為修飾因子。這項回溯性世代研究分析了292名重症心臟手術患者的數據，這些患者在加護病房前四天未能完全口服進食。結果顯示，多變數調整後，早期增加的能量和蛋白質供給與60天院內死亡率並無顯著關聯，且mNUTRIC評分未顯著修飾此關聯。研究結論指出，對於需要持續腸道或腸外營養的心臟手術患者，mNUTRIC評分未能識別出從早期增加能量和蛋白質供給中受益的患者。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇研究填補了在心臟手術患者中，營養風險是否會修飾早期能量和蛋白質供給與死亡率之間關聯的空白，結果顯示並無顯著修飾作用。研究的限制在於mNUTRIC評分未能識別出從早期增加能量和蛋白質供給中受益的患者，提示未來研究可能需要探索更精準的營養干預策略或評估工具。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Association between early increased energy and protein delivery and 60-day in-hospital mortality relative to nutrition risk after cardiac surgery: A retrospective cohort study.
**Abstract:** It is unclear whether nutrition risk modifies the associations of early increased energy and protein delivery with mortality in cardiac surgery patients. This retrospective cohort study used data from the International Nutrition Survey. The analysis included critically ill adults who underwent major cardiac surgery and did not permanently progress to exclusive oral feeding during the first 4 full days in the intensive care unit. Nutrition delivery and adequacy were averaged over these 4 days. Logistic regression was used to evaluate the mNUTRIC score as a modifier of the associations between early nutrition and 60-day in-hospital mortality. A total of 292 patients were included; 57 (19.5%) died in hospital within 60 days. Decedents received significantly more nutrition compared to 60-day hospital survivors. After multivariable adjustment, early increases in energy and protein delivery were not significantly associated with 60-day in-hospital mortality. The mNUTRIC score did not significantly modify the association between nutrition exposure and mortality. Patients with mNUTRIC scores of 6-9 had higher 60-day in-hospital mortality than those with scores of 0-5 (24% versus 13%; odds ratio 2.2; 95% CI: 1.1-4.2; P = 0.017). For each 1-point increase in the mNUTRIC score, the odds of 60-day in-hospital mortality were multiplied by 1.3 (95% CI: 1.1-1.6, P = 0.008). In cardiac surgery patients who continued to need enteral and parenteral nutrition, the mNUTRIC score did not identify patients who benefited from early increases in energy and protein delivery.
