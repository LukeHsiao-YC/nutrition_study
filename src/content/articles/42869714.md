### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討早期生活風險因子與心臟代謝疾病（CMD）轉變之間的關聯，並評估成人生活方式是否能調節這些關聯。研究分析了英國生物銀行174,718名成年人的前瞻性隊列數據，定義了CMD的五種轉變路徑，並使用早期生活風險分數和成人生活方式分數進行評估。結果顯示，同時具有高早期生活風險和不健康成人生活方式的個體，其CMD進展和死亡的風險最高。重要的是，早期生活風險因子對特定CMD轉變的不利影響，在具有較健康成人生活方式的個體中顯著減輕。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了過去對於早期生活風險因子與心臟代謝疾病（CMD）轉變之間的關聯是否會因成人生活方式而異的知識空白。它明確指出成人健康的生活方式能顯著調節並減輕早期風險因子對CMD進展和死亡的負面影響。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Life-course associations of early-life risk factors, modifiable adult lifestyles, and transitions of cardiometabolic disease and multimorbidity: A prospective cohort study.
**Abstract:** Whether associations of early-life risk factors with cardiometabolic disease (CMD) transitions vary by adult lifestyles remains unclear. We aimed to examine the associations between early-life risk factors and CMD transitions, and to assess whether adult lifestyles can modify these associations. We analyzed data from 174,718 adults participating in the United Kingdom Biobank prospective cohort study. We defined 5 CMD transitions using a multistate model: from CMD-free to first CMD (FCMD; including stroke, ischemic heart disease, and type 2 diabetes), from FCMD to cardiometabolic multimorbidity (CMM), and from CMD-free, FCMD, and CMM to death. An early-life risk score (ERS; 0-3) summed low birth weight, maternal smoking around birth, and absence of human milk feeding in infancy. Adult lifestyle was quantified using an adult lifestyle score (ALS) adapted from 4 behavioral metrics of the Life's Essential 8 framework, and data were collected at study enrollment when participants had a median age of 55 y. During a median follow-up of 14.6 y, individuals with both high early-life risk (ERS ≥2) and an unhealthy adult lifestyle (ALS: 0-49) had the highest risks for most adverse transitions, including CMD-free to FCMD [hazard ratio (HR): 2.04; 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.89, 2.21], FCMD to CMM (HR: 1.46; 95% CI: 1.27, 1.69), CMD-free to death (HR: 2.31; 95% CI: 1.95, 2.72), and FCMD to death (HR: 1.64; 95% CI: 1.34, 2.01). The associations of ERS with the risk of most CMD transitions were less pronounced among participants with a healthy adult lifestyle than among those with an unhealthy adult lifestyle. Joint exposure to adverse early-life experiences and an unhealthy adult lifestyle was associated with elevated risks of CMD progression and mortality. Notably, the detrimental associations of early-life risk factors with specific CMD transitions were less pronounced among individuals with healthier adult lifestyles.
