### 重點摘要
荷蘭兒童午餐的蔬菜攝取量普遍不足，導致超過半數兒童整體蔬菜攝取量未達標。本研究採準實驗設計，評估在三所小學推行為期數週的富含蔬菜午餐計劃，並比較介入組與對照組在認知、福祉、食物新奇恐懼症等指標的變化。結果顯示，介入組與對照組在大多數測量指標上沒有顯著差異，僅對照組的食物新奇恐懼症隨時間下降。研究者認為，午餐接受度低、社交動態及文化不熟悉性導致的膳食攝取不足，是未能觀察到顯著效果的主要原因。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻揭示了在特定文化背景下推行學校午餐計劃時，若未充分考量兒童對新餐飲模式的接受度及文化適應性，可能難以達到預期的健康效益。未來的學校餐點計劃應著重於逐步引導兒童熟悉新的餐飲模式，才能期望對飲食攝取量、認知和福祉產生有意義的影響。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Growing appetites: the impact of vegetable-rich school meals on child nutrition, cognition, and well-being.
**Abstract:** Typical lunch meals consumed by Dutch children fall short of national dietary guidelines. The average vegetable intake during lunch is around 8 g, contributing to inadequate overall vegetable consumption in over half of children under 12. A school-based lunch programme offering vegetable-rich meals may improve vegetable intake and potentially lead to other benefits. In the current controlled quasi-experimental study, such programme was assessed. Children aged 7-13 years at three primary schools received lunch on 3 school days per week, while three other schools served as controls. Pre- and post-intervention assessments measured cognition, well-being, food neophobia, food literacy, and skin carotenoid status using the Veggie Meter (VM). Post intervention, meal liking was assessed. 2 x 2 mixed ANOVAs were run (group x time). No significant differences were observed between groups and over time for any of the variables, but a decrease in food neophobia over time in the control group, but not in the intervention group, was observed. Exploratory analyses revealed positive associations between VM score and well-being, and between meal liking and VM score, while meal liking was negatively associated with food neophobia. Insufficient meal consumption, driven by low meal acceptance, social dynamics, and cultural unfamiliarity with warm lunches at school, is the most plausible explanation for the absence of effects. Future school meal programmes in the Netherlands should focus on gradual familiarisation with new meal formats before meaningful effects on dietary intake, cognition, and well-being can be expected.
