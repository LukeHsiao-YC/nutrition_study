### 重點摘要
為促進永續健康飲食，本研究評估了數位工具DISH（Dashboard for Improving Sustainable Healthy Food Choices）能否鼓勵更永續且營養均衡的餐點選擇。DISH以應用程式、網站和資訊站的形式在兩所大學自助餐廳部署10週，共189名參與者被隨機分配進行餐點選擇評估。結果顯示，DISH提供的環境與營養資訊並未顯著影響參與者的食物選擇，無論是營養品質或環境影響分數均無統計學差異。研究結論指出數位工具在支持飲食決策方面仍有局限性，未來需加強動機和情境相關性以達成行為改變。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了關於數位工具在大學校園環境中，對學生永續健康飲食行為影響的實證研究空白。然而，其主要限制在於數位提示（digital nudging）未能有效轉化意識為實際的飲食行為改變，這凸顯了未來研究需進一步探索如何強化動機與情境相關性，以提升數位干預工具的有效性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Digital nudging toward sustainable healthy foods: Insights from a trial on a university-based dashboard for promoting sustainable eating habits.
**Abstract:** Sustainable diets are promoted to reduce environmental impacts while supporting public health, yet translating sustainability information into healthier food choices remains challenging. This study evaluated whether the Dashboard for Improving Sustainable Healthy (DISH) Food Choices, a digital food environment providing real-time environmental and nutritional information, could encourage more sustainable and nutritionally balanced meal selections. DISH was deployed as a mobile app, website, and kiosk in two university campus cafeterias over 10 weeks. The primary behavioural outcomes were the nutritional quality and environmental impact of selected meals, operationalized through pre- and post-intervention meal scores: Food Compass Score and Health Star Rating for nutritional quality, and climate change score for environmental impact. Data were collected from 189 participants randomly assigned to control and intervention groups (n = 85 and n = 86, respectively), with surveys complemented by exploratory interviews with 26 intervention participants four months later. These interviews were guided by the Capability, Opportunity, Motivation-Behaviour (COM-B) model. Both an intention-to-treat repeated-measures model and complementary non-parametric tests (Wilcoxon and Mann-Whitney U) indicated that exposure to DISH's combined environmental-nutritional information did not significantly influence food choices on any behavioural measure (p > 0.47). Latent profile analysis identified a subgroup with high health motivation but low climate consideration; however, since this subgroup appeared in both study groups and was not differentially affected by the intervention, it should be viewed as a target for future tailored strategies rather than evidence of intervention effectiveness. Qualitative feedback suggested that DISH increased awareness and reinforced sustainability concepts. Overall, the findings highlight both the promise and limitations of digital tools for supporting informed dietary decisions and underscore the need to strengthen motivational and contextual relevance to achieve behavioural impact.
