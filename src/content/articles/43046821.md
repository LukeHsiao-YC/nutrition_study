### 重點摘要
鑑於目前缺乏衡量兒童週期性嘔吐症（CVS）疾病負擔的有效評估工具，本研究旨在評估不同複合評分與個別症狀參數在衡量CVS相關失能程度上的關聯性。研究分析了38名患有CVS的兒童患者數據，並以兒童偏頭痛失能評估量表（PedMIDAS）作為失能指標。結果顯示，複合評分（尤其是包含發作頻率、持續時間、噁心或腹痛嚴重度的組合）與失能程度的關聯性比任何單一症狀參數更強。這項研究初步驗證了以複合評分來衡量兒童CVS疾病負擔的可行性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了兒童週期性嘔吐症缺乏經驗證的疾病負擔評估工具的空白。然而，本研究僅為初步驗證，且樣本數較小（38名），未來仍需進行更大規模的研究來進一步確立這些複合評分的有效性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Composite scores as a measure of cyclic vomiting syndrome burden: Preliminary validation in children.
**Abstract:** Cyclic vomiting syndrome (CVS) is a debilitating, episodic vomiting disorder. There are no validated outcome surveys quantifying the burden of CVS episodes. Recent data demonstrate that specific parameters such as episode timing, duration, and associated symptoms are key to diagnosing pediatric CVS. It is unclear if these specific parameters alone or in combination relate to the disabling nature of CVS. This study aimed to evaluate the strength of various CVS composite scores based on episode parameters compared to individual parameters in relationship to a proxy index of disability. Secondary analysis of data from 38 patients aged 3-18 years presenting with episodic vomiting at Children's Wisconsin hospital with a formal CVS diagnosis, including a migraine-associated CVS (mCVS) subgroup (n = 21), were analyzed. Patients and caregivers completed surveys on specific symptoms during vomiting episodes and the pediatric migraine disability assessment (PedMIDAS) tool as index of CVS-related disability burden. Pearson correlations were computed between PedMIDAS disability scores and seven different CVS composite scores including the following parameters of episodes: frequency (F), duration (D), nausea severity (Sn), headache pain severity (Sh), and abdominal pain severity (Sa). For both CVS and mCVS, each composite score was more strongly associated with PedMIDAS than any individual parameter. For CVS patients, the F*D*Sn and F*D*Sn*Sa composite scores showed the strongest disability association (r = 0.628, p < 0.001 and r = 0.654, p < 0.001, respectively). For mCVS, the F*D*Sh composite score had the strongest association with disability (r = 0.731, p < 0.001). This study provides preliminary validation of a pediatric CVS burden scale based on an index of disability. Composite scores that include several individual episode parameters have stronger associations with disability than any individual parameters alone.
