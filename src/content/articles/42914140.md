### 重點摘要
這項混合方法研究旨在探討兒科基層醫療中，第一線工作人員（醫事助理和接待人員）在兒童健康檢查中關於疫苗的溝通實踐。研究透過直接觀察174次接觸，並對醫事助理進行半結構式訪談，以了解其溝通頻率、形式、角色、舒適度及培訓情況。結果顯示，醫事助理在51%的互動中進行疫苗溝通，但僅有46%使用推定式溝通，且在流感季節期間顯著下降。訪談揭示醫事助理的溝通方式主要來自實務經驗與同儕學習而非正規訓練，並將流感疫苗視為可選，突顯了加強團隊疫苗溝通培訓的重要性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
此研究填補了先前對於兒科基層醫療中，醫事助理和接待人員等第一線工作人員在疫苗溝通實踐方面知識空白，揭示了醫事助理在溝通中的頻繁參與但未能充分利用實證推定式溝通的現狀。研究也指出，由於其溝通方式多為非正式的同儕學習，未來需要針對性的正式培訓來改進。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Frontline Staff Vaccine Communication During Pediatric Well-Child Encounters: A Mixed-Methods Study.
**Abstract:** Evidence-based vaccine communication from clinicians is associated with improved parental vaccine acceptance. However, vaccine communication practices of frontline staff, including medical assistants (MAs) and receptionists, remain largely unknown. We aimed to characterize these practices during well-child encounters in pediatric primary care. We conducted a mixed-methods study with direct observations of frontline staff in 2 pediatric practices in Denver, CO, during well-child encounters in the summer (no influenza vaccine available) and fall (influenza vaccine available) of 2024, recording the frequency and format of vaccine communication. Following the observations, we conducted semi-structured interviews with MAs to explore their vaccine communication practices, perceived roles, comfort, and training. We observed 174 encounters (127 MA-parent, 47 receptionist-parent interactions). Vaccine communication occurred in at least 51% (95% CI, 43%-60%) of MA interactions vs 6% of receptionist interactions (P < .001). When vaccines were recommended, MAs used a presumptive format in 46% (95% CI, 34%-59%) of encounters overall, but far less during influenza season (21% vs 76%; P < .001). Interviews revealed that MA communication styles evolved through on-the-job experience and peer-to-peer learning rather than formal training, with influenza vaccines treated as optional rather than routinely recommended. MAs frequently communicate with parents about vaccines during well-child encounters but underutilize evidence-based presumptive communication, particularly for influenza vaccines. Communication practices are learned informally through peer-to-peer learning, highlighting a significant opportunity for targeted training to support team-based vaccine communication in pediatric primary care.
