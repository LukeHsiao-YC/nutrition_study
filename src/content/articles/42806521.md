### 重點摘要
這項研究旨在評估重症成人體內的尿素肌酐比 (UCR) 與28天死亡率之間的關聯，並探討其是否因測量時間和急性腎損傷 (AKI) 狀態而異。研究發現，第一天的 UCR 與死亡率沒有明顯關聯，但第七天的 UCR 與總體人群的28天死亡率有關。特別是，在沒有基線 AKI 的患者中，第七天的 UCR 呈現 U 型關聯，即過低或過高的 UCR 都與較高的死亡率相關，但在有基線 AKI 的患者中則無明顯關聯。因此，在解釋重症患者的 UCR 時，應謹慎考慮其基線 AKI 狀態，尤其在有 AKI 時應更為小心。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了尿素肌酐比 (UCR) 在重症成人中作為預後指標的複雜性，明確指出其預後價值會因測量時間（第七天而非第一天）和患者基線急性腎損傷 (AKI) 狀態而異。然而，對於有 AKI 的患者，UCR 的預後價值仍不明確，這點限制了其在該族群中的應用，並指出了未來研究的方向。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Urea-to-Creatinine-Ratio and the risk of 28-day mortality in critically ill adults with or without Acute Kidney Injury: A post-hoc analysis of a randomized clinical trial.
**Abstract:** The prognostic value of the urea-to-creatinine ratio in critically ill adults may vary according to the timing of measurement and baseline acute kidney injury status. We hypothesized that higher urea-to-creatinine ratios measured on study Days 1 and 7 would be associated with higher 28-day mortality, and that these associations would differ according to baseline acute kidney injury status. We performed a post hoc analysis of the multicenter cluster-randomized trial. The urea-to-creatinine ratio was measured on study Days 1 and 7 (mmol/mmol). Restricted cubic spline-adjusted Cox models assessed associations between the urea-to-creatinine ratio and time to death through Day 28 in the overall population and according to baseline acute kidney injury status. Multivariable linear regression identified factors associated with Day 7 urea-to-creatinine ratio. The Day 1 and Day 7 analyses included 2613 and 2516 patients, respectively. The urea-to-creatinine ratio was higher on Day 7 than on Day 1 and lower in patients with baseline acute kidney injury than in those without baseline acute kidney injury. No clear association was observed between the Day 1 urea-to-creatinine ratio and 28-day mortality in the overall population or after stratification by baseline acute kidney injury status. The Day 7 urea-to-creatinine ratio was associated with 28-day mortality in the overall population and showed a U-shaped association in patients without baseline acute kidney injury, whereas no clear association was observed in patients with baseline acute kidney injury. In the Day 7 cohort without baseline acute kidney injury, low and high urea-to-creatinine ratio cohorts had higher mortality than the middle urea-to-creatinine ratio cohort, with adjusted hazard ratios of 2.06 (95% confidence interval, 1.30-3.26) and 2.21 (95% confidence interval, 1.45-3.38), respectively. Higher protein delivery and older age were associated with higher Day 7 urea-to-creatinine ratio, whereas baseline acute kidney injury, male sex, and higher body mass index were associated with lower Day 7 urea-to-creatinine ratio. No clear association between the Day 1 urea-to-creatinine ratio and 28-day mortality was observed in the overall population or after stratification by baseline acute kidney injury status. On Day 7, the urea-to-creatinine ratio was associated with 28-day mortality, with a U-shaped association in patients without baseline acute kidney injury but no clear association in patients with baseline acute kidney injury. The urea-to-creatinine ratio should be interpreted cautiously in patients with acute kidney injury.
