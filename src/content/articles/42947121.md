### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討巴基斯坦高等教育學生自我藥療的盛行率、知識、態度、行為及其影響因素。研究採用橫斷面多中心設計，對1346名學生進行調查，結果顯示自我藥療的整體盛行率高達59.88%，且在21歲以上學生中更為普遍。健康科學學生雖具備較高的自我藥療知識水平，但其自我藥療行為卻較差，學生廣泛使用包括抗生素在內的各類藥物，並依賴人工智慧、社群媒體和網路等數位資源獲取相關資訊。本研究結果為公共衛生政策制定者提供了制定自我藥療干預措施的重要依據。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了在巴基斯坦高等教育學生中，自我藥療盛行率、相關知識、態度與實踐，以及其影響因素的具體數據空白，特別是揭示了學生利用數位健康資源獲取自我藥療資訊的現況。儘管研究指出健康科學學生知識與實踐的矛盾現象，但作為橫斷面研究，其無法確立因果關係，且便利抽樣可能限制了結果的普遍性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Self-medication among higher education students: prevalence, risk factors, and health information-seeking behaviours.
**Abstract:** The WHO estimates that more than two-thirds of university students engage in this practice. This study aims to determine the prevalence, knowledge, attitudes, and practices related to self-medication, along with the determinants that influence these behaviors, among higher education students in Pakistan. A total of 1346 students participated in a cross-sectional multicenter study from across Pakistan. The data was collected using a structured 28-item questionnaire (Cronbach's α value: 0.85 to 0.89), using a convenience sampling technique. The overall prevalence of self-medication was 59.88% among the higher education students. Self-medication was more prevalent among female students, but it was not statistically significant in AOR (64.18%, COR = 1.25, 95% CI 1.05-1.70, p = 0.008, AOR = 1.07, 95% CI 0.91-1.25, p = 0.061). Additionally, the prevalence of self-medication was higher among students aged > 21 (63.79%, COR = 1.46, 95% CI 1.20-1.98, p = 0.001, AOR = 1.18, 95% CI 1.03-1.40, p = 0.011). The health sciences students possessed a higher level of self-medication-related knowledge (M: 4.39, p < 0.001), but they demonstrated poorer self-medication practices (M: 3.53, p = 0.004) compared to non-health sciences students. Participants reported using a range of pharmacological agents for self-medication, including antibiotics (29.00%), multi-vitamins (22.00%), anti-allergics (18.00%), cold remedies (13.00%), eye drops (8.00%), nose sprays (6.00%), steroids (2.00%), and others (2.00%). Students also reported using artificial intelligence (AI), social media, and internet resources as sources for students to get knowledge regarding self-medication (13.00%), to seek advice before starting medication (9.14%), and get guidance in case of medication-related adverse effects (5.00%). Most of the participants (64.00%) reported storing 1 or more boxes of medication in their house for future use. The prevalence of self-medication was high among higher education students in our study. Additionally, the students are using on digital health resources for self-medication-related practices. Our study highlights the trends in self-medication among higher education students, guiding public health policymakers regarding self-medication interventions.
