### 重點摘要
高含量超加工食品（UPFs）攝取與肥胖及慢性病密切相關，傳統預防方法著重於個人行為修正。本文質疑此觀點，提出UPFs高攝取量應被視為暴露於致肥胖環境的標誌，而非個人失敗。研究分析了影響UPFs需求的生物學、價格、時間、環境與習慣等驅動因素。這種重新詮釋對臨床諮詢、風險溝通及預防策略至關重要，並建議採取分段且符合不同情境機制的預防措施。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文章填補了將高度加工食品攝取單純歸因於個人行為的解釋空白，轉而強調其作為致肥胖環境暴露標誌的深層意義。它挑戰了僅透過教育和諮詢來改變個人行為的局限性，並將預防重點引導至商業和監管決策所產生的環境因素。然而，文獻也指出高度加工食品的測量仍不完善，這可能構成未來研究的限制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Ultra-processed food intake as a marker of environmental exposure: reframing the prevention target.
**Abstract:** High intake of ultra-processed foods (UPFs) is consistently associated with obesity, cardiometabolic disease, and premature mortality, and preventive practice has largely answered that signal by treating intake as an individual behavior to be corrected through education, counseling, and provision of more information. This article asks whether that frame matches the determinants of demand that the behavioral and social sciences actually describe. Those drivers converge on a compact set of forces: the reward biology of sweet, fatty, and energy-dense foods; the hyper-palatability, soft texture, and rapid eating rate that promote passive overconsumption; habit and automaticity; the low cost per calorie of ultra-processed energy; time scarcity and convenience; and the availability structured by the local food environment. These operate through biology, price, time, environment, and habit, channels that deliberative knowledge reaches only weakly. Consistent with an environmental reading, the socioeconomic gradient of intake reverses between high-income and many low- and middle-income settings, so that the same category marks disadvantage in one place and affluence in another. This article argues that high UPF intake is better understood as a marker of exposure to an obesogenic environment than as a personal failing, with consequences for clinical counseling, risk communication, and the cautious use of an imperfectly measured indicator. The reframing changes interpretation rather than measurement, and keeps in view the commercial and regulatory decisions that produce the environment. Because demand is generated by different mechanisms in different contexts, segmented, mechanism-matched prevention follows more directly than a single universal message.
