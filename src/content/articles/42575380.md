### 重點摘要
這項研究旨在評估COVID-19大流行對乳癌篩檢模式及預後的影響。研究分析了國家乳房攝影資料庫2017-2022年的數據，並利用微觀模擬模型預測2020-2065年間的乳癌分期、超額死亡和生命年損失。結果顯示，2020年篩檢量急劇下降，直到2022年才恢復到疫情前水平，篩檢間隔顯著增加。模擬預測到2030年晚期癌症診斷將增加12%，到2040年每10萬名女性將額外增加16.8例死亡，並預計到2065年將損失565個生命年。研究結論認為，疫情期間篩檢中斷和恢復緩慢可能導致晚期癌症增加、超額死亡及生命年損失。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究利用大型全國性資料庫與微觀模擬模型，量化並預測了COVID-19大流行對乳癌篩檢造成的長期影響，包括晚期癌症診斷增加、超額死亡及生命年損失，填補了過去對此類長期公共衛生衝擊量化預測的空白。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Influence of the COVID-19 pandemic on breast cancer outcomes: A microsimulation model of the National Mammography Database.
**Abstract:** This study aimed to evaluate the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on screening patterns from a large, diverse, nationally representative breast cancer screening registry and to perform microsimulation modeling of breast cancer outcomes. Retrospective breast imaging and outcomes data from the National Mammography Database (NMD) from January 2017 through December 2022 were collected and partitioned into pre-COVID, peak-COVID, and post-COVID time periods. Microsimulation modeling was performed to predict breast cancer outcomes, including the cancer stage distribution, excess breast cancer deaths, and years of life lost from 2020 to 2065. There were 12,118,324 screening exams, 1,211,754 screening recalls, 194,096 biopsies, and 55,732 cancer diagnoses included for analysis. There was an acute drop in all metrics in 2020, and these metrics did not approach pre-COVID levels until 2022. This corresponded with a significant increase in the time interval between screening exams between the pre-COVID and post-COVID periods (16.3±7.7 vs. 17.8±9.3 months respectively; p<0.001). Simulations predicted that, by 2030, 12% more late-stage cancers would be diagnosed. The estimated cumulative excess deaths following peak-COVID were 6.7 per 100,000 women by 2030, an increase of 0.40%, increasing to 16.8 (0.51%) excess deaths by 2040. The corresponding number of years of life lost by 2065 was 565 per 100,000 women screened. The interruption in screening during peak-COVID and the slow return to pre-COVID screening levels may have resulted in an increase in late-stage cancers, excess cancer deaths, and years of life lost.
