### 重點摘要
本評論指出，營養科學領域的開放取用模式雖增加了研究的可取用性，但尚未能確保其被有效「使用」。文章提出應將文章處理費（APCs）視為傳播投資，而非單純的出版費用，以確保研究成果能觸及目標使用者並被採納。一項對98個營養學期刊的分析顯示，期刊影響因子與APCs的關聯性微弱，而開放取用類型和出版商群體對APCs的解釋力更強。研究建議應根據科學契合度、目標使用者和預期傳播效果來管理傳播組合，以實現「為使用付費」的目標。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻重新概念化了學術出版中文章處理費（APCs）的角色，將其視為確保研究成果被「使用」的傳播投資，而非僅是「取用」的成本。然而，研究並未直接衡量傳播價值或成果被使用的程度，這是未來研究可探討的面向。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Paying for Use: Rethinking Article Processing Charges as Dissemination Investments in Nutrition Science.
**Abstract:** Open access has become an important route for making nutrition research available, but availability is not the same as use. Nutrition evidence creates value only when it is interpreted, translated, trusted, and taken up in clinical nutrition, dietetic practice, public health programs, guideline development, policy, and professional education. This commentary argues that article processing charges (APCs) should not be treated merely as publication fees or automatic postacceptance reimbursements but as dissemination investments under resource constraints. Journal Impact Factor (JIF), publisher brand, and open-access (OA) status are visible signals, but they do not necessarily show whether publication spending helps evidence reach intended users. A cross-sectional descriptive analysis of 98 nutrition and dietetics publication venues found a modest positive rank correlation between average JIF and listed APC (Spearman ρ = 0.33). Average JIF alone explained little variation in listed APCs (adjusted R² = 0.03); the adjusted R² increased to 0.56 when OA type was added and to 0.64 when publisher group was added. Although dissemination value was not measured directly, these findings indicate APCs are not simply the price of journal impact. Together with prior research, they support dissemination portfolio management, aligning journal selection for each manuscript and resource allocation across manuscripts with scientific fit, intended users, expected reach, cost, time, and rejection risk. Paying for access is no longer enough; nutrition science must learn to pay for use by reconsidering APCs as dissemination investments designed around intended users and evidence use.
