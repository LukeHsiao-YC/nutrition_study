### 重點摘要
該研究旨在探討人體組織中絕對Omega-3 (n-3)和Omega-6 (n-6)多不飽和脂肪酸(PUFA)的儲備量，特別是其在重症系統性疾病（如致命性COVID-19）中的變化。研究者利用氣相層析法定量分析了猝死對照組和致命性COVID-19肺炎患者九種主要組織的脂肪酸濃度，並估計了全身脂肪酸儲備。結果顯示，致命性COVID-19患者的長鏈n-3底物（主要是EPA和DPA）在多個器官中普遍耗竭，導致脂肪組織中的n-6/n-3比例顯著升高，全身n-3儲備量也較低。本研究首次建立全面的人體PUFA儲備圖譜，並指出嚴重的系統性疾病會導致器官間長鏈n-3底物的耗竭，提示組織底物可用性可能是急性代謝壓力下炎症消解的重要限制因素。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了先前對於人體組織中絕對Omega-3和Omega-6多不飽和脂肪酸儲備量及其在重症系統性疾病中可用性的認知空白。它首次提供了人體多種主要組織的全面定量脂肪酸儲備圖譜，並揭示了致命性COVID-19等嚴重系統性疾病與器官間長鏈n-3底物普遍耗竭之間的關聯，進而支持組織底物可用性對急性代謝壓力下炎症消解信號傳導的重要性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Whole-body Omega-3 and Omega-6 fatty-acid reserves in healthy controls and fatal COVID-19: Evidence of systemic long-chain n-3 depletion.
**Abstract:** While circulating lipid biomarkers are widely used, little is known about the absolute tissue reserves of omega-3 and omega-6 polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) in humans or their availability during severe systemic illness. We therefore mapped absolute PUFA concentrations across major human tissues and examined whether fatal COVID-19 is associated with depletion of long-chain n-3 substrate pools. Absolute fatty acid concentrations (mg/g wet tissue) were quantified across nine tissues (adipose tissue, brain, heart, lungs, liver, spleen, skeletal muscle, small intestine, and kidney) using gas chromatography with flame-ionization detection (GC-FID). Whole-body fatty-acid reserves were then estimated from tissue concentrations and organ weights. The cohort included sudden-death controls and patients with fatal COVID-19 pneumonia. Controls exhibited distinct organ-specific lipid signatures with high n-3 concentrations in adipose tissue and brain. In fatal COVID-19, we observed a coherent cross-organ reduction in long-chain n-3 substrate pools driven primarily by depletion of EPA and DPA. DHA showed tissue-specific preservation or relative elevation, most notably in brain, adipose tissue, and spleen. Adipose tissue, the primary reservoir, showed a severe imbalance characterized by an approximately 3.3-fold increase in the n-6/n-3 ratio (median 50.66 vs. 15.56; p = 0.004) and lower total n-3 content (0.93 vs. 2.76 mg/g; p = 0.004). Brain tissue displayed species-specific remodeling: EPA and DPA were lower in COVID-19, whereas DHA was relatively higher (EPA, 0.018 vs. 0.16 mg/g; p = 0.004). In the lungs, EPA was similarly depleted (0.015 vs. 0.34 mg/g; p = 0.004), and the myocardium shifted toward a pro-inflammatory profile with an elevated n-6/n-3 ratio (16.87 vs. 4.01; p = 0.004). Estimated whole-body n-3 reserves were lower in the COVID-19 group (41.7 g vs. 86.6 g). This study establishes a comprehensive quantitative human tissue map of absolute PUFA reserves and suggests that severe systemic illness, exemplified here by fatal COVID-19, is associated with coherent depletion of long-chain n-3 substrates across organs. These findings support the concept that tissue substrate availability may be a clinically relevant constraint on resolution signalling during acute metabolic stress.
