### 重點摘要
本系統性回顧旨在探討非營養性甜味劑 (NNS) 對食慾調節神經機制的影響，該機制因甜味與熱量脫鉤而可能改變。研究篩選了21份神經影像學報告，分析NNS對健康成人味覺、獎勵相關腦區及下丘腦活動的影響。結果顯示NNS和營養性甜味劑均活化這些腦區，但部分研究顯示NNS的活性和愉悅度較低。目前證據仍有限，且未發現腦部反應與腸道激素或葡萄糖代謝的明確關聯。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻旨在探討非營養性甜味劑影響食慾調節的神經機制，填補了此領域知識不足的空白。然而，目前證據仍有限，且結果因甜味劑類型、劑量、個體差異等多種因素而異，未來的研究需考量真實世界條件、更大的人群多樣性及個體變異性，以更精確地指導飲食建議。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Neuroimaging Insights into the Effects of Non-Nutritive Sweeteners on Appetite Regulation: A Systematic Review.
**Abstract:** Non-nutritive sweeteners (NNS) are increasingly used as low- or zero-calorie alternatives to sugar while maintaining sweetness. However, uncoupling sweet taste from caloric content may alter appetite regulation and promote food-seeking behavior, although the underlying neural mechanisms remain poorly understood. To address this, we conducted a systematic review in accordance with PRISMA guidelines. PubMed/MEDLINE, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials were searched up to June 2025 using terms related to NNS, neuroimaging and appetite regulation. Of 137 screened records, 21 neuroimaging reports met predefined PICO-based eligibility criteria. Eligible reports included healthy adults across weight categories and examined artificial or naturally occurring NNS (e.g., sucralose, erythritol) compared with nutritive sweeteners (NS; e.g., sucrose) or non-sweet controls (e.g., water). Outcomes included appetite-related brain responses measured using functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) or positron emission tomography (PET), as well as associations with gut hormones, glucose metabolism, and eating behavior. NNS and NS both engaged gustatory and reward-related brain regions, with some reports showing reduced activity and lower pleasantness ratings for NNS. Hypothalamic activity and functional connectivity were also modulated by NNS intake, with no observed associations between brain responses and gut hormones or glucose metabolism in the available reports. Overall, the evidence remains limited and varies by NNS type, dose, delivery method, habitual intake, sex, weight status, and metabolic state. Future studies should investigate acute and long-term NNS consumption under real-world conditions with greater ethnic and demographic diversity, accounting for individual variability to better inform dietary guidelines.
