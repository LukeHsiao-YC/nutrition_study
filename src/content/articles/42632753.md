### 重點摘要
迴歸校準廣泛用於修正飲食暴露測量誤差造成的偏差，但在營養流行病學中，若生物標記本身位於攝取與結果的因果路徑上，可能違反迴歸校準的替代表假設，進而影響其有效性。本研究透過分析性偏差量化、模擬研究與實際數據應用，評估了使用生物標記校準飲食攝取的迴歸校準方法，以確保其有效性和效率。研究發現，擴展校準法和兩階段校準法在生物標記為中介變數時通常有效，其中擴展校準法在連續結果中顯示出較高的效率。最終，研究提供了解決生物標記作為中介變數時，如何有效且高效地校準飲食攝取並估計其影響的指導方法。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了在營養流行病學研究中，當用於校準飲食攝取的生物標記同時也作為因果路徑上的中介變數時，傳統迴歸校準方法有效性不足的知識空白。它提出了「擴展校準法」和「兩階段校準法」等解決方案，確保研究者在這種複雜情境下仍能準確估計飲食攝取的總體效應，進而提升了使用生物標記進行測量誤差校正的可靠性與有效性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** All covariates are not created equal in regression calibration: a review of methods on using biomarkers to calibrate dietary intake.
**Abstract:** Regression calibration is a widely used method for correcting bias in regression coefficient estimates caused by measurement error in continuous exposures. In nutritional epidemiology, recent studies often use nutrient-derived biomarkers to calibrate true dietary intakes, which are typically assessed through small feeding studies or weighed dietary records. However, biomarkers may themselves lie on the causal pathway between intake and outcome, violating the surrogacy assumption that underpins the validity of regression calibration. We evaluate regression calibration-based methods that have been applied or proposed for calibrating dietary intake using biomarkers, focusing on their validity and relative efficiency. Our assessment combines analytic bias quantification, simulation studies, and a real-data application. Two approaches are generally valid when using mediators to calibrate the true exposure: (1) the expanded calibration method, which recovers the total effect via the product method from mediation analysis, and (2) the two-stage calibration method. In simulations reflecting realistic scenarios, the expanded calibration method demonstrated superior efficiency for a continuous outcome. Finally, we show that estimating the effect of dietary intake using a calibrated biomarker is valid when the mean biomarker calibration model contains a linear term in true intake with coefficient one, possibly along with additional covariates.
