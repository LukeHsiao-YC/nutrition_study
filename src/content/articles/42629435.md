### 重點摘要
牛奶富含胎兒生長所需營養，本研究旨在探討孕期牛奶攝取量及其巨量營養素與新生兒出生體重的關聯。此日本前瞻性世代研究納入近八萬對母嬰，透過食物頻率問卷評估孕婦牛奶攝取量，並分析其與胎齡過小 (SGA) 及胎齡過大 (LGA) 的關聯。研究發現，孕期牛奶總攝取量最高五分位數的孕婦，其嬰兒發生SGA的風險顯著降低（勝算比0.87），且全脂牛奶的攝取頻率越高，SGA風險越低。本研究支持孕期牛奶攝取，特別是全脂牛奶，有助於促進胎兒生長並降低SGA風險，尤其在牛奶攝取量相對較低的族群中。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究透過日本大型前瞻性世代研究，在牛奶攝取量相對較低的族群中，證實孕期牛奶攝取（特別是全脂牛奶及其所含的巨量營養素）與降低胎齡過小嬰兒風險的關聯。這填補了在亞洲人群中此類關聯的實證數據空白。未來的研究可進一步探討全脂牛奶中特定成分促進胎兒生長的生理機制，或進行隨機對照試驗以確立因果關係，並考慮不同飲食習慣人群的差異性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Association between maternal milk intake during pregnancy and birth size of infants: results from the Japan Environment and Children's Study.
**Abstract:** Cow milk is rich in essential nutrients for fetal growth. We investigated associations of maternal milk intake and macronutrient intake from milk with birth size. We included 79,976 mother-infant pairs from an ongoing nationwide prospective Japanese cohort study. Maternal milk intake (full-fat and low-fat milk) was assessed through a food frequency questionnaire. Multiple logistic regression was performed to investigate the associations of total milk intake and macronutrient intake from milk with small for gestational age (SGA) and large for gestational age. Median total milk intake was approximately 96.9 mL/day during pregnancy. The highest quintile of total milk intake was associated with a decreased SGA risk compared with the lowest quintile of total milk intake (odds ratio: 0.87; 95% confidence interval: 0.80-0.95). Compared with women who consumed full-fat milk less than once per month, women with a higher frequency of full-fat milk intake were less likely to have SGA. No association was observed when the analysis was limited to those who did not consume full-fat milk. Compared with the lowest quintile, the higher quintiles of protein, fat, and carbohydrate intake from milk were associated with a reduced risk of SGA. There were no associations of milk intake with large for gestational age. We found that milk intake during pregnancy, particularly full-fat milk, was associated with a lower risk of SGA in a cohort of pregnant women with relatively low milk intake. Our results support the hypothesis that milk intake during pregnancy promotes fetal growth.
