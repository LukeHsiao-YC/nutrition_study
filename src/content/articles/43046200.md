### 重點摘要
紐西蘭因線上賭博的快速增長和線上賭場的合法化，使賭博危害成為重要的公共衛生議題。本研究透過範圍界定審查，分析了2003至2024年間紐西蘭同行評審賭博研究的重點、方法學和資金來源，共納入176篇文章。結果顯示研究領域廣泛，但主要採用橫斷面設計，臨床試驗極少，且未有研究直接來自賭博產業資金。結論指出紐西蘭雖然累積了大量研究，但仍缺乏評估減少賭博危害干預措施有效性的實證研究，並存在對特定族群和線上賭博研究的空白。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻明確指出了紐西蘭在賭博危害研究中的重要空白，特別是缺乏評估有效減少賭博危害干預措施的實證研究。研究限制包括對公共衛生和監管措施、治療與預防成果、線上賭博影響以及對毛利、太平洋島民和亞洲社區的文化適應性回應的評估不足。未來的研究應加強前瞻性設計，以追蹤線上賭場合法化後的影響並為政策制定提供依據。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Research on gambling and gambling harm in Aotearoa New Zealand: a scoping review.
**Abstract:** Gambling harm is a significant public health issue made more urgent by the rapid growth of online gambling. In Aotearoa New Zealand, gambling access expanded following the introduction of gaming machines in the 1990s and is expected to expand again with the regulation of online casinos. With online casinos now being regulated, it is timely to examine the local evidence and identify gaps in the research. This study aimed to determine: (i) the focus of peer-reviewed gambling research conducted in New Zealand between 2003 and 2024; (ii) the methodologies used and their adequacy; and (iii) the nature and extent of funding reported in peer-reviewed publications. MEDLINE, PsycINFO and Scopus were searched for peer-reviewed articles published between 2003 and 2024. Articles of any design were eligible if they focused on gambling, gambling harm or problem gambling, were conducted in New Zealand and included at least one New Zealand-based author. A total of 176 articles were included. Research addressed overlapping domains of individual factors (23.3%), epidemiology and harm (18.2%), social and cultural factors (18.8%), community and context (15.3%), screening and treatment (23.3%), and policy and industry regulation (14.8%). Cross-sectional studies were the most common design (37.5%), followed by non-systematic reviews (20.5%) and qualitative studies (13.1%). Only five clinical trials and seven experimental or quasi-experimental studies were identified. Qualitative methods were most common in research addressing social and cultural factors. Among 167 articles reporting funding information, 37% received gambling levy funding, 27% general research funding, 30% reported no funding and 6% received overseas funding; none reported direct gambling-industry funding. New Zealand has produced a substantial body of peer-reviewed gambling research, but relatively little research has tested what works to reduce gambling harm. Major gaps were evaluations of public health and regulatory responses, treatment and prevention outcomes, the effects of gambling on affected others and whānau, online gambling, and culturally grounded responses for Māori, Pacific and Asian communities. The introduction of regulated online casinos increases the need for prospective research that can track changes to gambling and gambling harm and inform policy and regulation.
