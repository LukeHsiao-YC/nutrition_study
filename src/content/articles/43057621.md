### 重點摘要
本研究旨在解決太空飛行期間微重力導致肌肉流失和體液轉移的監測挑戰。研究人員利用便攜式三維光學成像（3DO）和生物電阻抗分析（BIA），結合五分區模型，在短期太空飛行前、中、後量化太空人的身體組成及形狀變化。結果顯示，四名太空人在五天內體重平均減少2.27公斤，主要是由脂肪以外組織量（FFM）減少4.36%所致，且體內總水分（TBW）減少達3.0公升並未完全恢復。這項便攜式監測方法證實其在任務中的可行性，對太空人健康管理具有潛在價值。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了太空飛行中實用且精確的身體組成監測工具的空白，克服了傳統影像和實驗室系統在成本和可行性上的限制，為太空人的即時健康管理提供了新的解決方案。然而，研究的樣本數較小（四名太空人）且飛行時間短（五天），其結果推論至長期太空任務或更廣泛的太空人群體可能存在局限性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Nutritional monitoring during spaceflight: tracking body composition and three-dimensional shape across a five-day research expedition.
**Abstract:** Microgravity during spaceflight leads to rapid muscle loss and fluid shifts driven by skeletal unloading and dietary changes. Obtaining accurate measurements before, during, and after missions remains challenging due to the cost and feasibility limitations of traditional imaging and laboratory systems. This study quantified changes in whole-body and regional body shape using three-dimensional optical imaging (3DO) and body composition using bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA) before, during, and after a short-duration spaceflight. Fat-free mass (FFM) and fat mass (FM) were estimated by combining 3DO-derived body volume (BV) and BIA-derived total body water (TBW) within a five-compartment (5C) model. Ground-based measurements were obtained immediately pre- and post-flight using clinically validated 3DO and BIA systems. In-flight assessments used smartphone-based 3DO and smartwatch-based BIA, calibrated to ground systems. Absolute and proportional changes in body mass, circumferences, and 5C body composition were characterized as the difference between post- and pre-flight values, with in-flight measures used to monitor longitudinal changes in TBW and body shape. Four astronauts completed the five-day research expedition. Body mass decreased by -2.27 ± 1.66 kg, driven primarily by FFM loss (-2.17 ± 1.22 kg), representing a -4.36 ± 2.76% proportional reduction (range: -1.12 to -7.76%). In-flight TBW reductions reached -3.0 L. Post-flight circumferences decreased across all regions, with the largest proportional changes in the legs and torso. The portable 5C approach demonstrated feasibility for in-mission monitoring, capturing rapid TBW loss confirmed by incomplete recovery upon landing. Alterations in diet, activity, and microgravity contributed to greater than 4% FFM loss over five days, with the greatest regional changes in the legs and torso. In-flight monitoring captured TBW loss that did not fully recover upon landing, demonstrating the feasibility of portable body composition monitoring for astronaut health management.
