### 重點摘要
這項前瞻性世代研究旨在探討茶飲攝取及其循環生物標誌物與長期骨礦物質變化的關聯性。研究追蹤了2,133名參與者長達13年，定期測量骨礦物質密度（BMD）和骨礦物質含量（BMC），並分析基線血清黃烷-3-醇類。結果顯示，中度頻率飲茶者在全身、腰椎和股骨頸的BMD/BMC方面普遍表現出最有利的骨礦物質狀態。此外，較高類別的選定循環茶生物標誌物（特別是ECG和EGCG）也與較好的骨礦物質指標相關，本研究結論指出中度頻率飲茶和較高水平的特定循環茶生物標誌物與更佳的骨礦物質狀態有關。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了先前關於茶飲攝取及其循環生物標誌物與長期骨礦物質狀態之間前瞻性證據有限的空白。然而，研究發現生物標誌物與骨礦物質指標之間的關聯性並非總是線性，且部分呈現非單調模式，這提示未來研究需進一步探討其複雜的劑量反應關係或作用機制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Relationship of Tea Consumption and its Circulating Biomarkers with Longitudinal Bone Mineral Changes: A Prospective Cohort Study With Repeated Measurements.
**Abstract:** Tea is a dietary source of flavan-3-ols, which may influence bone health, but prospective evidence linking tea intake and its circulating biomarkers with longitudinal bone mineral status remains limited. To examine associations of tea consumption and its serum biomarkers with bone mineral status in Guangzhou Nutrition and Health Study (GNHS). In 2,133 participants, whole-body, total hip, lumbar spine, and femoral neck bone mineral density (BMD) and bone mineral content (BMC) were measured using dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry at four repeated visits during a 13-year follow-up period. Baseline serum flavan-3-ols, including Epicatechin [EC], Epigallocatechin gallate [EGCG], Epicatechin gallate [ECG], Epigallocatechin [EGC], and Catechin, were quantified using UHPLC-MS/MS. Tea consumption was categorized as non-, low-, moderate-, or high-frequency. Multivariable linear mixed-effects models, including exposure × visit interactions, were used to evaluate visit-averaged bone mineral measures and longitudinal trajectories. Among 1,708 participants, significant tea consumption group × visit interactions were observed for whole-body, lumbar spine, and femoral neck BMD and whole-body BMC (P-interaction ranged from < 0.001 to 0.022). Moderate-frequency tea drinkers generally had the highest visit-averaged adjusted marginal mean Z-scores, including 0.107 (95% CI: 0.025, 0.190) for whole-body BMD, 0.090 (0.012, 0.168) for lumbar spine BMD, and 0.108 (0.029, 0.187) for femoral neck BMD (P-diff ranged from < 0.001 to 0.017 ). In analyses of serum tea biomarkers, higher biomarker categories were generally associated with more favorable bone mineral measures; however, the associations were not consistently linear, and several biomarkers showed non-monotonic patterns. Visit-averaged differences were most extensive across ECG and EGCG categories. Moderate-frequency tea consumption and higher categories of selected circulating tea biomarkers were associated with more favorable bone mineral status.
