### 重點摘要
這項研究探討了母親懷孕早期和兒童時期植物性飲食（PBDs）的品質與青少年大腦型態及認知表現的關聯。研究分析了母親懷孕早期和兒童八歲時的飲食數據，並評估了兒童十或十四歲時的大腦型態與十四歲時的認知表現。結果顯示，母親較高的健康植物性飲食指數（hPDI）與子女較高的整體智商相關，而兒童時期較高的hPDI則與較大的總腦容量及更好的詞彙理解能力有關。相反地，母親不健康的PDI（uPDI）與較小的總腦容量及較低的智商有關，而兒童uPDI則與矩陣推理能力呈負相關，這些發現指出飲食品質的重要性，但其臨床意義尚待進一步研究。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了關於懷孕早期和兒童時期植物性飲食對青少年大腦型態及認知表現影響的知識空白。然而，未來仍需進一步探討這些觀察到的差異在臨床和發展上的實際意義與相關性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Maternal and childhood plant-based diets with brain morphology and cognitive performance in adolescence.
**Abstract:** Plant-based diets (PBDs) are increasingly discussed in the context of sustainable and healthy eating. However, evidence on PBDs during early pregnancy and childhood in relation to brain morphology and cognitive performance remains largely unknown. We aimed to investigate whether maternal and childhood overall, healthful, and unhealthful PBDs are associated with offspring brain morphology and adolescent cognitive performance. We included mother-child dyads with dietary intake measured during early pregnancy (n = 2952) and childhood at age eight years (n = 2922) and with at least one brain magnetic resonance imaging assessment at age 10 or 14 years from the prospective population-based Generation R Study. Dietary intake during early pregnancy and childhood was assessed with food-frequency questionnaires. We constructed three plant-based diet indices (PDIs): an overall PDI, a healthful PDI (hPDI), and an unhealthful PDI (uPDI). Cognitive performance was assessed at 14 years using 4 subtests of the Wechsler Intelligence Scale for Children-Fifth Edition. Associations were examined using multivariable linear mixed models and linear regression models. After adjustment for socioeconomic and lifestyle factors, higher maternal hPDI scores were associated with higher full-scale IQ (per SD, B = 0.54, 95% CI: 0.09, 1.00) but not with brain volumes, whereas higher childhood hPDI scores were associated with larger total brain (per SD, B = 7.97, 95% CI: 4.43, 11.51), white matter, gray matter, subcortical volumes, and better verbal comprehension. In contrast, higher maternal uPDI scores were associated with smaller total brain volume (per SD, B = -4.03, 95% CI: -8.02, -0.04), cerebral gray matter and subcortical volumes and lower IQ (per SD, B = -0.84, 95% CI: -1.35, -0.32), and childhood uPDI scores were inversely associated with matrix reasoning but not with brain volumes. Childhood PDI scores, particularly hPDI scores, showed widespread positive associations with cortical morphology, whereas maternal PDI scores showed no significant associations. Our findings suggest that plant-based diet quality during early pregnancy and childhood shows distinct associations with brain morphology and cognitive performance up to adolescence, although the clinical and developmental relevance of the observed differences should be further investigated.
