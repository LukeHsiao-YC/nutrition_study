### 重點摘要
當前餐飲服務趨勢結合多感官、藝術與科技，但既有學術文獻在數位效率與感官體驗研究上存在分歧，且常將用餐者視為被動接收者。本文提出「實數餐飲 (phygital dining)」這一後現代框架，將用餐重新概念化為在實體與數位融合環境中，由用餐者主動驅動的流動意義建構過程。此框架基於五大支柱，旨在整合數位增強、實體氛圍與人際中介，以提升用餐者主體性、專注力與感官參與，進而支持正念品嚐並增強內感受覺知與內在自我調節，最終影響食物選擇、情緒及感知身心健康。本研究不僅為食品體驗設計、酒店創新和健康導向環境提供了管理啟示，也為未來在實體-數位混合食品生態系統中，以主體性驅動的消費行為研究奠定了基礎。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
現有學術文獻在探討數位餐飲環境時，多半強調營運效率與便利性，而多感官研究則常將環境線索視為靜態、由上而下的干預，並將用餐者定位為被動接收者。本文所提出的「實數餐飲」框架，填補了此一研究空白，透過將用餐者重新定義為在物理與數位融合生態系統中，具備主體性並主動建構意義的參與者。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Phygital dining: A postmodern framework for food experience, agency, and well-being in blended physical-digital eating environments.
**Abstract:** Recent developments across food service settings reflect a structural shift toward multisensory, artistic, and technology-driven dining formats. However, academic literature remains split: scholarship on digital food environments emphasizes operational efficiency and convenience, whereas multisensory research commonly evaluates environmental cues as static, top-down interventions that position diners as passive recipients. This perspective article introduces phygital dining as a novel postmodern framework that reconceptualizes eating as a liquid, agency-driven, and evolving meaning-making process unfolding within integrated physical-digital food ecosystems. Grounded in postmodern consumer theory and positioned at the intersection of phygital scholarship, gastrophysics, and experiential food research, this framework proposes that harmonizing digital augmentation with physical atmospherics and human mediation can support mindful savoring and may enhance interoceptive awareness and intrinsic self-regulation through greater diner agency, attentional focus, and sensory engagement. To articulate this conceptual shift, the article presents a five-pillar model comprising: (1) experiential drivers, (2) hybrid eating contexts, (3) liquid meaning-making, (4) triadic sensory-digital-human design, and (5) inclusivity and equity. These pillars explain how agency-centered phygital experiential design may shape food choice, attentional focus, emotional tone, and perceived well-being. Kitchen Theory is then examined as an illustrative case showing how the five pillars operate in practice. Finally, the article outlines managerial implications for food experience design, hospitality innovation, and health-oriented environments, alongside an empirical research agenda on agency-driven consumption in blended physical-digital food ecosystems.
