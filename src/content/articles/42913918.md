### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討營養學碩士生對同理心（compassion）的理解與學習方式，因為在營養教育與實踐中，同理心對應對人際、倫理和情感複雜的工作至關重要，但現有課程常側重技術能力而忽略此面向。研究採用質性方法，對17名加拿大大學營養學碩士生進行訪談與主題分析。結果顯示學生將同理心理解為透過行動表達的人際關係工作，其學習多來自同儕支持、生活經驗等非正式途徑，而課程則多透過社會正義等間接概念來支持。研究建議透過角色扮演、小組討論及反思性對話等體驗式學習，使同理心與自我同理心更明確地融入課程，以更好地培養學生未來工作所需的人際與情感能力。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了營養學研究生在同理心教育領域的空白，特別是深入探討了學生如何理解和學習同理心，以及現有課程如何間接或不足地支持此發展。它為將同理心與自我同理心明確納入營養學課程提供了實證基礎和具體建議，以彌補現行教育側重技術能力而忽略人際與倫理面向的不足。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** 'We don't learn so much about compassion': A qualitative study of graduate nutrition students' ways of understanding and learning about compassion.
**Abstract:** Compassion is important in nutrition education and practice, particularly as students prepare for relational, ethical, and emotionally complex work, yet it remains underemphasized in graduate nutrition education. Existing curricula often prioritize technical competence, leaving gaps in the development of relational and ethical aspects of practice. To examine how graduate nutrition students understand and learn compassion and to identify opportunities to integrate structured compassion and self-compassion education into academic programmes. Qualitative study using semi-structured interviews analysed with reflexive thematic analysis under a poststructural lens. Course outlines were reviewed for compassion related concepts, and participants were invited to review key findings. Seventeen current or recently graduated master's-level students from a Canadian university participated in the study between 2022 and 2023. Most were international students enrolled in applied human nutrition programs. Participants understood compassion as relational work expressed through action. Compassion learning was often produced through informal experiences, including peer support, lived experience narratives, group work, and reflection, while structured curriculum more often supported compassion indirectly through related concepts such as social justice, empathy, and reflective practice. Four themes organised the results: 1) compassion as action in relations, 2) indirect learning through graduate experiences, 3) groundwork through curriculum and practice, and 4) self-compassion in context. Participants recommended role play, small group discussion, communication practice, storytelling, reflection, and peer debriefing to support compassionate action, self-compassion, and boundary setting. In the context of these graduate nutrition programs, compassion was valued but often remained implicit. The findings can inform the curriculum across other levels of nutrition and dietetics training contexts. Making compassion and self-compassion more explicit through experiential learning, reflective dialogue, and structured feedback may better prepare students for the interpersonal, relational, and emotional dimensions of future nutrition related work.
