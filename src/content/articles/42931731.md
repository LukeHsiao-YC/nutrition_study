### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討未被滿足的社會需求（如不穩定住房、食物不安全）與烹飪頻率的關聯，以及這些需求是否調節烹飪頻率和自我評估飲食品質之間的關係。透過對美國1,570名成年人進行橫斷面線上問卷調查分析，結果發現擁有較多未被滿足社會需求的個體烹飪頻率顯著較低。此外，多種未被滿足的社會需求，如支付帳單困難和不穩定住房，確實調節了烹飪頻率與飲食品質之間的關係，凸顯了在推廣烹飪作為改善飲食策略時需考量社會需求的重要性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
此研究揭示了未被滿足的社會需求在烹飪頻率與自我評估飲食品質之間扮演的關鍵調節角色，填補了過去可能忽略社會經濟因素對飲食行為影響的空白。然而，本研究為橫斷面設計，無法確立因果關係，且自我評估的飲食品質存在主觀性，未來的研究可採用縱向設計並客觀測量飲食攝取。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Unmet Social Needs Moderate the Relationship Between Cooking Frequency and Self-Rated Diet Quality.
**Abstract:** To assess (1) the associations between unmet social needs and cooking frequency and (2) whether unmet social needs moderate the relationship between cooking frequency and self-rated diet quality. Cross-sectional analysis of survey data collected online in 2024. 1,570 adults living in the US. Cooking frequency was the outcome for Aim 1. Self-rated diet quality was the outcome for Aim 2. Ordinal logistic regression tested the association between cooking frequency and 6 unique unmet social needs (e.g., unstable housing, food insecurity). An interaction term was used to evaluate whether each unmet social need moderated the relationship between cooking frequency and self-rated diet quality. Those with ≥ 5 unmet needs were 55% less likely (adjusted odds ratio: 0.45; 95% confidence interval: 0.28-0.72) to report a cooking frequency greater than one time per week compared to participants with no unmet social needs. Interaction analyses revealed that difficulty paying bills, receiving a utility shutoff threat, experiencing unstable housing, or experiencing food insecurity, moderated the relationship between cooking frequency and self-rated diet quality. All unmet social needs, except food insecurity, were associated with lower cooking frequency. Several unmet social needs moderated the relationship between cooking frequency and diet quality. Program planners must consider unmet social needs when promoting cooking as a strategy to improve diet quality.
