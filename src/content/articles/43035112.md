### 重點摘要
此系統性回顧旨在評估全球當代心血管研究縱向世代的分布、代表性及表型深度，這些研究日益整合影像、基因組學等多模態數據。研究者系統性搜尋MEDLINE、Embase和Global Index Medicus，納入2000年後建立、招募成人、樣本量≥5000且追蹤期≥12個月的心血管相關世代。從篩選的18,185筆記錄中，共識別出147個獨特世代，主要分布在東亞、歐洲和北美，且年輕成人及白人和東亞人以外的族群仍代表性不足。雖然當代世代研究為心血管研究提供空前機會，但需要加強全球合作、投資於服務不足的人群，並協調數據標準以確保研究的公平性與影響力。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻透過系統性回顧，全面性地描繪了全球當代心血管研究縱向世代的現狀，包括其地理分布、代表性、表型深度及數據整合趨勢，填補了對這些寶貴資源整體概況的了解空白。然而，研究也揭示了未來的限制，包括年輕成人和特定族裔的代表性不足、先進數據整合仍集中於高收入地區，以及數據標準化與全球合作的挑戰，這些都是未來研究需克服的瓶頸。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The global landscape of contemporary longitudinal cohorts for cardiovascular research: a systematic review.
**Abstract:** Longitudinal cohorts are central to cardiovascular research and increasingly integrate imaging, genomics, biomarkers, digital health, and health record linkage. This paper systematically reviews the global distribution, representativeness, and phenotypic depth of contemporary longitudinal cohorts relevant to cardiovascular research. MEDLINE, Embase (both 4 March 2025), and Global Index Medicus (20 August 2025) were systematically searched. Cohorts with cardiovascular relevance established after 2000, exclusively recruiting adults (≥18 years), with a sample size of ≥5000 participants, and ≥12 months of prospective follow-up were included. We characterized geographic distribution, diversity, phenotypic depth, and methodological features and consider implications for the future of population science. From 18 185 records screened, 147 unique cohorts were identified. Of these, mean weighted age was 55 years, 54% of participants were female, and sample sizes ranged from 5000 to ∼2.5 million. Most cohorts were based in East Asia, Europe, and North America, with few from Africa and South America. Young adults and ethnicities other than White and East Asian remain under-represented. Multi-modal imaging, genetic and biomarker profiling, and digital health integration are increasingly incorporated, but remain concentrated in high-income settings. We highlight the emergence of 'super cohorts' with exceptional breadth and scale, cohorts with specialist cardiovascular imaging and wearable data, and multi-omics enriched resources, while examining sources of bias and challenges in harmonization. Contemporary cohorts offer unprecedented opportunities for cardiovascular research. Coordinated global collaboration, investment to increase representation of under-served populations, and harmonization of data standards are essential to ensure equitable, reproducible, and clinically impactful cardiovascular science.
