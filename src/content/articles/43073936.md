### 重點摘要
這項混合方法研究旨在探討馬來西亞低收入社區中，與10年心血管疾病(CVD)風險相關的可修改因素，並了解健康生活方式的動機與障礙。研究分為兩階段：第一階段為312名成年人的橫斷面調查，計算弗雷明漢CVD風險分數；第二階段對高風險參與者進行焦點團體討論，以了解其健康行為動機和障礙。結果顯示，飲食多樣性、隨機血糖和腰臀比與CVD風險顯著相關；質性研究則發現自我效能、社會支持和CVD知識是動機，而健康洞察力不足、社會心理挑戰是障礙。整合這些發現，研究提出了一個初步概念框架，包含心血管代謝風險因素管理、自我照護賦能、克服障礙策略及社會與社區支持，以指導未來CVD預防介入措施的發展。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了低收入族群CVD風險原因不明及預防策略成效不一的空白，透過混合方法識別了特定社區中可修改的心血管代謝、行為和情境因素。然而，所提出的初步概念框架仍屬假設生成階段，需經利害關係人審查、外部驗證及前瞻性介入試驗才能作為實施框架，此為其當前限制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Primary cardiovascular disease prevention in a low-income Malaysian community: a sequential mixed-methods study informing a preliminary conceptual framework.
**Abstract:** Worldwide, cardiovascular disease (CVD) stands as the leading cause of mortality. Studies have shown that individuals of lower socioeconomic status have higher CVD risk, yet the reasons remain inconclusive. Also, primary CVD prevention strategies yield variable outcomes. This study aimed to use a sequential explanatory mixed-methods design to identify modifiable factors associated with 10-year CVD risk and to explore motivations and barriers to healthier lifestyles in a low-income Malaysian community. The integrated findings were then used to inform a preliminary conceptual framework for the development of future interventions. Phase 1 was a cross-sectional survey of 312 adults aged 30-74 years recruited by convenience sampling through a local non-governmental organisation. Eligible participants had a monthly household income below the state low-income threshold and no prior CVD. The Framingham General CVD Risk Score (FRS) was calculated from participant demographic, clinical and smoking data according to the published Framingham algorithm. Multiple linear regression was used to examine factors associated with FRS. Phase 2 comprised six focus group discussions (n = 34) with a criterion-based purposive subsample of Phase 1 participants with FRS ≥ 10% (moderate or high 10-year CVD risk). The Health Belief Model guided data collection and thematic analysis. Quantitative and qualitative findings were integrated using an adapted triangulation protocol and interpreted alongside relevant literature to generate a preliminary conceptual framework. Diet diversity, random blood glucose level, and waist-to-hip ratio were significantly associated with CVD risk, with adjusted ß (95% CI) values of -0.516 (-0.918 to -0.115), 0.270 (0.135 to 0.406) and 25.1 (17.0 to 33.1), respectively. Qualitative findings identified self-efficacy, social support, and knowledge and awareness of CVD as motivations for healthier behaviours, while poor health insight, psychosocial challenges and perceived lifestyle transitions acted as barriers. Integration of the quantitative and qualitative findings generated four provisional intervention domains: targeted management of cardiometabolic risk factors, self-care empowerment, strategies to overcome barriers, and social and community support. The mixed-methods findings highlight modifiable cardiometabolic, behavioural and contextual factors relevant to primary CVD prevention in this low-income community. They inform a preliminary conceptual framework that should be considered hypothesis-generating and requires stakeholder review, external validation and prospective intervention testing before use as an implementation framework.
