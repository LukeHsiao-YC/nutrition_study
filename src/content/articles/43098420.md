### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討癌症存活者從醫療團隊獲得飲食指導的盛行率，及其與同意參與AMPLIFI生活型態介入試驗的關聯性。研究分析了1210名癌症存活者的橫斷面次級數據，評估了自我回報的飲食指導接收情況及對試驗的同意率。結果顯示，約一半（51.2%）的受訪者曾接受飲食指導，其中女性、乳癌患者、肥胖者等特定族群比例較高。然而，研究發現接受飲食指導與否，與存活者同意參與生活型態介入試驗之間並無顯著關聯，這表明單純的飲食指導在鼓勵研究參與方面的作用有限。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究釐清了癌症存活者接受飲食指導的盛行率，並發現一般性的飲食指導對於鼓勵存活者參與生活型態介入研究的效果有限。未來的研究應探討是否提供更具體、與介入目標一致且在招募期附近提供的飲食指導，能更有效地提升癌症存活者的研究參與意願。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Factors associated with consent to a clinical trial: A cross-sectional secondary data analysis examining the potential role of dietary guidance.
**Abstract:** Cancer survivors face elevated risks for conditions that can be mitigated through dietary modification. Dietary guidance provided by health care teams may support behavior change and participation in health-promoting programs. However, the extent of dietary guidance receipt among survivors and its associations with consent to participate in lifestyle intervention trials are unclear. This secondary analysis aimed to examine the prevalence of self-reported receipt of dietary guidance from the health care team and associations with consenting to the Adapting MultiPLe behavior Interventions that eFfectively Improve cancer survivor health (AMPLIFI) randomized controlled trial. This study was a cross-sectional secondary analysis of data from survivors screened for trial eligibility (February 2020 - September 2021). Participants included survivors ≥1 year post diagnosis, aged ≥50 years identified from state and hospital registries in the Southeastern United States and screened for AMPLIFI (N = 1210). The main outcome measures were self-reported receipt of dietary guidance and consent to participate among the 300 eligible survivors. Chi-square tests assessed associations between guidance and demographic, clinical, and treatment variables. Chi-square tests and multivariable logistic regression examined associations with consent among AMPLIFI-eligible survivors. Approximately half (51.2%) of the 1210 screened individuals reported dietary guidance from the health care team. Survivors who were female (53.5%, p = .0204), diagnosed with breast cancer (57.3%, p = .0016), treated with hormone- (60.7%, p = .0248) or immuno-therapy (71.9%, p = .0014), with obesity (56.1%, p = .0061), or urban residents (54.1%, p = .0082) reported guidance more frequently compared to counterparts. Receipt of dietary guidance was not associated with consent to participate in the trial (OR = 0.773, 95% CI: 0.467-1.282, p = 0.3189). Receipt of dietary guidance from a health care team was more common among females, breast cancer survivors, survivors who had received hormone- or immune-therapy, individuals with obesity, and urban residents. However, dietary guidance was not associated with consent to participate in a lifestyle intervention trial. These findings suggest that dietary guidance alone may have a limited role in encouraging participation in lifestyle intervention research. Further research is needed to assess whether specific dietary guidance aligned with intervention goals and delivered closer to recruitment may instead be an effective strategy for engaging cancer survivors in this type of research.
