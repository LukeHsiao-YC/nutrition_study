### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討學校餐飲主管 (FSD) 在學校餐點中減少超加工食品 (UPF) 並增加現煮餐點時所面臨的障礙與促進因素。研究採用混合方法設計，透過訪談與問卷調查分析加州聖華金谷20位FSD的觀點。主要障礙包括勞動成本、人力短缺、廚房基礎設施不足和食材採購挑戰，而州和聯邦資助雖有幫助，但有限的廚房空間與資金不確定性阻礙了永續改革。本研究提供了寶貴的見解，以指導支持學校餐點從UPF轉向現煮餐點的政策與實踐。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了在學校餐點中減少超加工食品並增加現煮餐點的政策制定和實踐方面，來自餐飲服務主管視角的實際障礙與促進因素的知識空白。未來的研究可以進一步探討如何克服資金不確定性及基礎設施限制，以實現更具永續性的轉變。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Reducing ultra-processed and increasing scratch-cooked school meals: perceived barriers and facilitators from food service directors.
**Abstract:** To examine school food service directors' (FSD) perceived barriers and facilitators to increase scratch cooking as a strategy to reduce ultra-processed foods (UPF) in school meals. A mixed-methods study design was used, incorporating semi-structured interviews and surveys. A Community Advisory Board provided input on the study design, interview guide and interpretation of results. Interviews were audio-recorded, transcribed verbatim, de-identified and double-coded. A hybrid thematic analysis was conducted, integrating emergent and a priori themes based on the socio-ecological model. California San Joaquin Valley, USA, from January to June 2024. Twenty FSD were randomly selected from a stratified sample of San Joaquin Valley school districts, categorised by locale (rural/urban) and whether they received a California supplementary funding award requiring at least 40 % of school meals to be freshly prepared. Key barriers to increasing scratch cooking in school meals included labour costs, staff shortages, inadequate kitchen infrastructure and food procurement challenges, with many districts relying on UPF. State and federal funding supported scratch cooking, but limited kitchen space and funding uncertainty hindered sustainable changes. While some FSD identified student preferences as a challenge, others described successful strategies to engage both students and parents to increase acceptance of scratch-cooked meals and boost participation in school meals. Given the current momentum at the federal, state and local levels to reduce UPF in school meals, this study provides timely insights to guide policies and practices that support the transition from UPF to scratch-cooked meals.
