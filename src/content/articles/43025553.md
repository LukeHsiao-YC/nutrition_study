### 重點摘要
這項研究探討了加拿大青少年飲酒行為（AAI）與收入不平等之間的關聯，特別是透過憂鬱症狀的間接影響。研究利用2017-2019年COMPASS隊列數據與2016年人口普查資料，對14,939名青少年進行了性別分層路徑分析。結果顯示，收入不平等與男性青少年的AAI存在直接關聯，而對女性青少年而言，收入不平等是透過憂鬱症狀間接影響AAI。因此，延遲青少年飲酒的干預措施應結合處理收入不平等政策和心理健康支持，並考量性別特異性策略。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了收入不平等與青少年飲酒行為（AAI）之間具體關聯的知識空白，特別是區分了直接關聯以及透過憂鬱症狀的間接影響。此外，研究發現這些關聯在男性和女性青少年之間呈現不同模式，為未來制定更精準、考慮性別差異的公共衛生干預措施提供了重要見解。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Unequal Beginnings: Income Inequality, Depressive Symptoms, and Adolescent Alcohol Initiation in Canada.
**Abstract:** Adolescent alcohol initiation (AAI) is a risk factor of problematic use of alcohol. Income inequality has been associated with underage drinking. However, less is known about its specific association with AAI. This study examined: 1) whether income inequality was directly associated with AAI among Canadian adolescents and 2) whether income inequality was indirectly associated with AAI through depressive symptoms. The study used 2017-2019 cohort data from the Cannabis, Obesity, Mental Health, Physical Activity, Alcohol, Smoking, and Sedentary Behaviour study, with analyses conducted in October 2025. The cohort was linked to Census Division (CD) data from the 2016 Canadian census. The analytic sample included 14,939 adolescents (13-19 years) attending 116 schools across 39 CDs. Income inequality was measured using the standardized Gini coefficient. Depressive symptoms were assessed in 2017-2018 cycle using the 10-item CESD-R, followed by AAI in 2018-2019 cycle. AAI was coded as a binary outcome comparing adolescents who initiated alcohol use before age 18 with those who had never consumed alcohol. Gender-stratified path analyses were used to estimate the direct association between Gini and AAI and the indirect association through depressive symptoms. A direct association was observed only among males, for whom a one-SD increase in Gini was associated with 16% higher odds of AAI (OR = 1.16; 95% CI: 1.02, 1.33). Among females, income inequality was indirectly associated with higher odds of AAI through depressive symptoms (OR = 1.02; 95% CI: 1.01, 1.04), with no evidence of a total association (OR=0.96; 95% CI: 0.89-1.04). Associations between income inequality, depressive symptoms and AAI showed different patterns among males and females. Efforts to delay AAI should therefore combine policies addressing income inequality with mental health support for all adolescents. These efforts may also consider the different patterns observed among males and females.
