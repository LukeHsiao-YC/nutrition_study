### 重點摘要
這項單中心前瞻性世代研究旨在評估接受腸道外營養（PN）治療的成人患者中，再餵食低磷血症（RH）和再餵食症候群（RFS）的盛行率與相關風險因子。研究分析了354名PN患者的數據，發現RH的盛行率依據不同診斷標準介於4.5%至13.3%之間，但定義明確的RFS則未在該族群中觀察到。體重在3-6個月內下降超過10%、血清白蛋白和血紅蛋白水平與RH相關。研究提出RH合併不明原因的體液超載可能代表一種「次急性再餵食症候群」。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究評估了不同診斷標準下再餵食低磷血症（RH）的盛行率，並提出了「次急性再餵食症候群」的概念，試圖填補RFS定義共識不足的研究空白。然而，由於是單中心研究，且該研究中未觀察到符合標準的RFS案例，其結果的普遍性可能受限，需要多中心、更大規模的研究來驗證這些發現及次急性RFS的分類。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** A single centre experience of refeeding hypophosphataemia and refeeding syndrome in parenteral nutrition and a proposal for a sub-fulminant categorisation.
**Abstract:** Refeeding syndrome (RFS) is an uncommon, but potentially serious, complication of nutrition therapy. There remains a lack of consensus as to the clinical definition of RFS and refeeding hypophosphataemia (RH), and what measures should be included to assess risk. This study aims to evaluate the prevalence of RH and RFS using previously proposed criteria in our cohort of patients receiving parenteral nutrition (PN), and assess the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence guidelines as risk factors. A single-centre prospective cohort study of adult patients (age ≥18 years) receiving PN for more than 48 h over a 5 year period. Prevalence of RH was determined using four previously established diagnostic criteria and RFS by the King's College criteria. Risk factors were assessed using univariate analysis and multiple logistic regression. 354 cases fulfilled the inclusion criteria. 4.5-13.3% (17-47 of the 354 cases) met at least one of the criteria for RH. No cases met the criteria for RFS. Thirteen (3.7%) cases with RH manifested signs of fluid overload without an alternative explanation except possible refeeding syndrome. Weight loss above 10% over 3-6 months (P = 0.03), serum albumin (P = 0.02), and serum haemoglobin (P = 0.01) were factors associated with RH. Prevalence of RH varies considerably according to the established criteria used, but overall remains uncommon. Previously defined RFS was not seen in our cohort of patients. A combination of RH and fluid overload without an alternative cause may represent sub-fulminant RFS.
