### 重點摘要
本研究旨在利用網絡分析，探討大規模數位親職訓練計畫後，親職風格與兒童行為症狀網絡的短期與長期變化。研究對芬蘭2900個4歲兒童家庭進行了為期11週的數位介入，並在基線、6個月及24個月追蹤父母回報的行為與親職資料。結果顯示，基線時兒童攻擊行為是網絡核心，而介入後父母過度反應的變化與兒童攻擊行為的變化之間存在顯著且持久的關聯。本研究證實網絡分析有助於理解行為系統的持久轉變，並為未來介入目標的選擇提供依據。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了過去對於大規模、真實世界數位親職訓練計畫中，親職行為變化如何與兒童症狀的持續改善產生關聯的知識空白。透過網絡分析，它提供了一個系統層面的框架，以理解親職風格與兒童行為症狀在介入前後的動態互動與重組。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Short- and Long-Term Changes in the Network of Parenting Styles and Child Symptoms After a Digital Parent-Training Program: Network Analysis of a Nationwide Implementation Study.
**Abstract:** Digital parent-training programs are effective in reducing early childhood behavioral problems, yet little is known about how changes in parenting behaviors relate to sustained changes in child symptoms in real-world, large-scale implementations. Network analysis provides a systems-level framework for examining how parenting styles and child behavioral symptoms dynamically interact and reorganize following digital interventions. This study applied network analysis to examine how parenting styles and children's behavioral symptoms are interrelated before and after a digital-assisted parenting program implemented at a national scale, and how these associations evolve over short- and long-term follow-up (6 and 24 months). A total of 36,591 families of 4-year-old children were screened, of whom 4665 (12.7%) met the screening criteria and 2900 were enrolled and received an 11-week digital parenting intervention embedded in Finland's public health care system. Parent-reported measures of behavioral symptoms and parenting styles were collected at baseline and 6- and 24-month follow-ups. Network analysis was performed using mixed graphical models, and bootstrapping was used to assess changes in edge weights from baseline. Strength centrality indexes were also computed. At baseline, child aggressive behavior was the most central symptom (strength centrality 1.35), strongly linked to callousness (edge weight 0.45) and emotional reactivity (edge weight 0.34). Parenting variables were peripheral. The results support the utility of network analysis for describing durable shifts in behavioral systems and for informing the selection of central intervention targets in future controlled research.
