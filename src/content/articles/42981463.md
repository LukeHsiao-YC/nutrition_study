### 重點摘要
本系統性回顧旨在綜合分析人們對植物性肉類替代品（PBMAs）的感知與態度，並探討社會人口學因素的影響。研究透過綜合民族誌原則，檢視了來自31項研究共2425名參與者的質性數據。結果識別出六個主題，並發現PBMAs的接受度深受年齡、性別和收入地區等交叉社會人口學因素形塑。這些因素反映了社會認同、道德價值和結構性不平等，解釋了儘管PBMAs具潛力但採用率仍有限的原因。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻綜合了現有關於植物性肉類替代品感知與態度的證據，突顯了社會人口學因素對其接受度的複雜影響。然而，研究限制和未來方向指出，仍需採用交叉性別考量的方法，並優先關注代表性不足的中低收入國家背景。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Perceptions and Attitudes Toward Plant-Based Meat Alternatives: A Systematic Meta-Ethnography Review.
**Abstract:** Plant-based meat alternatives (PBMAs) can support the transition toward healthier and more sustainable dietary patterns. However, PBMA adoption remains limited despite availability and awareness of health and environmental benefits, highlighting a gap between potential and practice. This review sought to synthesize evidence on perceptions and attitudes toward PBMAs across populations and to examine how sociodemographic factors shape consumer perspectives. A comprehensive search was conducted across MEDLINE, CINAHL, Web of Science, Greenfile, and Cochrane from inception to August 2025. This systematic review was informed by meta-ethnographic principles (4 to 6), while qualitative data were synthesized using thematic synthesis. Qualitative data from eligible studies were analyzed using a thematic synthesis assisted by ATLAS.ti software and an inductive and iterative approach for coding. Quality assessment employed the Critical Appraisal Skills Programme (CASP) checklist, and confidence in findings was evaluated using the GRADE-CERQual (Confidence in Evidence from Reviews of Qualitative Research) approach. This review included 31 studies with a total of 2425 participants (aged 9 to > 65 years) from the Asia-Pacific (n = 14), Europe (n = 14), the United States (n = 1), and the United Kingdom (n = 4). Analysis of 914 quotations identified 6 themes (12 subthemes): with moderate GRADE-CERQual confidence, including dietary options, food ethics, dietary perceptions, food openness, food preferences, and dietary drivers; high confidence for dietary alternatives and substitutes; and low confidence for animal ethics. Younger participants (aged <18 years) linked PBMAs with identity and experimentation, while middle-aged adults showed both critical evaluation and resistance. Women demonstrated greater awareness and culinary engagement; men expressed stronger reluctance tied to masculinity. High-income countries exhibited culinary narratives linked to sociocultural values, whereas low- and middle-income countries emphasized affordability and access barriers. Acceptance of PBMAs is shaped by intersecting sociodemographic factors that reflect broader social identities, moral values, and structural inequities. Future research should adopt intersectional and gender-informed approaches while prioritizing underrepresented low- and middle-income country contexts. CRD420251114306.
