### 重點摘要
這項觀察性橫斷面研究旨在評估高齡田徑運動員與年輕大學校隊運動員的營養知識。結果顯示，高齡運動員的整體營養知識顯著低於大學校隊運動員（45.2% vs 51.0%），特別是在一般營養、運動營養和碳水化合物相關知識方面存在不足。儘管蛋白質相關知識無顯著組間差異，但男性在蛋白質知識方面得分高於女性。研究結論指出，高齡運動員的營養知識落差可能導致其飲食攝取未能達到建議，因此建議透過客製化的營養教育來改善其飲食行為及健康成果。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
過去文獻並未評估競技型高齡運動員的運動營養知識，本研究填補了此一研究空白，首次將高齡運動員的營養知識與受專業營養支持的年輕運動員進行比較。未來的研究限制可能包含未能探討營養知識水平對實際運動表現或健康結果的因果關係，以及缺乏對不同教育介入方案有效性的評估，且本研究為橫斷面設計，無法闡明長期影響。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Lower Sport Nutrition Knowledge in Masters Track and Field Athletes Compared with Younger Competitors.
**Abstract:** Appropriate nutrition is essential for supporting both athletic performance and healthy aging. In masters athletes, the physiological demands of aging and the nutritional needs of competitive sport are combined. However, dietary intakes of masters athletes do not generally meet sport nutrition recommendations. Despite evidence linking nutrition knowledge to dietary quality, sport nutrition knowledge among competitive masters athletes has not previously been evaluated. The primary objective of this observational cross-sectional study was to assess nutrition knowledge in masters athletes competing at the World Masters Athletics Championships compared to varsity athletes with structured dietetic support. Masters track and field athletes competing at World Masters Athletics Championships as well as varsity university track and field athletes completed the Abridged Nutrition for Sport Knowledge Questionnaire to assess both general and sport-specific nutritional knowledge. Differences in scores attained by masters and varsity athletes were assessed using independent t-tests. A total of 238 athletes participated (n=86 varsity athletes; n=152 masters athletes; n=120 males; n=118 females). Varsity athletes scored significantly higher on overall nutrition knowledge (51.0±12.8%) compared with masters athletes (45.2±15.0%; p=0.002). Varsity athletes also demonstrated higher scores in general nutrition (p=0.02), sport nutrition (p=0.01), and carbohydrate-related knowledge (p=0.01). No statistically significant differences were observed between groups for protein-related knowledge (p=0.09). A significant main effect of sex was observed for knowledge relating to protein, with males scoring higher than females (43.0±21.5% vs. 38.5±21.6%; p=0.039). Masters athletes demonstrated lower nutrition knowledge compared to varsity athletes across general and sport specific domains, with particular deficits observed in questions pertaining to carbohydrates. Given the nutritional needs of aging athletes, these gaps may contribute to the documented disconnect between dietary intake and recommendations. Tailored, evidence-based nutrition education may help improve dietary behaviors, training adaptations, and long-term health outcomes in masters athletes.
