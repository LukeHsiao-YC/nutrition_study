### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討土耳其在職成人中，糧食不安全感與主觀社會地位對超加工食品（UPF）攝取的影響，並考量感知壓力及心理健康。這項針對1,084名在職成人進行的橫斷面研究，使用高度加工食品攝取篩選問卷（sQ-HPF）評估UPF攝取量。結果顯示，年齡與UPF攝取呈負相關，而糧食不安全感與UPF篩選分數之間存在微小但不明確的正向關聯，主觀社會地位、感知壓力及心理健康則未顯示顯著關聯。總體而言，研究指出糧食不安全感與UPF攝取之間可能存在微弱的正向關聯，但需更多縱向研究與量化測量來進一步證實。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項橫斷面研究對糧食不安全感、主觀社會地位與UPF攝取間的關聯提供了初步探索，但因其橫斷面設計與結果的精確性不足，未能確立因果關係。未來的研究需要進行縱向追蹤，並採用更精確的UPF量化測量方法，以釐清這些變項之間的複雜關係。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Food insecurity, subjective social status and ultra-processed food consumption among working adults in Türkiye.
**Abstract:** Food insecurity and socioeconomic disadvantage may influence food choices, including the consumption of ultra-processed foods (UPFs). This cross-sectional study examined the associations of food insecurity and subjective social status with UPF consumption in a convenience sample of 1,084 employed adults in Türkiye, while also considering perceived stress and psychological well-being. UPF consumption was assessed using the Screening Questionnaire of Highly Processed Food Consumption (sQ-HPF). Participants had a mean age of 31.7 ± 10.2 years, and 64.7% scored ≥6 on the sQ-HPF as administered. In the fully adjusted hierarchical regression model, age was negatively associated with sQ-HPF scores (B=-0.090, 95% CI: -0.111 to -0.068, p<0.001), whereas food insecurity showed a small positive but imprecise association (B=0.072, 95% CI: -0.012 to 0.156, p=0.093). Subjective social status, perceived stress dimensions (self-efficacy and helplessness), and well-being were not significantly associated with sQ-HPF scores. In an exploratory cross-sectional single-mediator analysis, the indirect association between subjective social status and UPF screening scores through food insecurity was not statistically significant (B=-0.0068, 95% bootstrap CI: -0.0205 to 0.0013). A secondary exploratory serial indirect-association analysis incorporating the PSS total score also showed no statistically significant indirect associations. Overall, the findings suggest a small positive but imprecise association between food insecurity and UPF screening scores. Longitudinal studies with quantitative measures of UPF consumption are needed.
