### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討全球飲食品質評分 (GDQS) 及其子量表（GDQS+ 健康飲食攝取，GDQS- 不健康飲食攝取）與南非黑人成年人全因及心血管疾病死亡率的關聯。研究追蹤南非黑人成年人13年，使用基線飲食數據計算GDQS，並運用Cox比例風險模型分析死亡率風險。結果顯示，GDQS+ 分數較高（代表健康飲食攝取較多）與較低的全因死亡風險相關 (HR = 0.69)，然而GDQS- 分數較高（代表不健康飲食攝取較少）卻與較高的全因死亡風險相關 (HR = 1.39)。總體而言，GDQS對評估非洲人群全因死亡風險有價值，特別是透過GDQS+，但GDQS- 在飲食單一或糧食不安全環境下的應用需謹慎。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了全球飲食品質評分 (GDQS) 在非洲人群全因及心血管疾病死亡率關聯研究的空白，提供了該地區的實證數據。然而，研究也指出GDQS，特別是GDQS- 子量表，在飲食單一或糧食不安全環境中的應用可能存在限制，這表示未來研究需進一步探討其在多元飲食文化背景下的適用性。此外，研究並未發現GDQS與心血管疾病死亡率的明確關聯，這也是未來研究可以深化的方向。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The association between the Global Diet Quality Score and all-cause mortality in Black South African adults.
**Abstract:** Modifiable risk factors for cardiovascular diseases (CVD) include diet quality. The Global Diet Quality Score (GDQS) was designed to provide a comprehensive diet quality assessment, yet very few studies have assessed its relationship with CVD and all-cause mortality, particularly in an African setting. We determined the association between the baseline total GDQS, GDQS positive (GDQS+) and GDQS negative (GDQS-) and all-cause and CVD mortality after 13 years of follow-up in Black South African adults (aged 30 to 70 years). GDQS+ reflects healthy food intake, while GDQS- is reverse-scored (higher scores indicating lower intake of unhealthy foods). Across all GDQS measures, higher scores indicate better diet quality. The baseline dietary data from the PURE-NWP-SA study was used to calculate the GDQS, with mortality outcomes recorded 13 years later. Cox proportional hazards models were applied, stratified by sex, age group and observation time tertiles. Models were adjusted for lifestyle factors and health status. Hazard ratios (HRs) were calculated for baseline GDQS tertiles. The overall GDQS was low in both survivors and non-survivors, reflecting low intake of many GDQS healthy food groups, with only whole grains and liquid oils reaching desirable levels (to give maximum points to the GDQS+). Compared with Tertile (T1), participants in T3 of the GDQS+ had a lower risk of all-cause mortality (HR = 0.69; 95% CI: 0.56-0.86), whereas those in T3 of the GDQS- had a higher risk (HR = 1.39; 95% CI: 1.12-1.73). No associations were found with CVD mortality. The GDQS is a valuable tool for investigating all-causes mortality risk in African populations through its GDQS+ sub-metric. However, caution is warranted when applying the GDQS in settings of dietary monotony or food insecurity, particularly the GDQS- sub-metric which gives maximum points for low intake/avoidance of unhealthy food groups.
