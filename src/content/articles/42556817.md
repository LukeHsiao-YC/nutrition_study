### 重點摘要
傳統上，過敏性休克患者在注射腎上腺素後需觀察至少四小時，以監測雙相反應，但2020年新指南建議低風險患者可縮短觀察時間。本研究旨在透過一項品質改進計畫，在九個月內將兒科急診室低風險過敏性休克患者的觀察時間從240分鐘縮短至120分鐘，並實施教育、電子病歷優化及回饋等干預措施。儘管急診總停留時間增加，低風險過敏性休克患者的觀察時間仍從233分鐘成功減少至154分鐘（下降34%），並維持了七個月，且未增加再就診率。這項研究成功地證明，對於低風險患者，可以安全地縮短急診觀察時間，有效提升醫療效率。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻成功填補了在真實世界兒科急診環境中，實施和驗證新版過敏性休克臨床指南（縮短低風險患者觀察時間）的可行性與安全性方面的空白，提供了具體數據支持減少不必要的急診停留時間。未來研究可以考慮擴展至多中心驗證、成人族群或探討其成本效益。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Reducing Emergency Department Observation Time for Patients with Low-Risk Anaphylaxis.
**Abstract:** Historically, patients with anaphylaxis are observed for at least 4 hours after receiving epinephrine to monitor for biphasic reactions. In April 2020, the Joint Task Force on Practice Parameters (JTFPP) released updated anaphylaxis guidelines, recommending a 1-hour observation time for patients at low risk for biphasic anaphylaxis. We conducted a QI initiative with the aim to decrease our pediatric emergency department (ED) observation time for patients with low-risk anaphylaxis from 240 to 120 minutes within 9 months. A multidisciplinary team of allergy/immunology and ED physicians and nurses identified barriers to early discharge using a fishbone diagram. Interventions included education, electronic medical record optimization, and feedback. Risk factors for biphasic anaphylaxis included persistent symptoms after epinephrine, need for more than 1 epinephrine dose, hypoxia, hypotension, and reaction to a drug or unknown trigger. Balancing measures were observation times for anaphylaxis with risk factors and return visits within 72 hours of discharge for biphasic reactions. We used statistical process control to analyze changes in measures over time. Despite an increasing ED length of stay for all discharged ED patients during this QI initiative, the observation time for patients with low-risk anaphylaxis decreased from 233 to 154 minutes (34%) in 5 months, which was sustained for 7 months. There was no change for patients with risk factors. There were no missed biphasic anaphylactic reactions during the intervention period. We successfully reduced the ED observation time for patients with low-risk anaphylaxis by 79 minutes without increasing return visits.
