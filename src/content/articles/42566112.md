### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討疫情期間，州級醫療補助擴張政策對低收入家庭兒童醫療服務的可近性、利用率、健康狀況，以及父母健康的影響。研究利用2016-2023年全國兒童健康調查數據，發現與非擴張州相比，醫療補助擴張州的兒童在疫情期間常規照護和預防性就醫減少，但心理健康治療和其他專科就診增加。同時，兒童和父母的健康狀況在疫情期間均有所下降。這些結果可能反映了父母覆蓋率提高與疫情社會流動限制雙重作用下，常規照護受阻和心理健康惡化的影響。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究揭示了COVID-19大流行期間，州級醫療補助擴張對低收入家庭兒童醫療服務利用及整體健康的複雜影響，填補了疫情下政策效應的理解空白。然而，研究結果暗示了父母醫療覆蓋增加與疫情相關社會流動限制之間的交互作用，這表明未來研究應更深入探討這些複雜因素如何共同影響健康結果。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Effects of State Medicaid Expansions Affecting Parental Eligibility on Children's Healthcare Access and Health During the COVID-19 Pandemic.
**Abstract:** To examine whether pre-pandemic state Medicaid expansions for adults affected children's healthcare access and utilization, child health, and parental health during the COVID-19 pandemic. The sample includes children from households with incomes up to 138% of the Federal Poverty Level using data from the 2016-2023 National Survey of Children's Health. Child outcomes include insurance coverage, usual place for sick care, personal doctor or nurse, unmet healthcare needs, past 12-month utilization of any medical services, preventive medical and dental visits, mental health professional treatments, non-mental health specialist visits, emergency department visits, hospitalizations, and general/oral health ratings. Parent outcomes include physical and mental health ratings. Difference-in-differences models estimate year-by-year differences in outcomes between Medicaid expansion and non-expansion states. There is a decline in usual place for sick care, personal doctor or nurse, and preventive medical visits in the first or second pandemic year and in child health in 2020/2023 in expansion versus non-expansion states. In contrast, there is an increase in mental health professional treatments and other specialist visits in 2021-2023 in expansion states. Lastly, there are declines in maternal physical/mental health and father's mental health in 2020 but increase in father's mental/physical health in 2023 in expansion states. During the pandemic, children in low-income households had less care continuity and preventive care but more mental health treatments and non-mental specialist visits in Medicaid expansion versus non-expansion states. There were also declines in both child and parent health. Findings potentially reflect intertwined effects from greater parental coverage but more pandemic-related social mobility restrictions disrupting routine/preventive care and worsening mental health more in expansion states.
