### 重點摘要
這項研究旨在探討核桃攝取對健康老年人飲食炎症指數（DII）的影響。研究從Walnuts and Healthy Aging（WAHA）試驗中選取319名63-79歲健康老年人，隨機分為核桃組（每日攝取1-2盎司核桃，持續2年）和對照組（不攝取核桃）。結果顯示，核桃組的平均DII分數顯著低於對照組（-0.025 vs 0.615，p<0.01），表示其飲食具有更強的抗炎潛力。研究結論為，將核桃納入日常飲食與更具抗炎性的DII分數相關，這表明添加單一全食物可能是改善飲食炎症潛力的可行策略。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項隨機對照試驗填補了核桃作為單一全食物如何影響健康老年人飲食炎症潛力的實證空白。然而，此研究僅來自WAHA試驗的Loma Linda隊列，其結果的普遍性可能受限，且DII是一個指數而非直接的炎症生物標記。未來研究可進一步探討核桃對體內直接炎症生物標記的影響，以更全面地了解其抗炎機制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The Dietary Inflammatory Index is Lower Among Healthy Older Adults Consuming a Walnut-Supplemented Diet. Ancillary Results from the Walnuts and Healthy Aging Randomized Controlled Trial.
**Abstract:** Walnuts may lower diet-related inflammation by providing anti-inflammatory compounds and displacing pro-inflammatory nutrients. To explore the association between daily walnut consumption or abstention for 2 years and the Dietary Inflammatory Index (DII®). The Walnuts and Healthy Aging (WAHA) study is a dual-center (Loma Linda, California, USA and Barcelona, Spain), randomized trial, comparing a walnut-rich diet with a control diet over 2 years between May 2012 to May 2016, for age-related outcomes in healthy, free-living older adults aged 63-79 y. This ancillary study concerned only the Loma Linda cohort. A total of 319 older adults (mean age 69 y, 66% women) from Loma Linda, Ca were randomly assigned to walnut (n = 166) or control (n = 153) diets. The walnut group consumed 1-2 oz walnuts/day (30-60 g/day, ∼15% of energy), while the control group abstained from walnuts and limited other nuts to <2 servings/week. Both groups followed their usual background diet. DII scores were calculated from five 24-hour dietary recalls taken at random during the 2-year period. Linear mixed models tested DII differences between the walnut and control groups, adjusting for sex, age, BMI, race, energy intake, smoking status, physical activity, and educational attainment. The mean DII (SE) score was -0.025 (0.14) in the walnut group and 0.615 (0.14) in the control diet group (p<0.01). At the nutrient level, n-3 PUFAs (ß=-0.291; 95%CI: -0.450, -0.132; p<0.01), magnesium (ß=-0.014; 95%CI: -0.015, -0.013; p<0.01), carbohydrate (ß=-0.009; 95%CI: -0.014, -0.005; p<0.01), fiber (ß=-0.120; 95%CI: -0.133, -0.106; p=0.05), and protein (ß=-0.020; 95% CI: -0.031, -0.008; p<0.01), were associated with a more anti-inflammatory DII score, while intake of total and saturated fat were associated with a higher, more proinflammatory, DII score (ß=0.033; 95%CI: 0.020, 0.045; p<0.01 and ß=0.102; 95%CI: 0.080, 0.125; p<0.01 respectively). Incorporating walnuts into the habitual diet was associated with more anti-inflammatory DII scores compared with a walnut-free control diet. These findings suggest that adding a single whole food may be a feasible complementary strategy for favorably improving the inflammatory potential of a diet.
