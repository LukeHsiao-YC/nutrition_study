### 重點摘要
鑑於患者病情複雜度增加和醫療資源限制，醫療系統對效率的需求日益增長，本研究旨在驗證「通用營養病患複雜度工具 (UDPCT)」作為標準化評估工具的效度與信度。此多中心研究在英國國民保健署(NHS)營養部門進行，透過資深營養師問卷、配對評估與李克特量表，評估了內容效度、表面效度與評分者間信度。研究結果顯示，UDPCT在所有十個領域都具有高內容效度 (I-CVI 0.92-1.00)，評分者間信度呈現高度正相關 (Spearman's rho = 0.70)，且表面效度亦獲得高度認可。這表明UDPCT是一個在多元營養照護環境中有效且可靠的工具，潛力應用於人力資源規劃、服務發展和營運決策。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了營養學領域缺乏一個經過多中心驗證、標準化且能客觀評估患者複雜度的工具的空白，有助於提升營養照護的效率與資源配置。文章並未明確提及未來的研究限制，但強調了UDPCT在評估服務容量、人力規劃和服務發展方面的潛在應用。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Validation of the Universal Dietetic Patient Complexity Tool (UDPCT): a multi-centre study of content validity, face validity and inter-rater reliability.
**Abstract:** Observed changes in patient acuity over the past century and in more recent years due to the COVID-19 pandemic, plus financial constraints, are putting pressure on the healthcare system and those who work in it to become more efficient. This has highlighted the importance of job plans and skill mixing for caseload and staffing management. The Universal Dietetic Patient Complexity Tool (UDPCT) was developed taking into account all aspects of dietetic care, to provide a standardised assessment of caseload acuity and to facilitate timely and cost-effective screening of dietetic patients. This study aimed to evaluate the content validity, face validity and inter-rater reliability of the UDPCT. A multicentre validation study was conducted across National Health Service (NHS) dietetic departments throughout the United Kingdom. Content validity was assessed using structured questionnaires completed by senior dietitians from participating sites. Item-level Content Validity Index (I-CVI) was calculated for each UDPCT domain. Inter-rater reliability was evaluated through independent paired assessments of the same patients using the UDPCT. Agreement between assessors was analysed using Spearman's rank-order correlation coefficient. Face validity was assessed using a five-point Likert questionnaire and qualitative feedback. Twelve NHS dietetic departments representing all four UK nations participated. Twelve senior dietitians completed content validity assessments and 204 dietitians and dietetic assistants (104 assessor pairs) assessed 417 patients across acute, community, adult, paediatric and mental health services. All 10 UDPCT domains exceeded the predefined threshold for content validity (I-CVI range 0.92-1.00). Consensus regarding complexity categories and associated cut-off scores was achieved by 91.7% of expert respondents. Inter-rater reliability demonstrated a high positive correlation between paired assessors (Spearman's rho = 0.70, p < 0.001). Face validity demonstrated strong acceptability, with median and modal scores of 4/5 across all questionnaire domains. The UDPCT demonstrated strong content validity, face validity and inter-rater reliability across diverse dietetic settings. The tool provides a validated approach to assessing dietetic patient complexity and has potential applications in assessing capacity and demand, safe staffing/workforce planning, service development and business case planning.
