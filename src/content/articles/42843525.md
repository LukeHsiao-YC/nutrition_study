### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討ADHD兒童的飲食行為模式及其與特定ADHD症狀維度的關聯。研究比較了70名ADHD兒童與60名典型發展兒童的飲食行為，並透過父母和教師報告評估ADHD症狀。結果顯示ADHD兒童在食物反應性、情緒性過食、享受食物、情緒性少食及整體食物趨近行為上得分較高。多元分析發現，教師評估的衝動性是食物趨近行為的唯一顯著獨立相關因素，且情緒性過食可能代表了連結衝動性症狀與體重指數的潛在行為途徑。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了過去對ADHD兒童特定症狀維度與不同飲食行為模式（跨越不同資訊提供者）關聯性描述不足的研究空白。然而，由於本研究是橫斷面設計，其發現無法確立因果關係，需待未來的縱向研究進一步證實。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Eating Behavior Profiles and Associated Factors in Children with ADHD: A Multidimensional and Multi-Informant Perspective.
**Abstract:** Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) has been associated with atypical eating behaviors and weight-related outcomes in childhood; however, relationships between specific ADHD symptom dimensions and distinct eating behavior patterns across informants remain insufficiently characterized. This study compared eating behaviors between children with ADHD and typically developing peers and examined symptom-specific correlates of food-approach and food-avoidance behaviors. This cross-sectional case-control study included 70 medication-naïve children with ADHD and 60 typically developing controls aged 7-9 years. Eating behaviors were assessed using the Children's Eating Behaviour Questionnaire, while ADHD symptoms were evaluated using parent- and teacher-reported measures. Group comparisons, correlation analyses, multivariable regression models, and an exploratory indirect effect analysis were conducted. Children with ADHD exhibited higher food responsiveness, emotional overeating, enjoyment of food, emotional undereating, and overall food-approach scores than controls. In multivariable analysis, teacher-rated impulsivity emerged as the only statistically significant independent correlate of food-approach behaviors after simultaneous adjustment for all prespecified predictors. Exploratory indirect effect analysis showed a significant indirect association between teacher-rated impulsivity and BMI z-score through emotional overeating. Eating behaviors in children with ADHD appear heterogeneous, with food-approach and food-avoidance patterns showing distinct associations with symptom dimensions across informants. Emotional overeating may represent a potential behavioral pathway linking impulsivity-related symptoms to relative body weight. These cross-sectional findings warrant confirmation in longitudinal studies.
