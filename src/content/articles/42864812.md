### 重點摘要
本敘述性回顧旨在探討健康個體在無臨床指徵下，長期補充膳食營養品可能對生理、微生物及公共衛生的影響。研究方法為對2009年至2024年間發表的科學文獻進行回顧，分析其對腸道轉運蛋白、消化酶和腸道微生物群的影響。結果顯示，長期補充營養品會導致腸道轉運蛋白下調、消化酶分泌減少以及腸道菌群組成改變，進而損害腸道吸收功能與消化效率。最終，這種現象可能引發肝腎毒性等不良反應，並導致腸道對外部營養輸入產生依賴性，對生理、演化及公共衛生帶來重要影響。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了健康個體在缺乏臨床指徵下，長期膳食補充品可能對腸道功能、營養穩態、腸道微生物組及演化生理學造成的負面生理適應與公共衛生影響的研究空白，挑戰了補充品普遍有益的觀點。它強調了不必要補充品可能導致腸道功能退化甚至依賴的風險，並呼籲應推廣循證補充品使用及飲食多樣性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Evolution Toward a Useless Gut: Physiological Consequences of Unnecessary Supplementation: A Narrative Review.
**Abstract:** This narrative review aims to critically examine the physiological, microbiological, and public health consequences of chronic dietary supplementation in healthy individuals without clinical indications. The review explores the hypothesis that unnecessary supplementation may impair intestinal function and nutrient homeostasis through adaptive mechanisms. A narrative review was conducted using indexed scientific literature published during 2009-2024. Studies in both humans and animal models were included. The review examined the effects of prolonged micronutrient and macronutrient supplementation on intestinal transporters, digestive enzyme activity, gut microbiota composition, and evolutionary physiology. Chronic supplementation was found to induce the physiological downregulation of intestinal transporters (eg, sodium-dependent vitamin C transporter 1 [SVCT1]; transient receptor potential vanilloid 6 [TRPV6], a protein that acts as a calcium channel in cell membranes; and ferroportin), reduce digestive enzyme secretion, and alter gut microbiota composition. The effects included a loss of microbial diversity and short-chain fatty acid (SCFA) production and were associated with impaired absorption, reduced digestive efficiency, and the potential for long-term intestinal dependence on external nutrient input. Furthermore, evidence of pharmacological interactions and adverse effects such as hepatotoxicity and nephrotoxicity has been documented. In the absence of a qualified health care professional, the chronic use of nutritional supplements may trigger intestinal adaptations that compromise natural digestive and absorptive functions. This phenomenon has physiological, evolutionary and public health implications. Health policies and clinical practice should therefore promote the evidence-based use of supplements and reinforce the importance of dietary diversity.
