### 重點摘要
這篇討論論文概念化了醫療照護中因過度依賴人工智慧（AI）工具而產生的「認知迷霧」，將其定義為一種模糊輔助與權威界線、損害臨床推理、患者教育和共享決策的溝通與推理問題。透過文獻綜合，研究指出認知迷霧包含知識模糊、後設認知減弱和關係移位。文章強調AI過度依賴不僅是技術安全問題，更是健康溝通議題，可導致建議不準確、教育錯認和問責制弱化。為有效緩解，文獻提出需要明確角色界限、提升AI素養、實施強制性認知檢查、加強人為審查，並建立完善的治理機制以應對個人和系統性障礙。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻透過提出「認知迷霧」的概念，填補了AI在醫療領域的應用不僅涉及技術安全，更是一個重要的健康溝通和推理問題的理論空白。它為理解AI如何影響人類認知與關係提供了新的視角，並提出了具體的管理與緩解框架。然而，作為一篇概念性討論，未來的研究可能需要實證數據來量化認知迷霧的實際影響，並評估所提緩解策略的有效性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Cognitive fog in AI-assisted care: Preserving clinical reasoning, patient education, and shared decision-making.
**Abstract:** This discussion paper conceptualizes cognitive fog as a communication and reasoning problem that can emerge when clinicians, patients, or organizations over-rely on artificial intelligence (AI) tools in health care. Drawing on a selective, theory-oriented narrative synthesis of literature on AI-enabled clinical decision support, large language models, automation bias, cognitive offloading, patient-facing AI communication, and shared decision-making, this paper defines cognitive fog as a condition in which fluent, rapid, and institutionally embedded AI output blurs the boundary between assistance and authority. It comprises epistemic blurring, metacognitive weakening, and relational displacement. In clinical and patient-facing contexts, inaccurate or biased recommendations may reduce accuracy, while polished language can make generic education appear personal and automated recommendations appear deliberative. Management requires explicit role boundaries, AI literacy, cognitive forcing routines, visible human review, proportionate patient-facing disclosure, and governance that addresses workflow, accountability, equity, and patient understanding. AI overreliance is not only a technical safety issue; it is also a health communication issue because it can cloud reasoning, weaken accountability, and narrow shared decision-making. Effective mitigation must address both individual barriers, including time pressure, variable AI literacy, and reluctance to challenge automated output, and systemic barriers, including weak governance, opaque tools, misaligned incentives, and inadequate monitoring.
