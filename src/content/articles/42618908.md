### 重點摘要
身體活動不足是一個重要的公共衛生挑戰，且其分佈存在社會不平等。本研究旨在探討德國成年人口中身體活動相關健康能力（PAHCO）的分佈及其與社會不平等、健康脆弱性及社會資源的關聯。研究利用2023年1月至5月對德國3,986名成年人進行的電話調查數據，結果顯示38.2%的人口具有較低或偏低的PAHCO水平，且高齡、低教育程度、低收入、自評身心健康狀況不佳、慢性病及低社會支持均與較低的PAHCO顯著相關。研究結論指出，超過三分之一的人口需要加強PAHCO，並建議身體活動促進策略應特別針對高齡者、低教育程度者、健康狀況較差者及社會支持較少者的需求，以有效減少因社會不平等和健康脆弱性導致的身體活動不足。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了德國全國性成年人口中，身體活動相關健康能力（PAHCO）的分佈特徵及其與社會不平等、健康脆弱性及社會資源之間關聯的知識空白，突顯了需要優先介入的脆弱群體。然而，本研究採用橫斷面設計，因此無法確立這些因素與PAHCO之間的因果關係，未來研究可考慮進行縱向追蹤或介入性研究以探討其因果連結。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Physical activity-related health competence differs by social and health-related factors: findings from a nationwide population-based study in Germany.
**Abstract:** Insufficient physical activity remains a major public health challenge and is socially unequally distributed. Physical activity-related health competence (PAHCO) specifies the personal skills that enable health-enhancing physical activity. It integrates aspects of health literacy and comprises movement competence, control competence and self-regulation competence. This study examined the distribution of PAHCO in the adult population living in Germany and its associations with characteristics of social inequalities, health-related vulnerability, and limited social resources. We used data from a cross-sectional nationwide population-based survey of German-speaking adults conducted via standardised computer-assisted telephone interviews between January and May 2023 (n = 3,986). Bivariate and multiple logistic regression analyses were employed to examine the relationship between low PAHCO levels (overall score and its three sub-competencies) and characteristics of social inequalities, including sociodemographic factors (older age, female gender, lower education, and lower equivalent income), health-related vulnerability (poor self-rated general and mental health, chronic conditions), and social resources (low social support). Overall, 38.2% of the population showed low/rather low PAHCO levels. Higher odds of low/rather low PAHCO were observed among adults aged 65 years and older compared to 18-29-year-olds (OR = 3.77, 95% CI: 2.28-6.22), among individuals with low versus high formal education (OR = 1.96, 95% CI: 1.37-2.82), and among those in the lowest versus highest income group (OR = 1.95, 95% CI: 1.23-3.10). The further relevant predictors of lower PAHCO were poor self-rated general, mental health and chronic conditions as well as low social support. These patterns were consistent across all three sub-competences, but age and gender differences were observed only for movement competence. The findings indicate a need to strengthen PAHCO in more than one third of the population and suggest that PAHCO could help reduce insufficient physical activity associated with social inequalities and health-related vulnerability. Strategies to promote movement competence, control competence, and self-regulation competence should particularly address the needs and living conditions of older adults, individuals with lower education levels, poorer health status, or lower social support. Physical activity promotion programmes should address all three sub-competences of PAHCO in a target-group-specific manner.
