### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討志願服務對中老年人（50-64歲）高血壓盛行率的影響，並評估其效益是否因健康保險狀況而異。透過分析4,169名美國成年人的前瞻性世代數據，研究採用加權泊松回歸模型來估計志願服務的效果。結果顯示，志願服務與總體人群較低的高血壓盛行率顯著相關（PR=0.86），且無論是否投保，志願服務者的高血壓盛行率均較低。值得注意的是，志願服務對未投保者降低高血壓盛行率的影響（PR=0.64）顯著大於已投保者（PR=0.90）。這表明志願服務可作為一種非藥物性的社區介入措施，有助於支持中老年人的心血管健康，並可能為未投保者帶來額外的健康益處。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
過去文獻已顯示志願服務與老年人較低的高血壓盛行率相關，但本研究首次將此益處的探討延伸至中老年人（50-64歲），並釐清了其效益是否因健康保險狀況而異，發現志願服務對未投保者的健康增益可能更大，填補了此年齡層與保險狀況差異的知識空白。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Does Volunteering Give an Extra Health Boost? Volunteering and High Blood Pressure Among Insured and Uninsured U.S. Adults in Late Midlife.
**Abstract:** High blood pressure (HBP) is a major predictor of cardiovascular disease, a leading cause of death in the U.S. Growing evidence suggests that formal volunteering in later life is associated with a lower prevalence of HBP; however, it remains unclear whether these benefits extend to adults in late midlife and whether they differ by health insurance status. This prospective cohort study analyzed data from the 2012-2018 Health and Retirement Study. The analytic sample included 4,169 respondents between the ages of 50 and 64. Because blood pressure was measured in half the sample in alternating waves, data were pooled across waves. To address selection into volunteering, energy-balance weights were created using participants' pre-baseline (t₀; 2012/2014 subsamples) sociodemographic and health-related characteristics to balance the distribution of these characteristics between volunteers and non-volunteers at baseline (t₁; 2014/2016 subsamples). Weighted Poisson regression models with robust error variance estimated the population-average treatment effects of volunteering on HBP at post-baseline (t₂; 2016/2018 subsamples). Differences in effect estimates between insured and uninsured groups were assessed using seemingly unrelated estimation. Volunteering was associated with a lower prevalence of HBP in the full sample (prevalence ratio (PR)=0.86, p=0.005). In subgroup analyses, compared with non-volunteers, volunteers were less likely to have HBP in both the uninsured (PR=0.64, p=0.005) and insured (PR=0.90, p=0.044) groups. The estimated effects differed significantly across groups (F[1, 4168]=4.05, p=0.044), indicating that volunteering may have a greater influence on reducing HBP prevalence among uninsured individuals than among insured counterparts. Volunteering may serve as a non-pharmacological, community-based intervention to support cardiovascular health in late midlife. While not a substitute for health insurance, findings suggest that uninsured individuals may derive additional health gains from volunteering.
