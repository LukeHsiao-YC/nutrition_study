### 重點摘要
本研究旨在系統性評估兒茶素補充對人體肌肉疲勞和身體表現的影響，儘管兒茶素的抗氧化特性已廣為人知，但其對這些方面的具體效益仍不明確。透過對七項隨機對照試驗進行系統性回顧與薈萃分析，結果發現在特定介入條件下，兒茶素補充可能改善有氧表現並降低自覺用力程度，但這些發現因研究與結果而異，且整體上並未觀察到有氧、無氧表現、功能性運動能力或生理反應有顯著改善。薈萃分析顯示兒茶素補充對表現相關結果沒有顯著的總體效益。因此，目前證據有限，不認為兒茶素補充能有效提升肌肉疲勞恢復與身體表現，不支持其具有明確的增效作用。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究透過系統性回顧與薈萃分析，填補了兒茶素補充對肌肉疲勞和身體表現影響的綜合性評估空白。然而，目前僅有少量隨機對照試驗（七項）符合納入標準，顯示該領域的證據基礎仍屬新興且有限，未來的研究需更深入探索其在特定條件下的潛在作用，或進一步釐清為何總體效益不顯著。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Effect of Catechin Supplementation on Muscle Fatigue and Physical Performance: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials.
**Abstract:** The antioxidant properties of catechins are well established; however, whether these biological effects translate into meaningful improvements in muscle fatigue and physical performance remains uncertain. To systematically evaluate the effects of acute and chronic catechin supplementation on muscle fatigue and physical performance in humans. A literature search was conducted in the MEDLINE/PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials databases to identify randomized controlled trials (RCTs) examining the effects of catechin supplementation on muscle fatigue and physical performance outcomes. The search strategy was defined according to the PICOS framework (ie, participants, intervention, comparison, outcomes, and study design) and conducted in September 2025. Risk of bias was independently assessed using the JBI critical appraisal tool. Of the 771 records identified, 7 RCTs met the inclusion criteria, reflecting the still emerging evidence base available in this field. Catechin supplementation was associated with improvements in aerobic performance and reductions in perceived exertion in selected intervention settings; however, these findings varied across studies and outcomes. Additionally, no significant improvements were observed for aerobic and anaerobic performance, functional exercise capacity, or physiological responses to exercise. Meta-analytic synthesis showed no significant overall effects of catechin supplementation on performance-related outcomes. Catechin supplementation does not appear to enhance muscle fatigue and physical performance. Although potential effects on aerobic performance and perceived exertion were observed under specific intervention conditions, the current evidence remains limited and does not support a clear ergogenic effect. PROSPERO registration no. CRD42024605740.
