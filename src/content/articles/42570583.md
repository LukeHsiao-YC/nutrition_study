### 重點摘要
這項研究探討將動物個體化（如給予名稱和偏好）如何影響兒童對肉類的消費意願。兩項研究以5-6歲波蘭學齡前兒童為對象，透過玩具三明治建構任務，評估個體化識別對食用特定動物、同物種肉類（雞肉）及不同物種肉類（豬肉）偏好的影響。結果顯示，獨特的個體化識別能有條件地降低兒童對被識別動物及其同物種肉類的消費意願和實際偏好，但此效果僅限於原本不特別喜歡肉類的兒童，且未推廣至其他物種的肉類。總體而言，動物個體化可降低兒童的食肉意圖，但其影響受兒童既有飲食偏好調節，且跨物種推廣能力有限。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究指出動物個體化對兒童食肉意圖的影響受到兒童既有飲食偏好調節，且未能普遍推廣至不同物種的肉類。這意味著單純的個體化策略在改變所有兒童的肉類偏好方面存在局限性，並指出未來研究可探討更廣泛、更具普遍性的介入策略，或針對不同初始肉類偏好兒童的特定策略。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Naming the one to save the many: The effect of animal identifiability on children's reduced meat preference.
**Abstract:** This research examines how identifying an animal as an individual influences children's willingness to consume that animal, as well as meat derived from conspecifics and different species. Across two studies involving Polish preschoolers aged 5-6 (Study 1: N = 94; Study 2: N = 105), we examined whether presenting a chicken with a name and distinct preferences would reduce children's willingness to consume that specific animal, as well as their preference for chicken meat (poultry) and meat from a different species (pork) during a toy sandwich-building task. Study 2 further compared two forms of identification: unique exemplar identification versus similar exemplar identification (highlighting traits shared with the species). Results consistently revealed that identifying a unique exemplar had a conditional effect on willingness to consume the identified animal and its conspecifics, but did not extend to meat from other species. Animal's identifiability reduced both children's intentions to consume the identified animal (Study 1) and their actual preference for chicken slices in the sandwich-building task (Studies 1 and 2). However, these effects emerged among children who did not report a strong liking for meat. Overall, the findings suggest that although animal identifiability can reduce children's intentions to consume meat, its impact is moderated by preexisting food preferences and shows limited generalization across species.
