### 重點摘要
一碳代謝（1CM）在致癌過程中扮演關鍵角色，但其營養與遺傳因子對乳癌風險的影響尚不明確。本研究在EPIC世代中進行巢式病例對照研究，分析3067例乳癌病例與相符對照組，評估1CM相關營養攝取、B群維生素營養分數及組織特異性1CM基因多基因風險分數與乳癌風險的關聯。結果發現，較高的B群維生素攝取與降低乳癌風險相關，且脂肪組織中1CM基因下調對乳癌風險的影響可能因停經狀態而異。這些發現支持了一碳代謝在乳癌發生中的生物學合理性，並強調了足夠B群維生素攝取的重要性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了先前關於一碳代謝相關營養素與遺傳因子對乳癌風險影響不明確的研究空白。儘管研究支持了B群維生素的重要性，但仍需進一步探索其作用機制、最佳攝取量以及不同組織中1CM基因多基因風險分數的精確影響，特別是其與停經狀態的交互作用。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** One-carbon metabolism nutrients and polygenic risk scores in relation to breast cancer risk: A nested case-control study within the EPIC cohort.
**Abstract:** One-carbon metabolism (1CM) regulates critical processes to carcinogenesis. However, the influence of 1CM-related nutritional and genetic factors on breast cancer (BC) risk remains unclear. To assess the impact of 1CM-related nutrients and genetic variants with BC risk. Case-control study nested within the European Prospective Investigation into Cancer and Nutrition (EPIC) cohort, including 3067 incident BC cases and 3067 matched controls from seven European countries. Individual 1CM-nutrient intakes and nutritional scores for B-vitamins and all 1CM-related nutrients were evaluated. Dietary intakes were collected via country- and center-specific questionnaires. Tissue-specific polygenic risk scores (PRS) for lower expression of 1CM genes were developed using expression-quantitative-trait-loci (eQTLs) from BC-relevant tissues (mammary, adipose, hepatic, adrenal, ovarian and uterine). Odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) were estimated using conditional logistic regression, adjusting for established risk factors. Interactions between nutritional scores and PRS, and between B-vitamins score and alcohol intake, were evaluated. Per one standard deviation (OR Higher intake of B-vitamins was associated with reduced BC risk. Adipose tissue-specific downregulation of 1CM genes may differentially influence BC risk by menopausal status. These findings support the biological plausibility of 1CM in breast carcinogenesis and highlight the potential importance of adequate B-vitamin intake.
