### 重點摘要
極端天氣對公共衛生造成重大負擔，因此預防相關健康影響的研究至關重要。本研究分析了美國國家衛生研究院 (NIH) 在 2015-2024 財政年度資助的極端天氣與預防研究組合，以識別研究缺口與機會。在 243 個極端天氣危害相關專案中，78% 屬於預防研究，主要集中在熱浪和風暴/洪水/降水，但多為觀察性研究。研究結論指出，儘管 NIH 投入大量預防研究，未來仍需加強縱向與介入性研究，並關注高度脆弱族群。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究發現，部分高度脆弱族群，例如無家可歸者，在資助的研究組合中缺乏代表性。此外，現有研究組合中多為觀察性研究，未來需要增加對縱向和介入性研究的投入，以有效減輕極端天氣的健康影響，並特別關注高風險族群。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Prevention Research on Extreme Weather and Health: A Portfolio Analysis of NIH Funded Studies, 2015-2024.
**Abstract:** The public health burden from extreme weather is significant and research to prevent related adverse health impacts is needed. This study's objective was to characterize the portfolio of extreme weather and prevention research funded by NIH for fiscal years (FY) 2015-2024 and identify research gaps and opportunities. Within a broad portfolio of NIH-funded extreme weather-related grants, a coding schema was developed to identify prevention projects focused on six extreme weather hazards. A prevention research taxonomy was applied to categorize projects according to several study variables. A total of 243 projects focused on specific extreme weather hazards. Among these, 78% (n=189) were considered prevention research, primarily consisting of primary prevention. Of these, the largest proportion of projects focused on heat (39.4%), followed by hurricanes/floods/precipitation (31.7%). Projects assessed a range of health outcomes, and approximately 30% of the projects within the portfolio included a focus on one of the ten leading causes of death in the United States. The majority of projects were observational studies, compared to interventional. Children, the elderly, or low-income populations were each represented in about 20-25% of funded projects. However, some vulnerable populations, such as populations experiencing homelessness, were not represented in the portfolio. A large portion of the research funded by the NIH in FY 2015-2024 addressing extreme weather and health focused on prevention. An increased focus on longitudinal research, as well as interventional research to mitigate the health impacts from extreme weather, with attention to highly vulnerable populations, is needed.
