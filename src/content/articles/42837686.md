### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討極低出生體重早產兒出生第一週的蛋白質與脂質攝取，與學齡期語文理解能力及特定腦區體積之間的長期關聯。這項前瞻性世代研究追蹤了157名極低出生體重早產兒，並在學齡期進行神經心理評估和腦部MRI。研究發現，早期較高的脂質攝取與學齡期較佳的語文理解分數和較大的左緣上迴體積相關；蛋白質攝取則與多個語言處理相關的額葉和顳葉區域體積有關。儘管觀察到這些關聯，但由於其為觀察性研究設計，且在調整新生兒疾病嚴重程度後，脂質攝取與語文理解的關聯性有所減弱，因此研究結果應視為關聯而非因果證據。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了極低出生體重早產兒早期營養暴露（特別是脂質攝取）與特定認知領域（如語文理解）及特定腦部區域（如緣上迴）長期關聯的知識空白。然而，其觀察性設計限制了建立因果關係，且新生兒疾病嚴重程度可能混淆了營養與認知的關聯性，未來需要更嚴謹的干預性研究來進一步驗證。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Early neonatal lipid intake is associated with verbal comprehension and supramarginal gyrus volume at school age in very low birth weight preterm infants: A longitudinal cohort study.
**Abstract:** Early nutritional exposure may influence brain development in very low birth weight (VLBW) preterm infants, although its long-term relationship with specific cognitive domains remains unclear. The objective was to investigate the association between protein and lipid intake during the first week of life and verbal comprehension and brain regional volumes at school age. This longitudinal cohort study included 157 VLBW infants (<1500 g, <32 weeks gestation). Nutritional intake during the first week of life was prospectively recorded. At school age (6-16 years), 157 children underwent neuropsychological assessment and 115 underwent brain MRI. Regional brain volumes were obtained using FreeSurfer. Multivariable regression models were adjusted for gestational age, intracranial volume, age at assessment, and parental socioeconomic status. Higher lipid intake during the first week of life was associated with better verbal comprehension scores and with larger left supramarginal gyrus volume. Protein intake was not associated with verbal comprehension but was associated with the volume of several frontal and temporal regions involved in language processing. Children with lower verbal comprehension exhibited smaller volumes in multiple regions, although after correction for multiple comparisons only the left rostral middle frontal gyrus, precentral gyrus, and temporal pole remained significant. Sensitivity analyses including CRIB II score attenuated the association between lipid intake and verbal comprehension, suggesting a possible influence of neonatal illness severity. Higher lipid intake during the first week of life was associated with better verbal comprehension and larger supramarginal gyrus volume at school age in VLBW preterm infants, whereas protein intake was associated with the volume of several language-related cortical regions. Given the observational design and the attenuation of the association with verbal comprehension after adjustment for CRIB II score, these findings should be interpreted as associations rather than evidence of causality.
