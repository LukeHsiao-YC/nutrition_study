### 重點摘要
這項國際比較研究旨在探討2002至2018年間中風事件發生率和30天病例致死率如何影響中風死亡率的下降趨勢。研究分析了澳洲、英國、紐西蘭和加拿大部分地區的數據，結果顯示中風死亡率的下降主要歸因於30天病例致死率的顯著改善，其貢獻比例達55.3%至80.9%。尤其在65歲以下族群中，死亡率的下降幾乎完全由30天病例致死率的降低所驅動，儘管此年齡段的事件發生率持平甚至增加。研究結論強調改善病例致死率對降低中風死亡率的重要性，並指出年輕族群中風事件率的不利趨勢可能預示未來死亡率的潛在上升，因此應加強針對性預防。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了過去關於中風死亡率下降的當代驅動因素數據有限的研究空白，明確指出30天病例致死率的改善是主要貢獻者。然而，研究也指出65歲以下年輕族群中風事件發生率的不利趨勢，可能是未來中風死亡率潛在上升的警訊，這為未來的公共衛生策略和研究方向提供了限制或新挑戰。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The impact of temporal trends in stroke event rates and 30-day case fatality on trends in stroke mortality: an international comparative study.
**Abstract:** Stroke mortality rates have declined in recent decades; however, data on contemporary drivers of this trend are limited. This study compared the contribution of trends in stroke event rates and 30-day case fatality to declines in stroke mortality from 2002 to 2018. Linked hospitalization and mortality data were obtained from Western Australia (WA), England, New Zealand, British Columbia (BC), and Ontario. Age-standardized and age-specific stroke mortality and event rates and 30-day case fatality were calculated and age-adjusted trends estimated from Poisson and binomial regression models. The relative contribution of trends in stroke events and 30-day case fatality to trends in stroke mortality were calculated. There were 2 248 242 stroke events identified. Age-adjusted reductions in event rates ranged from -0.96%/year [95% confidence interval (CI) - 1.22, 0.70%] in men in WA to -2.51%/year (95% CI -2.61, -2.40%) in women in Ontario. Age-standardized 30-day case fatality was highest in England in 2002 (men 43.7%, women 45.5%), but declined at a greater rate than in other jurisdictions. Declines in stroke mortality ranged from -3.3%/year to -5.5%/year. Reductions in 30-day case fatality were the major contributor to declines in stroke mortality [range: men, 63.5% (Ontario)-80.9% (England); women, 55.3% (BC)-73.1% (England)]. In people aged <65 years, stroke mortality declines were almost completely driven by reductions in 30-day case fatality with increasing or attenuated event rates among those aged 35-64 years. Improvements in 30-day case fatality had a greater impact on stroke mortality than trends in event rates, suggesting that further gains could be achieved with targeted prevention. Adverse trends in event rates in people aged <65 years could signal future increases in stroke mortality.
