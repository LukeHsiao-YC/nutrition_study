### 重點摘要
本範疇審查旨在繪製針對5至25歲腦或脊髓損傷/疾病年輕族群，能提升健康素養的行動應用程式與互動式電子學習資源的現有證據。研究系統性搜尋了科學文獻、灰色文獻及應用程式商店，結果僅識別出8篇相關文獻及30個符合條件的應用程式。這些應用程式主要提供教育、症狀追蹤與自我管理功能，然而其功效的科學證據稀缺，且隱私資訊常難以理解。總結而言，此領域的數位工具證據有限，未來急需更嚴謹且以使用者為中心的有效與安全工具評估。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了針對腦或脊髓損傷/疾病年輕族群，提升健康素養的行動應用程式與電子學習資源的現有證據與工具概況的空白。然而，研究同時揭示了這些數位工具的功效缺乏足夠的科學證據，且隱私資訊通常難以理解，這也指明了未來研究需要針對安全有效的數位工具進行更嚴謹且以使用者為中心的評估。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Mobile Apps to Enhance Health Literacy in Children and Adolescents With Injury or Disease to the Brain or Spinal Cord: Scoping Review.
**Abstract:** Young individuals who are affected by an injury or disease to the brain or spinal cord must manage diverse functional and cognitive challenges. Digital tools can increase the users' understanding and management of their daily lives through increased health literacy. However, to realize this potential, we need to know which tools are available, whether they are tailored for end users, and whether they are developed and evaluated with scientific evidence. This study aimed to map evidence from published literature assessing digital tools in the form of mobile apps and interactive e-learning resources developed to increase health literacy for a target population of young individuals aged 5 to 25 years with injury or disease to the brain or spinal cord. Furthermore, it aimed to identify available apps and e-learning resources, and describe their features, intended use, and privacy issues. Following the Joanna Briggs Institute framework for scoping reviews, we conducted a search across 10 scientific databases. For gray literature, we searched Google, Google Scholar, and 15 selected websites of organizations affiliated with our target population. To identify apps, we used Fnd.io for the App Store and Google Play. Sources had to address our target population, involve digital tools with active engagement of the end user, and be published or updated within the last 6 years. Of 821 identified scientific records from databases, 2 met our eligibility criteria. The gray literature search resulted in 6 more eligible publications, giving a final number of 8 included papers: 3 randomized controlled trials, 3 reviews, and 2 theses. Across intervention studies, sample sizes ranged from 7 to 150 participants; intervention durations ranged from 2 weeks to 9 months. App searches identified 114 apps, of which 84 were excluded due to the following reasons: duplicates (n=10), wrong outcome (n=42), wrong population (n=26), no update within 6 years (n=4), and no longer available (n=2). Of the 30 included apps, 14 targeted brain conditions, 10 spinal cord conditions, and 6 both. A total of 16 apps reported affiliation with an organization, 12 explicitly required health professional involvement, 23 were designed primarily for home use, 23 were free, 18 were available on both iOS and Android, and 20 had been updated within the last 2 years. All apps linked to a privacy policy, 8 stated third-party authorizations, and 1 app reported full electronic health record integration. Evidence on digital tools to improve health literacy in young people with brain or spinal cord conditions is limited. Only 8 publications and 30 apps met eligibility criteria. Available apps emphasize education, symptom tracking, and self-management, but evidence for efficacy is scarce, and privacy information is often difficult to interpret. More rigorous, user-centered evaluation of safe and effective digital tools is needed.
