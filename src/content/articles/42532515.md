### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討VTRNA2-1表觀等位基因的甲基化狀態是否能預測減重手術後的長期超重減輕率，並解析其與脂肪組織轉錄特徵的關聯。研究分析了179名接受減重手術的患者，根據其術前外周血DNA的VTRNA2-1甲基化狀態進行分類，並追蹤術後24個月的超重減輕率。結果顯示，VTRNA2-1高度甲基化的患者在術後12和24個月均表現出顯著更高的超重減輕率。這項研究表明VTRNA2-1甲基化狀態與減重手術效果可預測的變異相關，為術前風險分層和個性化治療策略提供了潛在的可能性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
該研究填補了在精準醫學領域中，尋找減重手術療效預測性生物標誌物的空白，特別是將早期生命表觀遺傳印記與成年代謝可塑性及手術結果聯繫起來。未來的研究應進一步驗證脂肪組織中IL13和PRDM16 mRNA與VTRNA2-1低甲基化之間探索性的關聯。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Epigenetic Signature of the Metastable Epiallele VTRNA2-1: Adipose Dysfunction and Suboptimal Bariatric Outcomes.
**Abstract:** VTRNA2-1 is a metastable epiallele whose methylation is associated with metabolic homeostasis via PKR-mediated inflammation and adipocyte function. We aimed to determine whether preoperative VTRNA2-1 promoter methylation in peripheral blood predicts long-term excess weight loss (%EWL) after bariatric surgery. We explored transcriptional signatures in adipose tissue to clarify the relationship between early-life epigenetic imprinting on adult metabolic plasticity and surgical outcomes in patients with severe obesity. In the OCEAN prospective registry (2011-2024), 179 adults underwent bariatric surgery. Peripheral blood DNA VTRNA2-1 methylation was classified as hypo-, intermediate, or hyper-methylated. %EWL was recorded at 3, 6, 12, and 24 months. Adipose tissue was assayed for IL13, PRDM16, and related transcripts. VTRNA2-1 methylation showed a bimodal distribution, differing significantly from controls (p = 0.002). Hypermethylated patients had lower VTRNA2-1 expression (p < 0.001) and greater %EWL at 12 (67.8% ± 19.1% vs. 61.8% ± 17.5%; p = 0.047) and 24 months (68.7% ± 21.9% vs. 60.4% ± 19.3%; p = 0.040). The hypomethylated group maintained a stable weight loss plateau between 12 and 24 months. Hypomethylation was associated with nominally higher IL13 and PRDM16 mRNA in adipose tissue (exploratory; uncorrected p < 0.05). VTRNA2-1 methylation status is associated with predictable variations and offers potential for preoperative risk stratification and personalized therapeutic strategies in managing severe obesity.
