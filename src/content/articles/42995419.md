### 重點摘要
這項研究評估了2022年全球菸草吸菸導致的癌症負擔，並首次詳細分析了吸菸狀態（目前吸菸者與過去吸菸者）、性別、國家及人類發展指數 (HDI) 的差異。研究方法是透過計算人口歸因分數 (PAF) 來估算全球175個國家的癌症發病率與死亡率。結果顯示，全球約有六分之一的癌症病例和四分之一的癌症死亡歸因於菸草吸菸，其中男性和高HDI國家承受較大負擔。研究強調，即使戒菸多年，過去吸菸者仍是癌症負擔的重要來源。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究透過詳細區分吸菸狀態（目前吸菸者與過去吸菸者）、性別和人類發展指數 (HDI) 等因素，提供了全球癌症歸因於菸草的精確且差異化的負擔評估，填補了對吸菸相關癌症負擔長期影響及不同群體間差異的深入理解。它特別突顯了過去吸菸者即使戒菸後仍長期承擔的顯著癌症負擔。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Global cancer burden attributable to tobacco smoking in 2022: a differential analysis by smoking status, sex, and development level.
**Abstract:** Tobacco smoking is a leading preventable cause of cancer. We estimated its attributable cancer burden by smoking status (current vs. former), further stratified by cancer type, sex, country, and Human Development Index (HDI). The Population Attributable Fraction (PAF) for cancer incidence and mortality due to tobacco were estimated across 175 countries by using 2000 Global Burden of Disease smoking prevalence and 2022 Global Cancer Observatory cancer data. Relative risks (RRs) were derived from large pooled and meta-analyses, and population data from the World Bank. Countries were categorized by HDI. The global tobacco PAF was 17.2% for cancer incidence and 22.4% for mortality. Lung cancer had the highest RR for current smokers (8.96) and laryngeal cancer for former smokers (4.65). Males had a higher cancer incidence (26.1%) and mortality (32.1%) burden than females (8.0% and 10.2%, respectively). Cancers with the highest smoking PAF were laryngeal (70.1%), lung (66.9%), pharyngeal (63.5%), oral cavity (38.5%), and bladder (39.4%). PAF estimates for incidence were 5.4% in low-HDI (49 countries), 15.0% in medium-HDI [41], 19.3% in high-HDI [32], and 17.4% in very-high-HDI [32] countries; corresponding mortality estimates were 6.4% (low-HDI), 17.4% (medium-HDI), 26.0% (high-HDI), and 23.6% (very-high-HDI), respectively. Approximately one in six cancer cases and one in four cancer deaths globally are attributable to tobacco smoking. Despite declining smoking prevalence, former smokers account for a substantial burden long after cessation. Males and higher-HDI countries bear a greater burden.
