### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討澳洲新手媽媽如何理解嬰兒餵養應用程式 (IFAs) 的資料隱私風險及相關條款與條件。研究方法為對 24 位符合條件的媽媽進行兩輪訪談及焦點團體討論，並使用反思性主題分析。結果顯示，媽媽們普遍缺乏對應用程式條款的認知，儘管意識到隱私風險，仍因其感知到的實用性而持續使用，並採取風險緩解行為。文章強調了在數位育兒時代保護個人及兒童資料隱私的重要性，呼籲在應用程式設計中融入隱私保護原則，並提高政策透明度。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了關於嬰兒餵養應用程式使用者如何理解資料隱私風險，以及他們如何在隱私疑慮與應用程式實用性之間取得平衡的知識空白。它特別探討了澳洲新手媽媽對應用程式條款和條件的認知，並揭示了她們在數位育兒時代所面臨的隱私挑戰。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Infant Feeding Apps and Digital Parenting in Australia: Qualitative Study.
**Abstract:** Parents are increasingly using infant feeding apps (IFAs) for information and decision-making related to breastfeeding and other aspects of their baby's care. Few studies have explored how users conceptualize data privacy risks in relation to IFAs and how concerns are balanced with app use. This qualitative study forms part of the Infant Feeding App project exploring how IFAs influence parent-child communication in Australian families. A focus of this investigation was to explore mothers' understanding of the terms and conditions associated with the use of IFAs and their data privacy concerns. We included first-time parents of children aged 0 to 4 years who were breastfeeding at the time of the first interview and previously or currently used 1 or more IFAs. Twenty-four participants completed the first-round interview; of those, 22 returned for a second-round interview approximately 6 months later, and 7 participated in the focus group discussion. Reflexive thematic analysis was used to analyze the data. This paper draws primarily on findings from the key category of "Data and/or Privacy Practices." This category was further refined into 2 main themes: lack of awareness or acceptance of the terms and conditions associated with the mobile app and the risk mitigation behaviors mothers took to protect their privacy. Although mothers were aware of the privacy risks associated with IFAs, they nonetheless continued to engage with them, reflecting the broader growth in digital technology use. This paper has highlighted that privacy, a fundamental human right, is at risk when mothers share personal and child-related data through digital apps. Importantly, mothers' willingness to use these applications despite perceived concerns highlights the tension between perceived utility and potential risks. A call for a move toward privacy by design, transparency in privacy policies, and an approach of nonalienation in the use of data is called for.
