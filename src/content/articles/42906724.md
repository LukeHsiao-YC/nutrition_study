### 重點摘要
這篇系統性回顧探討了混合肉產品（結合動物與植物成分）的消費者研究現狀，指出儘管商業和政策關注度高，但相關研究仍零碎且概念發展不足。研究採用 TCCM 框架和 SPAR-4-SLR 協議，分析了 36 項消費者研究，發現其多數為量化、非理論性方法，且地理分佈集中於美國和西歐。結果顯示研究主要關注產品屬性與心理因素，鮮少探討中介或調節機制，且多集中於漢堡類產品與蘑菇成分。本回顧旨在填補理論與方法學上的空白，並為未來產品開發、溝通策略及政策提供實證基礎，以提升消費者對混合肉產品的接受度。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本系統性回顧填補了混合肉消費研究在概念上零碎、理論發展不足的系統性綜合分析空白。它指出未來研究應拓展地理範圍以探討文化差異，加強理論性探討，並關注中介/調節機制，以及更多元的產品形式與成分組合，以克服現有研究在方法、理論和地理上的局限性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The meat and plant protein blend alternative: A systematic review of factors influencing hybrid meat consumption.
**Abstract:** Because hybrid meat products blend characteristics of both meat and plant-based foods, they may represent an ambiguous or intermediate category that does not fit neatly within existing food classifications. Often referred to within industry as balanced proteins, these products combine animal-derived ingredients with alternative, non-animal ingredients or protein sources. Despite growing commercial and policy interest, consumer research on hybrid meat remains fragmented and conceptually underdeveloped. Using the theories, theoretical constructs, context, and methodologies (TCCM) framework, this systematic review examines how hybrid meat consumption has been conceptualised, empirically studied, and contextualised in the literature, with the aim of identifying theoretical and methodological gaps that can guide future consumer research. Following the SPAR-4-SLR protocol, 36 consumer-focused studies were identified and reviewed. The findings reveal a strong reliance on quantitative, predominantly atheoretical approaches. Empirical evidence is geographically concentrated in the United States and Western Europe, restricting understanding of how consumer responses to hybrid meat may vary across cultural contexts. Research attention is largely directed toward product-level attributes, psychological factors, and food-related traits, while mediating and moderating mechanisms are rarely examined. Moreover, the literature is heavily centred on burgers/ patties, most commonly incorporating mushrooms, with minimal investigation of alternative hybrid formats or systematic variation in hybrid composition. By synthesising and critically evaluating existing consumer research, this review outlines a focused future research agenda and practical implications to support evidence-based product development, communication strategies, and policy interventions aimed at increasing consumer acceptance of hybrid meat products.
