### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討飲食中總蛋白質、動物性與植物性蛋白質的攝取量與肌少性肥胖盛行率之間的關聯。研究採用前瞻性設計，分析來自英國 EPIC-Norfolk 世代研究的 14,816 名中老年男性與女性數據。結果顯示，肌少性肥胖患者的總蛋白質、動物性與植物性蛋白質攝取量顯著較低，且蛋白質攝取量越高（無論是總量、動物性或植物性蛋白質），罹患肌少性肥胖的可能性越低。這些發現指出，總蛋白質、動物性與植物性蛋白質的攝取對於預防年齡增長相關的肌少性肥胖和肥胖至關重要，尤其對女性、吸煙者或患有共病症者更為重要。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了關於不同來源（動物性與植物性）的蛋白質攝取量與肌少性肥胖之間關聯的知識空白，並明確指出蛋白質攝取對預防肌少性肥胖和肥胖的重要性。然而，研究結果表明單純肌少症的盛行率似乎不依賴於蛋白質攝取，暗示了未來研究需要更深入探討蛋白質如何影響肌少性肥胖中的「肥胖」成分，以及更明確的因果關係與作用機制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Dietary intake of total protein, animal- and plant-based protein is associated with lower likelihood of having sarcopenic obesity in 14,816 middle- and older aged men and women of the EPIC-Norfolk cohort.
**Abstract:** This study aimed to investigate the association between the amount and source of dietary protein and the prevalence of sarcopenic obesity. A prospective study using data from the European Prospective Investigation of Cancer (EPIC)-Norfolk cohort study. General population-based study conducted in the United Kingdom. 14,816 Community dwelling men and women aged 39 to 79 years. The prevalence of sarcopenic obesity was 8.9 % in men and 11.9 % in women. Median dietary intake of total, animal and plant protein intake, based on fat free mass standardised for BMI to assess sarcopenia, was significantly lower in those with sarcopenic obesity compared to those without sarcopenia and obesity (0.80 vs 1.04, 0.51 vs 0.66, and 0.25 vs 0.35 g/kg body weight/day, respectively, all P < 0.001). Higher intakes of protein, either total, animal or plant-based (in g/kg body weight/day) were associated with a significantly lower likelihood of sarcopenic obesity (OR 0.14 (95% CI:0.08-0.20), 0.58 (0.53-0.62) and 0.46(0.41-0.52), respectively, P<0.001 for all). The prevalence of both sarcopenic obesity and obesity was significantly (P<0.001) higher in those with lowest compared with those in the highest intake quartiles of total- animal- and plant protein, but the prevalence of sarcopenia did not appear to depend on protein intake. Our findings indicate that consumption of total, animal- and plant-based protein is important for both the prevention of sarcopenic obesity and obesity during ageing, especially in women, smokers, or those living with comorbidities such as diabetes and bronchitis.
