### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探索衣索比亞糖尿病患者在自我管理方面的挑戰與未滿足需求。透過訪談21名患者及5名全科醫生，結果發現主要挑戰包括情緒健康、胰島素與血糖計供應、健康飲食、糖尿病教育和社會污名等，同時也識別出對社會意識、客製化治療、更好的醫療服務和科技輔助的需求。研究顯示撒哈拉以南非洲的糖尿病患者面臨多重困境，因此干預措施應根據當地獨特需求進行調整。這些發現強調了加強糖尿病自我管理教育、心理社會支持及基本物資供應的重要性，並為發展因地制宜的mHealth功能提供了依據。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了對撒哈拉以南非洲（特別是衣索比亞）糖尿病患者在自我管理方面所面臨的具體挑戰和未滿足需求的知識空白，強調了該地區獨特的文化和資源背景下的困境，並為制定在地化干預措施提供了重要依據。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Challenges and needs of people living with diabetes in self-management in Ethiopia: A qualitative exploration.
**Abstract:** Diabetes is unique due to its cross-cutting nature, impacting multiple organ systems and increasing the risk for other communicable and non-communicable diseases if not managed properly. Managing diabetes involves frequent blood glucose measurement, medication management, healthy eating, emotional stability, and staying active. This study aimed to explore the challenges and unmet needs in diabetes self-management among adults living with diabetes in Ethiopia. We conducted one-to-one semi-structured interviews with 21 adults living with diabetes and 5 general practitioner physicians in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. Interviews were transcribed, translated into English, and analyzed using reflexive thematic analysis with NVIVO. Eleven and eight general themes of challenges and needs were identified, respectively. The outstanding challenges were related to emotional well-being, insulin management and supply, access to glucose meter, healthy eating and food insecurity, diabetes education and self-care skills, social stigma and misconceptions, and customized treatment and communication. Needs for awareness creation among the society, customized treatment and involvement in personal treatment plan, better healthcare service, and emotional support system are the identified non-technological needs. Moreover, needs for glucose measuring device, better insulin administration system, complete guiding tool, and mHealth system to aid in diabetes monitoring are the technological needs identified. People living with diabetes in Sub-Saharan Africa experience multi-faceted challenges and have many unmet needs. Interventions should be adapted to the region's unique requirements. Findings highlight priorities for strengthening diabetes self-management education, psychosocial support, and access to essential supplies, while informing context-adapted mHealth functions to complement core service and supply needs.
