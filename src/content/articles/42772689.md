### 重點摘要
高肉類消費引發健康與環境疑慮，促使混合肉產品被視為減少肉類攝取的可行策略。本研究為一範圍回顧，系統性地繪製並綜合了消費者對混合肉產品心理建構與結果的現有研究，納入截至2025年12月的79篇論文。結果顯示消費者對混合肉產品普遍接受度、喜好度與偏好度高，感官吸引力是主要預測因素，且環境與健康訊息能顯著提升其採用。這些發現有助於理解混合肉在永續飲食轉型中的作用，並指出溝通策略和長期行為適應是未來研究方向。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了消費者對混合肉產品心理建構與相關結果的系統性知識空白，突顯了混合肉產品在減少肉類消費中的潛力。然而，未來的研究需進一步深入探討有效的溝通策略以及長期行為適應對於混合肉產品採用的影響。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Consumers' Psychological Constructs regarding Hybrid Meat Products: a Scoping Review.
**Abstract:** Growing concerns about the health, social, and environmental impacts of high meat consumption have driven interest in plant-based diets and effective policies to reduce meat intake. Hybrid meat products, combining animal-derived ingredients and alternative protein sources, represent a promising strategy toward reduced meat consumption. This scoping review set out to systematically map and synthesise existing research on consumers' psychological constructs and outcomes related to hybrid meat products. Using the Population, Concept, and Context (PCC) framework, this review focused on consumers of any demographic exposed to hybrid meat in various contexts, including retail, restaurant, and experimental settings. Seventy-nine peer-reviewed publications published up to December 2025 were included in the review after meeting the predefined inclusion criteria. Data extraction used a custom tool, and analysis involved frequency counts and descriptive summaries of key characteristics and themes. Across studies, hybrid meat products generally showed high consumer acceptance, liking, and preference, often exceeding expectations. Sensory appeal emerged as a strong predictor of liking and acceptance, and a wide range of ingredients and non-meat ratios (1% to 80%) were generally well accepted. Information provision could influence hybrid meat adoption, with environmental and health messages showing the greatest positive impact. "These findings contribute to understanding how hybrid meat products may fit within sustainable dietary transitions, while highlighting communication strategy and long-term behavioural adaptation as areas requiring further research."
