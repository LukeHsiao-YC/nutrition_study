### 重點摘要
這項全國性登錄研究旨在釐清兒童慢性C型肝炎（CHC）患者在實現持續病毒學應答（SVR）後的肝臟長期結果及適當追蹤策略。研究分析了接受干擾素或直接抗病毒藥物治療的兒童，並長期追蹤肝酶、纖維化指標（如APRI）及肝臟相關併發症的變化。結果顯示，達成SVR的兒童患者能長期維持病毒清除，且未發現肝硬化或肝細胞癌的發展，肝功能與纖維化指標均顯著改善並穩定。鑑於APRI在SVR後三年內趨於穩定，研究建議大多數兒童在SVR後至少應追蹤三年。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了兒童慢性C型肝炎患者SVR後監測策略不明確的空白，提供了兒童長期肝臟結果良好的實證並建議了具體的追蹤時長。然而，研究中直接抗病毒藥物（DAA）治療組的患者數量和追蹤時間相對較短，且部分干擾素治療患者的長期數據隨時間減少，可能限制了對所有治療類型及更長期結果的全面評估。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Absence of progressive liver disease after treatment in children with chronic hepatitis C: A nationwide registry study.
**Abstract:** In adults with chronic hepatitis C (CHC) and advanced fibrosis, hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) surveillance is recommended after achieving a sustained virologic response (SVR). However, post-SVR surveillance strategies for children remain unclear. The aim of this study was to assess the durability of SVR and determine an appropriate follow-up duration by evaluating longitudinal changes in liver enzymes, fibrosis markers, and liver-related complications in children with CHC. Using a nationwide annual Japanese registry, we analyzed children with CHC treated with interferon (IFN)-based therapies or direct-acting antivirals (DAAs). Serum aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) levels, platelet counts, AST-to-platelet ratio index (APRI), and liver-related complications were evaluated. Among the 168 IFN-treated patients assessed 1 year after treatment, 140 (83%) achieved SVR. Although the number of evaluable patients decreased after 5 years of follow-up, all patients who were followed for 5-15 years maintained SVR, and none developed liver cirrhosis or HCC. Compared with pretreatment values, the IFN-SVR group showed significant reductions in serum aminotransferase levels at 1-5 years after treatment. APRI decreased significantly during the first 3 years after SVR and remained stable thereafter. These improvements were consistently observed among IFN-treated patients with mild-to-moderate fibrosis. All 43 evaluable DAA-treated patients achieved SVR and maintained SVR for up to 5 years. Children with CHC who achieve SVR demonstrate long-term viral eradication and favorable liver outcomes. The stabilization of APRI within 3 years after SVR suggests that follow-up for at least 3 years after SVR may be warranted in most children.
