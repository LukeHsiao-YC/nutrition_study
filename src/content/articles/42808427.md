### 重點摘要
這項MedEx-UK隨機對照試驗旨在解決飲食與身體活動對腸道微生物組影響研究結果不一致，以及兩者結合效應探索不足的問題。研究招募了93名參與者，分為習慣生活、地中海飲食，以及地中海飲食加運動三組，進行為期24週的介入。研究透過16S rRNA基因測序和糞便代謝物量化，分析介入前後腸道微生物組組成與糞便代謝物的變化。初步結果顯示，兩種介入組別的地中海飲食分數均顯著提升。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了飲食與身體活動對腸道微生物組影響研究結果不一致，以及少有研究探討其結合效應的知識空白。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Impact of a Mediterranean diet and physical activity intervention on the gut microbiome and faecal metabolites: findings from the MedEx-UK randomised controlled trial.
**Abstract:** Diet and physical activity (PA) are potential modulators of the gut microbiome, although research findings are inconsistent, and few studies have explored their combined effects. This study used data from ninety-three participants (forty-seven with pre- and post-intervention samples) from the MedEx-UK randomised controlled trial to investigate the impact of a 24-week Mediterranean diet (MedDiet) intervention, alone or with a PA intervention, on gut microbiome composition and faecal metabolites. Faecal samples were collected pre- and post-intervention from participants in each of three treatment groups: habitual lifestyle (control), MedDiet or MedDiet plus PA. Gut microbiome profiles were generated using 16S rRNA gene sequencing. Faecal SCFA, NMR-derived metabolites and bile acids were quantified. MedDiet scores were significantly increased in both intervention groups
