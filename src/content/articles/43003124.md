### 重點摘要
兒童急救時藥物劑量計算的準確性對病患安全至關重要。本系統性回顧與統合分析旨在評估長度測量帶等工具在體重估算、藥物劑量準確性、給藥時間及臨床結果方面的優劣。研究發現，具體型校正的PAWPER測量帶在體重估算、給藥時間及藥物劑量準確性上均優於Broselow測量帶及所有基於年齡的公式。因此，建議在各族群的兒童急救中使用具體型校正的長度測量帶以提高用藥安全性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
儘管本研究證實了具體型校正測量帶在準確性與給藥時間上的優勢，但目前的文獻普遍缺乏關於直接病患臨床預後（如死亡率、住院時間）的數據。此外，許多原始研究排除了體長超出測量帶、骨骼畸形或生長受限的兒童，這些特殊族群的用藥問題仍是未來研究需關注的限制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Weight Estimation and Dose Calculation in Resuscitation: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.
**Abstract:** The accuracy of instruments to estimate weight and calculate drug doses in children requiring medications during resuscitation is a critical patient safety issue. To determine if length-based tapes were superior to other instruments for drug dose calculation or improved the accuracy of weight estimation, time to drug administration, drug dose accuracy, or clinical outcomes during resuscitation. We systematically reviewed Embase, PubMed, and the Cochrane Library from inception to December 19, 2025. Systematic reviews, randomized controlled trials, nonrandomized studies were included. Country, sample size, study design, and results were extracted. In the 250 included studies, 9 enrolled children requiring resuscitation. Compared with the Broselow tape, the PAWPER tape (a length-based tape with habitus correction) was superior for time to drug administration, drug dose accuracy, and weight estimation accuracy in all analyses. In direct paired meta-comparison for the proportions of estimates within 10% (P10), the PAWPER tape was superior to the Broselow tape (odds ratio, 2.48; 95% CI, 2.04-3.02]). All age-based formulas were consistently inaccurate (P10 < 50%). Findings were consistent across countries classified by income levels. There were no patient outcomes data. Children exceeding the length of the tape and children with skeletal deformities and growth restrictions were excluded from many source studies. There were no studies of patient outcomes for instruments calculating drug dosing during resuscitation. Time to drug administration, drug dose, and weight estimation accuracy were superior with a length-based tape with body habitus correction compared with tapes without habitus correction in all populations.
