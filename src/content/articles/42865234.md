### 重點摘要
該系統性文獻回顧旨在探討人工智慧(AI)在兒童基層醫療及其相關臨床場域中，如何應用於兒童過重和肥胖的管理，以應對當前識別與管理面臨的挑戰。研究團隊透過系統性搜尋五個資料庫，篩選出2007年至2025年期間共20項符合條件的英文研究，這些研究涵蓋5至19歲的過重或肥胖兒童，且應用了至少一種AI方法。結果顯示，大多數研究(80%)以病患家庭為目標，其中機器學習是最常見的AI應用(60%)，主要用於肥胖風險預測或分層，而其他如家庭參與或生成式AI的應用較少。結論指出，AI工具主要支援早期識別過重與肥胖風險，但其工作流程整合、長期參與度及對臨床結果的影響證據仍有限。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
儘管人工智慧在兒童肥胖風險的早期識別方面展現潛力，但本研究指出其在基層醫療工作流程整合、長期參與度及對臨床成效的影響方面仍缺乏足夠證據。未來的研究應優先考量前瞻性、嵌入式工作流程設計、有意義的臨床結果，並需關注公平性、隱私、治理、醫護人員培訓及政策監管等層面。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Artificial Intelligence Applications in Pediatric Primary Care for Weight Management: A Systematic Review.
**Abstract:** Pediatric overweight and obesity (OW/OB) is a major public health concern associated with significant short- and long-term health risks. Primary care is a key setting for early identification and management, yet current approaches face barriers related to implementation, workflow, and family engagement. This systematic review examined how artificial intelligence (AI) has been used in pediatric primary care and closely connected clinical settings to support OW/OB management. We conducted a systematic review registered in PROSPERO (CRD420251103662) and reported according to Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses 2020 guidelines. MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL Ultimate, Compendex, and CENTRAL were searched for English-language studies published from 2007 to July 9, 2025. Eligible studies included children and adolescents ages 5-19 years with OW/OB, were conducted in or linked to primary care, and included at least one AI-based approach. Dual-reviewer screening, structured data extraction, and methodological appraisal using the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool were completed. Of 3854 records identified, 20 studies met inclusion criteria. Most studies (80%) targeted patient families, while 20% included both families and clinicians. Machine learning was the most common AI approach (60%) and was primarily used for obesity risk prediction or stratification. Fewer studies examined family engagement, counseling, documentation review, or generative AI tools. Methodological quality ranged from moderate to high. AI tools in pediatric primary care primarily support early identification of OW/OB risk, but evidence on workflow integration, sustained engagement, and long-term clinical impact remains limited. Future research should prioritize prospective, workflow-embedded studies, meaningful clinical outcomes, equity, privacy, governance, clinician training, and policy oversight.
