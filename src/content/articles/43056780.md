### 重點摘要
重症患者的快速骨骼肌流失與康復延遲、機械通氣時間延長及生活品質下降有關，因此有研究提出增加腸道蛋白質攝取量可能減輕此現象。本研究為一項前瞻性、單中心附屬研究，嵌入在評估標準（1.3 g/kg/day）與高劑量（2.0 g/kg/day）蛋白質補充對功能恢復影響的PRECISe隨機對照試驗中，旨在探討其對機械通氣重症患者股直肌橫截面積（RFCSA）變化的影響。研究共納入141名參與者，結果顯示兩組患者的RFCSA均隨時間下降，平均每日下降0.077 ± 0.065 cm。結論指出，與標準劑量相比，高劑量腸道蛋白質補充並未能有效減輕重症機械通氣患者的骨骼肌流失。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究透過使用超音波測量股直肌橫截面積，直接評估了在機械通氣的重症患者中，高劑量腸道蛋白質補充對骨骼肌流失的具體影響，為此前假設提供實證檢驗，填補了對高蛋白飲食效益在該特定族群中缺乏直接客觀證據的空白。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** High versus standard-protein provision and muscle loss in critically ill patients assessed by muscle ultrasound: A prospective ancillary study of a randomised controlled trial.
**Abstract:** Rapid skeletal muscle loss, a hallmark of critical illness, has been linked to delayed recovery, prolonged mechanical ventilation, and reduced quality of life. It has been suggested that increased enteral protein delivery may attenuate this muscle loss in critically ill patients. In this study, we examined the effect of high versus standard enteral protein provision on changes in M. rectus femoris cross-sectional area (RFCSA) in mechanically ventilated critically ill patients. We performed a prospective, pre-planned, single-centre ancillary study at Maastricht University Medical Centre, embedded within the quadruple-blinded randomised PRECISe trial, which evaluated the effect of standard (1.3 g/kg/day) versus high (2.0 g/kg/day) protein provision on functional recovery in critically ill patients. We included all patients who had baseline (<2 days after randomisation) and at least one follow-up ultrasound measurement of the M. rectus femoris. The primary outcome for this study was the change in RFCSA, measured during ICU stay and up to 30 days after randomisation. A total of 141 participants were included in this analysis (standard-protein group, n=75; high-protein group, n=66). RFCSA decreased over time in both groups, with a mean daily decrease of 0.077 ± 0.065 cm In mechanically ventilated critically ill patients, high enteral protein provision compared with standard enteral protein provision did not attenuate skeletal muscle loss, as assessed by ultrasound-derived RFCSA. The PRECISe trial was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04633421; https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT04633421) on 23 October 2020.
