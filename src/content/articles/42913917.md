### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討嬰兒餵養方式與兒童早期心血管健康風險的關聯。研究分析了澳洲一項前瞻性隊列中606名4歲兒童的數據，發現3個月純母乳餵養及6個月主要母乳餵養的嬰兒在4歲時，心血管健康狀況較為良好。結果表明，這些正向關聯可能部分透過嬰兒快速體重增加來中介。這項研究強調了母乳餵養對兒童早期心血管健康的益處。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
此研究填補了母乳餵養與兒童早期心血管風險之間關聯理解的空白，並揭示了嬰兒快速體重增加的潛在中介角色。未來的研究需要進一步深入探究這些觀察到關聯的具體生理機制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Infant Feeding Practices Are Associated with Cardiovascular Profiles in Early Childhood: Findings from a Prospective Australian Cohort Study.
**Abstract:** Breastfeeding is associated with reduced risk of childhood obesity, but associations with cardiovascular risks in childhood are less well understood. This study investigated associations between infant feeding practices and cardiovascular profiles in early childhood and the potential mediating role of infant rapid weight gain (RWG). This secondary data analysis was conducted using data from the Barwon Infant Study, an ongoing prospective pre-birth cohort in Australia commenced between 2010 and 2017, recruited through an unselected antenatal sampling frame. Children aged 4 years (n=606) who participated in the 4-year cardiovascular risk assessment follow-up. Infant feeding variables, including breastfeeding duration, exclusive breastfeeding at 3 months, and predominant breastfeeding at 6 months, were collected via caregiver questionnaires at 1, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months, and 2 years post-birth. Latent profile analysis, including 4-7 cardiovascular risk markers measured at age 4 years, was used to derive cardiovascular profiles. Multivariable logistic regression with adjustment for gestational age, maternal education, and pre-pregnancy maternal body mass index (BMI), examined the association between infant feeding practices and cardiovascular profiles, and whether these associations were mediated by RWG in infancy. Three cardiovascular profiles of 'Low' (44.1% of children), 'Medium' (46.4%), and 'High' (9.5%) were identified at age 4 years. Infants who were exclusively breastfed at age 3 months and predominantly breastfed at age 6 months were less likely to belong to the 'Medium' versus 'Low' cardiovascular profile (RRR 0.51, 95% CI 0.27 to 0.95, p= 0.034; and RRR 0.43, 95% CI 0.18 to 0.99, p= 0.048, respectively) than those who were not. No significant association was observed for the 'High' versus 'Low' cardiovascular profile or between breastfeeding duration and cardiovascular profiles. Mediation analyses suggested indirect associations between infant feeding practices and cardiovascular profiles involving infant RWG. Exclusive breastfeeding to age 3 months and predominant breastfeeding to age 6 months were linked with a more favorable cardiovascular profile at age 4 years. Mediation analyses suggested that infant RWG may partly account for these associations. Further studies are needed to investigate the mechanisms that may underlie the observed associations between breastfeeding practices and cardiovascular profiles.
