### 重點摘要
家庭食物不安全狀況（HFI）與嬰兒副食品階段更具壓迫性的餵食行為相關。本研究透過線上問卷調查231位6-18個月大嬰兒的父母，探討家庭食物安全狀況與餵食行為、食物採購策略及情緒之間的關聯。結果顯示，食物不安全程度較高的父母更傾向於使用施壓式餵食，並採取負面的食物採購策略（如負債或限制新食物），同時伴隨更多負面情緒。研究強調了支援食物不安全家庭實踐回應式餵食的重要性，以促進嬰兒的健康飲食發展。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究探討了在英國生活成本危機下，家庭食物不安全如何影響嬰兒副食品階段的餵食行為，填補了該特定背景下對此關聯性理解的空白。然而，本研究樣本的人口統計學代表性有限，未來研究需擴大樣本以提升結果的普遍性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Household Food Insecurity Is Associated With More Pressurising Feeding Practices During Complementary Feeding: The Role of Parental Worry and Stress.
**Abstract:** Complementary feeding, introducing infants to solid foods, is an important stage of learning new tastes, textures and eating behaviours. Responsive feeding during complementary feeding is associated with positive weight and eating outcomes. In the UK, austerity measures, post-BREXIT and the COVID-19 pandemic have created a cost-of-living crisis, drawing more families into financial insecurity. Consequently, Household Food Insecurity (a lack of access to sufficient, safe, nutritious food) has increased. Understanding how and why this may be associated with feeding practices and food choices during complementary feeding is important. Overall, 231 parents of infants aged 6-18 months completed an online survey exploring associations between Household Food Security Status (HFSS) and restrictive and pressurising feeding practices, strategies to cope with high food costs, and emotions around complementary feeding. Using the 6-item US HFSS survey model, participants were categorised into four HFSS groups (High, marginal, low and very low). Compared to parents experiencing High Food Security (n = 162), parents were more likely to use pressure to eat if they experienced Low Food Security (n = 36), though this narrowly missed significance. Greater food insecurity was associated with increased negative food purchasing strategies, including getting into debt, limiting new tastes or finger foods, and having to prioritise baby's diet over their own. Pressurising feeding practices were also associated with increased negative emotions around feeding and food purchasing strategies. Although demographic representativeness of the sample was limited, the findings highlight the importance of supporting families experiencing food insecurity with responsive feeding practices.
