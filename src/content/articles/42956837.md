### 重點摘要
這項多中心、縱向研究旨在探討代謝性減重手術 (MBS) 後患者在無飢餓感進食 (EAH)、進食愉悅感及相關飲食習慣方面的長期變化。研究追蹤了1058名患者在術前及術後24個月內的數據，發現術後EAH顯著下降，尤其是在愉悅感驅動的EAH方面，且EAH下降幅度越大，總體重減輕百分比越高。進食愉悅感雖有微小變化但整體趨向正面，如感官享受增加而負面情緒減少。本研究揭示了MBS對術後飲食行為的多面向影響，並強調監測非規範性飲食行為對優化術後結果的重要性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了MBS術後患者在無飢餓感進食、進食愉悅感和特定飲食習慣方面長期變化的理解不足。未來的研究限制可能包含：雖然描述了多方面的變化，但沒有深入探討這些變化的潛在心理或神經生物學機制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Eating Experiences and Behaviors after Metabolic Bariatric Surgery: A Multicenter, Longitudinal Study.
**Abstract:** Metabolic-bariatric surgery (MBS) often results in substantial changes of patients' eating behavior through physiological and behavioral mechanisms. However, its effects on different dimensions of eating, including eating in the absence of hunger (EAH), the subjective experience of eating (eating hedonics), and eating-related practices, remain insufficiently understood. This study examined longitudinal changes in these domains following MBS. N=1058 patients (mean age 46.76, 68.20% female) undergoing Roux-en-Y gastric bypass or sleeve gastrectomy completed clinical interviews and questionnaires assessing EAH, eating hedonics, and eating-related practices pre-MBS, 6-, 12- and 24-months postoperatively. Item-level longitudinal changes were assessed using linear mixed-effects models. Standardized mean differences were calculated to estimate effect sizes. Longitudinal associations with percentage total weight loss (%TWL) were examined using adjusted linear mixed-effects models. Descriptively, EAH decreased post-MBS, with the largest reductions for palatability-driven EAH. Eating hedonics showed very small to small changes, with increases in sensory enjoyment and decreases for negative affect during eating. Greater decreases in EAH were associated with 2.07 percentage-points higher %TWL, while changes in eating hedonics showed small, directionally mixed associations with %TWL. Compared to pre-MBS, some eating-related practices changed (e.g., increased chewing), whereas others (e.g., time spent on food preparation) remained largely unchanged. Findings of reduced EAH, small but overall positive changes in eating hedonics and specific changes in eating-related practices contribute to a differentiated characterization of postoperative eating across multiple domains of eating. Longitudinal associations between EAH and weight loss highlight the importance of monitoring non-normative eating behaviors for optimizing postoperative outcomes.
