### 重點摘要
傳統職業流行病學研究多基於預設假設，本研究則採用數據驅動的方法，探討職業與肺癌風險的關聯。研究整合了14項歐洲與加拿大肺癌病例對照研究數據，利用正規化迴歸模型，在調整已知肺癌致癌物等因素後，評估了多種職業與肺癌風險的關係。結果顯示，男性礦工及磚瓦匠與肺癌風險增加相關，而中學教師、生產經理和政府行政官員則與風險降低相關；女性方面，中學語言文學教師及政府行政官員亦與肺癌風險降低相關。這些模型識別出與肺癌風險增加或減少相關的職業，即便考慮了已知暴露和協變量，其關聯性依然存在。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究透過數據驅動模型，突破傳統以預設假設探討職業與肺癌關聯的限制，發現了過去可能未被關注的職業風險。然而，儘管研究已調整已知暴露，這些新識別的關聯背後潛在的「未識別職業因素」仍需進一步探究，這也是未來研究的限制與方向。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Data-driven exploration of occupations and lung cancer risks using regularized regression models.
**Abstract:** Occupational epidemiological analyses typically focus on a priori hypotheses relating disease to specific exposures or occupations. We used regularized regression models to agnostically identify occupations associated with lung cancer while accounting for known lung carcinogens. We pooled data from 14 European and Canadian lung cancer case-control studies. The main analysis included 13 587 cases and 16 410 controls for men and 3199 cases and 4408 controls for women. Associations between ever-employment in various occupations and lung cancer risks were evaluated using two selection approaches: a least absolute shrinkage and selection operator regression and a two-stage Bayesian hierarchical horseshoe model accounting for age, study, smoking, and occupational exposure to known lung carcinogens. Unconditional logistic regression models were used to estimate odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for selected occupations. For men, occupations consistently identified as positively associated with lung cancer included miners (OR = 1.28, 95% CI: 1.06-1.54) and bricklayers (OR = 1.30, 95% CI: 1.12-1.52), while secondary education teachers (OR = 0.37, 95% CI: 0.22-0.60), production managers (OR = 0.66, 95% CI: 0.54-0.81), and government executive officials (OR = 0.72, 95% CI: 0.62-0.83) were negatively associated. For women, secondary language and literature teachers (OR = 0.39, 95% CI: 0.20-0.72) and government executive officials (OR = 0.63, 95% CI: 0.48-0.81) showed negative associations. Two agnostic, data-driven models identified several occupations associated with both increased and decreased lung cancer risks. These associations persisted after accounting for known exposures and covariates, highlighting the need to investigate unidentified occupational factors.
