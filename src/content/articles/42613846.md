### 重點摘要
這項義大利全國多中心前瞻性觀察研究旨在評估住院兒童中偶然發現的肝轉胺酶升高盛行率及原因，排除了慢性肝病患者。研究涵蓋1652名住院兒童，結果顯示肝轉胺酶升高的盛行率為34.4%，其中多數為輕中度升高。儘管盛行率高，但出院時僅有5.70%的兒童被診斷出肝臟疾病，大多數病例與急性非肝臟疾病（如呼吸道感染）相關。研究建議對出院時仍持續肝轉胺酶升高的兒童進行後續追蹤，以排除慢性肝臟疾病。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了在住院兒童中，非慢性肝病相關的肝轉胺酶升高之實際盛行率與主要原因的知識空白，顯示其在急性非肝臟疾病中普遍存在且大多不直接指向肝臟原發疾病。然而，研究本身並未對出院後仍有肝轉胺酶升高的兒童進行長期追蹤，以確認最終的診斷，這是一個未來研究的限制或方向。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Hypertransaminasemia in hospitalized children: Insights from a National Multicenter Study by the Italian Society of Pediatric Gastroenterology, Hepatology, and Nutrition.
**Abstract:** Hypertransaminasemia is a frequent finding in hospitalized children with common pediatric illnesses, often considered a transitory phenomenon associated with systemic inflammation/injury. This study aims to assess the prevalence and causes of incidentally detected hypertransaminasemia in children admitted to general pediatric units for non-chronic liver-related illnesses. This is a prospective, multicenter observational study. Children with transaminase measurement at hospital admission were enrolled from June 2021 to July 2023. Inclusion criteria were: Age >30 days to 17 years; hospitalization for any clinical condition; Informed consent. Patients with a previous diagnosis of liver disease were excluded. A total of 1652 patients (1 month to 17 years) were enrolled in nine Italian Pediatric hospital centers. Overall, the prevalence of hypertransaminasemia was 34.4% (n = 570). The majority had mild to moderate liver enzymes elevation. Positive correlation were found between aspartate aminotransferase and creatin P-kinase (CPK), body mass index z-score and cholesterol. At discharge, 298 of the 570 re-tested transaminases: 21.4% (64/298) had persistent combined elevation, 13.8% (41/298) isolated alanine aminotransferase, and 37.6% (112/298) isolated elevation. Only 5.70% of the patients (n = 30) with elevated transaminase levels (n = 526) were eventually diagnosed with liver disease at discharge, whereas the majority of patients (19.9%, n = 105) received a diagnosis of pneumonia/bronchitis/upper respiratory tract infection, followed by unspecified infections (10.6%, n = 56). Hypertransaminasemia is common in children admitted to the general pediatric units, affecting approximately 35% of patients. Liver-related discharge diagnoses were uncommon among children with elevated transaminases (5.70%), whereas most cases occurred in the setting of acute non-hepatic illnesses. Children with persistently elevated transaminases at discharge should be followed to rule out chronic liver disease.
