### 重點摘要
現有新生兒和兒童加護病房數據集資訊稀缺，本研究旨在評估德國一家醫院建立單中心高頻新生兒與兒童加護病房數據集（AIx-Neo-Guard）的可行性。這項隊列研究納入2024年9月至2026年1月期間359名患者的400次入住記錄，收集了高頻床邊監測和呼吸數據。該數據集包含3600個患者日，累積超過20,000個手動註釋（約3 TB），並已被用於多種研究，其高時間解析度有助於詳細描述患者狀態。本研究結果證實此數據集的可行性，並鼓勵跨機構合作以擴展研究應用。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了歐洲在高頻新生兒或兒童加護病房數據集方面缺乏已發表描述的研究空白。目前的數據集為單中心性質，未來的研究限制在於其代表性和通用性，但研究者建議可透過多中心數據匯集或聯邦學習來增加其應用並開發更穩健的演算法。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Feasibility of a German High-Frequency Neonatal and Pediatric Intensive Care Dataset (AIx-Neo-Guard Dataset): Secondary Data Cohort Study.
**Abstract:** Available information about existing neonatal and pediatric intensive care datasets is scarce. The objective was to evaluate the feasibility of a monocentric high-frequency dataset of neonatal and pediatric intensive care patients in a cohort study. This study included patients treated in the neonatal and pediatric tertiary care intensive care unit at Uniklinik RWTH Aachen, Germany. The dataset comprised high-frequency data on bedside monitoring and ventilation, CO In total, 400 admissions (227 neonatal and 173 pediatric) from 359 patients were included between September 2024 and January 2026. Of these, 28 neonates were extremely preterm, 34 were very preterm, 68 were moderate to late preterm, and 97 were term infants, with an overall median gestational age of 35 (IQR 31-38) weeks and a median birth weight of 1805 (IQR 1446-3251) g. The pediatric population comprised 34 infants aged younger than 1 year, 45 toddlers aged 1 to 5 years, 43 children aged 6 to 12 years, and 51 adolescents aged 13 to 18 years, with a median age of 10.6 (IQR 1.2-13.0) years and a median admission weight of 22 (IQR 11-50) kg. Overall, 77% (400/517) of all eligible patients were enrolled in the study. The dataset comprised 3600 patient days with more than 20,000 manual annotations (approximately 3 TB) and has been used for multiple use cases. The high temporal resolution allows for detailed characterization of patient states. To our knowledge, there are no published descriptions of comparable European high-frequency neonatal or pediatric intensive care datasets. We share information about our dataset to encourage cross-institutional cooperation. The multicenter pooling of data or federated learning increases possible use cases by enabling new research questions or the development of more robust algorithms.
