### 重點摘要
本研究為第三次系統性回顧與統合分析，旨在更新飲食品質（以HEI、AHEI和DASH分數評估）與非傳染性疾病發病率及死亡率之間的關聯證據。研究分析了207篇報告（涵蓋近400萬名一般民眾及逾5.5萬名癌症倖存者），發現較高的飲食品質與全因死亡率、心血管疾病、癌症及第二型糖尿病的發病率或死亡率呈反向關聯。對於癌症倖存者，飲食品質亦與全因死亡率呈反向關聯。儘管證據確定性為中度至低度，研究結果仍強調了飲食品質在預防慢性疾病中的重要性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻更新並鞏固了飲食品質與多種非傳染性疾病發病率及死亡率之間的反向關聯。然而，研究結果普遍具有中度至低度的證據確定性，且不同飲食品質指標對於特定疾病的確定性各異。這凸顯了未來研究需進一步提升證據確定性，並需在營養指南中更精確地定義高飲食品質，以克服現有發現的異質性並提供更具體化的指引。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Diet Quality as Assessed by the Healthy Eating Index, Alternate Healthy Eating Index, and Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension Score, and Health Outcomes: a Third Systematic Review Update and Meta-Analysis of Cohort Studies.
**Abstract:** How diet quality, as measured by different diet quality indices, is associated with non-communicable disease incidence and mortality remains uncertain. This study aimed to update the body of evidence on the associations between diet quality, as assessed by the Healthy Eating Index (HEI), Alternate Healthy Eating Index (AHEI), and Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (DASH) score, and health outcomes. PubMed, Embase, and Scopus databases were used to update the search until February 3, 2025. Pooled relative risk (RR) with 95% CI for the highest versus the lowest category of diet quality was estimated using a random-effects model. Heterogeneity was explored using Cochran´s Q test and I The current update identified 96 new reports (total now 207), including data from 3,976,111 individuals in the general population and 55,572 cancer survivors. All HEI, AHEI or DASH scores were inversely associated with risk of all-cause mortality (all indices combined: RR 0.82, 95% CI: 0.77 to 0.87), cardiovascular disease incidence (combined RR 0.83, 95% CI: 0.74 to 0.92) or mortality (combined RR 0.78, 95% CI: 0.71 to 0.85), cancer incidence (combined RR 0.88, 95% CI: 0.78 to 0.99) or mortality (combined RR 0.85, 95% CI: 0.76 to 0.95), and type 2 diabetes incidence (combined RR 0.78, 95% CI: 0.67 to 0.92), with moderate to low certainty of evidence. Among cancer survivors, moderate to low certainty of evidence was rated for the inverse association between diet quality indices and all-cause mortality (combined RR 0.83, 95% CI: 0.77 to 0.90). The etiology of non-communicable diseases contributes to the heterogeneity of findings. Different diet quality indices have varying levels of certainty of evidence for each non-communicable disease incidence and mortality, underscoring the importance of defining high diet quality in nutritional guidelines.
