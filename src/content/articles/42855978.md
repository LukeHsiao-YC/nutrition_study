### 重點摘要
本系統性回顧旨在探討家庭混亂在兒童肥胖預防與治療中，是否能預測介入參與度、依從性或治療反應，以及介入措施是否能減少家庭混亂。研究篩選出六份報告，其中三項預測研究顯示，較高的家庭混亂程度與家長飲食自我監測的持續性較差及青少年早期BMI改善不佳有關。另有三項隨機試驗評估家庭混亂作為介入結果，僅一項側重常規的肥胖預防試點能減少家庭混亂，且所有研究的偏差風險均高。儘管家庭混亂與兒童肥胖治療效果相關，但因證據稀少且異質性高，其因果關係仍不確定。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究首次系統性地彙整了家庭混亂在兒童肥胖防治介入中的角色，填補了此領域的知識空白。然而，由於證據稀少且異質性高，所有研究的偏差風險均未達低風險標準，使得家庭混亂與介入成效之間的因果關係仍不確定。未來的研究需要更多前瞻性研究和足夠樣本量的試驗，以釐清家庭組織是否僅反映了更廣泛的結構和家庭負擔，抑或是一個可被修改的介入目標。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Household Chaos in Pediatric Obesity Prevention and Treatment: A Systematic Review.
**Abstract:** Family-based pediatric obesity prevention and treatment programs require sustained organization within the home, yet the role of household chaos in intervention implementation and response has not been synthesized. We evaluated whether household chaos predicts engagement, adherence, or response and whether interventions reduce household chaos. PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, PsycINFO, and CINAHL were searched through April 2026, with citation and targeted Google Scholar searches. Eligible quantitative studies examined household chaos within pediatric obesity prevention or treatment as a predictor, moderator, mediator, or intervention outcome. Risk of bias was assessed using QUIPS or RoB 2, and findings were synthesized narratively. Six reports met eligibility criteria. Among three predictor studies, the FACES-IV Chaotic subscale, a related but distinct family-system measure, was not significantly associated with treatment attendance. Greater household chaos assessed using Confusion, Hubbub, and Order Scale-based measures was associated with higher odds of a low-decreasing rather than high-consistent parental dietary self-monitoring trajectory and less favorable early body mass index (BMI) change in adolescents. Three randomized trials assessed household chaos as an intervention outcome. A routines-focused obesity-prevention pilot reduced household chaos at 12 weeks, whereas two broader obesity interventions did not. No study was judged at low overall risk of bias. Evidence is sparse and heterogeneous. Household chaos was associated with less sustained parental dietary self-monitoring and less favorable early BMI response, but causal effects remain uncertain. Prospective studies and adequately powered trials are needed to determine whether household organization reflects broader structural and family burden, represents a potentially modifiable implementation target, or both.
