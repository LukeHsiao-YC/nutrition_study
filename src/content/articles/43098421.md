### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討飲食炎症指數 (DII) 與發炎性腸道疾病 (IBD) 患者及健康對照組的疾病活動度、腸道發炎和腸道菌群之間的關聯。研究分析了299名IBD患者和243名健康對照組的飲食記錄、糞便鈣衛蛋白和16S rRNA基因測序數據。結果顯示，抗發炎飲食與腸道細菌的α多樣性增加以及Blautia、Faecalibacterium等益生菌屬的豐度顯著相關，無論受試者是否患有IBD。然而，研究並未發現DII與臨床疾病活動度或腸道發炎指標糞便鈣衛蛋白之間有顯著關聯。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了飲食炎症指數 (DII) 與發炎性腸道疾病患者及健康成人腸道菌群組成關聯的知識空白，顯示抗發炎飲食有助於益生菌豐度及菌群多樣性。然而，研究未能證實DII與臨床疾病活動度或腸道發炎指標之間存在直接關聯，這可能受限於其橫斷面研究設計，未來需進行長期或介入性研究以進一步釐清因果關係。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** An anti-inflammatory diet is associated with enrichment of beneficial bacteria and increased microbial richness/evenness in adults with and without Inflammatory Bowel Disease: results from a cross-sectional Dietary Inflammatory Index study.
**Abstract:** Diet is a modifiable lifestyle factor of increasing interest in managing Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD). Yet, the influence of diet on inflammation, disease activity and the gut microbiome in IBD remains unclear. This study examined associations between the Dietary Inflammatory Index (DII), clinical disease activity, intestinal inflammation and gut microbiota in Australian adults with IBD and healthy controls (HC). This is a secondary analysis of baseline clinical and dietary data collected from adult participants enrolled in the Australian IBD Microbiome (AIM) Study. /setting: Participants were aged 18-80 years and recruited into the AIM study July 2019 to September 2023. Participants included 299 adults with IBD (Crohns Disease [CD] = 155, Ulcerative Colitis [UC] = 144) and 243 HCs. The DII was computed from 3-day baseline food records. Faecal calprotectin (FCP) and 16S rRNA gene sequencing of the gut microbiota were measured in paired stool samples. Clinical disease activity was assessed using the Crohn's Disease Activity Index (CD) or partial Mayo score (UC). Associations between the DII score categorised into quartiles and disease activity were analysed with the chi-squared test, independent t-test or one-way ANOVA. Associations between DII quartiles and gut microbes were assessed using Linear Discriminant Analysis Effect Sizes, Kruskal-Wallis and Differential Abundance Analysis using Deseq2 package. DII scores ranged from -5.97 (maximally anti-inflammatory) to 7.08 (maximally pro-inflammatory). The mean DII score for the total cohort was 0.17 ± 2.29 (pro-inflammatory) and was not found to be significantly different between CD, UC and HCs. As expected, median FCP was higher in IBD vs HC groups (33.8 [IQR 20.0 - 179]μg/g vs 0.0 [IQR 0.0 - 20.0]μg/g, p<0.001). No significant association was found between DII and clinical disease activity or FCP. An anti-inflammatory diet score was significantly associated with higher bacterial α-diversity scores and enrichment of beneficial genera including Blautia, Intestinimonas, Faecalibacterium, and Eubacterium in both the IBD and HC groups. Despite no relationship observed between DII and disease activity in the current study, an anti-inflammatory diet score was associated with enrichment of beneficial gut bacteria.
