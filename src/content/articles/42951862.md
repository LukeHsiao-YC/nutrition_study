### 重點摘要
本研究旨在描述住院營養不良成人的特徵，並探討營養照護與住院天數的關聯。這項描述性世代研究分析了2019年12月至2025年1月期間，由營養師診斷或ICD-10編碼的住院營養不良成人數據。研究發現，營養師識別的營養不良比例高於ICD-10編碼，且早期（24小時內）的營養師評估以及口服或腸道營養支持，與約31%的住院天數縮短顯著相關。結果顯示營養照護文件記錄存在差異，但早期營養師介入和特定營養支持措施與較短的住院天數有關。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻描述了住院營養不良的盛行率、營養照護與住院天數的關聯，並突顯了營養師診斷與ICD-10編碼之間文件記錄的差異。然而，它僅顯示了關聯性而非因果關係，未來的研究需要進一步評估其因果關係和營養照護的長期影響。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Malnutrition severity and nutrition care in hospitalized adults: A descriptive cohort study.
**Abstract:** Malnutrition is prevalent in acute hospital populations. Morrison Healthcare launched a data system enabling hospitals to track and report malnutrition, including dietitian diagnosis, assigned ICD-10 codes, nutrition interventions, and length of stay. This study aimed to describe characteristics of adults with malnutrition and examine associations between nutrition care and hospital length of stay. This descriptive cohort study analyzed de-identified data from hospitalized adults with malnutrition identified by a dietitian and/or ICD-10 coded at discharge between December 2019 and January 2025. Categorical variables are reported as frequencies (%) and continuous variables as means ± SD. Chi-square tests assessed associations between malnutrition diagnosis and patient characteristics. Regression was used to examine associations between patient characteristics, nutrition interventions, and hospital length of stay. Among patients with malnutrition identified by either source, dietitians diagnosed severe malnutrition in 102,484 (57.2%) and mild/moderate malnutrition in 50,780 (28.4%). Malnutrition was identified by dietitians in a higher proportion of patients than was documented using ICD-10 codes (85.6% vs. 76.1%, P < 0.001). Mean length of stay was 9.6 (11.7) days and was significantly different between malnutrition diagnosis groups. Early assessment by a dietitian within 24 h was associated with ~31% shorter length of stay. Length of stay was shorter in patients with malnutrition who received oral or enteral nutrition support. Differences between dietitian-identified malnutrition and ICD-10 coded malnutrition highlight variability in documentation practices. Earlier dietitian assessment and selected nutrition interventions were associated with differences in hospital length of stay. Further research is needed to evaluate causal relationships and long-term impacts of nutrition care.
