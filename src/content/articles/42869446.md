### 重點摘要
本研究旨在評估針對體重低於1200克的極低出生體重（VLBW）嬰兒，美國補充保障收入（SSI）自動資格對佛羅里達州接近臨界值嬰兒SSI參與率的影響。利用迴歸不連續設計，分析了1990年至2015年間超過2萬名出生體重介於1000至1400克嬰兒的數據。結果顯示，在1200克分界點，SSI參與率顯著增加了19.1個百分點，且非裔、教育程度較低、WIC參與者或低收入母親的嬰兒參與率增幅最大。研究結論指出，SSI的1200克門檻非常關鍵，儘管該計畫有效針對高風險族群，但整體參與率仍遠未普及，提示需要加強推廣。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究量化了SSI自動資格門檻對極低出生體重嬰兒參與率的影響，並證實該計畫能有效觸及高風險群體。然而，研究結果也明確指出，即使是自動符合資格的嬰兒，其SSI參與率仍遠低於普及水平，且未自動符合資格但可能受益的嬰兒參與率更低。這突顯了當前計畫宣導與 outreach 的研究空白，亟需探究並解決所有符合資格嬰兒在參與該福利計畫時所面臨的障礙。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Differential Take-Up of Supplemental Security Income in Florida for Very Low Birth Weight Infants.
**Abstract:** Supplemental Security Income (SSI) eligibility is automatically granted for the subset of very low birth weight (VLBW) infants born below 1200 g. We estimated the effects of automatic eligibility on SSI take-up among VLBW infants near the cutoff, both overall and for key subgroups. We analyzed Florida birth certificates merged with Social Security Administration records from 1990 to 2015. Our sample included 21 606 infants with gestational age under 32 weeks and birth weight between 1000 to 1400 g. Using a regression discontinuity approach, we tested whether SSI take-up by VLBW infants was higher under the 1200 g cutoff and whether take-up differed by individual characteristics. Our results indicated that SSI take-up increased by 19.1 percentage points at the 1200 g cutoff. Baseline take-up rates were the highest for mothers who were Black, less educated, Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants, and Children (WIC) participants, or low-income county residents. Increases in take-up at the SSI eligibility threshold were the largest for mothers who were non-Hispanic Black, other race and ethnicity, or WIC participants. Our results suggest that the SSI 1200 g eligibility threshold matters-infants just below it were significantly more likely to participate in the program. Take-up among eligible infants varied by individual characteristics, in ways consistent with the program being well-targeted-reaching many infants with worse health and lower income-and yet take-up was far less than universal even for birth weight eligible infants, suggesting a need for additional outreach. Participation was even lower for VLBW infants who were not automatically eligible but could have benefited from the program.
