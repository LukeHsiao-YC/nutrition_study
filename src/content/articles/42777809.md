### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討癌症患者營養不良與治療耐受性之間的關聯，並識別相關風險因子。這是一項回溯性觀察性研究，分析361名患者的數據，利用PG-SGA評估營養狀況並觀察治療結果。結果顯示，營養不良的患者提早終止治療及因不耐受而改變治療的機率顯著增加，其中化學治療和胰臟癌是營養不良的顯著風險因子。研究建議應優先為胰臟癌或接受化學治療的高風險患者提供營養師資源，以改善治療耐受性並預防治療中斷。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了營養不良對癌症患者醫療治療耐受性影響不明確的研究空白，明確指出營養不良與治療提早終止及因不耐受而調整治療之間存在顯著關聯。然而，作為一項回溯性觀察研究，它無法確立因果關係，且可能存在未納入考量的混淆因子，限制了結果的普遍適用性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Association Between Malnutrition and Therapy Intolerance in Cancer Patients: An Observational Cohort Study.
**Abstract:** Optimal nutrition is integral for health, particularly in oncologic patients undergoing active treatment. However, the impact of inadequate nutrition on tolerance of medical therapy remains unclear. This study aimed to discern whether patients with malnutrition have worse treatment tolerance and identify malnutrition risk factors to guide dietitian-implemented interventions. This retrospective observational study scored nutritional status throughout treatment using Box 3 + Worksheet 1 of the Patient-Generated Subjective Global Assessment (PG-SGA). Scores >3 out of 8 indicated need for dietitian involvement. Eligible patients included those approached for the Moving Through Cancer initiative at the Hillman Cancer Center in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania with an initial diagnosis between January 2015 and August 2025, no prior treatment, and at least one follow-up visit. Main outcomes included early therapy termination and therapy changes secondary to intolerance. Linear and logistic regressions analysis were performed; significance was set a priori at 0.05 using Stata. Among 361 records: 169 (46.8%) were female; average age ± SD was 61.4 ± 14.0 years; 123 (34.1%) scored >3 on the PG-SGA. Patients scoring >3 had greater odds of terminating therapy early (adjusted odds ratio (AOR), 6.35 [95% CI, 3.30-12.21]; P < 0.001), changing therapy secondary to intolerance (AOR, 2.79 [95% CI, 1.55-5.02]; P = 0.001 for cytopenia; AOR, 1.93 [95% CI, 1.13-3.30]; P = 0.02 for fatigue; AOR: 2.97 [95% CI, 1.63-5.40]; P < 0.001 for gastrointestinal distress), and seeing outpatient dietitians (AOR, 1.96 [95% CI 1.18-3.25]; P = 0.01). Chemotherapy exposure was associated with 3.10 greater odds of malnutrition ([95% CI, 1.51-6.37]; P = 0.002). Patients with pancreatic cancer had 4.64 times greater odds of scoring >3 on the PG-SGA ([95% CI, 2.29-9.41]; P < 0.001). These study findings support that dietitian resources should be triaged to higher-risk groups, namely patients with pancreatic cancer or those receiving chemotherapy, to prevent early therapy termination or changes secondary to intolerance.
