### 重點摘要
儘管篩檢站距離是COVID-19篩檢參與度的已知障礙，但高收入國家關於篩檢設施可近性影響的實驗證據卻很有限。本研究透過兩項準實驗研究，在荷蘭探討了COVID-19篩檢設施可近性對篩檢參與度的影響。結果顯示，不論是設置固定篩檢站或使用移動篩檢巴士，當篩檢設施距離居民更近時，篩檢參與率均顯著提升。這表明在地化篩檢設施能大幅提高COVID-19篩檢量，進而增強「檢測、追蹤、隔離」策略的整體效益。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了在高收入國家中，缺乏關於篩檢設施可近性對COVID-19篩檢參與度影響的實驗證據之研究空白。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The effect of proximity of COVID-19 test facilities on test uptake: two quasi-experimental trials.
**Abstract:** Access to testing sites is a critical component in reducing the spread of a virus during infectious disease outbreaks. For example, distance to the testing site has been mentioned as an important self-reported barrier for test uptake during the COVID-19 pandemic. Despite its importance, there is still little experimental evidence on the effect of proximity of testing sites on test uptake in high income countries. In this study we test the effect of proximity of COVID-19 test facilities on test uptake in the Netherlands during a community wide testing initiative during the COVID-19 pandemic. We conducted two quasi-experimental studies in three villages in The Netherlands between February 8th and March 21st, 2021. In Study 1 (n = 11,317) a testing site was placed in one village (reducing distance to testing site from 8.4 km to 800 m) but not in a comparable control village (distance to testing site 9.9 km). In Study 2 (n = 1,880) a mobile test bus alternated between two areas codes in one village, changing the average distance to the testing site from 3.5 km (bus absent) to 200 m (bus present) and from 1.6 km (bus absent) to 850 m (bus present). Study 1 assessed the number of individuals who tested for COVID-19 and the number of times a unique individual tested (combined as the total number of tests conducted). Study 2 assessed the total number of tests conducted per week. In Study 1, a log linear regression controlling for baseline differences (8.6% control vs. 10% intervention) found that reducing the distance to the testing site significantly increased the percentage of tested residents (17.5% control vs. 29% intervention [OR = 1.84, 95% CI = 1.68-2.02]). In Study 2, a Poisson regression revealed higher test uptake when the mobile test bus was present (33.6% when absent vs. 42.6% when present [incidence rate ratio = 1.35, 95% CI = 1.15-1.59]). Reducing the distance to test facilities increased test uptake. Localising test facilities can substantially increase testing for COVID-19, and thus the effectiveness of a Test, Trace, and Isolate strategy in general.
