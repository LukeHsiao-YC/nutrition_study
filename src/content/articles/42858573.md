### 重點摘要
傳統醫療溝通常聚焦於口語交流，但在重症、脆弱及臨終照護情境下，僅靠語言可能不足。本文透過跨學科的文獻回顧，提出「靜默陪伴」的概念框架，將其定義為一種刻意且專注回應的關係參與形式。此框架包含在場、調和、沉默及媒介四個相互關聯的維度，強調沉默是更廣泛關係過程的一部分，而非單純的言語缺失。研究結論指出，「靜默陪伴」提供了一個新的視角，以理解口語非唯一或最合適支持的醫療情境，並建議將此關係能力納入未來的醫療溝通培訓中。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
此文獻填補了傳統醫療溝通模型未能充分解釋和納入沉默在照護中關係意義的空白，它提供了一個理論框架，將沉默視為一種積極的、有意義的關係參與形式。然而，作為一個概念性提案，此框架需要進一步的實證研究和跨臨床與文化場域的驗證，以鞏固其應用價值。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Beyond communication: Toward a relational model of silent accompaniment in healthcare.
**Abstract:** Healthcare communication is commonly conceptualized through verbal exchange, information sharing, empathy, and shared decision-making. Yet in situations of serious illness, profound vulnerability, and end-of-life care, patients' experiences may not always be fully expressible or adequately addressed through words alone. This article aims to examine the relational significance of silence and presence in healthcare and to develop the concept of silent accompaniment as a framework for understanding forms of care that emerge when verbal communication becomes insufficient, secondary, or inappropriate to the moment. This conceptual review draws on interdisciplinary theoretical and empirical literature from healthcare communication, palliative and end-of-life care, nursing, relational care, and scholarship on silence and presence. Existing conceptualizations of therapeutic presence, nonverbal communication, and relational engagement are critically examined to identify their contributions and limitations and to clarify the conceptual boundaries of silent accompaniment. The analysis conceptualizes silent accompaniment not as the absence of speech, but as an intentional form of relational engagement in which silence is sustained through attentive and responsive presence. Four interrelated dimensions are proposed: presence, referring to intentional availability to the patient; attunement, referring to sensitivity and responsiveness to the patient's verbal and nonverbal cues; silence, understood as a context-dependent relational space rather than an inherently therapeutic act; and mediation, referring to the embodied and contextual means (such as gaze, posture, proximity, and touch when appropriate) through which presence and attunement remain perceptible during silence. The framework distinguishes silent accompaniment from silence alone and from related constructs such as therapeutic presence and nonverbal communication. Silent accompaniment offers a relational perspective for examining healthcare encounters in which speaking is neither the only nor necessarily the most appropriate form of support. Rather than opposing silence to communication, the framework situates silence within a broader relational process shaped by presence, attunement, context, and responsiveness. As a conceptual proposal, the framework requires further empirical investigation and validation across clinical and cultural settings. Healthcare communication training may benefit from extending its focus beyond verbal competencies to include the recognition and appropriate use of silence, relational presence, sensitivity to nonverbal cues, and tolerance of uncertainty. The silent accompaniment framework may provide a basis for developing and evaluating such relational competencies, particularly in serious illness and end-of-life care.
