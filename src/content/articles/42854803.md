### 重點摘要
辦公室工作者久坐與身體活動不足構成嚴重全球健康風險，定位遊戲被視為新穎的遊戲化介入方法。本研究採非隨機對照試驗，讓70位40歲以上久坐的辦公室工作者參與為期8週的定位遊戲（Pikmin Bloom）結合聊天機器人監測步數的介入。結果顯示，實驗組在步行與總身體活動量方面顯著改善，並在體重、身體質量指數、體脂肪率及骨骼肌量等體組成參數上優於對照組。這些發現指出定位遊戲是有效且易於使用的行動健康工具，能改變久坐族群的健康行為並促進職業健康。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究提供了將定位遊戲作為行動健康工具，有效提升久坐辦公室工作者身體活動量並改善體組成的實證，填補了此類創新遊戲化介入在職業健康促進領域的有效性研究空白。然而，非隨機設計和有限的8週介入期，提示未來需要隨機對照試驗與長期研究以確認其普遍性與持久效果。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Efficacy of an Augmented Reality Location-Based Game on Physical Activity in Middle-Aged Sedentary Workers.
**Abstract:** Prolonged sitting and physical inactivity among office workers pose severe global health risks. Location-based games have emerged as a novel gamified approach to counteract these sedentary lifestyles. This study investigated the effectiveness of a location-based game on physical activity behaviors, psychological determinants, and body composition. The study design used a two-arm, non-randomized controlled trial between February and May 2025. Participants were 70 sedentary office workers aged 40 years and above who were assigned to experimental and control groups. The experimental group engaged in an 8-week intervention that integrated location-based gameplay, Pikmin Bloom, with progressive daily step goals monitored via a chatbot. The evaluation used a self-reported questionnaire and objective body composition metrics. The experimental group had achieved significant within-group improvements in walking and total physical activity after the intervention. Significant group-by-time interactions were observed for all body composition parameters, with the experimental group demonstrating superior improvements in body weight, body mass index, body fat percentage, and skeletal muscle mass compared to the control group. These findings suggest that location-based games can serve as efficacious and accessible mHealth tools for modifying health behaviors in sedentary populations. By transforming routine walking into an interesting and engaging experience, such interventions yield meaningful enhancements in physical activity for occupational health promotion. The study protocol was preregistered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06910995).
