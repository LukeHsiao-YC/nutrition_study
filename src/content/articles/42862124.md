### 重點摘要
鉛是一種神經毒物，槍械使用因彈藥和底火中的氣溶膠鉛，成為一個未被充分認識的鉛暴露來源，可能導致家庭內二次暴露並影響兒童。本研究對1,565名使用含鉛彈藥（或不確定類型）的美國槍械擁有者進行全國性橫斷面調查，分析其射擊後衛生習慣。結果顯示，97%的射擊者使用含鉛彈藥或不確定類型，但有高達46%完全沒有射擊後衛生習慣，另有30%僅採取一種。這項研究指出，含鉛彈藥的普遍使用和欠缺射擊後衛生行為，構成住宅鉛暴露的重要途徑，估計約600萬美國兒童因此面臨風險。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了過去文獻中，缺乏對槍械使用者的彈藥類型選擇和射擊後衛生習慣量化數據的空白。它首次全國性地量化了美國槍械擁有者的射擊後衛生行為，並強調了其對住宅鉛暴露，特別是兒童健康的潛在影響。然而，本研究為橫斷面設計，無法建立因果關係，且依賴自我報告數據可能存在偏差。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Lead Ammunition Use and Post-Shooting Hygiene Practices Among U.S. Firearm Owners.
**Abstract:** Lead is a neurotoxicant with no safe exposure level. Firearm use represents an underrecognized source of exposure through aerosolized lead from ammunition and primers, with the potential for secondary and tertiary take-home exposures that may affect children. Few studies have quantified firearm use, and to the authors' knowledge, none have considered ammunition type or post-shooting hygiene behaviors. A cross-sectional analysis of the National Firearm Survey, a nationally representative survey conducted in December 2024 and analyzed in 2025-2026, was performed. The sample included adults aged 18 or older who owned a firearm, reported firing a firearm in the past year, used lead-based ammunition (or were unsure of the type), and answered questions about post-shooting hygiene behavior (n=1,565). Participants reported demographic and geographic characteristics (e.g., race, U.S. region, child in household) and firearm behaviors (e.g., shooting frequency, duration, indoor shooting, and storage practices). Outcomes were self-reported post-shooting hygiene behaviors (e.g., showering, changing clothes or shoes, isolating gear before entering the home), examined individually and as a summed continuous index using quasi-Poisson regression. Weighted percentages and rate ratios were estimated. Among U.S. firearm owners who shot in the past year (75% male, 57% aged 31-59 years), 97% reported using lead-based ammunition or were unsure of the type. Most reported shooting monthly or less (94%) but for ≥30 minutes (91%); 32% primarily shot indoors. Overall, 46% (95% CI=43%, 49%) engaged in no post-shooting hygiene behaviors, and 30% (95% CI=27%, 33%) engaged in only 1. Hygiene behaviors were more common among non-White, younger, urban-dwelling individuals with higher education and income and among those who shot more frequently and for longer durations. Approximately 6 million U.S. children live in households where firearms with lead-based ammunition are shot annually and where caregivers report no post-shooting hygiene behaviors. The use of lead-based ammunition is nearly universal among U.S. shooters, while post-shooting hygiene behaviors are uncommon and may represent a substantial pathway for residential lead exposure.
