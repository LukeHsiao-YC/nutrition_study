### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討俄勒岡州的整合性照護組織（CCOs）對低收入、患有身心共病（MPCs）的懷孕女性，其醫療服務利用狀況的影響，以因應美國慢性病盛行率上升對此弱勢群體的不均衡影響。研究採用準實驗性的差異中差異法，回溯分析2008-2016年的俄勒岡州醫療補助數據，評估CCOs對及時與充足的產前照護、可預防住院及急診利用的效益。結果顯示，CCOs顯著提高身心共病女性在懷孕初期進行首次產前檢查及產前照護充足性的機率，但對可預防住院與急診利用則無統計學上的顯著關聯。總結而言，CCOs能有效改善身心共病孕婦及時且充足地利用產前照護，但其對降低昂貴的可預防性醫療利用的潛力仍需進一步研究。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究證實整合性照護模式（CCOs）能改善低收入身心共病孕婦的產前照護利用。然而，CCOs對於降低可預防性住院與急診利用的效果尚未明確，此為未來研究需深入探討的領域，以了解其長期效益和成本效益。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The Effect of An Integrated Accountable Model on Healthcare Utilization During Pregnancy Among Low-Income Women with Comorbid Mental and Physical Conditions: Evidence from Oregon's Coordinated Care Organizations.
**Abstract:** The prevalence of chronic conditions continues to increase in the United States, disproportionately affecting low-income women and Medicaid beneficiaries. This study examined whether an accountable care approach implemented in Oregon Medicaid, known as Coordinated Care Organizations (CCOs), which integrates the delivery of physical and mental health services, may improve high-value healthcare utilization during pregnancy among female Oregon Medicaid beneficiaries with chronic mental-physical comorbidities (MPCs). We performed a retrospective analysis of Oregon Medicaid data from 2008 to 2016, linked with birth certificates and hospital discharge records. Measures of high-value utilization included timely and adequate prenatal care visits, preventable hospitalizations, and preventable emergency department (ED) visits. We utilized a quasi-experimental difference-in-differences approach, comparing the pre-post changes in the outcomes between CCO enrollees and other Medicaid beneficiaries. CCO enrollment was associated with greater odds of having the first prenatal care visit in the first trimester of pregnancy (OR 1.51, CI 1.21, 1.88) and the adequacy of prenatal care (OR 1.40, CI 1.10, 1.78) among women with MPCs. However, no statistically discernible associations were found for preventable hospitalization and ED visits. CCOs are effective in improving timely and adequate utilization of prenatal care among women with MPCs. Further research is needed to understand whether and how CCOs may reduce preventable costly healthcare utilization among women with MPCs during and beyond pregnancy.
