### 重點摘要
PARKS研究是一項分組隨機對照試驗，旨在評估公園層級的密集社區外展活動對城市兒童身體活動的影響，因城市兒童鮮少使用公園。研究隨機分配20個城市公園進行為期12個月的密集或常規外展，並追蹤430對親子。結果顯示，介入組兒童在中度至劇烈身體活動與久坐時間上無顯著變化，但在輕度身體活動的下降幅度顯著較小，且介入組家長的久坐活動增加幅度也顯著較小。研究結論指出，基於公園的社區外展或能預防兒童輕度身體活動的下降並減少家長久坐活動的增加，但未來介入需加強力度以提升對兒童身體活動的影響。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究評估了密集社區外展活動在提升兒童公園使用和身體活動方面的潛力，特別是在延緩兒童輕度身體活動下降及減緩家長久坐行為增加方面展現了效果。然而，研究未能實現其主要假設，即提升兒童的中度至劇烈身體活動，這指出目前的外展強度對於達成此目標仍有不足，為未來公園基礎介入設計提供了明確的限制與改進方向。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Physical Activity and Recreation for Kids Study (PARKS): Park-intensive community outreach for child physical activity group-randomized controlled trial.
**Abstract:** Park use among urban children can contribute to higher child physical activity (PA), yet most children do not use parks or park sports programs. The PARKS (Physical Activity and Recreation for Kids Study) group randomized controlled trial evaluated the effect of park-level intensive community outreach on change in child MVPA, light and sedentary activity. It was hypothesized that children in the intervention parks would increase their MVPA and decrease their sedentary activity compared to children in the usual parks. Twenty urban parks were randomized to intensive park outreach or usual outreach. Intervention included monthly meetings with park managers and community partner organizations to support existing and create new community marketing efforts. An evaluation cohort of 430 parent-child dyads who resided near these parks completed PA measures at baseline and 12 months. Generalized linear mixed models estimated mean differences (95% confidence intervals) between treatment groups on child and parent PA outcomes at 12 months. Cohort retention was 90% at 12 months. No significant intervention effects were observed on child MVPA or sedentary time. Children in the intervention parks showed significantly less decline in their accelerometry-based light PA on average by 9.6 mins/day (95% Confidence Interval: 1.4, 17.7) compared with children in the control group. Parents in the intervention parks showed significantly less increase in accelerometry-based sedentary activity on average -27.3 mins/day (95% Confidence Interval: -52.9, -1.6) compared with parents in the control group. Park-based intensive community outreach may prevent age-related declines in children's light PA and may reduce increases in parents' sedentary activity. Future park-based interventions must amplify community outreach efforts to strengthen their impact on child PA.
