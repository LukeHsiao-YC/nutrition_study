### 重點摘要
本研究透過質性訪談22位具經驗的註冊營養師，探討他們在減重實務中導入線上體重管理方案與應用程式的障礙與促進因素。研究發現，主要障礙包括患者缺乏時間、動機及較低的教育程度，以及機構的保險覆蓋不足與僵硬流程。促進因素則為患者對數位工具的偏好、提升自我監測與問責性，以及營養師潛在的時間節省。研究建議，數位平台需透過簡化工作流程、機構支持與報銷結構來更廣泛整合，以擴大減重服務的可及性並提升依從性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了從註冊營養師（RDNs）視角出發，探討數位體重管理工具在實際臨床應用中面臨的實施障礙與促進因素的研究空白，為技術融入營養照護提供了關鍵的實踐性見解。然而，本研究主要探討營養師對新開發程式的初步看法，而非長期實際應用經驗，且其質性研究性質限制了結果的廣泛推論性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Barriers and Facilitators of Implementing an Online Weight Loss Program and App with Patients: A Qualitative Study of Registered Dietitian Nutritionists' Perspectives.
**Abstract:** Registered dietitian nutritionists (RDNs) play a pivotal role in providing dietary education to patients seeking weight loss. Technology such as apps and online education may support RDNs and patients by providing platforms for enhanced communication and tracking. To explore RDNs' perceptions of using an online weight management program and app in dietetic practice, including perceived barriers and facilitators to implementation. Qualitative study using semi-structured individual interviews. Participants (n=22) were credentialed RDNs currently working in weight loss practice with at least one year of experience. RDNs were purposively recruited to represent a range of years in practice, regions of the United States, and employer types INTERVENTION: Participant RDNs completed an eight-week study reviewing a newly developed online weight management program and companion web application. Activities included reviewing online educational sessions and testing the web application from patient and professional perspectives before participating in interviews. Interview data were analyzed using reflexive thematic analysis within a constructivist research paradigm. Atlas.ti (Version 9.1.4) software was used to support data organization, coding, and management. Perceived barriers included patients' lack of time, self-motivation, and lower education level. Institutional barriers included a lack of insurance coverage and rigid workplace processes. Facilitators included patient preference for digital tools and improved accountability and self-monitoring. RDNs also described potential time savings by providing these materials to patients. Broader integration of digital platforms into nutrition care may benefit from streamlined workflows, institutional support, and reimbursement structures that facilitate technology-assisted practice. As demand for weight management services increases, RDNs are well positioned to lead implementation of evidence-based digital interventions that expand access, support long-term adherence, and promote equitable nutrition care.
