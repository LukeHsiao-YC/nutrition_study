### 重點摘要
本研究旨在解決現有證據三角化主要為定性而非定量的限制，提出一個量化整合多來源證據的新框架。此框架著重於連結研究問題與目標因果問題（相關性），評估各研究偏誤（嚴謹性），然後透過偏誤調整後的統合分析進行結果整合。應用於β-胡蘿蔔素對冠心病（CHD）和心血管疾病（CVD）的影響，該框架整合隨機對照試驗、孟德爾隨機化和傳統多變量迴歸的證據，最終所有研究設計一致顯示β-胡蘿蔔素對CHD風險無顯著影響，結果與CVD相似。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
此研究填補了現有證據三角化方法主要為定性而非定量的空白，提出了一個能將來自不同研究設計（如隨機對照試驗、孟德爾隨機化和多變量迴歸）的證據進行量化整合，並同時考量其嚴謹性與相關性的框架。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** A quantitative approach to evidence triangulation: development of a framework to address rigour and relevance.
**Abstract:** Triangulation is an approach to strengthen causal inference by integrating evidence from multiple sources. Most studies using triangulation have qualitatively examined whether different studies agree upon the presence of a causal effect, rather than estimated the effect by quantitatively integrating results. Here, we develop a framework for quantitative triangulation. We first address how to relate study-specific research questions to an overall target causal question (relevance) and then assess the directions and magnitudes of bias in each study (rigour), before combining the results using meta-analysis adjusted for the biases. We illustrate our framework by triangulating evidence from randomized controlled trials (RCTs), Mendelian randomization (MR) and conventional multivariable regression (MVR) to estimate the effect of beta-carotene on coronary heart disease (CHD) and cardiovascular disease (CVD). Three RCTs and one MR study showed little evidence of a causal relationship between beta-carotene and CHD (risk ratio (RR) = 1.00 with 95% CI = 0.98 to 1.01 and RR = 1.02 with 95% CI = 0.98 to 1.07, respectively). Seven MVR studies indicated that high intake of beta-carotene reduces CHD risk (RR = 0.83 with 95% CI = 0.76 to 0.91). After applying our framework, the three study designs agreed that there is no strong evidence against the null hypothesis that beta-carotene intake does not affect risk of CHD (RR = 0.99 with 95% CI = 0.82 to 1.21). Findings were similar for CVD. Our framework shows how to address rigour and relevance quantitatively when triangulating evidence from different study designs. We highlight the importance of explicitly defining the target and study-specific research questions.
