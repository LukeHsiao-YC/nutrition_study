### 重點摘要
肯亞乾旱地區的五歲以下兒童急性營養不良問題持續存在。本研究透過在桑布魯和圖爾卡納縣進行的社區參與式對話，旨在從社區視角識別並繪製急性營養不良的強、中、弱驅動因素。研究發現，社區普遍認為食物缺乏、兒童疾病、不當照護、不潔水源與貧困是主要的強力驅動因素；母親飲酒為中度因素。這些由社區提出的發現與既有框架相符，並為制定具體且有地域性的干預措施提供了重要資訊。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究透過收集肯亞乾旱地區社區居民對急性營養不良驅動因素的在地化視角，填補了傳統研究在地方性文化與社會經濟背景理解上的空白。這些以社區為中心的發現，有助於未來開發更具文化敏感性和地區適應性的營養干預策略。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Community Perceptions of Drivers of Acute Malnutrition Among Children in Northern Kenya.
**Abstract:** Acute malnutrition in children < 5 years is persistent in Kenya's drylands. The aim of this study was to engage communities in Samburu and Turkana Counties in participatory dialogues to develop causal maps highlighting strong, moderate, and weak drivers of acute malnutrition from a community perspective. The community dialogues included free listing, ranking, and causal mapping of the listed and ranked factors contributing to acute malnutrition. In both counties and across livelihoods, the factors communities perceived to be strongly related to acute malnutrition included lack of food or hunger; child illness; inadequate caring and feeding practices; unclean water, poor hygiene, and lack of sanitation; and poverty or lack of money. Maternal alcohol use was the only factor identified by communities as contributing moderately to acute malnutrition in both counties. Factors that were considered moderate in one county and weak in the other included lack of health, nutrition, or childcare knowledge (Samburu moderate, Turkana weak); poor child spacing (Samburu weak, Turkana moderate); unemployment (Samburu weak, Turkana moderate); loss or insufficient livestock (Samburu weak, Turkana moderate); and livestock raids (Samburu moderate, Turkana weak). Four factors were considered weak in both counties: early marriage or early pregnancy; family conflict or divorce; disability; and traditional beliefs about parents' sex and infidelity. Several other moderate and weak factors were mentioned only in one county or livelihood. These community-generated findings align with a previous framework for acute malnutrition in African drylands and provide information about context-specific factors that can inform programmatic interventions.
