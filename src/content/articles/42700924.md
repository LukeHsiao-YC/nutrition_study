### 重點摘要
食道胃癌患者普遍存在營養不良且與預後不良相關，但目前尚無針對此類患者的最佳營養篩查工具。本研究旨在評估「營養不良通用篩查工具 (MUST)」在食道胃癌患者中篩查營養不良風險的有效性，並以「患者自評主觀性整體評估 (PG-SGA)」作為參考標準，對80名新診斷的食道胃癌患者進行了驗證性研究。結果顯示，MUST的敏感度為61.2%，特異度為74.2%，未能達到預設的有效性標準（敏感度和特異度均需≥70%）。因此，研究結論認為MUST不太適合用於新診斷食道胃癌患者的營養不良篩查。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了目前尚不清楚哪種營養篩查工具最適合食道胃癌患者使用的研究空白，明確指出MUST在篩查此類患者營養不良風險時的有效性不足。這也暗示了未來需要評估其他篩查工具或開發更精確的專用工具。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool is not sufficiently valid in screening for risk of malnutrition in patients with newly diagnosed esophago-gastric cancer: a validation study compared with Patient-Generated Subjective Global Assessment.
**Abstract:** Malnutrition is prevalent in patients with esophago-gastric cancer and is associated with poorer outcomes. Malnutrition screening tools are used to screen for risk of malnutrition and to determine whether full nutritional assessment is required. The optimal malnutrition screening tool to use in esophago-gastric cancer is unknown. To assess the validity of the Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool (MUST) in esophago-gastric cancer by measurement of its sensitivity, specificity, positive and negative predictive values for detecting risk of malnutrition. A validation study comparing MUST with full malnutrition assessment using Patient-Generated Subjective Global Assessment (PG-SGA) as the reference standard. /setting. 80 patients with newly diagnosed esophago-gastric cancer awaiting radical treatment at an oncology specialist hospital (Royal Marsden NHS Foundation Trust, London, United Kingdom) were recruited between December 2011 and June 2013. Validity (accuracy) of MUST compared with PG-SGA as a reference method. Association of individual MUST features (BMI, percent weight loss) with PG-SGA score. Sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value (NPV) of MUST compared with PG-SGA. Spearman's rank correlation (ρ) between BMI and percentage weight loss with PG-SGA score. MUST was found to have agreement with PG-SGA in 53/80 (66.3%) patients, misclassifying 19 (23.8%) patients as being at low risk of malnutrition when they were not and 8 (10%) at risk when they were not. MUST demonstrated a sensitivity of 61.2% (95% CI: 46.2-74.8%) and a specificity of 74.2% (95% CI: 55.4-88.1%). PPV for MUST was 78.9% (95% CI: 66.5-87.6%) and NPV was 54.8% (95% CI: 44.6-64.6%). Lower BMI was weakly associated with higher PG-SGA score (correlation -0.259, p=0.021) while greater percentage weight loss was moderately associated with higher PG-SGA score (correlation +0.653, p< 0.001). MUST did not meet a priori criteria (sensitivity and specificity ≥70%) for validation against PG-SGA. MUST is unlikely to be an appropriate malnutrition screening tool in patients with newly diagnosed esophago-gastric cancer.
