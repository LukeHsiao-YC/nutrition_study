### 重點摘要
本研究為一篇敘述性回顧，旨在探討產前葉酸暴露與自閉症類群障礙（ASD）之間的關係，透過系統性搜尋觀察性研究，區分不同葉酸來源。儘管部分研究報告葉酸補充與ASD呈負相關，但來自大型世代研究的結果多為微弱、無關或正相關，且負向對照分析指出可能存在殘餘混淆。綜合目前證據，研究結論不支持產前葉酸暴露與ASD存在一致關聯，亦不建議在無明確臨床指徵下改變標準葉酸補充或增加劑量。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
此文獻回顧整理了目前產前葉酸暴露與自閉症類群障礙關聯的證據，指出研究結果的不一致性，並強調未來研究應整合重複的生物標誌物、補充劑組成與劑量、飲食模式、共同暴露以及社會經濟背景等因素。研究也提及重複紅血球葉酸評估的不足，以及缺乏將強化食品中的葉酸作為獨立暴露進行建模的研究。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Prenatal folate exposures and autism spectrum disorder: timing, exposure definition, and population context.
**Abstract:** Folic acid supplementation before conception and during pregnancy is widely recommended for neural tube defect prevention, but its relation to autism spectrum disorder (ASD) remains uncertain. This narrative review examines evidence linking prenatal folate exposures with ASD, distinguishing folic acid supplement use, multivitamin/prenatal vitamin use, total folic acid intake, dietary folate, and biomarkers. PubMed/MEDLINE and Google Scholar searches through August 2026, supplemented by targeted searches and reference checking through October 2026, were used to identify observational studies by exposure construct. Several studies report inverse associations with prenatal folic acid use, but larger Norwegian, Danish, Japanese, and Swedish cohorts show modest, null, or positive associations. In addition, negative-control analyses reveal stronger inverse associations outside the pregnancy exposure window than during pregnancy, suggesting that residual confounding may contribute to observed findings. Direct dose studies have not demonstrated a clear relation between higher folic acid intake and ASD. Multivitamin associations cannot be attributed to folic acid alone and may reflect socioeconomic and health-related behavioral patterns. Dietary findings are limited, and no included study modeled folic acid from fortified foods as a separate exposure. Upper-tail biomarker associations arise principally from delivery and cord blood samples, and vary by analyte, compartment, and sampling window; repeated red blood cell folate assessment remains a gap. Variation in findings likely reflects a combination of methodological, exposure-related, and population differences across studies. Together, current evidence does not support a consistent association between prenatal folate exposures and ASD, and does not justify altering standard folic acid supplementation or escalating dose without a recognized clinical indication. Future studies should integrate repeated biomarkers, supplement composition and dose, dietary pattern, co-exposures, and socioeconomic context.
