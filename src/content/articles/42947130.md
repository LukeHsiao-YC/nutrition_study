### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討大學生久坐行為的性別化具身經驗及其對心理健康的影響。研究採用混合方法，量化階段測量60名物理治療系學生的久坐模式與心理健康，質化階段則探討久坐的性別化經驗。結果顯示，女性短暫久坐(<30分鐘)與較高心理健康相關，而長時間久坐(>60分鐘)則與較低心理健康相關，男性則無此趨勢，且兩性對久坐的感知與心理體驗存在顯著差異。這表明久坐是一種社會建構的具身經驗，未來學生心理健康介入應納入性別與文化規範考量。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了過去研究在理解久坐行為對心理健康影響時，忽略其「具身化」與「性別化社會建構」面向的空白，強調了客觀行為模式相似但主觀體驗可能存在顯著差異。未來的研究應進一步探討跨文化或不同學術領域中，這些性別化久坐體驗的普遍性及其具體機制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Gendered embodiment of sedentary behaviour and its impact on mental wellbeing in physiotherapy higher education: a sequential mixed methods study.
**Abstract:** Prolonged sedentary behaviour (SB) among university students is a growing concern, yet its impact on mental wellbeing (MWB) cannot be fully understood without considering its embodied nature: how it is experienced through the body in relation to social expectations. This study employs a sequential explanatory mixed-methods approach, integrating quantitative and qualitative phases. The quantitative phase examined SB patterns using activPAL™ devices and MWB through the Warwick-Edinburgh Mental Well-being Scale, among 60 physiotherapy university students. The qualitative phase, based on an ethnographic intended approach and focus groups, explored gendered experiences of SB through Csordas' theory of embodiment. Gender-stratified correlation analyses revealed key patterns: among women, shorter SB bouts (< 30 min) were positively associated with MWB, whereas prolonged bouts (> 60 min) corresponded to lower MWB scores. These associations were not observed among men. Two themes were identified through an abductive thematic analysis during the qualitative phase: gendered constructions of rest-time, and SB as shaped by gendered constraints. Women reported internalized pressures to remain productive, often experiencing guilt during non-productive rest, whereas men framed SB as leisure, free from self-surveillance. While device-measured SB patterns were similar across genders, their embodied experiences differed, as well as the perception of MWB attributed to the SB patterns. These findings highlight SB as a socially constructed and embodied experience. Gender-sensitive approaches to student MWB must consider how SB is internalized and regulated through cultural norms to avoid reinforcing existing inequalities.
