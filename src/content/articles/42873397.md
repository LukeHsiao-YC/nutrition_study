### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討2001年至2018年間美國5-11歲兒童螢幕時間增加的驅動因素，是來自於同隊內變化(IC)還是世代替換(CR)。研究分析了9個NHANES數據週期中9,841名兒童的資料，並使用線性分解技術將總變化分解為IC和CR成分。結果顯示，兒童每日平均螢幕時間從2.95小時顯著增加至4.26小時，其中同隊內變化是螢幕時間增加的主要來源，而世代替換則部分抵消了這一增長。此外，出生世代間螢幕時間的差異也受到人口統計和家庭環境因素的影響。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了關於美國學齡兒童螢幕時間增加驅動因素的空白，明確區分了螢幕時間增加是主要來自於同隊內變化（即隨著時間推移，同一批孩子花費更多螢幕時間）還是世代替換（即後續世代的孩子一開始就花費更多螢幕時間）。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Contextualizing Trends in Television Viewing and Computer Use Among U.S. Children Aged 5-11 Years, 2001-2018.
**Abstract:** Excessive screen time among children is linked to adverse physical, mental, and developmental outcomes. The putative drivers of increasing screen time among school-aged children in the United States remain unclear. Population-level changes may arise from intracohort change (IC)-change in average screen time across survey cycles-or cohort replacement (CR), as later-born cohorts with different levels of screen time replace earlier-born cohorts. We analyzed nine 2-year National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) cycles from 2001 to 2002 through 2017-2018, including 9,841 children aged 5-11 years old. Screen time was measured as the sum of proxy-reported estimates of daily hours of TV/video viewing and computer use. Using linear and algebraic decomposition techniques, we partitioned total change (TC) in screen time into IC and CR components. Models adjusted for demographic, wellness, family, and food security factors. The weighted mean estimated daily screen time increased from 2.95 to 4.26 h (Δ = 1.31;44%). Boys consistently had higher proxy-reported mean screen time than girls. In the unadjusted model, IC contributed positively to the increase in screen time, whereas CR partially offset it. These opposing contributions persisted across all adjustment models. Combined estimated daily screen time among U.S. children aged 5-11 years increased significantly between 2001 and 2018. Increases within birth cohorts were the main source of this rise, while cohort replacement partially offset it. Differences in screen time across birth cohorts were shaped by changing demographics and family environments.
