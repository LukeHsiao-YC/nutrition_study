### 重點摘要
本研究旨在評估COVID-19大流行對捷克共和國各年齡、性別與死因的超額總體死亡率的直接與間接影響。研究利用2015-2022年的國家死亡率數據，將大流行期間（2020-2022年）的死亡率與疫情前（2015-2019年）進行比較。結果顯示，2020、2021和2022年的總體超額死亡率分別為15%、27%和6%，其中大部分是由COVID-19相關死亡所驅動。男性超額死亡率高於女性，且心臟衰竭、糖尿病和神經退化性疾病的死亡率增加了20-40%，儘管心血管疾病、癌症和呼吸系統疾病等主要慢性病並未出現超額死亡。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究詳細量化了捷克共和國COVID-19大流行期間不同年齡、性別和特定死因的超額死亡率，區分了直接與間接影響，彌補了對特定地區大流行多面向影響的認識空白。文章指出，心臟衰竭、糖尿病和神經退化性疾病等與粒線體功能障礙相關的死亡顯著增加，這部分原因值得未來深入探討。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Excess mortality related to the COVID-19 pandemic by age, sex, and cause of death in the Czech Republic.
**Abstract:** The aim of the study was to estimate the direct and indirect effects of the COVID-19 pandemic on excess all-cause mortality in the Czech Republic by sex, age group and cause of death. National mortality data for the period 2015-2022 were used. For each of the COVID-19 pandemic years (2020-2022), national mortality rates were compared with 2015-2019, and mortality excess was estimated for all causes and selected causes of death separately by sex and age group. In the COVID-19 pandemic years 2020, 2021 and 2022, the overall excess in all-cause mortality was 15%, 27% and 6%, respectively; excess deaths due to causes other than COVID-19 were 6%, 4% and 1%, respectively. The overall mortality excess was larger in men than women, both for all causes (31% vs. 23% in 2021) and for COVID-19 deaths (relative risk 1.40 in 2021). There was no overall excess mortality in children and adolescents but in the age group 25-49 years, there were more non-COVID-19 deaths in the pandemic years. During the pandemic, there was no mortality increase in three main groups of causes of death (cardiovascular diseases, cancers, respiratory diseases) but there were increases by 20-40% in mortality from heart failure, diabetes mellitus and neurodegenerative diseases. The large increase in all-cause mortality during the pandemic was driven by deaths coded as COVID-19. The lack of excess mortality from the major groups of chronic diseases suggests that the health system coped well with these conditions. The marked rise in deaths from conditions which have been linked with mitochondrial dysfunction merits further investigation.
