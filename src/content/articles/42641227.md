### 重點摘要
現有的乳房重建決策輔助工具BREASTChoice主要以英文開發，對西班牙語裔拉丁美洲裔乳癌倖存者（SSL）的適用性有限，而該群體常面臨次優的乳癌治療和知情決策障礙。本研究旨在為美國的SSL乳癌倖存者及其臨床醫生嚴謹地調適BREASTChoice，採用了多語言、創傷知情和快速質性分析方法，並結合創新的社區參與模式。調適後的工具增加了與SSL倖存者相關的內容，並重新設計為可分享的網站以改善可近性，同時多語言社區審查的回饋也提升了跨西班牙語方言的理解性。這種結合質性研究和多語言社區參與的方法，有效提升了BREASTChoice的內容、相關性、可理解性和傳播策略，對於改善美國非英語使用者的醫病溝通和治療決策具有重要意義。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了針對美國西班牙語裔拉丁美洲裔乳癌倖存者，缺乏文化與語言適應性乳房重建決策輔助工具的研究空白，特別是針對該群體在獲取資訊和做出符合個人偏好決策上的障礙。未來的研究應著重於評估此調適工具的實際成效及其對改善此人群醫病溝通和治療決策的長期影響。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Adaptation of a breast reconstruction decision aid for Spanish-speaking, Latina survivors using rapid qualitative analysis and a multilingual community engagement model.
**Abstract:** Patient decision aids improve shared decision-making and patient-reported outcomes. BREASTChoice is an effective aid for breast reconstruction decisions after mastectomy. BREASTChoice was developed and tested in English and has limited external validity for Spanish-speaking Latina (SSL) breast cancer (BC) survivors, a population among the least likely to receive guideline-concordant BC treatments or make informed, preference-aligned BC surgery decisions. U.S. researchers report challenges communicating and implementing research with multilingual populations. Our objective was to rigorously adapt BREASTChoice for SSL BC survivors and their clinicians in the U.S. We applied the Cultural and Linguistic Adaptation Framework using multilingual, trauma-informed, rapid qualitative methods and a novel multilingual community engagement model. Iterative data collection and analysis included semi-structured interviews with 11 SSL BC survivors and 21 clinicians, followed by rapid qualitative analysis, tool translation and programming, and usability interviews with five additional SSL participants. Multilingual Community Review Board (MCRB) members from five Latin American countries/territories reviewed the translation. Adaptations were tracked using the Framework for Reporting Adaptations and Modifications-Enhanced (FRAME). Adaptations included adding content and clarifying information relevant to SSL survivors (e.g., insurance coverage, job impacts). We also redesigned the tool as a shareable website with printable features to improve accessibility and facilitate subsequent communication with clinical care providers. MCRB feedback targeted translation understandability across Spanish dialects and strengthened relevance for broader SSL communities. These qualitative and multilingual community-engaged methods yielded iterative enhancements to BREASTChoice's content, relevance, understandability, and strategies for dissemination to SSL BC survivors. This approach could facilitate research to improve patient-provider communication and treatment decision-making for the estimated 67.2 million U.S. residents who speak a language other than English.
