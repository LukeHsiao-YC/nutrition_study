### 重點摘要
PI3K/AKT/MTOR通路活化與癌症相關，肥胖是已知風險因子，而癌症惡病質則預示不良預後，兩者在臨床前研究中與該通路活化或抑制相關，但人體研究結果不一致。本範圍性文獻回顧系統性檢索了139項研究，探討體型與身體組成（從肥胖到惡病質）與PI3K/AKT/MTOR訊號在癌症風險和預後中的關係。結果顯示體型與通路組件相關但結果異質，胰島素阻抗和糖尿病與通路標記的關聯比BMI更一致，且體重管理干預研究未能評估癌症實際預後。現有證據多依賴BMI而非身體組成，亟需標準化設計、身體組成測量及代謝狀態評估以推進精準營養腫瘤學。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇範圍性文獻回顧首次全面探討了體型及身體組成（從肥胖到癌症惡病質）與PI3K/AKT/MTOR訊號通路在多種癌症類型中對臨床預後和患者存活的關聯。它揭示了研究設計的異質性，並指出了多項研究空白，包括需要更一致的身體組成測量方法、納入宿主代謝背景（如胰島素阻抗），以及針對惡病質的飲食介入研究和體重管理干預對癌症實際預後的評估。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** From Obesity to Cancer Cachexia: PI3K/AKT/MTOR Signaling Across the Energy Balance Spectrum - A Scoping Review of Body Size, Composition, and Cancer Outcomes.
**Abstract:** The PI3K/AKT/MTOR signaling pathway is overactivated in cancer. Obesity is an established risk factor for several cancers, also variably associated with prognosis. Cancer cachexia is at the opposite end of the energy balance spectrum and is similarly linked to poor prognosis. Preclinical studies link obesity to activation of this pathway, whereas cachexia is associated with its suppression in skeletal muscle. However, human findings are inconsistent and often ignore the metabolic influence of body composition on MTOR activity. This scoping review summarizes how body size and composition-spanning obesity to cachexia-relate to PI3K/AKT/MTOR signaling in cancer risk and outcomes. A systematic search in Web of Science, Embase, and PubMed through January 19, 2023, identified 6,285 studies; 139 met the inclusion criteria across 15 cancer types: gastrointestinal (n=48); gynecological (n=34); breast (n=30); male reproductive (n=7); lung (n=6); thyroid (n=6); renal (n=2); and skin (n=1) cancers, plus cachexia (n=5). Body size was associated with components and effectors of the PI3K/AKT/MTOR pathway, but findings were inconsistent, reflecting heterogeneity in cancer type, subtype, exposure and biomarker measurement, and unmeasured host factors. Insulin resistance and diabetes were more consistently associated with pathway markers than BMI. Across six studies, obesity and pathway markers often interacted, modifying each other's associations with cancer outcomes. Interventional studies of weight loss, metformin, and bariatric surgery reported changes in some tumor biomarkers, but none assessed cancer outcomes, limiting weight management guidance for patients with obesity. In cachexia, pathway analysis suggested increased catabolic and decreased anabolic muscle signaling, though findings varied by muscle site, and the effects of dietary interventions remain untested. Evidence predominantly relied on BMI rather than body composition components, leaving their relationship to PI3K/AKT/MTOR signaling understudied. Standardized and longitudinal designs, body composition measurement, and assessment of metabolic status, diet, and physical activity are needed to advance precision nutritional oncology. REVIEW REGISTRATION: INPLASY202450036 https://doi.org/10.37766/inplasy2024.5.0036 STATEMENT OF SIGNIFICANCE: This scoping review is the first to comprehensively examine the associations between alterations in body size and composition-including both obesity and cancer-associated cachexia-and PI3K/AKT/MTOR signaling in tumors, adipose tissue, and skeletal muscle across cancer types in relation to clinical outcomes and patient survival. Findings reveal heterogeneity in study designs and highlight research gaps, including the need for more consistent measurement of body composition and incorporating host metabolic context, such as insulin resistance and metabolic dysfunction. These efforts could harmonize results, improve comparability across studies, and advance nutritional and precision oncology.
