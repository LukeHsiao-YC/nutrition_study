### 重點摘要
這項跨國研究旨在釐清母親孕期吸菸與兒童癌症之間的關係，透過整合來自多國、前瞻性記錄的數據，並運用機率性偏誤分析方法，克服了以往研究的潛在偏差。研究分析了來自美國、瑞典、芬蘭和丹麥的超過兩萬例兒童癌症病例和三十萬名對照組數據。結果顯示，在調整偏誤前後，母親孕期吸菸與大多數兒童癌症並無顯著關聯，但卻與兒童黑色素瘤和皮膚癌呈現負相關，而與未指定惡性腫瘤呈正相關。總體而言，本研究支持先前的發現，即母親孕期吸菸對兒童癌症風險的影響證據甚微，但仍強調鑑於吸菸的其他危害，公共衛生工作應繼續降低其盛行率。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究透過採用前瞻性記錄連結數據和機率性偏誤分析，成功地解決了以往許多依賴病例對照研究的潛在偏差問題，從而更可靠地評估了母親孕期吸菸與兒童癌症的關係。它填補了因方法學限制導致結果不一的研究空白。然而，儘管研究方法嚴謹，但對於某些特定癌症的發現（例如與黑色素瘤的負相關，或與未指定惡性腫瘤的正相關），可能需要未來進一步的研究來深入探討其背後機制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Maternal smoking and risk of childhood cancer: a report from the Childhood Cancer and Leukemia International Consortium.
**Abstract:** Although tobacco is an established carcinogen and components of tobacco smoke may cross the placental membrane, past studies of maternal prenatal smoking and childhood cancer have resulted in mixed or null results. However, many studies have relied on case-control methods and may be susceptible to various biases. We aimed to elucidate the nature of the relationship between maternal prenatal smoking and childhood cancer through a multinational analysis of prospectively recorded record-linkage studies with probabilistic bias methods. Data for this analysis came from existing population-based case-control record-linkage studies in the Childhood Cancer and Leukemia International Consortium, with studies from the USA, Sweden, Finland, and Denmark, including more than 20 000 cases and 300 000 controls. We assessed the association between maternal prenatal smoking and childhood cancer using logistic regression. Additionally, we conducted probabilistic bias analysis to investigate the effect of exposure misclassification using sensitivities and specificities from biomarker-based validation studies. Both before and after bias adjustment, maternal smoking was not found to be associated with most cancers investigated. Maternal prenatal smoking was inversely associated with paediatric melanoma [bias-adjusted odds ratio (OR) 0.68, 95% simulation interval (SI): 0.47-0.98] and skin carcinomas and other/unspecified carcinomas (bias-adjusted OR: 0.72, 95% SI: 0.52-0.96), and positively associated and unspecified malignant tumours (bias-adjusted OR: 1.50, 95% SI: 1.21-1.84). This analysis supports previous findings that there is little evidence of an effect of maternal prenatal smoking on childhood cancer risk, with few potential exceptions. However, due to numerous other detrimental effects, public health efforts should continue to further reduce its prevalence.
