### 重點摘要
本研究旨在系統性回顧美國社會公共政策與成人心血管疾病(CVDs)之間的關聯，因CVDs是美國成人主要死因。研究人員篩選出1990年至2024年間發表的46篇研究報告進行分析，主要聚焦於吸菸相關立法、住房援助、食物援助和空氣污染法規四類政策。結果顯示，吸菸相關立法與CVDs發病率和盛行率降低有關，但住房援助和食物援助的影響則不一，空氣污染法規未顯示關聯。總體而言，吸菸相關立法對預防CVDs具保護作用，而其他政策類型的研究結論則較不確定。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了對美國社會公共政策影響CVDs的系統性評估空白。然而，未來研究應深入探討特定政策的具體特徵、社會人口差異，並克服觀察性研究設計造成的因果推論限制，特別是針對住房和食物援助政策。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Social Public Policies for Cardiovascular Health among U.S. Adults: A Systematic Review.
**Abstract:** Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are the most common chronic diseases and leading causes of death among United States (U.S.) adults. Social public policies in the U.S. (including public assistance programs) have the potential to address the social determinants of CVDs and may indirectly contribute to a decline in cardiovascular morbidity and mortality. This review sought to examine associations between social public policies and CVDs among U.S. adults. Using PubMed, Web of Science, and Embase, a systematic search of full-text articles published between January, 1990 and July, 2024 that examined the effect of public policies on CVDs among adults in the U.S. or in U.S. territories was conducted between September and October, 2024. Study characteristics and statistical results were presented. Out of 11,585 potentially eligible records identified, 46 reports (studies) were included in this review after sequential title, abstract, and full-text screening. Four public policy groups emerged: smoking-related legislation (n=32), housing assistance (n=7), food assistance (i.e. Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program) (SNAP) (n=6), and air pollution regulation (n=1). The most commonly-examined outcomes were acute myocardial infarction, cardiovascular-related mortality, and hypertension. Smoking-related legislation was associated with decreased incidence and prevalence of CVDs. Findings were mixed regarding the associations of housing assistance and SNAP with CVDs, while air pollution regulation was not associated with CVDs. Overall, smoking-related legislation was protective against CVDs, while the patterns of findings about the other policy types and CVDs were less conclusive. Inferences about causality were limited given observational and/or cross-sectional study designs, particularly for studies examining housing assistance or food assistance. Regarding the effect of public policies on CVDs, future studies should examine the role of specific policy characteristics and further explore sociodemographic disparities.
