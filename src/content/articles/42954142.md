### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討孕期食物不安全與四種圍產期結果（產後憂鬱、妊娠高血壓、妊娠糖尿病、妊娠體重增加）的關聯，並檢視美國農業部婦女、嬰兒與兒童特別補充營養計畫（WIC）的參與是否調節這些關係。研究利用「懷孕風險評估監測系統」(PRAMS) 的橫斷面數據，對符合WIC資格的母親進行分析，結果顯示孕期食物不安全與所有四種不良圍產期結果的較高風險顯著相關。然而，WIC的參與並未顯著調節這些關聯，表明儘管WIC提供營養支持，但與食物不安全相關的其他壓力源可能持續存在。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
過去研究鮮少探討食物援助計畫（如WIC）是否能調節食物不安全與圍產期不良結果之間的關聯。本研究填補了這項空白，發現WIC參與並未顯著調節這些關係，暗示未來研究應進一步探索食物不安全的其他持續性壓力源或社會決定因素，以更全面地改善孕產婦健康。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Examining the Association Between Food Insecurity and Pregnancy Outcomes.
**Abstract:** Food insecurity (FI) is a critical yet under-identified social determinant of health among pregnant populations. Prior research links FI to adverse perinatal outcomes, but few studies have examined whether these associations are modified by participation in food assistance programs such as the USDA's Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants, and Children (WIC). This study assessed the association between prenatal FI and four perinatal outcomes-postpartum depression (PPD), gestational hypertension, gestational diabetes, and gestational weight gain (GWG)-and examined whether prenatal WIC participation moderated these relationships. We conducted a cross-sectional analysis using state-representative data from Phase 8 of the Pregnancy Risk Assessment Monitoring System (PRAMS). Ten states with relevant data were included. The sample was limited to WIC-eligible mothers (income < 185% of the federal poverty level or Medicaid enrollment). Logistic regression models estimated associations between FI and each outcome in unadjusted and adjusted models, with interaction terms used to assess moderation by WIC participation. Models adjusted for sociodemographic and pregnancy-related characteristics. Among WIC-eligible mothers, 13.65% reported FI during pregnancy. After adjustment, FI was associated with higher odds of PPD (OR: 2.47, 95% CI: 2.16-2.82), gestational diabetes (OR: 1.22, 95% CI: 1.00-1.49), gestational hypertension (OR: 1.38, 95% CI: 1.18-1.60), and GWG below recommendations (OR: 1.29, 95% CI: 1.09-1.52). Adjusting for depression before and during pregnancy attenuated associations. WIC participation did not significantly moderate these relationships. Prenatal FI remains associated with adverse perinatal outcomes, suggesting that while WIC provides nutritional support, additional stressors linked to food insecurity may persist.
