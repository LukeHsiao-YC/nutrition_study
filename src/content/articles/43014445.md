### 重點摘要
這篇系統性回顧旨在探討發炎性腸道疾病（IBD）成人患者中，食物規避、異常飲食行為及飲食失調的定義、盛行率與相關因素。研究納入33項文獻，發現食物規避現象普遍存在（36-97%），異常飲食行為盛行率差異大（4.8-77%），且克隆氏症、疾病活動期及心理因素與限制性飲食行為顯著相關。儘管限制性飲食在IBD患者中很常見，但術語不一致和測量工具缺乏驗證性，限制了研究結果的解釋和臨床應用，未來需要更清晰的概念和評估工具。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻指出，由於發炎性腸道疾病患者飲食限制行為的定義和測量方法存在顯著異質性，且目前依賴於未經驗證或非疾病特異性的測量工具，限制了對研究結果的解釋和臨床應用。因此，未來的研究需要提升概念清晰度，並開發具有臨床意義的評估工具。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Food avoidance, disordered eating behaviors, and eating disorders in inflammatory bowel disease: a systematic review of prevalence and associated factors.
**Abstract:** People living with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) commonly modify their diet, but when restriction becomes persistent or rigid, it may reflect one of a spectrum of disordered eating behavior(s). To better understand restrictive eating behaviors in IBD, this systematic review synthesized how food avoidance, disordered eating behaviors and eating disorders are defined and measured, estimated their prevalence, and identified associated factors in adults with IBD. Six databases (CENTRAL, EMBASE, MEDLINE, APA PsycINFO, CINAHL and AMED) were searched from inception to January 2026. Eligible studies were full-text, quantitative studies assessing restrictive eating behaviors and/or associated factors in adults with IBD. Thirty-three studies were included. There was substantial heterogeneity in measurement and definition of restrictive eating in IBD. Food avoidance was commonly reported (36-97%), particularly when framed around symptom avoidance or disease flares. Prevalence of disordered eating behaviors ranged from 4.8-77%, with limited evidence on diagnosed eating disorders. Restrictive eating was more frequently reported in Crohn's disease and during periods of active disease. Psychological factors, including gastrointestinal-specific anxiety, general psychological distress and restrictive dietary beliefs, were consistently associated with restrictive behaviors, often independently of disease activity. Restrictive eating behaviors are commonly reported in adults with IBD. However, inconsistent terminology and reliance on non-validated or non-disease-specific measures limit interpretation and clinical application. Greater conceptual clarity and development of clinically meaningful assessment tools are needed to distinguish adaptive dietary self-management from more rigid, potentially harmful eating patterns and to inform appropriate dietetic and psychosocial support.
