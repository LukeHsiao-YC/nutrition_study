### 重點摘要
鑑於飲食發炎潛力與心血管疾病和癌症的關聯，且亞洲族群的前瞻性研究有限，本研究旨在前瞻性地探討亞洲飲食發炎模式分數 (EDIP-A) 與多族裔亞洲人群中風、心血管疾病、癌症及死亡風險的關聯。研究納入10,640名華人、馬來人和印度裔成人，透過食物頻率問卷計算EDIP-A分數，並與國家登記資料連結以追蹤健康結果。結果顯示，EDIP-A分數最高的族群患非致命性中風的風險顯著增加，尤其在吸菸者中更為明顯；然而，與非致命性心肌梗塞、心血管疾病死亡率、癌症發生率和癌症死亡率則無顯著關聯。本研究結論指出，具有較高促發炎潛力的飲食模式與較高的中風風險相關，特別是吸菸者，提示降低飲食的發炎潛力可能是預防心血管疾病的潛在策略。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了亞洲族群中，飲食發炎潛力與心血管疾病及癌症風險之間前瞻性研究的空白。然而，研究僅發現高促發炎飲食與中風風險增加有關，而未在非致命性心肌梗塞、心血管疾病死亡率、癌症發生率和癌症死亡率上觀察到顯著關聯，這限制了其在這些特定結果上的推論，並提示未來研究可針對這些未觀察到顯著關聯的結果進行更深入的探討，或探索其他潛在影響因素。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Prospective associations between empirical dietary inflammatory pattern score and cardiovascular diseases and cancer.
**Abstract:** Inflammation plays an important role in cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) and cancer development, and dietary factors can modulate inflammation. Several studies have shown an association between Empirical Dietary Inflammatory Pattern (EDIP) and CVDs and cancer, but there are limited prospective studies in Asian populations. We aimed to prospectively investigate the association between the inflammatory potential of the diet, as measured by an EDIP score adapted to an Asian population (EDIP-A) and the risks of CVDs, cancer, and mortality from CVDs and cancer in a large multi-ethnic Asian cohort. We used data from the Multi-Ethnic Cohort (n = 10,640), a prospective study comprising ethnic Chinese, Malay, and Indian adults aged 21-65 years, recruited between 2004 and 2010. The EDIP-A score was calculated using a validated food-frequency questionnaire. Outcomes including incidence of nonfatal stroke, nonfatal acute myocardial infarction (AMI), CVDs mortality, cancer incidence and cancer mortality were ascertained through data linkage with national registries. Cox proportional hazards models were used to evaluate the association between the EDIP-A score and these outcomes. During 887,505 person-years of follow-up, 435 nonfatal AMI cases, 317 nonfatal strokes, and 188 CVD deaths were documented. For cancer outcomes, 653 incident cases and 176 cancer deaths were recorded. Participants in the highest quintile of EDIP-A score had a higher risk of nonfatal stroke [hazard ratio (HR) = 1.64, 95 % CI 1.06, 2.55, P-trend = 0.01] than participants in the lowest quintile. These associations remained significant after further adjustment for body mass index. These associations were modified by smoking status (P-interaction <0.05). No significant associations were observed between EDIP-A score and risk of nonfatal AMI, CVD mortality, cancer incidence and cancer mortality. Dietary patterns with a higher proinflammatory potential were associated with higher stroke risk, particularly among smokers. These findings suggest that reducing the inflammatory potential of the diet may be a promising strategy for prevention of CVDs.
