### 重點摘要
儘管越南營養狀況有所改善，但在貧困地區的少數民族兒童中仍存在顯著差異，本研究旨在提供在地證據以制定公平的營養政策。此橫斷面研究調查了越南十個貧困地區共7747名0-71個月兒童及其母親的營養狀況。結果顯示，發育遲緩等營養不良問題普遍存在且盛行率高，並存在顯著的族群差異，其中苗族和拉格萊族兒童的風險顯著較高。本研究結論強調越南貧困地區兒童營養不良的普遍性與持續的族群、地理和生物學差異，亟需有針對性的公平營養干預措施。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究提供了越南貧困地區兒童營養不良的盛行率及其社會經濟與族群差異的在地化證據，填補了該區域缺乏此類詳細數據以制定公平營養政策的研究空白。然而，由於是橫斷面研究設計，無法建立因果關係，未來的研究可進一步探討介入措施的成效。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Socioeconomic and Ethnic Disparities in Malnutrition Among Children Under Six Years of Age in Vietnam's Poor Districts.
**Abstract:** Despite progress in improving nutritional status in Vietnam, persistent disparities among ethnic minority populations and children in poor districts remain, underscoring the need for context-specific evidence to inform equitable nutrition policies. This study assessed the prevalence and determinants of malnutrition among children under 6 years of age in poor districts of Vietnam. A cross-sectional study was conducted among 7747 children aged 0-71 months and their mothers from ten poor districts selected through multistage random sampling. Anthropometric measurements and nutritional status were classified following WHO protocols. Survey-adjusted logistic regression accounting for clustering by district and commune examined factors associated with stunting, underweight, wasting, and overweight. Nearly half of households were classified as poor (48.8%), and access to improved water and sanitation was limited. Ethnic minorities comprised 92.3% of participants, namely Hmong, Dao, Thai, and Ra Glai. The prevalence of stunting, underweight, wasting, and overweight was 40.1%, 21.4%, 5.7%, and 3.0%, respectively, with stunting exceeding high public-health-significance thresholds in all 10 districts. Substantial ethnic disparities were observed, with Hmong (aOR = 2.74) and Ra Glai (aOR = 2.11) children showing markedly higher odds of stunting than Kinh children, and Ra Glai children uniquely at higher risk of wasting (aOR = 3.27) and coexistence of stunting-wasting (aOR = 8.47). Fewer than four antenatal care visits, low birthweight (< 2500 g), male sex, and older age were independently associated with higher odds of stunting and/or underweight, while birthweight ≥ 3000 g was protective. Child undernutrition remains highly prevalent in Vietnam's poor districts and is marked by persistent ethnic, geographic, and biological disparities, underscoring the need for targeted, equity-focused nutrition interventions in this population.
