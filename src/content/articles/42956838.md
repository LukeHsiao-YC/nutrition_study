### 重點摘要
青少年飲食習慣的形成深受社會心理因素影響，但相關研究呈現概念、理論與方法上的碎片化。本範圍性回顧系統性分析了101項實證研究，發現社會規範、榜樣、同儕與家庭影響、社會支持、自我效能、態度及動機是青少年飲食習慣的關鍵決定因素，其影響方向取決於行為與規範的價值導向。研究指出現有證據多為橫斷面設計且理論基礎薄弱，因此提出一個整合性社會心理框架，旨在指導未來更具理論依據的研究設計。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本回顧統整了青少年飲食習慣相關的社會心理因素，填補了研究領域碎片化的空白。然而，它也指出了未來研究的限制與方向：現有研究多為橫斷面設計，缺乏縱向、實驗性或介入性研究；許多研究缺乏明確的理論基礎；此外，連結社會認同與飲食行為的機制，以及青少年發展差異所扮演的角色，是當前研究中被嚴重低估且需深入探討的重點。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Mapping Social-Psychological Factors Associated with Adolescents' Nutritional Habits - A Scoping Review.
**Abstract:** Adolescence is a formative period for the development of dietary habits, characterized by increasing autonomy in food choices and heightened susceptibility to social influences. Although numerous studies have examined social-psychological determinants of adolescent eating, the evidence remains conceptually, theoretically, and methodologically fragmented. This scoping review mapped social-psychological factors associated with adolescents' nutritional habits and examined the methodological and theoretical characteristics of this research. Following the Participant-Concept-Context framework, Web of Science, PsycINFO, PubMed, and Scopus were systematically searched for peer-reviewed qualitative and quantitative empirical studies published since 2000 involving adolescents aged 10-19 years. A total of 101 studies were included. Social norms, social modeling, peer and family influence, social support, self-efficacy, attitudes, and motivation emerged as prominent determinants of adolescent nutritional habits. Importantly, social influences were not inherently health-promoting or health-decreasing; their effects depended substantially on their behavioral and normative valence. The evidence base was dominated by cross-sectional research, with comparatively few longitudinal, experimental, or intervention studies. Analysis of the theoretical approaches used in the studies revealed that forty-one studies lacked an explicit theoretical foundation, while the extent to which theoretical frameworks informed hypotheses, study design, and interpretation varied considerably across the remaining studies. The mechanisms linking social identity (n = 3) to dietary behavior and the role of developmental differences within adolescence (n = 2) were comparatively underexamined. Based on these findings, we propose an integrative social-psychological framework distinguishing four interrelated levels of influence: individual processes, social identities, interpersonal social processes, and the broader social context, which may support the design of theoretically more informed studies targeting adolescent nutritional behavior.
