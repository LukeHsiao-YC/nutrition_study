### 重點摘要
這項橫斷面研究旨在評估純素者、蛋奶素者和雜食者之間微量元素狀態的潛在差異，因為相關研究尚缺乏。研究分析了38名純素者、37名蛋奶素者和40名雜食者尿液中的12種元素水平。經調整後，蛋奶素者和純素者的尿液砷水平顯著低於雜食者，但純素者較低的汞和較高的鎘水平在肌酸酐調整後未獲證實。總體而言，蛋奶素者和純素者體內的砷負擔較雜食者為低，本研究呼籲更多類似研究來驗證結果，並建議生物監測研究應考慮素食狀態。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了目前對純素者、蛋奶素者和雜食者之間微量元素狀態比較研究不足的空白。然而，研究結果在肌酸酐調整前後存在部分差異，尤其對於汞和鎘的發現，因此需要更多類似研究來驗證這些結果，以提供更確鑿的結論。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Urinary trace element levels in vegans compared to lacto-ovo-vegetarians and omnivores.
**Abstract:** Studies assessing potential differences in trace element status among vegans, lacto-ovo-vegetarians (LOV), and omnivores (OMN) are largely lacking. In this cross-sectional study, urinary levels of 12 elements were analysed in 38 vegans, 37 LOV, and 40 OMN. After adjustment for covariates, non-creatinine-adjusted arsenic levels were lower in LOV (-54%) and vegans (-54%) compared to OMN (p = 0.009). Compared to OMN, vegans (but not LOV) had lower mercury (-48%; p = 0.028) and higher cadmium levels (+42%; p = 0.023). Other elements (aluminium, cobalt, copper, lead, molybdenum, nickel, thallium, tin, and zinc) were similar between groups. Analyses normalised to urinary creatinine confirmed lower arsenic levels in LOV (-63%) and vegans (-59%; p<0.001), but suggested similar mercury and cadmium levels in OMN, LOV, and vegans, higher cobalt in LOV (+53%; p = 0.035), and higher copper in vegans (+17%; p = 0.040). Trends (non-significant after adjustment for multiple testing) were noted for nickel (LOV: +119%), cobalt (vegans: +43%), and zinc (vegans: -26%; compared to OMN). LOV and vegans had similar urinary levels (p ≥0.207). Urinary creatinine levels were similar across all groups (p ≥0.912). Eight additional elements were not detected (bismuth, gold, indium, iridium, palladium, silver) or were detected only in a few samples (platinum, zirconium). Overall, LOV and vegans exhibited a lower arsenic burden than OMN. A higher cadmium and lower mercury burden in vegans was not confirmed in creatinine-adjusted analyses. Similar studies are warranted to validate results. Biomonitoring studies may benefit from considering vegetarian status, particularly for arsenic exposure.
