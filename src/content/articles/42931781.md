### 重點摘要
野火是PM2.5日益增加的來源，但關於產前野火煙霧暴露與長期神經發展結果的證據仍有限。本研究是一項美國全國性Medicaid人口的隊列研究，分析了近90萬對母子數據，以評估孕期野火相關PM2.5暴露與兒童自閉症（ASD）及注意力不足過動症（ADHD）的風險。結果顯示，孕期野火PM2.5累積暴露量每增加一個IQR，與ASD風險增加30%相關，且中晚期孕程的關聯性最強，但與ADHD風險無顯著關聯。這些發現強調了野火煙霧對神經發展的潛在影響，並指出隨著野火活動加劇，孕期減少暴露策略的必要性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了產前野火煙霧暴露與兒童長期神經發展結果之間證據有限的空白，特別是提供了野火PM2.5暴露可能增加自閉症風險的具體實證，此前的研究對此類關聯的證據仍然不足。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Prenatal Exposure to Wildfire PM2.5 and Risk of ASD and ADHD in Children Enrolled in Medicaid.
**Abstract:** Wildfires are an increasing source of fine particulate matter (PM2.5), yet evidence on prenatal wildfire smoke exposure and long-term neurodevelopmental outcomes remains limited. To examine whether prenatal exposure to wildfire-related PM2.5 is associated with risk of autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) in children. This was a population-based cohort study of the US nationwide Medicaid population using Medicaid Analytic Extract (MAX) data from 2007 to 2014 with follow-up through childhood. Included were mother-child pairs with continuous enrollment and stable residential zip code during pregnancy. Weekly wildfire smoke-attributable PM2.5 at 1-km2 resolution, aggregated to the zip code level across pregnancy. Incident ASD and ADHD identified using validated claims-based algorithms. Associations were estimated using Cox proportional-hazards models with distributed lag nonlinear models. A total of 893 212 mother-child pairs (mother mean [SD] age, 24.7 [5.9] years) were included in this analysis. Children entered the cohort at birth (452 218 [50.6%] male). Mean (SD) observed follow-up was 6.3 (2.4) years for children diagnosed with ASD and 7.1 (1.9) years for children diagnosed with ADHD. Among 893 212 children, 8357 were diagnosed with ASD and 22 302 with ADHD. The cumulative incidence until age 8 years was 1.9% for ASD and 10.4% for ADHD. An IQR increase in cumulative wildfire PM2.5 exposure (0.75 µg/m3) during pregnancy was associated with increased risk of ASD (hazard ratio [HR], 1.30; 95% CI, 1.07-1.59) but not ADHD (HR, 1.03; 95% CI, 0.91-1.17). Associations for ASD were strongest during midpregnancy to late gestation compared with early gestation. Results were robust to sensitivity analyses. Results of this cohort study show that prenatal exposure to wildfire-related PM2.5 may be associated with increased risk of ASD but not ADHD. These findings highlight the potential neurodevelopmental impacts of wildfire smoke and underscore the need for strategies to reduce exposure during pregnancy as wildfire activity intensifies.
