### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討個人表型特徵（特別是飲食行為而非BMI）與食物加工程度及高適口性如何交互作用，影響人們對食物成癮性的感知。透過一項涵蓋1,664名美國成年人的橫斷面調查，參與者完成飲食失調問卷及食物成癮量表，並對食物項目依其加工和高適口性程度進行成癮性評分。結果顯示，飲食行為的表型差異與食物加工程度及高適口性存在顯著交互作用，影響對食物成癮性的評估。這表明對於有飲食失調或食物成癮症狀的人來說，超加工和高適口性食物被認為比最低限度加工或非高適口性食物更具成癮性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了先前文獻中，個人表型特徵（特別是飲食行為而非BMI）與食物加工程度及高適口性如何交互影響對食物成癮性感知的研究空白。它為理解超加工及高適口性食物在特定族群中被視為更具成癮性的框架提供了證據。然而，作為一項橫斷面研究，它無法建立因果關係，且其結果主要基於主觀感知。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Individual characteristics interacting with processing level and hyperpalatability are associated with food addictiveness: a cross-sectional study.
**Abstract:** Some people experience symptoms of food addiction and evidence indicates certain foods, particularly ultra-processed foods and those high in refined carbohydrates and fats, have addictive properties. The study explores whether specific phenotypic characteristics were associated with perceived food addictiveness ratings and whether food processing and hyperpalatability interact with these characteristics in their association with perception of addictiveness of foods. In a nationally representative cross-sectional survey (n=1,664), participants reported demographics, height, and weight; and completed the Eating Disorder Examination Questionnaire Short (EDE-QS) and Modified Yale Food Addiction Scale Questionnaire 2.0 (mYFAS 2.0). Participants rated the addictiveness of 99 randomly chosen food items from a set of 297 on a 100-point Visual Analog Scale, anchored by "Not at all" and "Very", from the question "How PROBLEMATIC is this food for you?", which referenced the Problematic foods in the mYFAS 2.0. The food items were categorized according to the Nova classification system and standardized Hyperpalatable foods (HPF) definitions. Individuals living in the U.S., over 18 years, and members of the research survey platform, Prolific were included in this 2024 study. The main outcome was rated perceived addictiveness of food items associated with participant phenotypic characteristics, food characteristics, and the interaction between the two. Linear mixed effects models were used to evaluate participant phenotypic characteristics associated with perceived addictiveness ratings of foods. Two interaction models for food classifications were fit, in which all phenotypic characteristics, the food classification system, and their interactions were included.
