### 重點摘要
丹麥國家健康調查(DNHS)雖使用權重，但參與者的健康狀況代表性仍是疑問。本研究旨在評估DNHS參與者在疾病盛行率（特別是精神疾病）和死亡率方面是否能代表丹麥總體人口。研究透過連結行政登記資料，比較了逾18萬名DNHS參與者與近470萬名一般人口，評估了202種健康狀況在參與前後的診斷盛行率和死亡率。結果顯示，加權後的參與者整體疾病盛行率與一般人口相似，但精神疾病在參與者中較不常見，且全因死亡率顯著較低，尤其是有精神疾病史者。這表明原始及更新的權重均未能完全解決自我選擇偏差問題，尤其是在死亡率方面，這對DNHS研究結果的普遍性產生影響。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇研究透過與行政登記資料的連結，量化了大型健康調查中自我選擇偏差對疾病盛行率和死亡率代表性的影響，特別是針對精神疾病這一常被低估的群體，並指出即使使用權重校正，「健康參與者效應」仍可能存在。未來的研究可以探索更精確的權重調整方法，或在數據收集階段採用更有效的策略來吸引不同健康狀況的參與者，以進一步提高調查的代表性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Representativeness of participants in the Danish National Health Survey in relation to mental disorders and a range of other medical conditions and mortality.
**Abstract:** Although the Danish National Health Survey (DNHS) uses survey weights to reduce self-selection bias, differences in health status may remain. We explored whether DNHS participants are representative of the general population in Denmark regarding morbidity, particularly mental disorders, and mortality. By individual linkage to administrative registers, we compared DNHS participants (N = 183,194) with the general population aged 16 years or older (N = 4,697,047). For 202 health conditions, we estimated period prevalence ratios (PPRs) of having received a diagnosis in the 5 years prior to survey participation and hazard ratios (HRs) of receiving a diagnosis in the subsequent 5 years. Additionally, we estimated mortality HRs, overall and among individuals with prior diagnoses of the specific conditions. Both original weights developed for the DNHS and updated weights better accounting for mental disorders were applied. Overall, weighted survey participants had similar prevalence compared to the general population, but mental disorders were less common before (PPR: 0.86 [0.85; 0.88]) and after (HR: 0.86 [0.84; 0.88]) survey participation. Participants experienced lower all-cause mortality rates than the general population (HR: 0.80 [0.79; 0.82]), especially among those with prior mental disorders (HR: 0.62 [0.59; 0.66]). Applying updated weights resulted in similar mental disorder prevalence (PPR: 0.99 [0.98; 1.01]) but unchanged mortality estimates. The lower mortality among survey participants compared to the general population, especially for individuals with prior mental disorders, suggests that self-selection into participation is not fully accounted for by the original or updated weights. This has implications for the generalisability of DNHS-based analyses.
