### 重點摘要
這項回溯性觀察研究利用2019年至2023年美國出生證明數據，分析了影響剖腹產後陰道生產（VBAC）的社會人口學、社會經濟和產科因素，並評估這些關聯在不同種族群體中是否存在差異。研究發現，高齡產婦、西班牙裔、擁有私人保險和較低教育程度通常與較低的VBAC機會相關，且分層分析揭示了不同種族群體內部的顯著差異和關聯逆轉。儘管VBAC率在研究期間略有增加，但利用上的不平等仍然存在，顯示VBAC這種有益的生產方式並未被公平利用。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了關於近年來（2019-2023）美國VBAC利用模式的知識空白，並詳細揭示了影響VBAC的因素在不同種族群體中的複雜差異，包括某些關聯方向的逆轉，提供了比過去研究更細緻的見解。然而，研究本身是觀察性的，未能探討造成這些差異的根本原因或提出具體的政策介入方案。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Factors Associated with Vaginal Birth After Cesarean (VBAC): An Analysis of 2019 to 2023 U.S. Birth Certificate Data.
**Abstract:** Vaginal birth after cesarean (VBAC) is a recommended delivery option for women with a history of one or two cesarean sections. Compared to repeat cesarean deliveries, VBAC is associated with better maternal and infant outcomes. Despite these benefits and national initiatives aimed at reducing unnecessary cesarean deliveries, VBAC rates remain low in the U.S. Research has shown that access to VBAC varies significantly by race, ethnicity, and socioeconomic status. This study aims to examine demographic, socioeconomic, and obstetric factors associated with VBAC in the U.S between 2019 and 2023 and assess whether these associations differ across race groups. A retrospective observational study was conducted using de-identified U.S. birth certificate data from the National Vital Statistics System (2019-2023). The study sample included 2,580,713 women with one or two prior cesarean deliveries, of whom 420,367 had a vaginal birth after cesarean. Sequential block logistic regression was performed for each year. Race-stratified analyses were conducted using the pooled 2019-2023 data to assess demographic, socioeconomic, and obstetric factors associated with VBAC. Increased maternal age, Hispanic ethnicity, private insurance, and lower educational attainment were generally associated with lower VBAC odds. Stratified analyses revealed significant within-group differences, including reversals in associations with maternal age and education across some race groups. VBAC rates modestly increased over the study period, though disparities remained. While VBAC is considered a beneficial delivery option, this study shows that it is not equitably utilized. These utilization gaps may point toward opportunities to increase national VBAC rates.
