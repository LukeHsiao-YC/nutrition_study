### 重點摘要
本研究為一項質性系統性回顧，旨在探討AI聊天機器人如何影響病患與醫療照護提供者之間的溝通，並依據醫學會談溝通生態模型進行分析。研究透過系統性檢索六個資料庫，納入了15篇符合資格的質性研究進行主題綜合。結果顯示，聊天機器人透過改變非人際（如支持以病患為中心的照護、資訊共享及隱私考量）與人際（如擴展資源、增強同理心認知及改變人際動力）情境，顯著影響溝通。本研究將聊天機器人定位為健康溝通系統中的「第三方」，並建議應提升病患的數位素養及開發共同設計的專用聊天機器人，以優化其應用效益。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究透過將AI聊天機器人定位為健康溝通系統中的「第三方」，並闡明其如何從病患與醫療照護提供者的視角影響非人際及人際溝通情境，從而擴展了醫學會談溝通生態模型(EMCME)的相關文獻，填補了對此機制理解的空白。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The influence of AI-driven chatbots on communication between patients and health care providers: A qualitative systematic review.
**Abstract:** The rapid advancement and widespread use of AI-driven chatbots are reframing communication between patients and health care providers (HCPs), but the mechanisms of such changes remain unclear. This systematic review draws on the Ecological Model of Communication in Medical Encounters (EMCME) and presents a qualitative synthesis of patients' and HCPs' perspectives on how chatbots influence their communication. This review was registered prospectively in PROSPERO (CRD420251070305). Following the PRISMA 2020 guidelines, six databases were systematically searched using terms related to chatbots, health care, and communication. Eligible studies were published in peer-reviewed journals from database inception to November 2025, reported empirical, qualitative findings, and focused on patients' and/or HCPs' perceptions of the impact of chatbots on their communication. Fifteen studies were included. Quality appraisal was conducted using the Critical Appraisal Skills Programme Qualitative Studies Checklist. Thematic synthesis was used for analysis. The findings demonstrate how chatbots impact the non-interpersonal and interpersonal contexts that frame patient-HCP communication. They change the non-interpersonal contexts by supporting patient-centred communication and care, introducing new opportunities and challenges for mediated information sharing, and changing considerations of health-related privacy and liability. The proliferation of chatbots reframes the interpersonal context through expanding linguistic and informational resources, enhancing awareness of empathy and relational needs, and changing interpersonal dynamics. This review expands the EMCME literature and positions chatbots as a 'third party' in broad health communication systems. It highlights the need for access to quality health information. The changed interpersonal dynamics can intensify relational work during patient-HCP communication. Policymakers, hospitals, HCPs, and patients need to recognise chatbots as a 'third party' in health communication and position them as additional tools alongside HCP care. Potential paths towards more effective use of chatbots involve improving patients' digital literacy through training and developing specialised chatbots co-designed by key stakeholders.
