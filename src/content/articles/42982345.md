### 重點摘要
這項系統性回顧與統合分析旨在探討自我報告飲食評估與24小時尿液收集估算蛋白質、鈉、鉀攝取量時，轉換因子如何影響兩者間的差異。研究整合了176篇比較文獻與41篇營養平衡研究，發現轉換因子顯著影響了報告偏差的解釋方向和幅度。結果顯示，鈉攝取量在自我報告中普遍被低估，而蛋白質和鉀的報告偏差則因轉換因子選擇而異，導致不同結論。本研究強調需要開發更準確且普遍適用的轉換公式，以改進自我報告飲食數據的評估準確性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻透過量化不同轉換因子對自我報告與24小時尿液收集數據差異的影響，明確指出轉換因子的選擇會導致對飲食報告偏差判斷的差異性結論，從而填補了過往研究中因使用異質性轉換因子而造成的結論不一致問題。未來的研究需要開發更準確且普遍適用的轉換公式，並探索應用統一參考方程作為協調比較策略的可行性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Influence of conversion factors on 24-h urine-derived estimates compared with self-reported protein, sodium, or potassium intake in adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis.
**Abstract:** Accurate dietary assessment is essential for quantifying absolute nutrient intake and diet-disease relationships. However, self-reports often lack precision, and their validity is commonly evaluated against recovery biomarkers derived from 24-h urinary collection (UC). However, biomarker-based intake estimates depend on the applied urinary conversion factors, which may influence conclusions about misreporting. We aimed to quantify the mean differences in protein, sodium, or potassium intake between self-reported dietary assessment methods and 24-h urinary excretion, stratified by assessment method and conversion factor, and to derive exploratory conversion factors from nitrogen, sodium, and potassium balance studies. Studies in adults reporting nutrient intakes via self-reports and 24-h UC were identified via PubMed and Web of Science (inception to March 31, 2025). Nutrient balance studies were identified via previous reviews and manual searches. Pooled mean differences between self-reported values and biomarker-based estimates, and excretion rates from 24-h UC, were calculated using random-effects meta-analysis. We systematically reviewed 176 articles comparing self-reported intake and 24-h UC estimate values and reviewed 41 nutrient balance studies. Using original study-specific conversion factors in general population samples, protein misreporting was small (-5.6% to +2.3%), sodium was consistently underestimated (-35.1% to -14.8%), and potassium tended to be overestimated (+12.8% for DRs). Misreporting interpretations depended on conversion factors, particularly for protein and potassium, with direction and magnitude shifting across factors. Nutrient balance studies yielded pooled urinary excretion percentages of 78.8% (nitrogen), 87.7% (sodium), and 80.8% (potassium). Applying exploratory conversion factors to general-population samples, mean relative differences were approximately -10% for protein, -25 to -40% for sodium, and near zero for potassium. Urinary conversion factors substantially influence 24-h UC-based estimate values, complicating the interpretation of the accuracy of self-reported dietary data. More accurate and generalizable conversion equations are needed to improve the evaluation of nutrient-specific differences in the accuracy of self-reported dietary assessment methods. This trial was registered at PROSPERO as CRD420251022999 (https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/PROSPERO/view/CRD420251022999). Statement of Significance: This study extends previous evidence by demonstrating that the magnitude of disagreement between self-reported dietary assessment methods and 24-h urinary collection-based estimates varies according to the applied conversion factors. These findings suggest that the use of heterogeneous conversion factors may contribute to inconsistent conclusions across studies and indicate that the application of a common reference equation warrants further investigation as a potential strategy for harmonizing comparisons.
