### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討年輕人數位科技問題使用與身體質量指數（BMI）及地中海生活方式（MEDLIFE）之間的關聯，並探究美味食物飲食動機在其中扮演的中介角色。這項橫斷面研究分析了610名年輕成人（平均年齡21.6歲）的數據。研究結果顯示，較高的數位成癮與較高的BMI和較低的地中海生活方式顯著相關，且這些關聯部分由美味食物飲食動機所中介。值得注意的是，美味食物飲食動機對數位成癮與BMI關聯的中介作用，比其對數位成癮與MEDLIFE關聯的中介作用更為顯著。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了數位科技問題使用與不健康生活習慣（如BMI升高和地中海生活方式降低）之間潛在機制不清楚的研究空白，發現美味食物飲食動機在其中扮演了部分中介角色。然而，由於本研究為橫斷面設計，未能確立因果關係，未來的研究應採用縱向設計或實驗設計來進一步驗證。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The mediating role of palatable eating motives in the associations of problematic digital technology use with body mass index and Mediterranean lifestyle in young adults.
**Abstract:** The problematic use of digital technology is closely related to a sedentary lifestyle and unhealthy eating habits; however, the underlying mechanisms of these associations are unclear. It is believed that problematic use of digital technology may increase motives for eating palatable foods through changes in the reward system and exposure to food cues in digital environments, and that this may be associated with unhealthy lifestyle behaviors. The aim of this study was to investigate the mediating role of palatable eating motives in the relationships between problematic digital technology use, body mass index (BMI), and Mediterranean lifestyle (MEDLIFE) among young adults. This cross-sectional study analyzed data obtained from 610 young adults. The data collection tools included Descriptive Information Form, Digital Addiction Scale (DAS), Palatable Eating Motives Scale (PEMS), BMI and MEDLIFE index. The mean age of participants was 21.6 ± 1.7 years. In the mediation model between DAS and BMI, the total (β:0.215, 95%CI:0.586; 1.237), direct (β:0.167, 95%CI:0.365; 1.051) and indirect effects of PEMS (β:0.048, 95%CI:0.074; 0.346) were all statistically significant. In the mediation model between DAS and MEDLIFE index, the total (β:-0.456, 95%CI:-2.212;-1.603), direct (β:-0.424, 95%CI:-2.101;-1.451) and indirect effects of PEMS (β:-0.032, 95%CI:-0.251;-0.024) were all statistically significant. Findings suggest that higher digital addiction is associated with higher BMI and lower MEDLIFE, and these relationships are partially mediated by palatable eating motivations. Notably, the mediating effect was more pronounced in the association with BMI, whereas it was comparatively limited in the association with MEDLIFE.
