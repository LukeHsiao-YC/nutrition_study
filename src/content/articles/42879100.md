### 重點摘要
美國產假政策的碎片化導致取得受保護職位及帶薪假期的不一致性，進而影響母嬰健康、經濟穩定和性別平等。本研究對100個標記為#maternityleave的TikTok影片進行橫斷面內容分析，以探討產假內容的呈現方式及其與觀眾參與度的關聯。結果顯示這些影片獲得了大量互動，未經調整分析發現提及特定政策的影片獲得更多收藏，而嬰兒相關內容則有較多讚的趨勢。然而，所有觀察到的關聯性在校正多重比較後均不顯著。研究結論認為TikTok是討論產假經驗和政策的平台，儘管特定內容特徵與參與度的關聯僅呈現探索性模式。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了社群媒體上產假內容與觀眾互動模式的探索性研究空白，指出TikTok作為一個非正式政策對話空間。然而，其限制在於樣本規模有限且為便利性抽樣，且平台具動態性。未來的研究應進一步探討觀眾對此類內容的參與度是否與其產假知識、態度或推動國家產假政策的公民行動相關。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Maternity-Leave Related TikTok Videos: A Cross-Sectional Analysis of Content and Viewer Engagement.
**Abstract:** Maternity leave policy in the United States is fragmented, leading to inconsistent access to job protection and paid leave. The lack of a universal policy and variation across states contributes to inequities in maternal health, economic stability, and gender equity. This study describes how maternity leave is portrayed on TikTok and examines which content features are associated with viewer engagement as a part of informal policy dialogue. A cross-sectional content analysis was conducted on 100 TikTok videos tagged with #maternityleave. Videos were coded for thematic content (e.g., humor, policy references, music, presence of infants) and engagement metrics (views, likes, comments, favorites). Descriptive statistics summarized content features and engagement metrics. Independent two-tailed Welch t-tests (α = 0.05) compared views, likes, favorites, and comments between videos with and without each of the four most common content features. False Discovery Rate (FDR) adjustments were applied to account for multiple comparisons, and Cohen's d was used to estimate effect size. The 100 videos generated substantial engagement (41.3 million views, 3.14 million likes, 187,145 favorites, and 114,574 comments), and most featured only women (88%). Videos referencing a specific policy and videos without music received more favorites, while videos featuring an infant showed a trend toward more likes. Humor was not associated with engagement. None of the observed associations remained significant after false discovery rate correction. Maternity leave content on TikTok attracted substantial viewer engagement, and several content features demonstrated exploratory patterns of association with engagement. Policy-referencing videos received more favorites in the unadjusted analysis, while infant-related content showed a non-significant trend toward greater likes. The results suggest that TikTok is one space where maternity leave experiences and policies are discussed. Given the modest convenience sample and the platform's dynamic nature, future research should examine whether engagement with such content relates to viewers' maternity leave knowledge, attitudes, or civic action for national maternity leave policy.
