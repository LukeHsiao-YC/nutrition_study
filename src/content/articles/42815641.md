### 重點摘要
多部門營養（MSN）方法在低收入和中等收入國家日益受到重視，而衡量多種干預措施在個人和家庭層面的「共同覆蓋率」對於追蹤MSN進展至關重要。本回顧分析了73項研究，識別出8種不同的共同覆蓋率衡量方法，主要來自低收入和中等收入國家，並以產前照護、兒童免疫和熟練助產服務為常見指標。研究發現，儘管部分研究會驗證其指標，但共同覆蓋率的衡量方法仍存在顯著差異。結論強調，為實現多部門營養進展的一致性監測，亟需加強這些方法的協調與驗證。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本回顧填補了對全球健康與營養干預措施共同覆蓋率分析方法缺乏系統性盤點的空白，揭示了現有方法的多樣性與應用現況。然而，研究限制在於其指出當前共同覆蓋率的衡量方法缺乏一致性且驗證不足，尤其在家庭層面多部門干預措施的衡量證據有限，這表明未來研究需專注於開發更標準化、經協調且經過嚴格驗證的測量工具。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Measuring Co-Coverage in Global Health and Nutrition Interventions: A Scoping Review of Analytical Approaches.
**Abstract:** Multi-sectoral nutrition (MSN) approaches coordinate interventions across sectors such as health, agriculture, education, and social protection to improve nutrition outcomes, and have gained prominence in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). Measuring co-coverage - the clustering of multiple interventions at the individual and household level - remains uncommon yet offers a viable method for tracking MSN intervention coverage. This scoping review examined existing analytic approaches and applications for assessing co-coverage of health and nutrition interventions. We searched Web of Science Core Collection, PubMed, and EBSCOhost for studies that used, described, or developed co-coverage methods to measure nutrition- and health-related intervention coverage. Titles and abstracts were screened by two independent reviewers using Covidence, followed by full-text screening based on pre-defined criteria. Screening 4,478 abstracts yielded 73 eligible studies applying 8 distinct methods to quantify co-coverage across health and nutrition interventions. Most studies were observational (90%) and conducted in LMICs, particularly Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia, with publications increasing over the past decade. Data were primarily drawn from surveys (93%), particularly Demographic and Health Surveys (41 studies). Commonly included individual-level interventions in co-coverage indices were antenatal care (ANC, 66 studies) or ANC components, child immunizations (48 studies), and skilled birth attendance (42 studies). Evidence on measuring a range of multisectoral interventions at the household level was limited. Intervention selection was often guided by established frameworks or guidelines. Coverage indices were calculated using simple counts, weighted averages, and scoring systems. While 18 studies validated their indices through methods such as Cohen's kappa and sensitivity analyses, most relied on previously used approaches without specific validation. These findings underscore the considerable variability in co-coverage methodologies and the need for greater harmonization and validation to enable consistent monitoring of multisectoral nutrition progress.
