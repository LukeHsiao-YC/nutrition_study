### 重點摘要
麩質過敏症（CD）與第一型糖尿病（T1DM）可共存。本回溯性研究旨在比較同時患有兩種疾病與僅患CD兒童的抗組織轉穀胺酶抗體（anti-tTG IgA）正常化速率。結果顯示，在確診CD後兩年，CD合併T1DM組僅有56%患者的anti-tTG IgA正常化，而單純CD組則有93%正常化（p < 0.01），證實前者抗體正常化時間較長。因此，研究建議在CD確診後的最初幾年，應對同時患有CD和T1DM的兒童進行更密切的監測。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究揭示了同時患有麩質過敏症和第一型糖尿病的兒童，其抗組織轉穀胺酶抗體正常化速度較慢的臨床現象，為這類共病患者的臨床管理提供了重要指導。儘管本研究填補了特定族群抗體正常化時間的知識空白，但作為一項回溯性研究，且研究樣本量相對較小，未來仍需更大規模的前瞻性研究來探討其潛在機制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Delayed normalization of tissue transglutaminase antibodies in children with celiac disease and type 1 diabetes mellitus.
**Abstract:** Diabetes mellitus type 1 (T1DM1) and celiac disease (CD) can coexist in a patient. The aim was to evaluate the characteristics of children with both CD and T1DM and children with CD alone and to compare normalization rates of tissue transglutaminase immunoglobulin A class antibodies (anti-tTG IgA) normalization. This retrospective longitudinal study included children with both T1DM and CD and children with CD alone found by family screening, followed up from 2013 to 2023. Anthropometric and serologic parameters were evaluated at the time of diagnosis and during follow-up. Included were 18 patients in T1DM + CD group (39% girls, median age 8.9 years) and 27 patients in CD group (52% girls, median age 8.8 years). There were no statistically significant differences in gender, age, anthropometry parameters, frequency of patients with initial anti-tTG levels >10 times of upper limit of normal (78% vs. 63%, p > 0.05), frequency of follow-up visits (1.06 visits/year of follow-up vs. 0.76, p > 0.05) and follow-up period (4.3 vs. 3.89 years, p > 0.05). The only statistically significant difference was in timing of anti-tTG normalization. At 2 years after diagnosis anti-tTG normalized in 56% of patients in T1DM1 + CD versus 93% in CD alone group (p < 0.01). If only patients with high antibody titers were analyzed, the normalization was still slower in CD + T1DM group (p < 0.05). It takes longer to normalize anti-tTG in children with coexisting T1DM and CD than in CD alone found by family screening. We suggest monitoring these patients more closely during the first years after the diagnosis of CD.
