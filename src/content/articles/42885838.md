### 重點摘要
本研究調查了2021-2022年間，於澳洲COVID-19機構式隔離期間，嬰幼兒餵養所面臨的挑戰。透過對216位與0-36個月兒童一同隔離的父母進行線上問卷調查，發現父母在母乳餵養、配方奶餵養及母乳餵養中普遍面臨困境，包括壓力影響乳汁供應、資源匱乏及衛生條件不佳等。研究顯示，隔離環境常危及兒童健康，並損害父母遵循國家嬰兒餵養指南的能力。此結果強調了在緊急規劃和臨時住所標準中納入嬰兒餵養支持需求的重要性，並對自然災害、家庭暴力、無家可歸或監獄等其他封閉環境具有啟示。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了在澳洲COVID-19檢疫等封閉、臨時住所環境中，關於嬰幼兒餵養支持與挑戰的現有研究空白，揭示了在緊急情況下兒童健康和家庭支持規劃的不足。它特別突顯了現有應急規劃對兒童照護需求的忽視，並為未來針對不同類型臨時住所（如自然災害、無家可歸或拘留設施）的嬰兒餵養支援提供了關鍵考量。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Breastfeeding, Breastmilk Feeding and Formula Feeding Within the Closed, Temporary Accommodation of Australia's COVID-19 Quarantine.
**Abstract:** During 2021-2022, families with infants and young children were among the > 450,000 people who spent 2 weeks in Australia's institutional COVID-19 quarantine. Given pre-existing lack of emergency planning for children, there were concerns about whether parents would be adequately supported in this temporary accommodation. This paper reports on research considering the breastfeeding, breastmilk feeding and formula feeding of infants and young children in Australia's COVID-19 quarantine. Two-hundred and sixteen parents who quarantined with children aged 0-36 months completed an online survey with closed- and open-ended questions. One hundred and twenty-seven children were being fed breastmilk (103) and/or infant formula (40) when entering quarantine. Breastfeeding, formula feeding and breastmilk feeding challenges were reported. Breastfeeding mothers were concerned about frequent feeding and stress reducing milk supply. Parents who were formula feeding commonly lacked appropriate resources with half having to wash feeding bottles in bathrooms that also contained a toilet. Mothers who were breastmilk feeding experienced the challenges associated with both breastfeeding and formula feeding. On-site staff and health providers did not always respond to requests for help with appropriate assistance, and parents were rarely referred to health or parenting helplines. Parental capacity to follow the national infant feeding guidelines was commonly compromised by the quarantine environment, and child health was placed at risk. This study has implications for infant feeding resourcing and support in other types of temporary accommodation, including those related to natural disasters, family violence, or homelessness or any closed environment, such as prisons or immigration detention. Infant feeding support requirements should be included in emergency planning and standards developed for temporary accommodation for families.
