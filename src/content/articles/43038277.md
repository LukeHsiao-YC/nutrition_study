### 重點摘要
嬰兒配方奶粉中的玉米糖漿固形物（CSSs）對兒童長期健康影響的探討至關重要。本系統性回顧透過檢索多個資料庫，分析了5篇觀察性研究和2篇介入性研究，發現含CSSs配方奶粉的攝取可能與早期兒童肥胖風險增加、飲食行為改變、腸道菌群變化及急性代謝反應差異相關。研究結果提示嬰兒期攝取CSSs可能對兒童健康產生潛在影響。然而，本回顧強調未來需更多設計良好的隨機對照試驗來進一步釐清這些關聯。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本系統性回顧整合了現有證據，指出了嬰兒配方奶粉中玉米糖漿固形物與多種兒童健康結果的潛在關聯。然而，研究的限制在於缺乏高品質的隨機對照試驗、暴露數據不夠精確，且排除了非英文研究。未來的研究需要設計更完善的隨機對照試驗來進一步確認這些連結。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Corn Syrup Solids in Infant Formula and Child Health Outcomes: A Systematic Review.
**Abstract:** The potential impact of corn syrup solids (CSSs) in infant formula on long-term child health outcomes is a critical area of investigation, informing nutritional guidelines and parental choices. This systematic review aimed to investigate the effects of CSSs in infant formula on various child health outcomes. Following PRISMA guidelines, our PROSPERO-registered protocol (CRD420261306977) guided a comprehensive search of PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library, completed on January 23, 2026. From 150 identified studies, titles and abstracts were screened, followed by full-text review. Data from eligible studies were extracted independently in duplicate, and study quality and risk of bias were assessed. We extracted data from 5 observational and 2 controlled interventional studies. Two studies reported a higher early childhood obesity risk in children fed CSS-containing formula. Other findings included increased food fussiness, decreased food enjoyment, frequent sweet intake, and gut microbiota changes (depleted Bifidobacteriaceae, enriched Lachnospiraceae). Short-term postprandial differences in plasma metabolites and gastric acidity were also noted. Four studies had low risk of bias, 3 had moderate risk. This systematic review suggests that CSS-containing formula intake during infancy may be associated with altered eating behaviors, gut microbiota changes, acute metabolic responses, and childhood obesity. Future well-designed randomized controlled trials (RCTs) are needed to elucidate these connections. Limitations include a scarcity of robust RCTs, imprecise exposure data, and exclusion of non-English studies. PROSPERO registration No. [CRD420261306977].
