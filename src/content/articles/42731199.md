### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討癌症照護領域中醫護人員對「病人參與」的不同理解方式，採用質性現象圖譜法，對瑞典三家醫院的16位外科及泌尿科癌症照護醫師和護理師進行半結構式訪談。研究識別出五種對病人參與的理解類別，從狹隘地將其視為醫療許可的條件，逐步發展到廣泛地鼓勵病人主動管理自身狀況。這些發現突顯醫護人員對病人參與存在廣泛差異的理解，要充分實現病人參與需要組織層面的教育、實施、討論及後續追蹤，並需培養優先考慮病人參與的醫療文化。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究透過現象圖譜法，具體描述了癌症照護中醫護人員對病人參與的多元理解方式，填補了過去對此概念缺乏細緻入微認知的空白。未來的研究或實踐需進一步探討如何將這些知識轉化為有效的教育訓練與組織文化變革，以彌合政策與實踐之間的鴻溝。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Different ways of understanding patient participation in cancer care: A phenomenographic study.
**Abstract:** To describe different ways of understanding patient participation among healthcare professionals in cancer care. The study employed a qualitative design using a phenomenographic method. Phenomenography aims to describe the qualitatively different ways that a group of people make sense of, experience, and understand a phenomenon in the surrounding world. This study focuses on the phenomenon of patient participation, as perceived by healthcare professionals in cancer care. Sixteen physicians and registered nurses in surgical and urological cancer care, strategically sampled from three Swedish hospitals, were interviewed using a semi-structured interview guide. Five descriptive categories (A-E) of different ways of understanding patient participation were identified: Viewing patient participation as conditional upon healthcare permission (A), Providing information about treatment, with decisions primarily being made by healthcare professionals (B), Reaching joint decisions through attentive dialogue and comprehensible information (C), Enabling the patient to be active and take responsibility through collaboration and engagement (D), to Holistically seeing the patient and, in collaboration with the team, encouraging the person in managing their situation (E). The internal relations between the categories show a gradual shift in understandings from a narrower to a broader and more complex view of the concept. This study highlights widely different understandings of patient participation among healthcare professionals in cancer care. Fully achieving patient participation requires education, implementation, and repeated discussion and follow-up in the organization. Bridging the gap between policy and practice requires that healthcare systems foster a culture that prioritizes patient participation, from the organizational level to individual healthcare professionals. Knowledge of the different views of patient participation can be used in education and professional competence development and transferred into different ways of working toward the overall aim of enhancing patient participation in cancer care.
