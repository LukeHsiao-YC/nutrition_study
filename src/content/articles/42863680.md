### 重點摘要
這項研究探討自然影像暴露對健康食物選擇的影響，以及感知復原性在其中的中介作用。一項對925名成人進行的線上實驗發現，與都市影像相比，觀看自然影像顯著增加了健康食物的選擇機率。此效應透過提升參與者的感知復原性來解釋，但對蔬果渴望度則無顯著影響。研究結論指出，自然影像暴露可促進健康飲食選擇，且此效果可歸因於增強的感知復原性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究成功揭示了自然影像暴露透過增強感知復原性來促進健康飲食選擇的潛在機制，填補了此前研究中對此機制理解的空白。然而，研究未能發現自然影像暴露對蔬果渴望度的顯著影響，這表明其促進健康飲食的效果可能存在特定性，或需更長時間、更沉浸的自然體驗才能產生更廣泛的飲食偏好改變。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** A sight for restored minds: examining the impact of nature image exposure on perceived restorativeness and healthy food choices.
**Abstract:** Nature exposure (including through images) offers physical and psychological benefits, including cognitive restoration. Growing evidence suggests it may also promote healthy food choices, though the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. This pre-registered study examined whether perceived restorativeness (the perceived capacity of an environment for cognitive restoration) mediates the effect of nature image exposure on healthy food choices. We also examined effects on fruit and vegetable (FV) desirability as an alternative outcome measure. 925 adults (M age = 39.46 y, SD = 15.67, 54.3% female) participated in an online between-subjects experiment and were exposed to a natural or urban image for 30 seconds. They then completed a food choice task - selecting a main course, side dish and drink (each from four options; two healthy, two unhealthy), followed by perceived restorativeness and fruit and vegetable desirability measures. There was a significant main effect of healthiness on the likelihood of choice (OR = 1.77, p < .001) and a significant healthiness × image exposure interaction (OR = 1.40, p < .001), indicating that healthy food choices were more likely following nature (vs. urban) image exposure. Additionally, nature (vs. urban) image exposure had a significant indirect effect on healthy food choices through perceived restorativeness (ACME = 0.04, p = .022). However, nature (vs. urban) image exposure did not significantly affect fruit and vegetable desirability (p = .087, d = 0.11). These findings suggest that nature image exposure can promote healthy food choices (but not fruit and vegetable desirability), an effect explained by enhanced perceived restorativeness.
