### 重點摘要
癌症復發恐懼 (FCR) 是婦科癌症患者普遍但常未被討論的問題，臨床醫師缺乏相關對話指引。本研究透過分析 30 份婦科腫瘤門診錄音，旨在識別啟動 FCR 對話的溝通實踐。結果發現，僅有 6 份諮詢中討論到 FCR，且都由同一位醫師進行。研究識別出兩種常用的溝通方式：「普遍性陳述」將 FCR 對話視為例行性，以及「一般性陳述」將 FCR 正常化並邀請患者分享。這兩種簡單的策略為臨床醫師提供了在門診中主動討論 FCR 並提供支持的實用方法。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了臨床上缺乏具體指引，以協助醫師開啟癌症復發恐懼相關對話的研究空白。未來的研究限制包括需要探討這些溝通實踐在更多臨床醫師和不同患者群體中的應用，並評估 FCR 對話對患者及家庭的實際影響。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Practices for initiating a conversation about fear of cancer recurrence after cancer treatment: A conversation analytic study of gynae-oncology outpatient consultations.
**Abstract:** Fear of cancer recurrence (FCR) is a top-rated concern and unmet need for support among gynaecological cancer patients yet seldom discussed during healthcare consultations. Clinicians report several challenges in initiating conversations about FCR with patients and limited practical guidance is available. This study aimed to identify communication practices for initiating conversations about FCR during outpatient consultations after treatment for gynaecological cancer. A collection of 30 audio-recorded outpatient consultations, involving 4 gynae-oncology clinicians, 30 people who had completed treatment for endometrial or ovarian cancer, and 11 accompanying family members and friends, were reviewed to identify instances where FCR was discussed. These instances were analysed using conversation analysis. Six of the 30 consultations, all involving the same gynae-oncology clinician, included talk about FCR. Two recurrent communication practices were used to initiate these conversations: (1) Universal claims (e.g., "I always ask…") framed talk about FCR as a routine part of post-treatment follow-up for all gynaecological cancer patients, and (2) General claims (e.g., "Some people find that…") normalised FCR post-treatment and provided opportunity for the patient to indicate if FCR was relevant to them and to describe their experience. Both practices provided avenues for the gynae-oncology clinician to discuss available support for FCR. FCR was not regularly discussed in this collection of recordings, reflecting previous reports of clinical practice. Nonetheless, direct observation and detailed analysis of real-word consultations where FCR was discussed identified two simple strategies that clinicians can use to initiate these conversations. First, universal claims can shift the conversation to FCR specifically and frame this talk as routine. Second, general claims normalise FCR and invite patients to share their experiences. Future research could investigate use of these practices across multiple clinicians and patient groups and examine the impact of FCR conversations for patients and families.
