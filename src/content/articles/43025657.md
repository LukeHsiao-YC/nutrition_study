### 重點摘要
這項研究旨在探討美國父母在嬰幼兒補充餵食階段提供肉類給孩子的動機，因為目前對此知之甚少。研究透過對46位父母進行虛擬半結構式訪談，並運用5N框架進行演繹式主題分析。結果顯示，父母普遍認為給孩子吃肉是正常、必要且愉快的，動機包括營養、感官、文化和家庭因素，但他們對肉類提供的特定微量營養素認識有限。研究結論指出，提供肉類的決定受營養、文化、感官和人際關係等多重因素影響，理解這些動機有助於提供更適切的營養教育和指導。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了過去對於美國父母在嬰幼兒補充餵食階段提供肉類的深層動機理解不足的空白，特別是揭示了這些決策不僅基於營養考量，還受到文化、感官和人際關係等多元因素的影響。此外，研究結果也暗示了父母對肉類所含特定微量營養素的認識有限，這為未來發展更具回應性的營養教育和指導提供了方向。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Beyond Nutrition: A Qualitative Study of Beliefs and Attitudes Influencing Parents' Decisions about Introducing Meat to their Children.
**Abstract:** Although meat plays a prominent role in many dietary patterns in the United States, relatively little is known about the motivations underlying parents' decisions to offer meat during infancy and toddlerhood. Using the 5N framework as a conceptual lens (natural, normal, necessary, nice, and neutralization), we conducted a deductive thematic analysis of virtual semi-structured interviews with 46 parents in the United States to explore motivations for meat provision during the complementary feeding period. Parents commonly viewed meat as normal, necessary, and nice to feed to their children and cited nutrition, hedonic, cultural, and family-related reasons for doing so. While parents generally described younger infants as responding positively to meat, perceptions of older children's acceptance were more variable. Many parents also demonstrated limited awareness of the specific micronutrients provided by meat and their role during complementary feeding. These findings suggest that decisions regarding meat provision are shaped by a combination of nutritional, cultural, sensory, and relational considerations. More broadly, the findings highlight the value of understanding parents' motivations for food provision in order to support responsive nutrition education and guidance during complementary feeding.
