### 重點摘要
這項研究旨在描述烏干達結直腸癌（CRC）的流行病學，因其數據不足且發病率有上升趨勢。研究分析了坎帕拉和古盧兩個主要癌症登記處的數據，發現共1118例CRC病例，平均診斷年齡為54.3歲，其中近四成在50歲前被診斷。結果顯示，烏干達的CRC發病率總體仍低，但在坎帕拉地區男性中呈現顯著上升趨勢，且生存預後極差，突顯了加強預防、早期發現和治療服務的迫切需求。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了烏干達結直腸癌流行病學數據不足的空白，首次提供了基於人口的發病率和生存趨勢分析。它明確指出烏干達CRC發病率雖低但呈上升趨勢，特別是在男性中，並揭示了早期發病的嚴重負擔和預後不良的現狀。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Colorectal cancer incidence and survival trends in Uganda: a population-based registry analysis (2000-20).
**Abstract:** The epidemiology of colorectal cancer (CRC) in Uganda is not sufficiently described, with the limited available data suggesting an increasing incidence. This study assessed CRC incidence and survival trends by using data from the two major population-based cancer registries in Uganda. We extracted incident CRC cases (ICD C18-C20) from the Kampala Cancer Registry (KCR; 2000-19) and the Gulu Cancer Registry (GCR; 2015-20). Crude and age-standardized incidence rates (ASIRs) and 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival estimates were calculated by using SEER*Stat software. Kaplan-Meier methods were applied to generate survival curves, with subgroup differences assessed by using log-rank tests. Joinpoint regression was used to evaluate trends in the incidence rates. A total of 1118 CRC cases were identified. The mean age at diagnosis was 54.3 years (16.2 SD), with 37.8% diagnosed before age 50 years. The incidence was higher in the KCR compared with the GCR (crude: 2.4 vs. 0.8; ASIR: 9.2 vs. 2.0 per 100 000). A significant rise in ASIRs occurred exclusively among males in the KCR, with a 2.1% annual increase over 20 years (P < .001). In a sensitivity analysis restricted to histologically confirmed cases, the 1-, 3-, and 5-year relative survival in the KCR was 52.7%, 14.5%, and 4.3%, respectively. CRC incidence in Uganda remains low but is increasing among males. Four in 10 cases are diagnosed before age 50 years, suggesting a substantial burden of early-onset disease. Survival outcomes remain poor, stressing the need for strengthened prevention, early detection, and treatment services.
