### 重點摘要
「Connect2Thrive」是一項針對青少年飲食與身體活動行為的虛擬角色介入試驗性可行性研究，旨在評估其可接受性與可行性。這項為期四週的混合方法研究招募了43對青少年與家長，青少年透過虛擬角色進行視訊聊天與文字訊息交流。結果顯示，絕大多數青少年（96%）喜歡該計畫，且大部分家長（81%）認為其對孩子的健康行為有正面影響，同時研究協議完成率亦高。研究結論指出，虛擬角色介入是促進青少年健康行為的可行且可接受的方式。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了虛擬角色介入在促進青少年健康行為方面，缺乏初期可行性與可接受性評估的空白。然而，其限制在於並未嚴謹地評估此方法對實際行為改變和健康結果的影響，這將是未來研究需深入探討的方向。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Connect2Thrive: A Mixed Methods Pilot Feasibility Study of a Virtual Character Intervention to Improve Diet and Physical Activity Behaviors in Youth.
**Abstract:** Examine the acceptability and feasibility of Connect2Thrive, an interactive virtual character approach designed to promote a healthy diet and physical activity among youth. A mixed-methods, 4-week, single-arm, pre-post quasi-experimental study was conducted with youth-parent dyads (n = 43). Youth (ages 8-14) selected their virtual character, completed 8 video chats (2 per week), and communicated with the character about diet and physical activity via text messages. Feasibility, acceptability, and engagement data were collected from a study server, baseline and follow-up surveys of youth and parents, and follow-up youth interviews. Most youth loved or liked the program (96%), and most parents felt it positively changed their youth's health behaviors (81%). Most youth completed study protocols (88%), and 98% of video chats sent were completed. Virtual characters appeared to be a feasible and acceptable way to promote healthy behaviors among youth. Future research should rigorously examine behavioral and health outcomes of this approach.
