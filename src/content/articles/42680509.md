### 重點摘要
本研究旨在比較埃德蒙頓肥胖分級系統（EOSS）與刺胳針委員會肥胖診斷模型（DMO）兩種評估肥胖嚴重度的方法。研究團隊在英國生物樣本庫中分析了這兩種系統修改後的分類模式和一致性。結果顯示，兩者對晚期肥胖具高一致性，但在早期階段，DMO能捕捉更廣泛的亞臨床器官功能障礙，而EOSS則側重更晚期的表現，且結合BMI與脂肪分佈會大幅影響早期分期。研究結論指出，EOSS和DMO提供了互補的肥胖分類視角，反映了肥胖相關疾病嚴重度的不同概念。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了EOSS與DMO兩種肥胖分類系統在不同疾病階段（特別是早期）分類差異的理解空白，並揭示了結合BMI與脂肪分佈對肥胖分類，尤其是在早期階段，所產生的顯著影響。這凸顯了當前肥胖診斷模型在操作定義上的差異，並暗示未來研究可能需要更標準化這些評估標準。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Divergent Obesity Classification Between the Edmonton Obesity Staging System and the Lancet Commission Model.
**Abstract:** BMI alone does not capture obesity-related health heterogeneity. The Edmonton Obesity Staging System (EOSS) grades obesity severity based on comorbidities and functional impairment, whereas the Lancet Commission Diagnostic Model for Obesity (DMO) distinguishes preclinical from clinical obesity based on organ dysfunction. We compared classification patterns and concordance across both obesity frameworks in a large population-based cohort. A modified EOSS and DMO were applied to the UK Biobank (N ≈ 411,000). Stage distributions, cross-classification, and the impact of combining BMI with fat distribution on obesity categorization were analyzed. About one-quarter of participants were classified with obesity under both frameworks. Most were assigned to advanced stages, with high concordance for established disease. Differences were most pronounced in early stages: DMO captured a broader spectrum of mild/subclinical organ dysfunction, whereas EOSS emphasized more advanced manifestations of obesity-related disease. Discrepancies reflected differences in operationalization of, for example, metabolic, cardiovascular, and mental health domains. Obesity thresholds influenced classification, with ~50% reclassified when BMI was combined with different fat distribution parameters, highlighting the sensitivity of early-stage assignment. EOSS and DMO provide complementary approaches to obesity classification, with differing classification patterns highlighting distinct concepts of obesity-related disease severity.
