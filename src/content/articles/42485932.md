### 重點摘要
這篇系統性回顧探討肌肉內脂肪與肌肉間脂肪和胰島素阻抗、第二型糖尿病、代謝症候群之間的關聯。研究者依循 PRISMA 2020 指南，納入 17 項成人研究，分析透過 CT、MRI 或 pQCT 測量的肌肉脂肪與代謝結果的關係。結果顯示，肌肉間脂肪與胰島素阻抗、第二型糖尿病和代謝症候群顯著相關，肌肉內脂肪則與較高的胰島素阻抗和代謝症候群有關。本研究結論指出，肌肉脂肪浸潤與心臟代謝風險持續相關，並可能存在性別特異性差異。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇系統性回顧整合了現有文獻，確認了肌肉脂肪浸潤與心臟代謝風險之間的持續關聯。然而，研究結果存在高度異質性，且目前證據尚不足以確認因果關係。未來需要更多長期性、針對特定性別與疾病，以及方法標準化的研究，以確立其因果關係並指導精準介入措施。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Association of intermuscular and intramuscular fat with insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, and metabolic syndrome: A systematic review.
**Abstract:** Intermuscular and intramuscular fat may exacerbate cardiometabolic risk through insulin resistance. This systematic review explores the association of intermuscular and intramuscular fat with Homeostatic Model Assessment for Insulin Resistance (HOMA-IR), type 2 diabetes (T2D), and metabolic syndrome (MetS). Following the PRISMA 2020 guidelines, PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library were searched from inception to March 2025 for studies reporting ORs for muscle fat (assessed via computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), or peripheral quantitative CT (pQCT)) and metabolic outcomes in adults above 18 years. Quality was assessed using the Newcastle Ottawa Scale (NOS), with narrative synthesis due to high heterogeneity. Seventeen studies (n = 2945-8081; 61.7% men; age range of 34.3-74.6 years; majority of participants were community-dwelling) were included. Intermuscular fat showed significant associations with HOMA-IR (OR 2.72-2.67), T2D (OR 1.22-3.27, in multiple studies), and MetS (OR 1.2-2.3). Intramuscular fat was linked to higher HOMA-IR (OR 2.83) and MetS (OR 2.46), with mixed results related to T2D. Muscle attenuation was associated with T2D (OR 1.26-1.36) and MetS (OR 1.35-1.77) varied by sex. Quality of the studies ranged from moderate (NOS of 5 and 6, in the majority of studies) to high (NOS of 8). Muscle fat infiltration is consistently associated with cardiometabolic risk, demonstrating potential sex-specific differences. Longitudinal, sex- and disease-specific, and methodologically standardized studies are needed to confirm causality and guide targeted interventions. The protocol is registered in the Open Science Framework (https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/DK5VP).
