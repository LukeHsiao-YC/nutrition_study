### 重點摘要
這項系統性回顧與統合分析旨在評估先兆子癇孕婦與正常血壓對照組相比，母體DHA水平的差異。研究方法包含系統性文獻搜尋及隨機效應模型分析，初步統合分析顯示先兆子癇孕婦DHA水平較低，但統計上不顯著。排除兩個離群研究的敏感性分析則發現具統計意義的負相關，且在懷孕初期觀察到最大的效應量。儘管DHA水平與先兆子癇的關聯性具有方向一致性，但證據的確定性被評為「非常低」，因此無法得出因果結論。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究透過系統性回顧與統合分析，量化總結了先兆子癇孕婦DHA水平與正常血壓孕婦的差異，顯示兩者存在方向一致的關聯性。然而，由於證據確定性「非常低」，無法建立因果關係。未來的研究應著重於具標準化方案和飲食混淆因子控制的前瞻性縱向研究，以探討懷孕初期DHA與先兆子癇的因果關聯。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Revisiting the Role of Omega-3 Fatty Acids in Preeclampsia: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.
**Abstract:** Preeclampsia remains one of the major causes of neonatal and maternal morbidity. Omega-3 fatty acids, particularly docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), can influence placental function and vascular health, potentially affecting preeclampsia risk. The objective of this review was to systematically evaluate and quantitatively synthesize evidence on maternal DHA levels in women with preeclampsia compared with normotensive controls. A systematic search was conducted across the PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane Library databases to identify to identify eligible studies published between January 1990 and February 2026. We extracted data on study characteristics, sample details, gestational timing, and omega-3 fatty acid levels from each included study. A random-effects model was used to calculate standardized mean differences (SMDs). Six pre-specified subgroup analyses and a leave-one-out sensitivity analysis were conducted to investigate sources of heterogeneity. Certainty of evidence was assessed using the GRADE framework. The primary pooled analysis demonstrated lower DHA levels in women with preeclampsia compared with normotensive controls (SMD = -0.20; 95% CI: -0.57 to 0.17; P = .289; I² = 89.05%). A pre-specified sensitivity analysis excluding 2 methodologically outlying studies yielded an exploratory significant effect (SMD = -0.24; 95% CI: -0.39 to -0.09; P = .002; I² = 29.1%). Across 6 subgroup analyses, significant negative associations were observed in Indian, Gas Chromatography-Flame Ionization Detection (GC-FID), cross-sectional, and at-delivery subgroups, with the largest effect size being observed in early pregnancy studies (SMD = -0.42). The certainty of the evidence was rated Very Low for all outcomes. Lower DHA levels are consistently associated with preeclampsia across diverse populations; however, Very Low certainty of evidence precludes causal conclusions. The directional consistency, particularly in early pregnancy, warrants investigation through prospective longitudinal studies with standardized protocols and dietary confounder control. PROSPERO registration No. CRD420261378084.
