### 重點摘要
本篇摘要指出，體組成研究正從傳統靜態組織隔間測量轉變為動態生理表型分析，以提供更具可操作性的臨床決策、健康預測與運動表現評估。除了雙能量X光吸收儀（DXA）等基礎方法外，新興技術如人工智慧、進階影像與穿戴式裝置正被整合應用，並強調對肌質量、液體隔間、內臟及異位脂肪的關注。文章提出未來體組成測量應整合至健康、表現與生理恢復力的預測模型中。體組成技術的成功將以其對生理理解、預測和決策的有效性來衡量，而非僅限於精確的組織隔間估計。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了將傳統體組成測量轉化為具備動態生理意義、可行動且能融入預測模型的應用空白，並提供了一個未來研究的框架。然而，未來的研究限制可能包括如何有效整合並標準化多種新興技術，確保其在不同性別與族群間的普適性，以及解決資料治理的挑戰。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** From compartments to dynamic physiology: toward actionable body composition.
**Abstract:** The 12th International Symposium on In Vivo Body Composition Studies (ISBCR 2025) highlighted a fundamental transition from estimating static tissue compartments to dynamic physiological phenotyping, actionable interpretation, and demonstrable value for clinical decision-making, health prediction, and human performance assessment. This represents an evolution in technological capability and conceptual framing, as an extension of the Functional Body Composition concept. Classical multicompartment models and established methods such as dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry, isotope dilution, bioimpedance, computed tomography, and magnetic resonance imaging remain foundational; however, emerging approaches increasingly integrate advanced imaging, artificial intelligence, bioimpedance spectroscopy, stable isotope methods, wearable sensing, and multidimensional phenotyping. Several themes were prominent at ISBCR 2025, including expansion of imaging beyond traditional DXA paradigms, increasing emphasis on muscle quality and fluid compartments, greater attention to visceral and ectopic adipose tissue, clinical applications in oncology and critical illness, the evaluation of obesity pharmacotherapy, the importance of generalizability to individuals across sex and ethnicity, and data governance. This perspective proposes a framework for future research in which body composition measurements become integral components of predictive models of health, performance, and physiological resilience. Future body composition assessment practices must include demonstrated added value over previous methods, and the greatest advances will come from technologies that bring physiologically meaningful assessment to large populations. The success of future technologies should be judged less by how precisely they estimate tissue compartments than by how effectively they improve physiological understanding, prediction, and decision-making.
