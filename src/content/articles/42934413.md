### 重點摘要
全球研究普遍將超加工食品與慢性病風險增加相關聯，而NOVA分類法在全球範圍內對飲食指南和公共衛生政策具有深遠影響。然而，本研究認為，若NOVA分類法未經情境適應，可能不適用於東南亞等中低收入國家的食物系統，進而錯誤引導應對營養不良與過度營養雙重負擔的政策介入。文章指出，過度強調加工食品的負面影響可能掩蓋某些加工食品在改善營養和促進公共衛生方面的關鍵作用，並簡化複雜的飲食模式，甚至可能削弱過去四十年在營養方面取得的進展。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本文揭示了NOVA分類法在全球（特別是中低收入國家如東南亞）應用時，缺乏情境適應性可能導致的政策誤導與潛在負面影響，這是一個重要的研究視角空白。它強調了在將全球性分類工具應用於特定地域背景時，必須考量當地食物系統的獨特性，而非盲目推行，為未來探討如何在不同文化和經濟背景下調整或重新評估飲食分類法提供了方向。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Rethinking the application and adoption of NOVA classification in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs): a Southeast Asian food systems perspective.
**Abstract:** Ultra-processed foods have increasingly been associated with chronic disease risk in global analyses. The NOVA classification is positioned to influence dietary guidelines, food manufacturing, and public health policy worldwide. This paper argues that the NOVA classification, when applied without contextual adaptation, may be inappropriate for Southeast Asian food systems. It could misdirect policy interventions aimed at addressing both the double burden of under- and overnutrition. By labelling foods as UPFs, NOVA may obscure the essential role that certain processed foods play in improving nutritional outcomes and advancing public health. An overemphasis on the negative impact of processed and ultra-processed foods risks oversimplifying complex dietary patterns in Asia and other LMICs. It may also undermine nutritional gains achieved over the past four decades.
