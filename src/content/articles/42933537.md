### 重點摘要
兒童肥胖是重要的公共衛生議題，早期識別代謝特徵對精準營養至關重要。本研究評估了3個月以下嬰兒的能量消耗（EE）個體差異，透過雙標水和嬰兒代謝艙測量總日常與睡眠EE。結果顯示嬰兒在總能量消耗及調整後的EE上存在顯著個體差異，並識別出40%具有「節儉型」代謝表型的嬰兒。研究也發現現行嬰兒營養建議（2023 DRI）存在約10%的預測誤差，強調需進一步研究早期EE與肥胖風險的關係，並精進營養指南。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究透過精確的代謝表型分析，填補了對3個月以下嬰兒能量消耗個體差異認知的空白，並揭示了「節儉型」代謝表型可能與肥胖風險相關。它也指出現行嬰兒營養建議（2023 DRI）的預測誤差，強調了其精進的必要性。未來的研究限制在於，此為早期探索性研究，尚需進行更全面的縱向代謝表型研究，以追蹤早期能量消耗如何影響生命週期的肥胖風險。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Metabolic phenotyping identifies early interindividual variation in infant energy expenditure with implications for obesity risk.
**Abstract:** Pediatric obesity is a major public health crisis. Early identification of metabolic traits that increase individual risk are emerging. As such, identification of variability in early energy expenditure would reveal the need for precision nutritional approaches from birth. To evaluate the interindividual variability in components of energy expenditure for the purpose of understanding early nutritional considerations in very young infants. Infant participants (<3 months of age) underwent extensive energy expenditure phenotyping using a combination of doubly labeled water for total daily energy expenditure and a novel infant metabolic chamber for sleeping energy expenditure. Descriptive statistics, intraclass correlation coefficients, and univariable and multivariable linear regression analyses were used to assess reliability and characterize variability, predictors, and phenotypes of energy expenditure. We identified interindividual variability in total, mass-adjusted, and fat-free mass adjusted energy expenditures. Energy phenotypes (thrifty and spendthrift) were assigned based on the residuals between measured and predicted energy expenditure from metabolic mass. Discrepancies between measured energy expenditure and predicted by national dietary recommendations for infants (2023 DRI) were evaluated. Total daily and sleeping energy expenditures were 368±60 kcal/d and 250±60 kcal/d, respectively. Energy expenditure related to activity accounted for 21±9% of total measured. Age (r=.59), weight (r=.83), length (r=.75), and fat-free mass (r=.82) were positively associated with sleeping energy expenditure. We identified 40% of measured infants with a thrifty energy phenotype, classified as having sleeping energy expenditure under that predicted by cohort age, sex, and fat and fat-free mass. Mean prediction error of the 2023 DRI from our cohort was 10±7% and was positively associated with the amount of energy expenditure attributed to activity. These findings warrant further comprehensive metabolic phenotyping studies in young infants to understand how early energy expenditure tracks through the life course and influences obesity risk as well as the thorough evaluation and refinement of infant nutritional recommendations.
