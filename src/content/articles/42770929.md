### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討經濟限制與食物相關的身體功能限制這兩種糧食不安全面向，如何與老年族群參與美國營養補充援助計畫 (SNAP) 相關。透過分析2007-2018年全國代表性調查數據中4,429名符合SNAP資格的60歲以上成人，研究人員將參與者分為糧食安全、僅經濟不安全、僅身體功能不安全或兩者皆不安全等類別。結果顯示，經濟糧食不安全、身體功能糧食不安全及兩者皆不安全，皆與SNAP參與率顯著增加相關 (調整後勝算比分別為1.74、1.47和2.30)。這項研究強調，在符合資格的老年人中，經濟和食物相關的身體功能障礙均是影響糧食不安全及SNAP參與的重要因素。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了文獻中對食物相關的身體功能限制作為糧食不安全面向的探討不足之處。其指出，若僅評估經濟層面的限制，可能會忽略老年族群在獲取食物方面所面臨的關鍵身體功能障礙，因此未能全面理解和解決糧食不安全問題。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Financial and Food-Related Physical Functional Barriers to Food Security Among Older Adults: Associations With SNAP Participation.
**Abstract:** To examine how financial constraints and food-related physical functioning limitations, an underexplored dimension of food insecurity, are associated with Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) participation among older adults. Cross-sectional analysis of nationally representative survey data. US; National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey, 2007-2018. A total of 4,429 adults aged ≥ 60 years who were income-eligible for SNAP and had complete food security and participation data. Self-reported SNAP participation in the past 12 months. Food insecurity because of financial constraints was assessed using the US Department of Agriculture Adult Food Security Survey Module. Food-related physical functioning insecurity was measured using a validated tool. Participants were categorized as food secure, financially food insecure only, physically food insecure only, or insecure because of both. Weighted logistic regression estimated odds of SNAP participation by food insecurity type, adjusting for sociodemographic and health characteristics. Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program participants had higher prevalence of financial food insecurity (34.6% vs 15.0%) and combined financial and physical food insecurity (15.6% vs 5.6%) than nonparticipants. In adjusted analyses, financial food insecurity (odds ratio [OR] = 1.74 [95% confidence interval (CI), 1.35-2.26]), physical functioning food insecurity (OR = 1.47 [95% CI, 1.16-1.87]), and combined insecurity (OR = 2.30 [95% CI, 1.70-3.21]) were associated with greater odds of SNAP participation. Both economic and food-related physical functioning barriers to food access are associated with SNAP participation among income-eligible older adults. Assessments focused solely on financial constraints may overlook important physical functioning barriers. A multidimensional approach to food insecurity may improve food access and support healthy aging.
