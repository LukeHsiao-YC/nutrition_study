### 重點摘要
這項研究旨在評估日間飲食變異如何影響因子分析在識別飲食模式上的穩定性。研究讓302名成人完成20次非連續的24小時飲食回顧，並比較20天平均的「常規飲食模式」與2、3、4天組合的「n日飲食模式」。結果顯示，雖然至少2天的非連續飲食回顧可識別主要飲食模式，但第一個因子最為穩定，而後續解釋力較弱的因子則高度受到日間變異的影響。這可能損害較不顯著之常規飲食模式的複製性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了短時間飲食評估中，日間變異對飲食模式識別穩定性影響的研究空白，並提供識別常規飲食模式所需最少非連續飲食回顧天數的實證依據。然而，對於解釋力較低的次要飲食模式，其穩定性仍易受日間變異影響，這對未來欲精確捕捉完整飲食模式的研究構成限制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Effect of day-to-day variation in dietary intake on dietary patterns identification through factor analysis.
**Abstract:** Short-term dietary assessments, such as 24-h recalls, are affected by day-to-day variation, influencing the stability and identification of dietary patterns through factor analysis. We evaluated the effect of within-person random error on replicating these patterns. Twenty non-consecutive 24-h recalls were completed by 302 adults over about three months. Individual usual intakes were defined as the average of 20 days. Factor analyses identified: (i) usual dietary patterns (20dDP), based on usual individual intakes; and (ii) n-day (n = 2, 3, or 4) dietary patterns (ndDP), based on all possible recall combinations. Factor mirroring and interchangeability ensured alignment for comparison between 20dDP and ndDP factor loadings distributions. Three usual dietary patterns were identified: Prudent, Traditional, and Western, explaining 25% of the variance. Mean loading factors from 190 two-day combinations were weaker than the usual ones, with substantial attenuation in the third factor. Foods in the usual first and second factors were consistently identified in most 2-day combinations. In contrast, foods of the usual third pattern were identified largely by chance, depending on the sampled 2-day combination, and more foods not belonging to this factor were incorrectly assigned. This indicates that while two-day data capture major patterns, subsequent, less explanatory factors are highly susceptible to random error. Results were similar for 3 and 4-day combinations. Usual dietary patterns can be identified using at least 2 non-consecutive collection days; however, the first factor is most stable, while subsequent factors show greater susceptibility to day-to-day variation, potentially compromising replication of usual dietary patterns.
