### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討「適量飲食」訊息，特別是寬容性適量訊息，如何影響人們對食物健康程度的感知。研究將435名參與者隨機分派至無訊息、限制卡路里、一般適量或寬容性適量訊息組，隨後請他們將60種食物分類為健康或不健康。結果顯示，寬容性適量訊息顯著增加了參與者將模糊食物甚至不健康食物歸類為健康的頻率。這些發現表明，不同形式的適量訊息可能會改變飲食行為和自我控制，特別是通過改變對食物健康程度的感知。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了關於不同類型「適量飲食」訊息（尤其是寬容性訊息）對食物健康感知和自我調節過程具體影響的空白。它揭示了模糊的健康飲食建議如何可能產生意想不到的反效果，特別是對於那些健康特性不明顯的食物。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Seeing healthy in the middle: Permissive moderation messages change perceptions of ambiguous foods.
**Abstract:** To provide actionable guidance on how to manage the omnipresence of highly palatable foods, there is a common adage to "eat in moderation." Although such messages seem approachable, they may interrupt self-regulatory processes by changing perceptions of how healthy a food appears, particularly when a food lacks distinctively healthy or unhealthy characteristics. We evaluated the effect of moderation messaging on the perceived healthfulness of foods. We examined whether messaging about eating in moderation influences what foods are perceived as healthy. Participants (n=435) were randomized to one of four conditions. In one condition, participants did not read a message related to eating. In the other three conditions, participants read a message to limit calories, a general moderation message ("Eat unhealthy foods in moderation"), or a permissive moderation message ("It is okay to eat unhealthy foods in moderation"). Participants then classified 60 foods (pilot tested to be viewed as healthy, unhealthy, or ambiguous) based on whether they were perceived as healthy or unhealthy. Results indicate permissive moderation messages increased frequency of classifying ambiguous foods as healthy compared to all other messages, all p's <.028. Findings additionally extended to classification of unhealthy foods as healthy. The findings have implications for how moderation messaging may change eating behavior and advance self-control theory by highlighting the potential for situations to shift perceptions of foods as healthy.
