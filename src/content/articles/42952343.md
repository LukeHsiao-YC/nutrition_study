### 重點摘要
這篇系統性回顧與證據地圖旨在評估2010至2024年間低收入和中等收入國家人道主義環境中，以社區為基礎的營養干預措施，及其類型、成果與證據缺口。研究遵循PRISMA指南，共納入53份文件，主要來自非洲，涵蓋社區團體、營養諮詢和急性營養不良管理。結果顯示，預防性干預改善了嬰幼兒餵養行為，治療性干預提升了恢復率和項目覆蓋率，但飲食攝入、糧食安全和長期結果的證據仍有限。總體而言，社區營養干預在人道主義環境中具有巨大潛力，但未來需要更整合的評估框架以強化證據。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻系統性地整理並呈現了人道主義環境下社區營養干預措施的現有證據，填補了對此領域全面性理解的空白。然而，研究也指出目前在飲食攝入、糧食安全、照顧者能力、預防復發以及長期成果方面的證據仍然有限。此外，未來的研究需開發更整合且具情境敏感性的評估框架，以強化人道主義營養規劃的證據基礎。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Community-based nutrition interventions across the continuum of care in humanitarian settings: a systematic review and evidence map.
**Abstract:** To systematically review and map the evidence on community-based nutrition interventions implemented in humanitarian and emergency settings in low- and middle-income countries (LMIC), focusing on intervention types, outcomes, evidence gaps and implications for humanitarian nutrition systems. Systematic review and evidence mapping conducted according to Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. Eight electronic databases and twenty-six organisational websites were searched for studies published between 2010 and 2024. Studies are mapped by intervention and outcome categories. Findings are synthesised narratively. Humanitarian and emergency settings in LMIC affected by conflict, fragility, displacement or natural disasters. Women, infants, children, adolescents, caregivers and households receiving community-based nutrition interventions delivered through community health workers (CHW), volunteers, peer counsellors, care groups or other community platforms. Fifty-three documents met inclusion criteria, including thirty-nine primary study documents representing thirty-three interventions across twenty countries and fourteen systematic reviews. Most evidence originates from Africa and focuses on community groups, nutrition counselling and community-based management of acute malnutrition. Preventive interventions generally improved infant and young child feeding practices and caregiver behaviours but showed inconsistent effects on anthropometric outcomes. Treatment interventions delivered through CHW and community volunteers improved recovery, programme coverage and treatment performance. However, evidence on dietary intake, food security, caregiver capacity, relapse prevention and longer-term outcomes remains limited. Community-based nutrition interventions are central to extending nutrition service delivery in humanitarian settings and show strong potential to improve feeding practices and treatment outcomes. More integrated and context-sensitive evaluation frameworks are needed to strengthen evidence for resilient humanitarian nutrition programming.
