### 重點摘要
這項研究旨在探討美國成年人尼古丁袋（NP）與菸草合併使用（NP-菸草共用）和其與尼古丁依賴及精神症狀的關聯。研究分析了 11,514 名過去一年內使用菸草或 NP 的成年人數據，發現使用 NP 的成年人中有 95.7% 同時使用菸草。結果顯示，NP-菸草共用與最高的尼古丁依賴分數顯著相關，但在調整共變項後，與高內化或外化精神症狀並無顯著關聯。本研究結論指出，NP-菸草共用在 NP 使用者中很常見，且與較高的尼古丁依賴相關，建議對此族群進行尼古丁依賴評估並提供量身定制的戒菸支持。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻提供了尼古丁袋與菸草合併使用對尼古丁依賴和精神症狀的當前橫斷面關聯性洞察。然而，由於本研究的橫斷面性質，無法確立因果關係，因此未來的研究需要進行縱向追蹤，以更清楚地闡明這些觀察到的關聯性，是本研究的主要限制與未來研究方向。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Nicotine Pouch and Tobacco Use, Nicotine Dependence, and Psychiatric Symptoms Among U.S. Adults.
**Abstract:** Nicotine pouches (NPs), a rapidly growing category of non-combustible oral nicotine products, are frequently used alongside tobacco products. This study examined NP-tobacco co-use and its association with nicotine dependence and high internalizing and externalizing symptoms among U.S. adults. This study analyzed Wave 7 (2022-2023) of the Population Assessment of Tobacco and Health (PATH) Study, including 11,514 U.S. adults with past-year tobacco or NP use. Data were analyzed in 2026. Past-year NP and tobacco use were assessed along with nicotine dependence (16-item validated scale) and internalizing and externalizing symptoms (≥4 as high symptoms for each domain). Survey-weighted linear and modified Poisson regression examined associations of NP-tobacco co-use with nicotine dependence and psychiatric symptoms, respectively, adjusting for covariates and the other outcomes. Among U.S. adults with past-year tobacco or NP use, 5.4% reported NP use; 95.7% of adults who used NPs also used tobacco. NP-tobacco co-use was associated with the highest adjusted nicotine dependence scores (vs. tobacco-only use: b=7.75, 95% CI=6.29-9.20). NP-tobacco co-use (vs. tobacco-only use) was not significantly associated with high internalizing or externalizing symptoms after adjustment for covariates, including nicotine dependence. NP-tobacco co-use is common among adults who use NPs and is associated with greater nicotine dependence, but not significantly associated with psychiatric symptoms after covariate adjustment. Adults with co-use may benefit from nicotine dependence assessment and tailored cessation support. Longitudinal research is needed to clarify the observed associations.
