### 重點摘要
衣索比亞的牧民和農業牧民社區面臨輔助餵養不足的挑戰，本研究旨在評估透過傳統Dala領袖進行營養行為改變溝通（DALA-BCC）能否改善嬰幼兒輔助餵養實踐。一項群體隨機對照試驗顯示，為期六個月的介入顯著提高了介入組在最低飲食多樣性、最低餐食頻率和最低可接受飲食方面的指標。研究結果證實，DALA-BCC介入能有效改善輔助餵養實踐，突顯了在農業牧民環境中，基於文化且由社區主導的營養策略的有效性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了在流動性生計阻礙傳統健康服務獲取的牧民和農業牧民社區中，如何有效實施營養介入的研究空白，特別是透過利用當地文化根深蒂固的領導結構來推動健康促進，從而克服了服務難以到達的挑戰。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Effect of Dala-Led Behavior Change Communication on Complementary Feeding Practices in Infants and Young Children: A Cluster-Randomized Controlled Trial in Agro-Pastoral Settings of Afar Region, Ethiopia.
**Abstract:** Suboptimal complementary feeding remains a major public health concern in Ethiopia, particularly among pastoralist and agro-pastoral communities where mobile livelihoods hinder access to conventional health services. In these settings, numerous studies have highlighted the potential of leveraging culturally embedded leadership structures to enhance health promotion efforts. This study examined whether engaging traditional Dala leaders as agents of Nutrition Behavior Change Communication (DALA-BCC) could improve complementary feeding indicators among infants and young children in the agro-pastoral areas of northern Ethiopia. A cluster-randomized controlled trial was conducted in Dubti District, Afar Region, involving 16 non-adjacent clusters randomly assigned to intervention and control arms (eight each). A total of 474 mother-infant pairs (237 per arm) were enrolled at 6-17 months of age and followed over 6 months. Over a 6-month period, the intervention group received DALA-BCC delivered by Dala leaders, consisting of monthly group sessions and cooking demonstrations during the first 3 months, followed by home-based individualized counseling from Month 3 to Month 6, while the control group continued usual care. Core infant and young child feeding (IYCF) indicators were measured at baseline and endline. At end line, the intervention group achieved higher rates of minimum dietary diversity (MDD) (39.7% vs. 24.7%; p = 0.001), minimum meal frequency (MMF) (67.9% vs. 47.6%; p < 0.001), and minimum acceptable diet (MAD) (28.6% vs. 12.1%; p < 0.001). Generalized estimating equation (GEE) analysis showed significant time × group interactions, indicating greater improvements in the intervention group for MDD (RR = 2.43; 95% CI: 1.35-4.39), MMF (RR = 2.08; 95% CI: 1.25-3.48), and MAD (RR = 2.35; 95% CI: 1.10-5.04). The findings demonstrate that the DALA-BCC intervention significantly improved complementary feeding practices, highlighting the effectiveness of culturally grounded, community-led nutrition strategies in Agro-pastoral settings.
