### 重點摘要
這項定性研究旨在探討研究生（特別是健康科學領域）的糧食不安全問題，因其相較於大學生仍屬研究不足但關鍵的議題。研究透過對哈佛大學15名健康科學領域研究生進行半結構式訪談，以歸納式主題分析法探討糧食不安全對其福祉的影響及應對策略。結果顯示，糧食不安全導致經濟困境、影響食物獲取策略、損害身心健康、限制社交互動及學術表現，且多數學生因尷尬不願與師長討論。儘管學生會採取個人策略並尋求社區或政府援助，但仍需更全面的努力來確保研究生的福祉與學術成功。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了過去主要關注大學生糧食不安全，而研究生群體（特別是健康科學領域）相關研究匱乏的空白。然而，由於研究是針對哈佛大學特定健康科學領域的15名研究生進行的定性分析，其結果的普遍性可能受限，未來研究可考慮擴大樣本量、涵蓋更多元學科或院校背景。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** "Less like a Harvard student": A qualitative study exploring food insecurity among graduate students in health sciences.
**Abstract:** While considerable research has focused on undergraduate students, food insecurity among graduate students remains a comparatively understudied yet critical issue. The objective of this study was to investigate the challenges food insecurity creates for graduate students, the perceived influence of food insecurity on their academic, social, physical, and psychosocial well-being, and the coping mechanisms they employ within their unique institutional and local context. This is a qualitative study, where semi-structured interviews were conducted. A total of 15 graduate students in the health sciences (4 Master's, 11 PhD) at Harvard University participated in the study in Spring 2024. Interviews were audio-recorded, transcribed, and checked for accuracy. Narrative data were analyzed using inductive thematic analysis and themes were generated through discussion. Food insecurity was assessed using the Six-item Short Form Food Security Survey Module. Descriptive statistics were used to describe the sociodemographic characteristics of the students. Several themes were identified from graduate students' narratives demonstrating 1) the economic landscape of graduate student food insecurity; 2) perceived influence of food insecurity on food acquisition strategies; 3) the perceived influence of food insecurity on physical health; 4) the perceived influence of food insecurity on psychosocial health; and 5) student strategies and support pathways in navigating food insecurity, including individual coping strategies, community support, and government assistance through the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program. Most students did not discuss their food situation with faculty or advisors, partially due to embarrassment. Graduate students described how food insecurity contributed to challenges in food acquisition, poorer physical and mental health and well-being, limited social interactions, and difficulties in academic and research activities. Several students adopted individual coping strategies and sought support from the community as well as government assistance; however, more well-rounded efforts are needed to ensure graduate student well-being and success.
