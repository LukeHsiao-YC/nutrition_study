### 重點摘要
本研究旨在填補高風險年輕人中，減少社群媒體使用與內化問題改善之間缺乏全國性世代分析的空白。研究利用2013-2021年「菸草與健康人口評估研究」數據，納入18-24歲、有高度內化問題且頻繁使用社群媒體的年輕人，追蹤其社群媒體使用頻率變化與內化症狀的改善。結果顯示，減少社群媒體使用者在t+2時間點，內化症狀恢復至非升高水平的可能性顯著較高（盛行率比=1.15），且症狀計數也較少（調整發生率比=0.90）。本研究結論指出，減少社群媒體使用是高風險年輕族群預防和早期介入內化症狀的潛在可修改目標。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
全國性世代分析，探討高風險年輕族群（即最有可能從介入中受益者）減少社群媒體使用與後續內化問題改善之間的關聯仍然有限。本研究首次在全國層面提供此類證據。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Reduced Social Media Use and Change in Internalizing Symptoms Among High-Risk Young Adults.
**Abstract:** National cohort analyses of the association between reduced social media use and subsequent improvement in internalizing problems among high-risk young adults-ie, those most likely to benefit from intervention-remain limited. To assess whether reduced social media use is associated with improvements in internalizing problems among US young adults with frequent social media use and elevated internalizing problems. This cohort study used data from the Population Assessment of Tobacco and Health Study (2013-2021). Repeated observations were organized into 4 consecutive, overlapping 3-wave intervals, each comprising 3 sequential survey waves (t, t + 1, and t + 2); for example, interval 1 included waves 1-3, and interval 4 included waves 4-6. Young adults aged 18 to 24 years with elevated internalizing problems (all 4 symptoms) and frequent social media use (several times a day) at wave t were included. Data were analyzed from February to August 2026. Self-reported social media use frequency at wave t + 1 (nonfrequent [≤once daily] vs frequent [several times daily]). Past-year internalizing problems (nonelevated [≤3 symptoms] vs elevated [4 symptoms]) at wave t + 2 via the Global Appraisal of Individual Needs-Short Screener (depression, anxiety, psychological distress, sleep difficulties). A total of 3788 person-interval observations (2561 [64.7%] female; aged 18-24 years at baseline) were included. Approximately 14% (n = 540) transitioned to nonfrequent use at t + 1. Reduced use was associated with greater likelihood of nonelevated internalizing symptoms at t + 2 (0.57 vs 0.49; prevalence difference, 0.07; 95% CI, 0.02-0.13; prevalence ratio, 1.15; 95% CI, 1.04-1.27). Findings were generally consistent across sensitivity analyses, including alternative exposure definitions (weekly or less vs several times daily social media use: prevalence ratio, 1.17; 95% CI, 1.04-1.33), alternative missing-data handling approaches (prevalence ratio, 1.14; 95% CI, 1.04-1.25), design-based analyses (prevalence ratio, 1.16; 95% CI, 1.06-1.28), concurrent models (prevalence ratio, 1.13; 95% CI, 1.01-1.26), and alternative outcome definitions using internalizing symptom counts, with non-frequent use associated with fewer symptoms (adjusted incidence rate ratio, 0.90; 95% CI, 0.85-0.96). In this cohort study, reduced social media use was associated with subsequent improvement in internalizing symptoms among US young adults with elevated symptoms and frequent use. These findings identify reduced social media use as a potentially modifiable target for prevention and early intervention in this high-risk population.
