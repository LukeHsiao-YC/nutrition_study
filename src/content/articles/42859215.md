### 重點摘要
這項研究旨在評估加薩地區受衝突影響的6-24個月兒童的營養狀況、相關社會經濟因素、母親知識及初級保健中心營養服務的不足。這是一項於2025年8月至10月在加薩市Al-Daraj Martyrs健康中心進行的橫斷面研究，分析了200名兒童的身體測量數據與55名兒童的血紅蛋白結果。結果顯示，兒童發育遲緩率12.5%、體重不足率20.1%、消瘦率20.8%，貧血率高達63.6%，且體重不足與家庭糧食短缺和先前營養不良治療相關，貧血與父親失業相關。研究指出，衝突地區幼兒營養不良問題嚴重，主要受結構性剝奪和初級保健中心支持薄弱的影響，而非僅是母親知識不足，亟需恢復營養服務並改善兒童的食物獲取。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究量化了加薩衝突地區幼兒的營養不良盛行率及其相關因素，並指出結構性剝奪和初級保健服務薄弱是主要驅動因素，而非母親知識不足，從而填補了該地區在特殊背景下幼兒營養狀況數據的空白。其研究限制可能包括便利抽樣和僅限於單一診所的樣本，可能影響結果的普遍性，且血紅蛋白檢測的樣本量較小。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Nutritional status and associated factors among children aged 6-24 months at a primary health care centre in conflict-affected Gaza.
**Abstract:** Children aged 6-24 months are highly vulnerable to malnutrition during conflict because they depend on breastfeeding, complementary feeding and functioning nutrition services. This study assessed nutritional status, socioeconomic correlates, maternal knowledge and primary health care centre (PHCC) nutrition service gaps in Gaza. This cross-sectional study was conducted at Al-Daraj Martyrs Health Centre, one of the remaining functioning PHCCs in Gaza City during the study period, between late August and October 2025. Mother-child pairs were recruited by convenience sampling. Of 276 mother-child pairs approached, 250 completed the questionnaire. The anthropometric analytic sample comprised 200 children with the required anthropometric information, while haemoglobin results were available for 55 of the 250 completed participants. Stunting affected 12.5% of children, underweight 20.1%, wasting 20.8%, and anaemia 63.6% of the haemoglobin-tested subsample. Underweight was associated with household food shortage (p = 0.013) and previous malnutrition treatment (p = 0.002), wasting with child age category (p = 0.0024), and anaemia with paternal unemployment (p = 0.020). Maternal knowledge and practice scores were positively correlated (r = 0.177, p = 0.012), but neither was independently associated with stunting or underweight in adjusted models. PHCC nutrition support was limited, with 71.0% of mothers reporting nurse-provided nutrition advice and 52.5% reporting growth-chart review. In this clinic-based sample from conflict-affected Gaza, malnutrition among children aged 6-24 months was substantial. The pattern suggests that nutritional risk was shaped more by structural deprivation and weakened PHCC support than by maternal knowledge alone. These findings underline the need to restore nutrition services and improve access to adequate food for young children.
