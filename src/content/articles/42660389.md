### 重點摘要
地中海飲食因涉及多種行為，常導致意圖與行為間的落差，本研究旨在評估結合目標優先順序與執行意圖的介入措施，能否促進成人對地中海飲食的遵從性。一項包含410名成人的隨機對照試驗發現，該介入措施僅輕微增加了自我報告的目標優先級，但未能在追蹤期間維持，且對整體地中海飲食的遵從性並無顯著影響。探索性分析雖顯示參與者選擇優先的行為略有改善，但與非優先行為的差異僅達邊緣顯著性，且此為觀察性結果。研究結論指出，誘導優先順序雖然可行，但其本身不足以改變複雜飲食模式的遵從性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了將目標優先順序與執行意圖結合應用於複雜飲食模式（如地中海飲食）的有效性評估空白，並探討了對優先與非優先行為的潛在影響。然而，本研究也指出此介入措施未能充分提升整體飲食遵從性，且觀察到的優先行為改善模式需進一步透過控制性試驗來驗證其因果關係，這為未來研究指明了方向與限制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Can combined goal prioritization and implementation intentions promote adherence to the Mediterranean Diet? A randomized controlled trial.
**Abstract:** Adherence to the Mediterranean Diet (MD) requires the simultaneous regulation of multiple distinct dietary behaviors, often creating a significant intention-behavior gap. This study evaluated the efficacy of a combined goal prioritization and implementation intentions intervention in promoting adherence to specific MD components, testing whether individuals can prioritize selected targets while successfully maintaining adherence to non-prioritized health behaviors. In a longitudinal randomized controlled trial, 410 adults (Mage = 46.1) were randomized to an experimental condition, in which they selected two MD behaviors to prioritize and formed prioritization plans, or to an active control condition. Primary analyses were conducted on the full randomized sample with missing values handled using multiple imputation. The intervention produced a small increase in self-reported goal priority, which was not maintained at follow-up, and had no significant effect on adherence across the MD components relative to control. In exploratory within-person analyses, adherence to the behaviors participants had chosen to prioritize showed a small positive change over time while non-prioritized behaviors remained statistically equivalent to no change, although the difference between the two trajectories was significant only at the alpha level of 0.10. Because the prioritized behaviors were self-selected, this pattern is observational and is treated as a potential mechanism warranting a controlled test. These findings suggest that inducing prioritization is feasible but not sufficient, on its own, to shift adherence in a complex dietary pattern.
