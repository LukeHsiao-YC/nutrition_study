### 重點摘要
這項前瞻性縱向世代研究旨在評估在專業腦震盪照護中的青少年，配戴消費級穿戴式裝置Fitbit的依從性及其所需的提醒頻率。研究分析了70名青少年在13週內配戴Fitbit Sense 2的日間與夜間心率數據，並使用三種不同依從性閾值進行評估。結果顯示，儘管依從性隨時間略有下降，且約71%的參與者需要至少一次人工提醒，但該群體在13週內對Fitbit的配戴依從性良好。這項研究證實了在腦震盪青少年中使用Fitbit收集縱向數據的可行性，同時也強調了為促進依從性所需的人力支持。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了在專業腦震盪照護的青少年族群中，使用消費級穿戴式裝置（如Fitbit）進行長期生理數據監測依從性的研究空白。它證明了此方法的可行性，但也明確指出維持良好依從性需要額外的人工提醒。未來的研究可探討更自動化或更具成本效益的依從性促進策略，並評估不同介入措施對長期依從性的影響，以及在更長時間跨度內的依從性變化。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Feasibility of Using Consumer-Grade Wearables Among Adolescents Presenting for Specialty Concussion Care: Prospective Longitudinal Cohort Study.
**Abstract:** Autonomic dysfunction is common after concussion and may be compounded by inactivity. Accordingly, physical activity progression is an important component of concussion rehabilitation. Fitbit devices can provide objective longitudinal data on physical activity, physiologic response to activity, and sleep. However, participants must have adequate adherence to device wear to provide accurate data. The threshold for adequate adherence may change based on the research question, making it important to understand how adherence changes across different thresholds. Furthermore, it is unclear how much staff support (ie, reminders) may be required to maintain an adequate level of adherence. This study aimed to (1) evaluate adherence to Fitbit device wear during daytime and nighttime using 3 different thresholds (50%, 75%, and 90% wear time) over 13 weeks in adolescents presenting for specialty concussion care and (2) report the number of reminders required to achieve the observed level of adherence. A total of 80 adolescents were recruited from a specialty concussion clinic. Participants were asked to wear a Fitbit Sense 2 device day and night for 1 year. We analyzed data collected during the first 13 weeks. For each threshold, a day or night was considered valid if the percentage of minutes with recorded heart rate data was greater than or equal to the defined threshold. We fit 2 robust linear mixed-effects models with number of valid days or valid nights as the outcome, a fixed effect for time (week), a fixed effect for threshold (50%, 75%, and 90%), a time-by-threshold interaction, and a random slope and intercept for each participant. We also calculated the percentage of participants who required a reminder from the study team to wear the device and/or synchronize it and the average number of manual reminders sent per participant across the study period. A total of 70 participants were included in the analyses. Over 13 weeks, adolescents had average daily adherence ranging from 3.0 (SD 0.7) to 4.4 (SD 0.8) days/wk and average nightly adherence ranging from 3.6 (SD 0.7) to 3.9 (SD 0.7) nights/wk. Across all thresholds, adherence was highest during the first week. The number of valid wear days (β=-0.2; P<.001) and nights (β=-0.2; P<.001) decreased each week, regardless of the adherence threshold used. A total of 50 of 70 (71%) participants required at least 1 manual reminder from the study team. We found that adolescents presenting for specialty concussion care had adequate adherence to Fitbit device wear over 13 weeks. Approximately three-quarters of the participants required at least 1 reminder from the study team. This suggests that collecting longitudinal data with a Fitbit device is feasible in adolescents presenting for specialty concussion care but that reminders are needed to promote adherence.
