### 重點摘要
鐵質過量（IOL）的現行治療方法成本高昂且影響生活品質，但缺乏標準化的飲食指南。本篇範圍性文獻回顧旨在系統性地梳理飲食及飲食因子在IOL治療管理中的證據，共檢索了40篇文獻，歸納出多酚、維生素、礦物質等多種潛在相關飲食因子。研究發現，飲食因子可能改善患者預後，但現有高質量臨床試驗有限且報告不一致，亟需更多嚴謹研究以制定統一的飲食策略輔助標準治療。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本篇範圍性文獻回顧填補了鐵質過量治療管理中飲食與飲食因子證據整合的空白，首次系統性地繪製了相關研究。然而，文獻回顧結果揭示了高質量臨床試驗的數量有限，且不同飲食因子對鐵代謝影響的報告不一致，成為未來研究的重大限制，凸顯了制定統一、有實證基礎飲食策略的迫切需求。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Investigating the Role of Diet and Dietary Factors in the Therapeutic Management of Iron Overload: A Scoping Review.
**Abstract:** Iron overload (IOL) results from excessive iron accumulation and presents as primary (genetic) or secondary (from transfusions or iron therapy). Management is predominantly invasive-phlebotomy and iron chelation-which, while effective, are costly and can impair quality of life. No standardized dietary guidelines exist. The aim of this scoping review was to map the evidence on diet and dietary factors in the therapeutic management of IOL-associated conditions. We followed Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) scoping review methodology and a Population/Concept/Context (PCC) framework to define eligibility. A targeted, JBI 3-step search strategy was run in PubMed (National Library of Medicine), Web of Science (Clarivate), Embase (Elsevier), and CINAHL Ultimate (EBSCO), supplemented by gray-literature sources. Records were managed in Covidence; duplicates were removed, then citations were screened by title/abstract using prespecified eligibility criteria and research questions. Search quality was guided by the Peer Review of Electronic Search Strategies (PRESS) checklist, and the study selection is reported in a PRISMA-ScR flow diagram. Research articles were assigned JBI levels of evidence. Evidence was synthesized descriptively and mapped by dietary factor in narrative summaries and tables. Forty sources (38 peer-reviewed articles; 2 gray-literature articles) met the inclusion criteria. The reported dietary factors potentially relevant to IOL management included polyphenols; vitamins and minerals; diet alteration; foods/food additives; alcohol; and amino and fatty acids. The findings were integrated into a visual workflow, and the current evidence on dietary factors in the therapeutic management of IOL was mapped. Substantial gaps were identified, including limited high-quality clinical trials and inconsistent reporting of the effects of specific dietary factors on iron metabolism. The available literature suggests that diet and selected dietary factors may contribute to improved patient outcomes, highlighting the need for rigorous, well-designed studies to develop unified, evidence-based dietary strategies to complement the standard therapies.
