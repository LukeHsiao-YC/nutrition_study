### 重點摘要
這項研究利用歐洲28項人口研究的整合數據（n=81,828），分析了20世紀歐洲出生世代的近視盛行率與眼軸長度變化趨勢。研究發現，近視盛行率在20世紀的出生世代中增加了三倍多，從1900年代的18.0%上升到1990年代的61.4%，且各嚴重程度與性別間的增幅相似。同時，眼軸長度在連續的幾十年中也顯著增長，顯示出全人口範圍內的變化。這些結果強調了預防策略和干預措施的重要性，以應對日益增長的近視負擔，並減輕對醫療系統和社會造成的潛在壓力。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
過去歐洲地區關於近視長期流行病學趨勢的數據有限，本研究透過大規模、多國的出生世代分析，填補了歐洲近視盛行率和眼軸長度長期變化的研究空白，提供了重要的證據。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The growing burden of myopia in Europe: evidence from population-based cohorts.
**Abstract:** Myopia is a childhood-onset condition driven by excessive axial ocular elongation, associated with increased risk of sight-threatening complications and irreversible visual impairment. Despite rising global prevalence, epidemiological data on long-term trends in Europe remain limited. This study examines temporal trends in myopia prevalence and axial length (AL) across European adult birth cohorts. We conducted a comparative birth-cohort analysis using harmonised data from 28 population-based studies (n = 81,828) across nine countries participating in the European Eye Epidemiology (E3) Consortium. Myopia prevalence (overall, by severity, and sex) were estimated using individual participant data random-effects meta-analysis. AL distributions were assessed by estimating percentiles across birth cohorts. Myopia prevalence more than tripled across 20th-century birth cohorts, rising from 18.0% (95% confidence interval [CI]: 15.2%, 21.3%) in the 1900s to 61.4% (95% CI: 55.5%, 66.9%) in the 1990s (p < 0.001), with increased prevalence across all severity levels. Standardised to the 2024 European population, myopia prevalence was estimated at 42.7% (95% CI: 40.2%, 45.1%). Myopia prevalence was similar between males and females overall. AL was longer in successive decades (1920-1970) across all population centiles (p < 0.001), indicating a population-wide shift. Myopia prevalence and AL have increased markedly across European birth cohorts throughout the twentieth century, affecting all severity levels and both sexes. These findings underscore the importance of preventive strategies targeting modifiable environmental risk factors, alongside continued evaluation of interventions intended to slow myopia progression, as continued increases may strain healthcare systems and society.
