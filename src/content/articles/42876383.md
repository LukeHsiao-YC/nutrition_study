### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討敘利亞難民在挪威定居後，糧食不安全狀況隨居住時間的變化。研究採用前瞻性世代追蹤設計，在難民定居挪威後第1年和第4年對其進行問卷調查。結果顯示，糧食不安全盛行率在1年後為18%，4年後為23%，比例大致維持穩定，但單獨遷徙的難民比與家人一同遷徙的難民面臨更高的糧食不安全風險。研究結論指出，在難民定居3年後，糧食不安全盛行率仍保持穩定，並強調家庭團聚策略對於保障難民營養安全的重要性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了敘利亞難民在重新安置後，糧食不安全狀況隨時間變化的長期追蹤數據空白，並識別出單獨遷徙作為增加糧食不安全風險的關鍵因素。然而，研究的限制可能包括完成兩次調查的樣本量相對較小，這可能影響了對其他社會人口學或健康相關因素影響的檢測能力。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Temporal trends in food insecurity among Syrian refugees resettling in Norway: a prospective cohort study.
**Abstract:** To examine changes in food insecurity among Syrian refugees after resettlement in Norway by length of stay. A longitudinal cohort study surveying Syrian refugees 1- and 4-years post-resettlement to Norway as part of the CHART/Integration for Health study. Using the Seattle short form of the Household Food Security Survey Module, participants were asked if they had worried that food would run out or had experienced that food did not last over the recent year. Changes in food insecurity over time were examined. Additionally, the influence of socio-demographic, migration-related and health-related factors on these changes was assessed. Community setting. Adult Syrian refugees granted resettlement to Norway. Totally, 353 Syrian refugees participated at baseline, and 132 completed the survey at both timepoints. The proportion reporting food insecurity was nearly unchanged from 1 to 4 years after arrival (from 18 % to 23 %; OR 1·3, 95 % CI 0·8-2·0). Individuals escaping Syria alone experienced increased food insecurity compared with those migrating with family. No modifying effect of gender, age, education, cohabiting with partner, number of children, work status prior to displacement, trauma exposure or poor mental health at baseline on change in food insecurity over time was identified. One year after resettlement in Norway, slightly more than one in six Syrian refugees reported food insecurity, with prevalence remaining stable 3 years later. Solo migration increased the risk of food insecurity over time, suggesting the potential value of strategies that keep families together during migration to help safeguard nutritional security.
