### 重點摘要
這篇系統性回顧與統合分析旨在探討飲食糖攝取與認知障礙風險的關聯，以解決過往研究結果不一致的問題。研究篩選了10項前瞻性世代研究共257,761名成人數據，分析總糖及多種特定糖類攝取。結果發現，總糖攝取量最高者相較最低者有較高的認知障礙風險（HR=1.23），但證據確定性較低。然而，針對特定糖類子類型，目前證據未能支持其與認知障礙存在明確關聯。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了過去個別研究結果不一致且缺乏整合的空白，首次全面性地探討了飲食總糖攝取與認知障礙的關聯。然而，本研究發現證據確定性普遍較低，特別是針對特定糖類子類型未發現明確關聯。未來的研究應著重於釐清總糖攝取與認知障礙之間的劑量反應關係。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Dietary Sugar Intake and Risk of Cognitive Disorders: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.
**Abstract:** Although prior studies have linked excessive dietary sugar intake to adverse cognitive outcomes, the findings of individual studies remain inconsistent, and there is a lack of synthesis. The objective of this study was to comprehensively examine the association between dietary sugar intake and risk of cognitive disorders. The Cochrane, Embase, PubMed, ScienceDirect, and Web of Science databases were searched from inception to December 2025. Prospective cohort, nested case-control or case-cohort studies evaluating the association of dietary total sugar or its subtypes with the risk of cognitive disorders, including cognitive impairment and dementia (including Alzheimer's disease), in adults were reviewed. Details of the studies and main outcomes were extracted independently by 2 reviewers. Among 2171 records screened, 10 prospective cohort studies comprising 12 cohorts and 257 761 participants were included. The number of cohorts and participants for each exposure was as follows: total sugar: 5 (N = 200 812); sugar-sweetened beverages: 9 (N = 214 084); fruit juice: 2 (N = 194 847); fructose: 4 (N = 197 082); sucrose: 3 (N = 194 928); lactose: 3 (N = 194 928); maltose: 3 (N = 194 928); glucose: 3 (N = 194 928). Meta-analysis indicated that individuals with the highest total sugar intake were at a higher risk of cognitive disorders compared with those with the lowest intake (hazard ratio = 1.23, 95% CI = 1.10-1.38, low certainty). No significant associations were observed for any specific sugar subtypes examined, with certainty of evidence ranging from low to very low. The present review suggests that higher total sugar intake may be associated with an increased risk of cognitive disorders. In contrast, the current evidence does not support a clear association between specific sugar subtypes and cognitive disorders. Future studies should prioritize clarifying the dose-response relationship between total sugar intake and cognitive disorders. PROSPERO registration No. CRD42025640308.
