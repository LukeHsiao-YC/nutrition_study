### 重點摘要
這項研究旨在探討住院華人成年病患過去一週及營養日當天的飲食攝取量與其出院返家機率的關聯。研究分析了10,368名病患的數據，發現過去一週飲食攝取量少於或等於50%以及營養日當天飲食攝取量少於或等於25%，都與較低的出院返家機率及較長的住院天數顯著相關。特別是對於過去飲食狀況不佳或被禁止進食的病患，此關聯性更為明顯。研究結果強調在實施禁食方案時，評估病患近期飲食史的重要性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了先前關於飲食攝取量減少對患者出院結果影響閾值不明確的空白，具體量化了低於特定飲食攝取量（如過去一週≤50%和營養日當天≤25%）如何顯著降低病患出院返家機率並延長住院時間。它強調了在臨床實踐中，特別是實施禁食方案時，評估患者近期飲食歷史的重要性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Association between reduced food intake and hospital discharge to home in Chinese adults: A cross-sectional nutritionDay survey study.
**Abstract:** Reduced food intake is a major contributor to malnutrition, but validated thresholds remain uncertain. Our study aimed to examine the associations of preceding-week food intake and nutritionDay food intake with home discharge. We conducted a cross-sectional survey study using data from 10,368 Chinese adult inpatients participating in the nutritionDay initiative from 2010 to 2024. Cox proportional hazards regression and Kaplan-Meier survival analyses were used to examine associations between reduced food intake and home discharge within 30 days after nutritionDay. Preceding-week food intake ≤50% was associated with a lower probability of home discharge and prolonged median length of stay after nutritionDay compared with preceding-week intake > 50%, particularly among patients with nutritionDay intake ≤25% (9 vs. 7 days, P < 0.001) or who were forbidden to eat (11 vs. 7 days, P < 0.001). Additionally, nutritionDay food intake ≤25% was associated with a lower discharge probability and a longer median length of stay after nutritionDay among patients with preceding-week intake >50% (HR 0.93, 95% CI [0.87-0.98], P = 0.01; 7 vs. 6 days, P < 0.001) and ≤ 50% (HR 0.86, 95% CI [0.76-0.98], P = 0.03; 9 vs. 6 days, P < 0.001). Preceding-week food intake ≤ 50% and nutritionDay food intake ≤25% were each associated with a lower likelihood of home discharge. Our findings highlight the importance of assessing recent dietary history when implementing fasting protocols, particularly for patients with poor preceding-week intake or patients prohibited from eating on nutritionDay.
