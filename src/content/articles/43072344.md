### 重點摘要
這篇系統性回顧評估了維生素C補充劑對全髖關節和膝關節置換術後臨床結果的影響。分析了十項隨機對照試驗共1,172名患者，結果顯示維生素C顯著降低術後疼痛分數、嗎啡消耗量及發炎指標，並改善關節活動度。此外，一項大型研究指出維生素C能降低複雜性局部疼痛症候群的發生率，且未報告顯著不良事件。儘管初步證據顯示其在疼痛管理和炎症反應方面有潛在益處，但研究方法的品質中等，未來需進行高質量、大規模隨機對照試驗以確立明確的臨床建議。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了維生素C補充劑對全髖關節和膝關節置換術後結果影響的研究空白，初步提供了其在疼痛管理和炎症反應方面的潛在益處證據。然而，本系統性回顧未預先註冊，且納入研究的方法學品質中等（如分配隱匿和盲法不一致）及結果異質性是主要限制。未來的研究需要高質量、大規模的隨機對照試驗，以建立明確的臨床建議。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The effect of vitamin C supplementation on outcomes of total hip and knee arthroplasty: A systematic review.
**Abstract:** Vitamin C plays a crucial role in collagen synthesis, wound healing, and inflammatory response modulation. This systematic review evaluates the effect of vitamin C supplementation on clinical outcomes following total hip (THA) or knee arthroplasty (TKA). A systematic search was conducted across Web of Science, Scopus, Embase, PubMed, and CENTRAL databases through June 11, 2025. Randomized controlled trials, prospective cohorts, cross-sectional studies, and case-control studies examining adult patients undergoing primary THA or TKA who received vitamin C supplementation were included. Exclusion criteria included: Pediatric populations; Revision arthroplasty; Malignancy; Studies combining vitamin C with other interventions when results could not be isolated; non-English articles; Narrative reviews, Expert opinions, and corrections to papers. Two reviewers independently assessed risk of bias using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (RoB 1). Due to heterogeneity in outcome measures, dosing regimens, and intervention timing, results were synthesized narratively and organized by outcome categories. This review received no specific funding and was not prospectively registered. Ten randomized controlled trials involving 1,172 patients were included. Six studies examined TKA, three examined THA, and one examined bilateral TKA. Vitamin C dosing ranged from 500 mg oral daily to 15 g intravenous. Vitamin C supplementation significantly reduced postoperative pain scores and morphine consumption in multiple studies. Inflammatory markers (CRP, IL-6, ESR) were consistently lower in patients who received vitamin C perioperatively. Complex regional pain syndrome incidence was reduced from 12.2% to 3.9% (p = 0.008) in one large TKA study. Range of motion improvements and reduced oxidative stress markers were observed in patients who received vitamin C. No significant adverse events were reported. Vitamin C supplementation shows potential benefits in THA or TKA outcomes, with preliminary evidence suggesting improvements in pain management and inflammatory response. However, findings are limited by moderate methodological quality, including unclear allocation concealment and inconsistent blinding. While promising, these results warrant cautious interpretation, necessitating high-quality, large-scale randomized controlled trials to establish definitive clinical recommendations. This systematic review was not prospectively registered.
