### 重點摘要
這項統合分析旨在探討運動員低能量可用性 (LEA) 對身體組成與荷爾蒙狀態的影響，因LEA與生理功能下降和表現受損有關。研究從多個資料庫篩選出11篇橫斷面研究，共分析了1006名運動員，其中半數有LEA。結果顯示，LEA運動員的體重、BMI和全身骨礦物質密度Z分數較低，且皮質醇水平較高，提示生殖腺荷爾蒙和骨骼健康可能受損。本研究系統性地檢視了LEA運動員的內分泌反應，證實LEA與其不良健康結果之間存在關聯。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究首次系統性地探討了運動員低能量可用性與內分泌反應之間的關聯，填補了該領域的統合分析空白。然而，由於納入的皆為橫斷面研究，本研究僅能揭示相關性而非因果關係，未來研究應考慮採用前瞻性或介入性設計以進一步確認因果效應。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** A study for markers of low energy availability in athletes: a meta-analysis of cross-sectional studies.
**Abstract:** When low energy availability (LEA) falls below threshold levels, it is associated with declines in physiological functions such as bone health and endocrine function. This is associated with diminished physical capabilities and impaired performance in athletes. Therefore, this study aimed to conduct a meta-analysis examining body composition and hormonal status in athletes according to low energy availability (LEA) status, with the aim of identifying physiological characteristics associated with energy imbalance. To compare LEA and normal energy availability (NEA), a literature search was conducted using keywords related to body composition and hormones by limiting it to studies from January 2007 to July 2023 in Web of Science, Scopus, SPORTDiscus, PubMed, Google Scholar, Cochrane Library, and ScienceDirect databases. As a result, 11 studies were finally selected. A total of 11 studies met the inclusion criteria for this meta-analysis, encompassing 1006 athletes (471 females [49.4%] and 535 males [50.6%]), of whom 509 (50.6%) were identified as having LEA. Athletes with LEA tended to have lower weight, body mass index (BMI), and total body BMD Z-score. Furthermore, several physiological markers were lower, while cortisol levels were higher and leptin levels did not differ significantly. The results suggest that athletes with LEA may experience impairments in physiological functions, including alterations in gonadal hormones and bone mineral density markers. This study systematically examined endocrine responses in athletes with low energy availability. These findings suggest that LEA is associated with adverse health outcomes in athletes.
