### 重點摘要
這篇敘述性回顧旨在綜合評估間歇性斷食（IF）在代謝症候群（MetS）管理中的臨床證據、分子機制和未來方向。研究發現，特定的IF方案能對體重、腰圍和部分心臟代謝風險指標產生適度改善，但對血糖、血脂和肝臟相關指標的影響則因試驗而異且異質性高。雖然高品質人類機制數據有限，但史前研究指出其機制涉及生理時鐘同步和營養感應網路調節。總體而言，間歇性斷食可能是代謝症候群可行的非藥物輔助介入，但現有證據尚不一致，未來需更多研究以實現精準生活型態醫學。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇回顧文獻指出，間歇性斷食在代謝症候群中的臨床有效性、確切分子機制，以及其益處是源於斷食時機還是體重減輕所導致的能量限制，都仍存在關鍵的不確定性。未來的研究需要解決方案優化、長期依從性、個體間代謝變異性等未解爭議，並需改進證據品質評估、開發個人化斷食方案，以及嚴格評估其長期安全性和可持續性，以推動精準生活型態醫學的發展。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Intermittent Fasting in Metabolic Syndrome: Clinical Evidence, Molecular Mechanisms, and Future Perspectives.
**Abstract:** The objective for this narrative review was to synthesize and critically assess the clinical evidence, molecular mechanisms, and future directions of intermittent fasting (IF) for managing metabolic syndrome (MetS). Metabolic syndrome is a complex cardiometabolic disorder associated with abnormal dietary patterns, epigenetic regulation, and gene activity, and characterized by central adiposity, insulin resistance, hypertension, and dyslipidemia. Intermittent fasting regimens, including time-restricted eating, alternate-day fasting, and the 5:2 fasting regimen, are promising nutritional interventions. Nonetheless, large, randomized trials have reported mixed clinical outcomes, and critical uncertainties persist regarding clinical effectiveness, molecular mechanisms, and whether benefits arise from fasting timing or weight-loss-driven energy restriction. A comprehensive literature search was conducted in major databases through July 2026. Articles, including those reporting on randomized controlled trials, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and relevant preclinical mechanistic investigations in MetS, were selected and their data synthesized. Findings show that certain IF regimens produce modest improvements in body weight, waist circumference, and selected cardiometabolic risk markers; however, effects on glycemic control, lipid profiles, and liver-related end points are heterogeneous across trials. Observed clinical responses vary by fasting protocol, participant phenotype, and adherence, and high-quality human mechanistic data remain limited. Mechanistic insights, largely derived from preclinical models, highlight circadian rhythm synchronization and nutrient-sensing network modulation as key biological pathways. Important unresolved controversies cover protocol optimization, long-term adherence, and inter-individual metabolic variability. Intermittent fasting may serve as a feasible nonpharmacological adjunct intervention for MetS and related conditions, yet existing evidence is inconsistent. Translation toward precision lifestyle medicine requires resolution of ongoing controversies, improved evaluation of evidence quality, development of personalized fasting protocols, and rigorous assessment of long-term safety and sustainability across diverse patient populations.
