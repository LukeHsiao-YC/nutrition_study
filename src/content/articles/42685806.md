### 重點摘要
2022年全球食品價格飆升，本研究調查了五個國家成人對此的感知及其對食品採購行為的影響，並考慮了社會人口特徵和糧食安全狀況。透過分析20,240名成人於2022年國際食品政策研究的線上問卷數據，發現78%至85%的受訪者感受到食品價格上漲，並普遍轉向購買較便宜的品牌。受訪者不僅減少了非核心食物的購買，連核心食物如水果和蔬菜的購買也減少，尤其在收入較低和糧食不安全的家庭中更為顯著。研究結果凸顯食品可負擔性下降的嚴峻問題，強調需要制定社會與營養政策，以確保所有人都能獲得負擔得起的營養食品。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
此研究填補了全球範圍內針對2022年食品價格上漲對消費者感知、實際採購策略及核心/非核心食物消費影響的實證數據空白，並首次將這些影響與收入充足感和家庭糧食安全狀況進行了跨國連結。然而，由於其橫斷面研究設計，無法建立因果關係，且數據為自報式，可能存在回憶偏誤。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Adults' perceptions of rising food price and their impact on food purchasing: findings from the 2022 International Food Policy Study.
**Abstract:** Rising food prices reached record levels in 2022, raising global concern. This study examined consumer perceptions of food price increases in 2022 and their perceived effects on food purchasing in five countries, as well as differences by socio-demographic characteristics and household food security status. Data from 20,240 adults in the International Food Policy Study (2022), a cross-sectional online survey conducted in Australia, Canada, Mexico, the United Kingdom, and the United States, were analyzed. Respondents reported whether food had become more expensive for them in the last year and whether they altered ten food purchasing strategies and purchasing of three core and four non-core food categories in response to higher food prices. Regression models assessed differences by country, socio-demographic characteristics and household food security status. Across all countries, 78% to 85% of respondents perceived an increase in food prices in 2022. In response to higher food prices, the most commonly reported food purchasing strategy was switching to cheaper brands (34% to 43%). Despite some cross-country differences, respondents in all countries reported reductions in purchasing of non-core foods, such as desserts and non-alcoholic beverages, but also of core foods, such as fruits and vegetables. In all countries, reported reductions in core food purchases were associated with lower perceived income adequacy and household food insecurity. Most respondents perceived food as less affordable in 2022 and reported diverse shifts in food purchasing as a result, highlighting the need for social and nutritional policies promoting access to affordable food, including nutritious options, for all.
