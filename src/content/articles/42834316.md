### 重點摘要
由於阿拉巴馬州孕產婦和嬰兒健康狀況不佳，死亡率高且面臨醫療資源不足等挑戰，阿拉巴馬州圍產期品質合作夥伴(ALPQC)舉辦了全州性的傾聽會議，旨在整合病患視角以改善孕產婦健康。該研究於2024年7月至9月間，在全州舉辦了九場「媽媽們想要什麼」傾聽會議，共250多名參與者（其中75.8%為黑人）分享了其孕產期的親身經歷。會議歸納出六大主題，包括照護的可近性與連續性、教育與資源、自信與賦權、溝通與選擇、同情與尊重，以及感恩。這些質性資料揭示了阿拉巴馬州孕產婦健康照護中的關鍵缺口與改進機會，強調了以病患為中心的策略在制定政策和臨床實踐中的價值。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究透過蒐集阿拉巴馬州孕產婦及其支持者的親身經驗，填補了該地區在以病患為中心視角下對孕產婦健康照護障礙與促進因素的質性理解空白。它強調了質性資料在形塑公共政策、臨床實踐與社區參與方面的重要性，特別是在改善高風險區域的孕產婦健康方面。鑑於研究的質性性質，其結果的普遍性可能受限於參與者的地理與人口特徵，未來研究可考慮結合更廣泛的量化數據進行驗證。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Maternal Health Community Engagement: Insights from Statewide Maternal Health Listening Sessions in Alabama.
**Abstract:** Alabama ranks among the worst states for maternal and infant health outcomes, with maternal mortality rates rising to 56.9 per 100,000 live births between 2018 and 2021. Contributing factors include rural hospital closures, limited obstetric care access, high prevalence of chronic conditions, and lack of Medicaid expansion. To address these disparities, the Alabama Perinatal Quality Collaborative (ALPQC) launched statewide listening sessions to incorporate patient perspectives into maternal health improvement efforts. This study aimed to (1) capture lived experiences of individuals during prenatal, labor/delivery, and postpartum periods; (2) identify barriers and facilitators to maternal health care access and quality in Alabama; and (3) inform clinical, programmatic, and policy strategies to reduce maternal mortality and morbidity through patient-centered approaches. Nine "What Moms Want" listening sessions were conducted across Alabama from July to September 2024. Community organizations hosted sessions to ensure geographic and demographic representation, particularly in rural and underserved areas. Participants included mothers, fathers, and support partners. Sessions followed a structured guide addressing prenatal, labor/delivery, and postpartum experiences. Over 250 participants shared experiences, with 75.8% identifying as Black. Six major themes emerged: (1) access and continuity of care, (2) education and resources, (3) self-confidence and empowerment, (4) communication and choice, (5) compassion and respect, and (6) gratitude. Listening sessions revealed critical gaps and opportunities for patient-centered strategies to improve maternal health in Alabama. Findings underscore the value of qualitative data in shaping policy, clinical practice, and community engagement.
