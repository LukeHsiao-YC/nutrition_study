### 重點摘要
過去對照護者兒童發燒管理的研究多歸因於發燒恐懼症和知識不足，且常使用未經驗證的問卷。本研究開發並驗證了首個標準化情境式工具，評估290位照護者在兒童發燒管理中的判斷技能，並分析其與社會人口學及心理因素的關聯。結果顯示照護者判斷與專家有中等程度的一致性，且發燒知識多寡與焦慮程度顯著影響判斷準確性。此工具證實了情緒與認知因素在親職決策中的關鍵作用，並為未來改善發燒管理提供了教育介入的基礎。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了過去兒童發燒管理評估工具多為未經驗證問卷的空白，首次開發並驗證了一個標準化的情境式工具，能夠評估照護者在發燒管理中的判斷技能，而非僅限於知識缺陷。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Development and validation of a scenario-based judgment skills scale on parental fever management.
**Abstract:** Fever management among caregivers has often been described through the lens of "fever phobia," that is, the exaggerated fear of fever, frequently attributed to knowledge deficits and assessed using non-validated questionnaires. This study developed and validated the first standardized instrument to assess caregivers' judgment skills in managing childhood fever, focusing on how knowledge and other factors interact to shape decision-making. A cross-sectional study was conducted among 290 caregivers of children recruited from pediatric clinics and social media in Southern Switzerland (March 2024-May 2025). Participants completed nine realistic fever-management scenarios, each featuring four response options ranked by expert pediatricians. Agreement between participants and experts was evaluated using Spearman's rank correlation coefficients (ρ) and compared to 200 sets of simulated random responses. Hierarchical multiple regression identified sociodemographic (sex, education, country) and psychological (knowledge, concern, self-efficacy) predictors of expert-aligned judgment. Caregivers demonstrated moderate alignment with expert judgment, performing substantially better than chance. Sociodemographic characteristics explained a modest proportion of differences in judgment accuracy. Adding psychological factors significantly improved prediction: greater fever knowledge was associated with more accurate judgment, whereas higher levels of concern were associated with less accurate decisions. Self-efficacy showed a small additional contribution. This validated, scenario-based instrument offers a reliable way to assess parental decision-making about childhood fever. Findings emphasize that emotional and cognitive factors, rather than sociodemographic background alone, drive judgment accuracy. The tool provides a foundation for targeted education and intervention strategies to improve fever management.
