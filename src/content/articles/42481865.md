### 重點摘要
這項研究旨在探討青少年母親的身體組成是否與其血液和母乳中的代謝激素（瘦素、飢餓素和脂聯素）相關，以及這些激素是否與嬰兒的身體組成和人體測量指標相關。研究發現，較高的母親體脂肪量，特別是手臂和腹部區域的脂肪，與血清和母乳中較高的瘦素濃度有關。此外，母乳中的瘦素濃度較高與嬰兒身長較短有關，而飢餓素則與嬰兒的多項人體測量指標呈弱負相關。結果表明，母親血清和母乳中的瘦素與母親肥胖有關，且瘦素和飢餓素可能參與嬰兒生長和身體組成。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
此研究填補了青少年母親身體組成、其血液與母乳中代謝激素，以及這些激素與嬰兒身體組成關係的知識空白，特別是在青少年母親這一特定人群中。儘管研究提示瘦素和飢餓素可能在嬰兒生長中發揮作用，但未來的研究需要更深入地闡明其確切的機制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Adolescent mothers' body composition and metabolic hormones (adiponectin, ghrelin, and leptin) in blood and human milk: associations with infant body composition.
**Abstract:** This study aimed to investigate whether maternal body composition is related to blood and human milk metabolic hormones (adiponectin, ghrelin, and leptin) and whether these hormones are associated with infant body composition and anthropometric indicators. Fifty-five adolescent mother-infant pairs were evaluated at postpartum. Maternal and infant body composition was assessed using dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry. Leptin, adiponectin, and ghrelin were measured in maternal circulation and human milk. Multiple linear regression models adjusted for days postpartum assessed associations between (1) maternal characteristics and hormones in maternal blood and milk and (2) milk hormones and infant measures (also adjusted for birth weight). Serum and human milk leptin were directly associated (r = 0.472; p = 0.014), whereas serum and human milk ghrelin were inversely correlated (r = -0.320; p = 0.017). Greater maternal fat mass, particularly in the arms and android region, was associated with higher leptin concentrations in serum (β = 5.95; 95% CI: 1.65-10.3 and β = 5.90; 95% CI: 0.871-10.9, respectively) and milk (β = 0.294; 95% CI: 0.132-0.455 and β = 0.280; 95% CI: 0.118-0.443, respectively). Higher milk leptin was associated with lower infant length (β = -1.21; 95% CI: -2.24, -0.183). Milk ghrelin was weakly and inversely associated with several infant anthropometric measures, whereas no significant associations were found for milk adiponectin. The results suggest that maternal leptin in serum and milk, but not ghrelin or adiponectin, is associated with maternal adiposity. Leptin and ghrelin in human milk may be involved in infant growth and body composition, whereas adiponectin does not appear to play a major role.
