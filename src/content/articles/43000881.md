### 重點摘要
關於維持性血液透析患者膳食鉀攝取與死亡率的關係存在爭議，特別是來自不同食物來源的鉀。本多中心前瞻性世代研究使用24小時飲食回憶評估鉀攝取量，並利用Cox模型分析其與全因和心血管死亡率的關聯。結果顯示，總膳食鉀攝取量最低的三分位組與較高的全因死亡率相關，而植物性鉀攝取量最低的三分位組則與全因及心血管死亡率均增加有關，且膳食鉀攝取量與血清鉀水平無關。研究結論指出，較低的膳食鉀攝取，特別是來自植物性食物的鉀，與維持性血液透析患者較高的死亡風險相關，支持個別化的膳食鉀管理。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇研究填補了維持性血液透析患者膳食鉀攝取（特別是來自不同食物來源）與死亡率之間長期存在的爭議。它挑戰了傳統上對所有膳食鉀一律嚴格限制的觀點，指出過低的膳食鉀攝取量（尤其是植物性來源）反而增加死亡風險，並提供證據支持對膳食鉀管理採取更個別化的策略。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Dietary potassium intake from different food sources and mortality in maintenance haemodialysis patients: a multicentre prospective cohort study.
**Abstract:** The association between dietary potassium intake and mortality in maintenance haemodialysis (MHD) patients remains controversial, particularly regarding potassium from different dietary sources. In this multicentre prospective cohort study, dietary potassium intake was assessed using 24-hour dietary recalls. Cox proportional hazards models were used to evaluate associations with all-cause and cardiovascular mortality. Restricted cubic splines assessed dose-response relationships, and sensitivity analyses included adjustment for dietary quality indicators, exclusion of serum potassium, exclusion of deaths within the first year, and competing-risk models. The lowest tertile of total dietary potassium intake was associated with higher all-cause mortality (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR]: 1·36, 95 % CI: 1·02-1·81). The lowest tertile of plant-based potassium intake was associated with increased all-cause mortality (aHR: 1·54, 95% CI: 1·19-1·99) and cardiovascular mortality (aHR: 1·50, 95% CI: 1·05-2·14). Restricted cubic spline analyses demonstrated inverse dose-response relationships between total dietary potassium intake and both mortality outcomes and between plant-based potassium intake and all-cause mortality. Dietary potassium intake was not associated with serum potassium levels or hyperkalaemia risk, whereas severe hyperkalaemia (serum potassium ≥6·0 mmol/L) independently predicted cardiovascular mortality (aHR: 1·59, 95% CI: 1·06-2·38). Lower dietary potassium intake, particularly from plant sources, was associated with increased mortality risk among MHD patients. These findings support a more individualised approach to dietary potassium management in MHD patients.
