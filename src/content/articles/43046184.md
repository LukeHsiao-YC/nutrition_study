### 重點摘要
該研究旨在探討久坐行為與常見眼疾（如老年黃斑部病變、青光眼、乾眼症、視網膜靜脈阻塞）之間的關聯，此前相關證據有限且不一致。研究人員利用英國生物樣本庫數據進行了一項大規模前瞻性世代分析，追蹤約14年，並使用多變項 Cox 比例風險模型調整潛在混淆因子。初始分析顯示久坐行為與所有眼疾風險增加有關，但在多變項調整後，僅老年黃斑部病變呈現邊緣統計學意義的關聯，其餘眼疾則無顯著關聯。總體而言，在調整潛在混淆因子後，久坐行為與這些常見眼疾的發生風險並無獨立的統計學顯著關聯。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了久坐行為與多種常見眼疾（如老年黃斑部病變、青光眼、乾眼症、視網膜靜脈阻塞）之間關聯證據不足的空白。然而，研究結果不應被解釋為排除因果關係，未來的研究需要納入客觀的暴露評估、重複測量和因果推論方法，以更明確地闡釋久坐行為在眼疾發展中的潛在作用。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The association between sedentary behavior and eye diseases: a prospective study from the UK Biobank.
**Abstract:** Sedentary behavior (SB) is a well-established risk factor for multiple chronic diseases. However, evidence regarding its association with common ocular disorders remains limited and inconsistent. This study investigated the prospective associations between SB and the risks of incident age-related macular degeneration (AMD), glaucoma, dry eye disease (DED), and retinal vein occlusion (RVO). We conducted a prospective cohort analysis using data from the UK Biobank. Separate analytical cohorts were established for AMD (n = 374,552), glaucoma (n = 370,997), DED (n = 374,398), and RVO (n = 374,924). During a median follow-up of approximately 14.04 years, 7,266 AMD, 8,112 glaucoma, 452 DED, and 786 RVO cases were identified. Pre-existing ocular diseases were identified using ICD-10 records and self-reported diagnoses. Incident AMD, glaucoma, and RVO were identified from ICD-10 records, whereas incident DED was identified by self-report. Cox proportional hazards models, restricted cubic spline (RCS) analyses, interaction analyses, and sensitivity analyses were performed. In unadjusted models, higher SB was associated with increased risks of AMD, glaucoma, DED and RVO. After multivariable adjustment, only a borderline statistically significant association remained for AMD (HR = 1.01; 95% CI: 1.00-1.02), whereas no significant associations were observed for glaucoma, DED, or RVO. RCS analyses demonstrated mild J-shaped associations between SB and AMD and glaucoma, with the lowest risk observed at approximately 3.7 h/day; however, these associations became nonsignificant after full adjustment. Stratified interaction analyses identified significant effect modification for AMD by body mass index (BMI), glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c), and physical activity (PA) level, and for DED by sex and BMI. Sensitivity analyses, including additional adjustment for physical activity and sleep duration and analyses of domain-specific sedentary behaviors, did not alter the overall conclusions. After adjustment for potential confounders, no statistically significant independent associations were observed between SB and incident AMD, glaucoma, DED, or RVO. However, these findings should not be interpreted as evidence against a causal relationship. Further studies incorporating objective exposure assessment, repeated measurements, and causal inference approaches are warranted to clarify the potential role of sedentary behavior in ocular disease development.
