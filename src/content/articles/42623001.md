### 重點摘要
本研究旨在瑞典全國性成人群體中，評估多維度診斷共病指數 (MDCI) 和藥物共病指數 (DCI) 相較於Charlson共病指數 (CCI) 在預測死亡風險和共病調整預期壽命 (CALE) 方面的表現。這項開放世代研究納入逾千萬瑞典成人，結果顯示MDCI (C指數 0.882) 和 DCI (C指數 0.871) 在區分一年期死亡風險上顯著優於CCI (C指數 0.811)。研究證明，MDCI與DCI的組合能更精確地劃分死亡風險，並在同齡個體中識別出比CCI更廣泛的CALE範圍。結論指出，這些更全面的共病指數在所有年齡組和性別中均改善了死亡風險區分能力，並能提供更寬廣的共病調整預期壽命評估。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
先前的研究已在較小規模的特定人群中驗證了MDCI和DCI的優越性，而本研究透過涵蓋瑞典全國逾千萬成人數據，首次全面且大規模地評估了這些綜合共病指數在不同年齡和性別群體中的實際表現。這填補了將這些更精確的工具應用於全國性人口數據的空白，並證明它們能夠比傳統CCI提供更廣泛且更精細的共病調整預期壽命評估。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Comorbidity-adjusted life expectancy using comprehensive summary measures of medical history: nationwide, population-based study in Sweden.
**Abstract:** A multidimensional diagnosis-based comorbidity index (MDCI) and a drug comorbidity index (DCI) have demonstrated superior discrimination of mortality risk compared to the Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI) in smaller specific populations. We aimed to evaluate performance of these indices in an entire population according to age, sex, and to estimate comorbidity-adjusted life expectancy (CALE), i.e. life expectancy conditional on age and comorbidity. Open cohort study of all adults residing in Sweden in 2006-2022. Discrimination of mortality risk at one year of follow-up was assessed through the C-index. CALE was estimated using parametric survival models based on age and comorbidity. 10 079 529 individuals were included. The MDCI (C-index 0.882 [95% CI 0.882-0.882]) and DCI (C-index 0.871 [95% CI 0.870-0.871]) improved discrimination of mortality risk compared to the CCI (C-index 0.811 [95% CI 0.811-0.812]). For each age above 40 the combination of MDCI and DCI clearly separated risk of death within levels of CCI and identified a wider range of CALE than CCI. At age 70 CALE based on the combination of MDCI and DCI varied from 7.0 to 17.9 years at the 5th and 95th percentile in men, and from 8.9 to 20.2 years in women. CALE based on CCI varied from 11.0 to 16.1 years in men and 11.7 to 18.4 years in women. Comprehensive comorbidity indices improved discrimination of mortality risk over the CCI in all age groups in men and women and identified a wider range of comorbidity-adjusted life expectancy in individuals of the same age.
