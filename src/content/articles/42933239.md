### 重點摘要
罕見疾病患者常面臨延遲診斷、照護碎片化及資訊不足等挑戰，而健康素養 (HL) 則反映個人技能與其如何與醫療體系互動。本研究於義大利對81名罕見疾病患者及照護者進行橫斷面混合方法研究，使用線上問卷調查其照護路徑、健康素養 (HLS19-Q12) 並進行開放式問題訪談。結果顯示整體健康素養偏低，但較高的健康素養與對專科中心不知情、缺乏專科護理師或對其角色不確定有關；質性分析則揭示治療複雜性、照護路徑破碎化與資訊需求未被滿足。研究推論健康素養可能是一種對碎片化照護的適應性反應，而非僅是個人能力，或與更高的病患參與度、醫療利用差異或對醫療系統限制的認知有關。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻提出了健康素養可能不僅是個人能力，而是一種對碎片化照護的適應性反應的新觀點。它也指出，需要進一步研究來區分高健康素養是否源於更高的病患參與度、不同的醫療利用模式，或是對醫療系統限制的更深層次認知。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Health literacy in rare diseases: Associations with perceived healthcare organization, and nursing visibility.
**Abstract:** Rare diseases are often associated with delayed diagnosis, fragmented care, limited access to specialized services, and substantial informational burden for patients and caregivers. Health literacy (HL) may reflect individual skills and how those individuals interact with the organization of healthcare systems. To examine the association between HL and selected organizational and care-related factors among people living with rare diseases and caregivers. A cross-sectional mixed-methods study was conducted in Italy between February and March 2026. Adults with a rare disease and caregivers completed an online questionnaire including care pathway variables, open-ended questions, and HLS19-Q12. Quantitative data were analyzed using descriptive statistics and multivariable linear regression; qualitative responses underwent inductive thematic analysis. Eighty-one participants were included (mean age 34.5 years; 67.9% female). Overall HL was low (mean 48.5, SD 27.8). Higher HL was associated with being unaware of specialized centers (β=20.038, p = 0.022) and with reporting absence of a specialized nurse (β=24.269, p = 0.016) or uncertainty regarding such a role (β=23.760, p = 0.008). Qualitative findings showed therapeutic complexity, fragmented care pathways, unmet informational needs, and the importance of professional support. HL may reflect an adaptive response to fragmented care rather than as an individual competence only. Greater patient engagement, differences in healthcare utilization, or increased awareness of healthcare system limitations among individuals with higher health literacy, are equally plausible explanations.
