### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討紐約州帶薪家庭假(PFL)政策對紐約市職業孕婦親密伴侶暴力(IPV)的影響。研究利用孕產婦風險監測系統(PRAMS)數據，採用雙重差分(DiD)方法，比較了紐約市與沒有PFL政策州的職業孕婦。結果顯示，紐約市的職業孕婦在懷孕期間經歷IPV的比例減少了近30%，表明經濟支持政策可能對預期照護者具有預防效果。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了帶薪家庭假等經濟支持政策在預防懷孕期間親密伴侶暴力方面實證證據的空白。然而，作者也指出未來仍需進行額外研究，以進一步理解這種關聯性背後的作用機制及更廣泛的適用性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Effects of Paid Family Leave Policies on Intimate Partner Violence in New York City from 2012 to 2022.
**Abstract:** Experiencing intimate partner violence (IPV) during the prenatal period can result in maternal mental health challenges, adverse birth outcomes (e.g., pre-term birth and low birth rates), and children's mental, cognitive, and physical health issues (Donovan et al., 2016; Hahn et al., 2018). Access to economic support policies, like state-sponsored paid family leave (PFL), during the perinatal period may be a strategy to reduce financial stress and prevent IPV (Niolon et al., 2017). This paper explores whether living in a state with PFL (i.e., New York) reduces IPV for working mothers during pregnancy. Data for this study are drawn from the Pregnancy Risk and Monitoring System (PRAMS). We used difference-in-differences (DiD) accounting for multiple time periods and multiple groups to estimate the average treatment effect (ATE) of paid family leave enactment in New York State using data from New York City. We found nearly a 30% reduction in IPV during pregnancy for working mothers in NYC compared to those living in states without a state-sponsored PFL policy. These findings suggest economic support policies may have an anticipatory effect on expectant caregivers. Additional research is needed to further understand this association.
