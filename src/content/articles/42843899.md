### 重點摘要
肌少症涉及肌肉質量與功能漸進式流失，植物性蛋白雖具潛力但常因消化率及必需胺基酸（特別是亮胺酸）不足而限制其效用。本研究評估發酵作為一種技術策略，透過整合體外、動物與人體研究證據，以提升植物性蛋白的營養品質與合成特性來預防及管理肌少症。結果顯示發酵能降低抗營養因子、改善胺基酸分佈與蛋白質消化率，進而增加亮胺酸等必需胺基酸的生物利用度，並透過調節肌肉合成與分解路徑、產生生物活性物質及影響腸-肌軸來支持肌肉健康。儘管發酵提供了一種優化植物性營養以維持肌肉的永續方法，其作用機制具潛力。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
儘管機制性研究結果令人鼓舞，但目前將這些發現轉化為肌少症臨床建議的人體證據仍有限，未來需要更多長期人體臨床試驗。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The Potential for Fermented Plant-Based Foods to Aid in the Prevention and Management of Sarcopenia.
**Abstract:** The potential of fermentation was evaluated as a technological strategy to enhance the nutritional quality and anabolic properties of plant proteins specifically for the prevention and management of sarcopenia. Sarcopenia involves a progressive loss of muscle mass and function linked to "inflammaging," digestive changes, and anabolic resistance. Although plant-based proteins are increasingly popular and important for planetary health, their anabolic potential is traditionally limited by lower digestibility and suboptimal essential amino acid profiles, particularly low leucine levels. This review synthesizes evidence from in vitro models, animal studies, and human clinical trials to examine how fermentation transforms plant-based foods and influences muscle metabolism pathways. Evidence suggests that fermentation reduces antinutritional factors, alters amino acid profiles, and improves protein digestibility, leading to increased bioavailability of essential amino acids, notably leucine. Mechanistically, fermented plant products stimulate anabolic cell signaling pathways (eg, Akt/mTORC1) and suppress the catabolic ubiquitin-proteasome pathway (eg, atrogin-1 and MuRF-1). Fermentation also generates bioactive isoflavone aglycones and angiotensin-converting enzyme-inhibitory and other peptides that may improve muscle perfusion and reduce oxidative stress. Furthermore, fermentation modulates the gut-muscle axis via short-chain fatty acid production, which may mitigate the systemic inflammation and oxidative stress associated with muscle loss. Human evidence translating these findings is currently limited. Fermentation offers a sustainable approach to optimizing plant-based nutrition to support muscle maintenance. Mechanistic findings are promising; however, further long-term human trials are required to translate this evidence into formal clinical recommendations for sarcopenia.
