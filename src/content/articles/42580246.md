### 重點摘要
這項研究旨在探討美國成年患者的體重狀況及其種族/族裔背景對醫病溝通品質的影響。研究使用2015-2020年醫療支出小組調查的橫斷面數據（N=23,047）進行次級分析，並透過多變量迴歸模型評估溝通品質。結果顯示，體重過重及肥胖的患者，特別是BMI ≥ 40.0者，其整體溝通品質得分顯著較低，且他們回報醫療人員仔細聆聽或表示尊重的可能性較小。此外，研究發現肥胖的種族/族裔少數群體在溝通品質上存在特定差異，凸顯了肥胖患者與醫療提供者溝通品質不佳的問題，尤其是在聆聽與尊重方面，且此不平等現象在不同種族/族裔群體中持續存在。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
該研究量化並證實了肥胖患者，特別是不同種族/族裔背景的肥胖患者，在醫病溝通品質上存在顯著的不平等現象，強調了體重、種族與族裔交織影響醫病關係。未來的研究可以基於這些發現，進一步開發和評估改善醫病溝通的介入措施，並探討造成這些溝通障礙的深層原因，以提升肥胖族群的醫療照護品質。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Patient-provider communication quality: Impact of patients' weight, race and ethnicity.
**Abstract:** This study examined variations in patient-provider communication quality by weight status, and weight-race/ethnicity status, among adult patients aged ≥ 18 years in the U.S. We conducted a cross-sectional secondary analysis of 2015-2020 data from the Medical Expenditure Panel Survey (MEPS) household component (N = 23,047). Descriptive statistics and bivariate analyses were used to characterize the study population. Multivariable logistic and linear regression were used to examine the relationship between patients' weight status and communication quality. Patients with a BMI ≥ 40.0 (obesity class III) had a statistically significantly lower composite communication quality score (86.7), compared to healthy weight patients (87.8). Compared to healthy weight patients, patients with obesity had a statistically significant 19% lower odds of reporting providers listened carefully or showed respect for what they said (adjusted odds ratio [aOR]: 0.81, 95%CI: 0.69-0.94 and aOR: 0.81, 95% CI: 0.69-0.95, respectively). NH-Whites with obesity had a lower odds of reporting providers listened carefully, showed respect, and spent enough time with them. Hispanics with obesity showed a lower odds of experiencing high-quality explanations (aOR: 0.71, 95% CI: 0.54-0.92). NH-Blacks with obesity had a statistically significant 24-49% lower odds of reporting providers listened and gave high-quality explanations in the unadjusted model (OR: 0.76, 95%CI: 0.58-0.98 and OR: 0.51, 95% CI: 0.40-0.65, respectively), but not in the adjusted models. Adult patients with obesity experience a lower quality of communication with their health care providers, particularly relating to listening and shows of respect, compared to adults with a healthy weight. Disparities in patient-provider communication quality persist among racial/ethnic minorities with overweight and obesity. Findings from this study may inform educational and research endeavors which aim to enhance patient-provider communication and the quality of care among adults with overweight and obesity.
