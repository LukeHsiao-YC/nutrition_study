### 重點摘要
學校營養政策對學童飲食的影響尚待釐清。本跨州橫斷面研究分析了四個州20所小學7,027份午餐托盤，旨在探討午餐時長、實際用餐時間與餐點選擇及攝取量的關聯。研究發現，學童的用餐時間越長，牛奶、全穀類配菜及水果的攝取量越高，且有州級用餐時間政策的學校能提供更長的用餐時間。結論指出，充足的用餐時間可增加校園餐點攝取量，州級用餐時間政策有助於確保學童有足夠的用餐時間。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究透過大規模跨州實證分析，明確指出學童實際用餐時間長度與營養攝取量之間的關聯，特別是州級政策在確保充足用餐時間方面的作用，填補了相關政策影響的實證空白。然而，作為一項橫斷面研究，其無法推論因果關係，且結果主要適用於研究中的小學學童群體。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Time to eat school lunch associated with increased meal consumption among elementary school students in a cross-sectional multi-state study.
**Abstract:** School nutrition policies related to lunch period lengths and free school meals have potential to improve children's diets, but more research is needed to understand their impact. The aims were to examine the associations between lunch period lengths, seated time to eat, and meal selection and consumption. A secondary aim was to examine differences among schools with varying free meal policies and state-level time to eat policies. This was a multi-state cross-sectional study in Arizona, California, Connecticut, and Massachusetts. Twenty elementary schools across four states with varying free meal and time to eat policies were examined during the 2024-25 school year, and 7,027 school lunch trays were analyzed. Seated time and selection were measured using direct observation, and consumption was measured using plate waste. Differences in seated time, meal selection, and consumption were examined using multi-level modeling, accounting for school as a random effect, and adjusting for school-level characteristics and student demographics. Students with 20-minute lunch periods had on average 16 minutes to eat (range 7-24 minutes), whereas students with 30-minute lunch periods had on average 25 minutes (range 12-33 minutes). Compared with federal recommendations (i.e., 20 minutes of seated time), only 16% of students with 20-minute lunch periods met these recommendations, whereas 89% with 30-minute lunch periods met these recommendations. State-level time to eat policies were associated with schools providing longer lunch periods and more time to eat compared with schools in states without these policies (24 minutes vs 18 minutes; difference 6 minutes [95% confidence interval 5.99- 6.50; p<0.0001]). Longer seated time was associated with greater milk, whole grain side, and fruit consumption (p<0.05). State-level free meal policies were not found to be associated with reduced school meal selection or consumption. Sufficient seated time has potential to increase school meal consumption, and state-level time to eat policies may help to ensure all students have sufficient time to eat.
