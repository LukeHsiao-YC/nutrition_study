### 重點摘要
本系統性回顧與統合分析旨在比較富裕地區純素食者與雜食者之維生素A攝取量及營養狀態，以探討純素飲食可能帶來的維生素A缺乏風險。研究分析了41項觀察性與4項介入性研究，發現純素食者雖攝取較多維生素A前驅物（如β-胡蘿蔔素），但其視網醇攝取量及血液中視網醇濃度顯著低於雜食者。儘管如此，純素食者的血液視網醇絕對濃度多數仍在充足範圍內。研究結論指出，由於現有證據的確定性低，未來仍需進一步研究以評估維生素A是否為純素食族群的關鍵營養素。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻透過統合分析首次系統性地比較了富裕地區純素食者與雜食者的維生素A攝取與狀態，明確指出純素食者雖攝取較多維生素A前驅物，但血液視網醇濃度較低。然而，由於研究結果的證據確定性普遍偏低（低/極低），且純素食者的血液視網醇絕對濃度多數仍在充足範圍內，因此這項研究的限制在於無法明確判斷維生素A是否已成為純素食族群的臨床上關鍵性營養素，需要未來更多高品質研究來填補此一不確定性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Vitamin A intake and status in vegans versus omnivores in affluent populations: a systematic review and meta-analysis.
**Abstract:** Vitamin A is an essential nutrient obtained as preformed vitamin A from animal-sourced foods or as provitamin A carotenoids mainly from plant foods. Because carotenoids have lower bioavailability and conversion rates are difficult to predict, vegan diets may risk vitamin A deficiency even under affluent conditions. This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to compare vitamin A intake and status between vegans and omnivores living in affluent settings. We searched four databases for observational and interventional (duration ≥3 months) studies, calculating pooled mean differences (MD) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) using random-effects models. Risk of bias was assessed using RoB-NObs and Cochrane Risk of bias 2.0, certainty of evidence was assessed using the GRADE approach. 41 observational (from 42 articles) and four interventional studies were included. Compared with omnivores, vegans had higher intakes of retinol equivalents (RE) (Observational studies: MD 0.29mg/d, 95% CI 0.07, 0.50; n=35, very low certainty of evidence, intervention studies: MD 0.24mg/d, 95% CI -0.03, 0.51, n=3, low certainty of evidence) and β-carotene (observational studies: MD 2.88mg/d; 95% CI 1.54, 4.22, n=10, very low certainty of evidence, intervention studies: MD 1.85mg/d, 95% CI 0.80, 2.90, n=4, low certainty of evidence), but significantly lower retinol intake (observational studies: MD: -0.55mg/d, 95% CI: -0.74, -0.36, n=7, very low certainty of evidence). Furthermore, vegans showed significantly lower blood retinol concentrations (MD: -0.26μmol/L, 95% CI, -0.33, -0.18, n=6, very low certainty of evidence), and no difference in blood β-carotene concentrations (MD: 0.32μmol/L, 95% CI: -0.53, 1.17, n=5, very low certainty of evidence). Despite the fact that vegans had lower blood retinol concentrations than omnivores, absolute concentrations were mostly in the adequate ranges. Findings should be interpreted cautiously and in light of low/very low certainty of the evidence. Further studies are needed to evaluate whether vitamin A is a critical nutrient in vegan populations. This trial was registered at PROSPERO as CRD42023436860. The protocol can be assessed at https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/PROSPERO/view/CRD42023436860 STATEMENTS OF SIGNIFICANCE: This systematic review and meta-analysis showed that while vegans consumed larger amounts of provitamin A carotenoids (β-carotene), their blood retinol levels were significantly lower than those of omnivores, suggesting that vitamin A may be a critical nutrient for vegan populations.
