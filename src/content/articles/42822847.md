### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討純母乳哺育、任何母乳哺育及嬰兒配方奶餵養與兒童成長之間的關聯，透過一項在加拿大多倫多進行的縱向世代研究，追蹤了5379名兒童至平均3歲。研究結果顯示，每增加一個月的純母乳或任何母乳哺育時間，都與zBMI的降低及超重/肥胖風險的減少有關。相對地，每增加一個月的嬰兒配方奶餵養則與zBMI的升高及超重/肥胖風險的增加相關。本研究結論指出，延長母乳哺育時間有助於兒童的健康成長，並建議有效的母乳哺育介入措施應包括由專業醫護人員提供的產後、密集及面對面支持。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究透過大規模縱向世代分析，量化了不同嬰兒餵養方式（純母乳、任何母乳、配方奶）與兒童成長指標（zBMI、超重及肥胖）之間的具體關聯，明確指出餵養時間長度對這些結果的影響，為早期餵養對兒童長期健康的影響提供了重要的量化證據。然而，本研究主要聚焦於關聯性，未來研究可進一步探討這些餵養模式影響兒童成長的生物學機制，或評估不同類型母乳哺育介入措施的長期效益。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Exclusive and Any Breastfeeding, Infant Formula Feeding, and Childhood Growth.
**Abstract:** To understand the association between exclusive breastfeeding, any breastfeeding, and infant formula feeding and childhood growth. A longitudinal cohort study was conducted among children from birth up to 10 years in Toronto, Canada. The primary exposure was exclusive breastfeeding. Secondary exposures were any breastfeeding and infant formula feeding. The primary outcome was body mass index z-score (zBMI). The secondary outcome was overweight and obesity (zBMI > 1). In total, 5379 children were included and followed to 3 years of age on average. Each 1-month increase in exclusive breastfeeding was associated with 0.01 lower zBMI (95% CI, -0.02 to -0.00) and lower odds of overweight and obesity (OR, 0.96; 95% CI, 0.94-0.98) with similar associations for any breastfeeding (zBMI, -0.01; 95% CI, -0.01 to -0.00; OR, 0.98; 95% CI, 0.97-0.99). Each 1-month increase in infant formula feeding was associated with 0.01 higher zBMI (95% CI, 0.00-0.01) and higher odds of overweight and obesity (OR, 1.02; 95% CI, 1.01-1.03). There was little evidence for modifying effects between infant feeding practices. Infant formula feeding for 24 months was associated with 0.27 higher zBMI (95% CI, 0.14-0.41) and 72% higher odds (OR, 1.72; 95% CI, 1.22-2.44) of overweight and obesity when compared to exclusive breastfeeding to 6 months of age and any breastfeeding to 24 months of age. Longer exclusive breastfeeding and any breastfeeding were associated with lower zBMI and lower odds of overweight and obesity, whereas the opposite was true for infant formula feeding. Effective breastfeeding interventions include postnatal, intensive, and in-person support provided by trained health care professionals.
