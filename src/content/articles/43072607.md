### 重點摘要
這項研究旨在探討土耳其伊斯坦堡大學生中，糧食不安全與通貨膨脹相關的消費者行為變化如何共同影響永續健康飲食。研究採用橫斷面設計，訪問了520名學生，發現22.9%的參與者存在中度至重度糧食不安全。結果顯示，糧食不安全程度越高，永續健康飲食分數越低，而感知到越多的通貨膨脹相關行為改變則與永續健康飲食分數呈正相關。研究結論指出，糧食不安全會限制健康與永續飲食，但部分通貨膨脹驅動的行為調整可能與這些實踐相符，凸顯了經濟壓力下飲食選擇的複雜性，並為大學食品政策提供參考。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了過去研究對於糧食不安全與通貨膨脹相關的消費者行為變化如何共同影響永續健康飲食的證據空白。然而，本研究為橫斷面設計，無法確立因果關係，且僅限於伊斯坦堡大學生，未來研究可擴大地理範圍並採用縱向研究以進一步驗證這些複雜的關聯性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Food Insecurity and Inflation Related Constraints on Sustainable Healthy Eating Among University Students.
**Abstract:** Food insecurity and rising food prices may constrain university students' ability to adopt healthy and sustainable dietary practices. Although previous research has examined food insecurity and dietary quality, limited evidence addresses how food insecurity and perceived inflation-related changes in consumer behavior are jointly associated with sustainable and healthy eating. This study aimed to examine these associations among university students in Istanbul, Türkiye, and investigate differences in food insecurity by university type. In this cross-sectional study, face-to-face questionnaire data were collected from 520 students attending public and foundation universities between April and October 2025. Food insecurity, perceived inflation-related consumer behaviors, and sustainable and healthy eating (SHE) behaviors were assessed using validated instruments. Associations were examined using correlation and multivariable regression analyses, alongside exploratory subgroup analysis. Moderate-to-severe food insecurity was identified in 22.9% of participants. In adjusted analyses, greater food insecurity was negatively associated with SHE scores (β= -0.202, P<0.001), whereas greater perceived inflation-related behavioral changes showed a strong positive association (β= 0.685, P<0.001). Higher SHE scores were associated with lower odds of moderate-to-severe food insecurity (OR=0.37, 95% CI 0.25-0.52), while foundation university students had lower odds than public university students (OR=0.54, 95% CI 0.33-0.88). These contrasting associations highlight the complexity of food choices under economic pressure, where food insecurity may constrain healthy and sustainable eating while some inflation-related behavioral adaptations may align with these practices. Understanding these distinct patterns may help inform university food policies that address affordability barriers while supporting healthier and more sustainable dietary choices.
