### 重點摘要
飲食是影響腸道菌群和人類健康的關鍵因素，植物性飲食模式日益受到關注。本系統性回顧分析了37項人體與動物研究，探討植物性飲食對腸道菌群組成與功能的影響。結果顯示，植物性飲食能促進短鏈脂肪酸生成菌及益生菌（如Roseburia spp., Akkermansia muciniphila）的生長，並增加Bacteroidetes/Firmicutes比例，這與較低的BMI和健康的代謝狀況相關。總體而言，研究指出植物性飲食對腸道菌群有益，可能對人類健康產生正面影響。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本文獻透過系統性回顧彙整了植物性飲食對腸道菌群的正面影響，填補了此領域的綜合性知識空白。然而，其未來研究限制在於，目前各項研究對於飲食模式導致的腸道菌群變化程度與方向尚未完全達成共識，提示未來需進一步釐清這些差異。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** How Plants Feed Our Microbes: Plant-Based Diets and Gut Microbiota Modulation-A Systematic Review.
**Abstract:** Diet is a key factor shaping gut microbiota composition and function, with important implications for human health. The growing popularity of plant-based dietary patterns has increased interest in their effects on the gut microbiota. This systematic review examines specific changes in gut microbiota among individuals following a plant-based diet (PBD), as well as shifts occurring after transitioning from an omnivorous diet. PubMed and Google Scholar were searched for articles reporting original English-language human and animal studies investigating the effects of PBDs on gut microbiota composition or function. Studies published from 2015 onward were primarily considered, with most included articles published during 2020-2024. Four reviewers independently screened records according to eligibility criteria and extracted data on study characteristics, populations, dietary patterns, and outcomes related to gut microbiota composition and function. A total of 37 studies met the inclusion criteria. A PBD appeared to promote the growth of short-chain fatty acid-producing and probiotic bacteria, which help maintain intestinal integrity and support carbohydrate metabolism and may offer protection against certain diseases. A PBD was also reported to encourage the growth of beneficial taxa such as Roseburia spp., Akkermansia muciniphila, Clostridium symbiosum, and Faecalibacterium prausnitzii, which are considered indicators of a healthy microbiome. An increased Bacteroidetes/Firmicutes ratio, which was found to be a common occurrence in study participants following a PBD, correlated with a lower body mass index (BMI) and a more favorable metabolic profile, supporting healthy weight maintenance. In addition, PBDs were also associated with improvements in alpha- and beta-diversity compared with omnivorous diets. This review indicates that PBDs appear to beneficially influence the gut microbiota and may have positive effects on human health. Nonetheless, not all studies agree on the extent or direction of microbiota changes associated with dietary patterns.
