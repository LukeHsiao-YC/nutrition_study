### 重點摘要
這項多城市研究旨在探討後疫情時代主要呼吸道病毒之間的群體水平干擾作用，以了解其對疫情時機、醫療需求和免疫策略的影響。研究分析了2024年6月至2026年8月間從七個城市收集的40,987份呼吸道樣本，並使用貝氏分佈滯後非線性模型量化了病毒干擾的程度和時間模式。結果顯示，所有共循環病毒之間均存在相互抑制作用，其中呼吸道融合病毒(RSV)的抑制作用最強，且干擾模式在病毒對之間及不同年齡層間存在顯著差異。本研究證明了呼吸道病毒之間獨特的病原體特異性抑制關聯，其時間動態具有異質性，這有助於改進流行病預測、免疫規劃及醫療資源分配。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了在後疫情時代，對主要呼吸道病毒間群體水平干擾的程度、時間動態和年齡層異質性認識的空白，為了解病毒相互作用如何影響疫情時機、醫療需求和免疫策略提供了實證依據。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Magnitude and temporal dynamics of respiratory viral interference: a multi-city study in Central China.
**Abstract:** Interactions among respiratory viruses may alter epidemic timing, healthcare demand, and immunization strategies in the postpandemic era. We analyzed multi-city surveillance data to characterize population-level viral interference among four major respiratory viruses. From June 2024 to August 2026, 40 987 respiratory specimens were collected through sentinel surveillance across seven cities and routinely tested for multiple respiratory pathogens using nucleic-acid assays. City-specific Bayesian Distributed Lag Non-linear Models with multivariate meta-analysis were used to quantify the magnitude and lag patterns of viral interference. Age-stratified analyses assessed heterogeneity across life stages. All co-circulating viruses demonstrated reciprocal inhibitory associations [all cumulative relative risks (CRRs) < 1.00]. Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) exerted the strongest inhibitory associations: a 10% increase in RSV positivity was associated with a 49.2% reduction in severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 risk [CRR: 0.508 (0.402-0.640)], with less pronounced associations observed for influenza virus and human rhinovirus. Interference patterns differed substantially across virus pairs in both magnitude and temporal duration. Influenza produced immediate and sustained inhibition, whereas RSV exhibited delayed inhibitory associations peaking at weeks 5-6. Age-stratified analyses revealed substantial heterogeneity across age groups. Respiratory viruses exhibit distinct pathogen-specific inhibitory associations with heterogeneous temporal dynamics, consistent with population-level viral interference. Incorporating these interactions into surveillance and forecasting models may improve epidemic prediction and support immunization planning and respiratory healthcare resource allocation in post-pandemic settings.
