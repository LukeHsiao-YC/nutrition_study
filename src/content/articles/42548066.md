### 重點摘要
這項研究調查了炎症性腸病 (IBD) 診斷時兒童身高的差異。透過回顧分析近 1.4 萬名未成年 IBD 患者的資料，結果顯示沒有私人保險的兒童，以及西班牙裔/拉丁裔兒童，在診斷時出現生長遲緩（身高 Z-分數 < -1）的比例顯著較高。其中，沒有私人保險的西班牙裔/拉丁裔兒童的生長遲緩比例最高。本研究強調，診斷延遲和糧食不安全可能是造成這些差異的原因，呼籲醫療照護應具公平性並重視及時診斷。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了 IBD 診斷時兒童生長遲緩與社會經濟地位及族裔之間關聯性的研究空白，明確指出特定弱勢群體面臨更高的生長遲緩風險。它假設診斷延遲和糧食不安全是潛在貢獻因素，但研究本身並未直接衡量或驗證這些因果關係。未來的研究可進一步探究這些因素與生長遲緩之間的機制，並開發針對性的干預措施以確保所有患者都能及時獲得公平的診斷與照護。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Disparities in child height at the time of inflammatory bowel disease diagnosis.
**Abstract:** Growth delay is associated with severe disease at inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) diagnosis. Variation in height at diagnosis is unknown. We hypothesize that growth delay at diagnosis is more common among individuals of low socioeconomic status. We retrospectively evaluated prospectively collected data from patients <18 year and enrolled in the ImproveCareNow (ICN) Network <90 days after IBD diagnosis. The primary outcome was growth delay at enrollment, defined as height Z-score <-1. Insurance was dichotomized as presence or absence of private insurance. Among 13,691 patients included, 6015 were female (44%), median age 13.6 year (interquartile range 11.0-15.8), 1583 (11.6%) Black, 9951 (72.7%) White, 935 (6.8%) other race, 723 (5.3%) Hispanic/Latino and 10,154 (74.2%) non-Hispanic/Latino ethnicity, and 9425 (68.8%) with, and 3011 (22.0%) without private insurance. Patients without private insurance were 20% more likely to have growth delay (N = 728, 24.2%) than those with private insurance (N = 1894, 20.1%; p < 0.001). Among Hispanic/Latino patients, 25.7% (N = 186) had growth delay, compared to 20.7% (N = 2,098) non-Hispanic/Latino (p = 0.001). Hispanic/Latino patients without private insurance had the greatest proportion with growth delay (N = 107, 28.6%) compared to all others (N = 2762, 20.7%; p < 0.001). Hispanic/Latino children and those without private insurance are more likely to have growth delay at IBD diagnosis. Diagnostic delay and food insecurity are likely contributors. Medical care should be equitable despite life circumstance. This study identifies opportunities to improve care by emphasizing the importance of timely diagnosis. Improving access is necessary at each phase of care to ensure equitable timely diagnosis for all patients.
