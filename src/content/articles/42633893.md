### 重點摘要
本研究系統性分析美洲地區幼兒奶粉的行銷手法與營養成分。透過對14個國家389種產品的內容分析，發現近乎所有產品的包裝上都包含營養或健康聲明及圖像。營養分析顯示，幼兒奶粉的總糖含量平均高於普通牛奶，而蛋白質含量則平均較低。這些發現引發對幼兒奶粉行銷方式的擔憂，並強調需加強法規以確保透明度並支持知情的食品選擇。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
此研究系統性地綜合了美洲地區幼兒奶粉的行銷手法與營養成分，填補了此前缺乏全面性分析的空白，並揭示了行銷聲明與實際營養成分之間可能存在的落差。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Marketing Practices and Nutrition Content of Toddler Milk Across the Americas.
**Abstract:** Toddler milk is a breastmilk substitute for young children, yet its marketing and nutritional profile across the Americas has not been systematically synthesized. We conducted a content analysis documenting the prevalence of front-of-package claims and imagery on 389 toddler milk products from 14 countries in the Americas. We also compared the nutritional profile of toddler milk with plain cow's milk and chocolate-flavored cow's milk. Nearly all products (99%) displayed at least one front-of-package claim. Claim categories included nutrition-related (97%), health-related (56%), and other marketing (25%). Nutrition-related claims highlighting breastmilk-related components were common; for example, "Contains docosahexaenoic acid" appeared on 52% products. The most common health-related claims referenced brain and cognitive benefits (29%), such as, "Supports brain development." Among other marketing claims, endorsements (eg, "#1 recommended by pediatricians") appeared on 11% of packages. Imagery appeared on 92% of packages. Visual metaphors included shields (32%) and hearts (27%). Nutritionally, toddler milk contained an average total sugar of 6 g/100 mL, higher than plain cow's milk (5 g/100 mL, P < .01) but lower than chocolate-flavored cow's milk (9 g/100 mL, P = .97). Protein averaged 2 g/100 mL, lower than both plain cow's milk (3 g/100 mL, P < .01) and chocolate-flavored cow's milk (3 g/100 mL, P < .01). Toddler milk products contain more total sugar and less protein than plain cow's milk, yet claims and imagery promoting nutritional and health benefits are nearly universal on their packaging, raising concerns about how these products are being marketed to caregivers. These findings highlight the need for stronger regulations to ensure transparency and support informed food choices.
