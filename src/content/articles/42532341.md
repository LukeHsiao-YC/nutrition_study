### 重點摘要
該研究旨在探討長期補充蔬果是否能預防西方高脂飲食在老齡雄性小鼠中引起的認知障礙，並探究其對血清鞘脂代謝的影響，因為現有證據尚不明確。研究將雄性小鼠分為低脂控制、高脂控制、低脂加蔬果及高脂加蔬果四組，餵食18個月後評估其新物體辨識能力，並於21個月時分析血清脂質組。結果顯示，高脂控制組小鼠出現認知功能障礙，而長期補充蔬果的高脂組小鼠則能維持與低脂組相當的認知表現，顯示蔬果補充具有保護作用。此外，高脂控制組的血清鞘磷脂水平顯著升高，且與較差的認知表現相關，而蔬果補充能使這些脂質水平正常化，提示鞘磷脂變化可能與其保護機制有關。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了關於長期蔬果攝取對高脂飲食引發認知功能下降影響的證據不一致性，明確指出蔬果補充能有效預防此類認知損傷，並提出了血清鞘磷脂作為潛在的作用機制。然而，這是一項動物研究，其結果仍需進一步的人體研究來驗證其在人類中的普適性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Long-term supplementation with fruits and vegetables prevents cognitive impairment induced by a Western-style high-fat diet in male mice.
**Abstract:** Aging, obesity, and high-fat Western diets are linked to increased risk of cognitive decline. Although higher fruit and vegetable (FV) intake may protect against cognitive decline, evidence remains inconsistent. This study examined whether long-term FV supplementation preserves cognitive function and modulates serum sphingolipid metabolism in mice fed low-fat or high-fat diets. In this long-term in vivo mouse study, male C57BL/6J mice were randomly assigned to low-fat control (LF-C), high-fat control (HF-C), or each diet supplemented with 15% FV (LF-FV, HF-FV) (n = 20/group). After 18 months on the diets, cognitive function was assessed using the novel object recognition test (NOR), and recognition index (RI) and discrimination index (DI) were calculated. At 21 months, all mice were euthanized and serum was collected for lipidomic analysis. Group comparisons were performed using ANOVA with post hoc tests and correlation analyses between lipids and cognitive measures. LF-C, LF-FV, and HF-FV mice showed significant increases in RI during testing (18.7%, 23.6%, and 22.2%, respectively), whereas HF-C exhibited no improvement. HF-C also had markedly reduced DI (-0.0456) relative to all other groups (0.2100 - 0.2323), consistent with cognitive impairment. In contrast, HF-FV mice demonstrated preserved DI and RI values comparable to LF controls, indicating that FV supplementation prevented high-fat diet-induced deficits. Serum lipidomic analysis revealed that HF-C mice displayed elevated total sphingomyelins (SM) and several long-chain SM species; these elevations were largely normalized in HF-FV mice. Across all animals, higher SM levels were consistently associated with poorer NOR performance, suggesting that changes in SM changes may contribute to cognitive differences. In aged male C57BL/6J mice, long-term FV supplementation mitigates cognitive decline caused by a high-fat diet. Changes in serum sphingomyelin species were associated with cognitive outcomes and may be related to the protective effect of FV supplementation.
