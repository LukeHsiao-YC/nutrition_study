### 重點摘要
該研究探討了針對癌症倖存者的網路飲食介入方案 (AMPLIFY-Diet) 如何透過社會認知理論 (SCT) 的構念影響體重、能量攝取及飲食品質。分析了 346 名過重或肥胖癌症倖存者的數據，他們接受了為期 24 週的網路介入。結果顯示，體重減輕在早期（3 個月）主要透過社會支持改善，在後期（6 個月）則透過減少飲食障礙感知而實現。然而，這些SCT構念未能顯著解釋能量攝取或飲食品質的變化，提示未來的研究需探索其他機制。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究闡明了網路飲食介入方案在癌症倖存者中，透過時間依賴性的社會支持和飲食障礙感知來促進體重減輕的潛在機制。然而，研究結果也指出，社會認知理論的這些構念未能解釋飲食攝取或飲食品質的改變，提示未來的網路飲食介入研究應探索並衡量更多理論構念，以全面理解飲食行為變化的基礎機制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Social Support and Perceived Dietary Barriers Mediate Weight Loss in the AMPLIFY Web-Based Diet Intervention.
**Abstract:** Web-based interventions that promote healthy diet and weight loss among cancer survivors are growing, but theoretical constructs driving their success remain unclear. This study explores whether Social Cognitive Theory (SCT) constructs (self-efficacy, social support, perceived barriers) mediate the effects of the AMPLIFY-Diet intervention on energy intake, diet quality, and body weight. This mediation analysis used data from the AMPLIFY-Diet randomized controlled trial (RCT). Data collected from March 2020 to March 2024 at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months were analyzed from 346 overweight-or-obese cancer survivors enrolled nationwide. Cancer survivors received a serialized, theoretically-grounded, 24-week, web-based intervention to improve diet quality and weight loss. Energy intake and diet quality were measured using two non-consecutive 24-hour dietary recalls, and body weight was measured using digital scales at baseline and 6 months. SCT mediators were assessed using self-reported instruments at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months. Mediation analyses were conducted to estimate indirect effects. At 3-months, the effect of the AMPLIFY-Diet intervention on weight was significantly mediated through improvements in social support from baseline (unstandardized indirect effect estimate=-0.42 (SE=0.21); 95% CI [-0.894, -0.084]); whereas, at 6-months, the effect was significantly mediated through reductions in perceived dietary barriers from baseline (unstandardized indirect effect estimate=-0.45 (SE=0.21); 95% CI [-0.928,-0.103]). Self-efficacy, social support, and perceived dietary barriers did not emerge as significant mediators for energy intake or diet quality models. Findings suggest that the AMPLIFY-Diet intervention achieved weight loss among cancer survivors through time-dependent mechanisms, improving social support earlier in the intervention and reducing perceived dietary barriers later on. However, these constructs did not explain changes in models for dietary intake. Future web-based dietary interventions should measure and examine additional theoretical constructs to better understand the mechanisms underlying dietary change.
