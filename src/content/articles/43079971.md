### 重點摘要
短腸症伴腸衰竭患者需仰賴腸外營養，死亡率高，Teduglutide能改善腸道吸收。本回溯性研究探討在腸道重建手術前，對11名潛在可逆性短腸症伴腸衰竭患者使用Teduglutide作為過渡性治療的效益。結果顯示，所有患者均對治療有反應，其中5名患者達到腸道自主，功能、症狀和生活品質均顯著改善，且重建後80%患者仍能維持腸道自主。這表明Teduglutide作為重建手術前的過渡性治療可能可行，但仍需更多研究證實其對術前優化和手術結果的影響。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了Teduglutide在腸道重建手術前作為過渡性治療的應用空白，為具有潛在可逆性短腸症伴腸衰竭患者提供了一種新的治療策略。然而，本研究屬於回溯性觀察性研究，樣本量較小（11例），且為單一醫學中心資料，其結果需更多前瞻性、多中心研究來進一步驗證Teduglutide在術前優化和手術結果方面的長期影響。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Teduglutide prior to reconstructive surgery for short bowel syndrome with intestinal failure: A descriptive cohort study.
**Abstract:** Short bowel syndrome with intestinal failure is characterized by severe malabsorption requiring parenteral support for survival, which carries the risk for high morbidity and mortality. The glucagon-like peptide-2 analogue teduglutide enhances intestinal absorption and reduces parenteral support dependence. The indication for teduglutide use is the stable nonmalignant short bowel syndrome with intestinal failure, excluding short bowel syndrome patients in the early phase of spontaneous intestinal rehabilitation. Here we describe the use of teduglutide as bridging therapy after an intestinal catastrophe and before reconstructive surgery. We performed a retrospective, observational cohort analysis of patients with potentially reversible short bowel syndrome with intestinal failure treated with teduglutide as bridging therapy prior to reconstruction at a single university medical center (May 2019-October 2024). We assessed functional (parenteral support, oral fluid intake, and urinary output) and symptomatic outcomes (stool characteristics) of teduglutide bridging therapy as well as the effect on quality of life. Eleven patients receiving teduglutide bridging therapy before intestinal reconstruction were included. Median small bowel length was 80 cm (IQR, 55-95). Over a bridging period of 24.7 weeks (IQR, 19.4-36.4), all patients responded to the therapy, with five patients reaching enteral autonomy. Teduglutide bridging therapy improved functional and symptomatic outcome and quality of life. After reconstruction, 80% of patients remained in enteral autonomy. Bridging intestinal failure may be a feasible approach in selected patients with potentially reversible short bowel syndrome, warranting further studies to confirm its impact on preoperative optimization and surgical outcomes.
