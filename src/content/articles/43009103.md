### 重點摘要
更年期過渡期婦女因荷爾蒙變化及體組成改變，在代謝上易受影響，而腸泌素模擬藥物雖為肥胖治療新利器，其作用機制卻可能導致營養攝取不足。本綜述探討了更年期婦女合併使用腸泌素模擬藥物治療肥胖時所面臨的營養風險，特別是骨質疏鬆及肌少性肥胖的風險。文章強調這些風險對肌肉骨骼健康、代謝彈性和生活品質的影響，並提出如蛋白質優化、微量營養素監測及多學科護理等臨床策略。研究旨在填補當前知識缺口，以確保中年及以後女性肥胖管理的安全性與永續性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻透過綜述現有證據，填補了更年期與肥胖藥物治療交界處營養風險綜合性理解的空白。文章同時指出了未來研究的重點，需進一步發展針對性的營養框架，以確保更年期婦女使用腸泌素模擬藥物時能安全、有效且永續地管理肥胖。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Nutrition Care Strategies at the Intersection of Menopause and Obesity Pharmacotherapy.
**Abstract:** The premenopause to menopause transition, known as perimenopause, is a critical period of metabolic vulnerability, characterized by hormonal changes, shifts in body composition, and increased cardiometabolic risk. Concurrently, incretin mimetic pharmacotherapies have emerged as highly effective treatments for obesity, achieving weight reductions that are approximating those previously observed only after bariatric surgery. While these agents confer substantial benefits, their mechanisms-appetite suppression, delayed gastric emptying, and enhanced satiety-introduce nutritional risks that intersect with pre-existing vulnerabilities of women experiencing this transition. Reduced energy intake and inadequate micronutrient intake, combined with estrogen deficiency, heighten the risk for osteoporosis and sarcopenic obesity. Likewise, gastrointestinal adverse events further compromise dietary quality and hydration, while aging physiology amplifies susceptibility to malnutrition. This narrative review synthesizes current evidence on nutritional risks during perimenopause in the context of incretin mimetic pharmacotherapy, highlighting overlapping pathways that influence musculoskeletal health, metabolic resilience, and quality of life. Clinical strategies to mitigate these risks, including protein optimization, micronutrient monitoring, menopausal hormone therapy (osteoporosis/musculoskeletal health), and multidisciplinary care, are discussed alongside research priorities to inform targeted nutrition frameworks. Addressing these gaps is essential to ensure safe, effective, and sustainable obesity management for women in midlife and beyond.
