### 重點摘要
英國嬰兒配方奶粉品牌成本差異大，但無證據顯示高價產品有額外健康益處。本研究採用混合方法，對478位英國配方奶粉使用者進行線上問卷調查，並對20位經濟困難的家長進行訪談，以探討影響配方奶粉支出的因素。結果顯示，不同經濟狀況的家庭在每週配方奶粉支出上無顯著差異，但近三分之二的家長選擇高價品牌，尤其經濟困難的家長更易受誤導性行銷影響。研究建議應加強對母乳替代品的誤導性行銷規範，並確保低價產品的可近性與可及性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究揭示了英國嬰兒配方奶粉的高支出不僅關乎經濟能力，更深受誤導性行銷、醫院供應及家長對產品品質、專業推薦與社會觀感的擔憂所驅動，特別凸顯了這對低收入家庭造成的不公平影響，從而填補了「為何經濟困難家庭仍會購買高價配方奶粉」的具體機制知識空白。未來的研究應專注於開發有效的政策與介入措施，以應對誤導性行銷並提升低價配方奶粉的可用性與可及性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Expenditure on Infant Formula, Brand Decisions and Misleading Marketing in the UK: Inequitable Impacts Upon Low-Income Families.
**Abstract:** Infant formula brands in the United Kingdom (UK) differ significantly in cost despite no evidence of preferential impacts upon infant health and development. This mixed methods study explored the factors that drive higher spending on formula milk, comparing this for families who were struggling to afford formula milk or not. UK parents with a baby aged 0-12 months who use infant formula completed an online survey (n = 478). Additionally, 20 parents who were struggling to afford infant formula taking part in interviews. Expenditure and brand decisions were compared for those struggling to afford infant formula and eligible for financial support (n = 94), struggling but not eligible for support (n = 167) and not struggling to afford infant formula (n = 217). There was no significant difference in weekly amount spent between the three groups. Two thirds of parents chose higher-cost brands although mothers who were mixed feeding were less likely to buy a higher-cost milk than those exclusively formula feeding. Parents who were struggling to afford formula were more likely to buy a brand due to perceived better-quality/additional ingredients and because it was supplied in hospital and perceived to have healthcare professional endorsement than those who were not struggling. Qualitative analysis showed that parents bought higher-cost brands in case future research showed an impact, from fear of judgement, worries around brand availability, and perceptions that brands were closest to breast milk. Stronger regulation is needed of misleading marketing of breastmilk substitutes alongside ensuring availability and accessibility of lower-cost products.
