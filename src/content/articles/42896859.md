### 重點摘要
這項系統性回顧與統合分析旨在評估蛋白質攝取對運動活躍個體腸道菌群影響的不一致性。研究納入18項文獻，分析蛋白質攝取與腸道菌群指標，結果顯示蛋白質攝取顯著增加了腸道菌群多樣性（Shannon和Chao1指數），且與劑量呈現U形關聯，最低點為每日每公斤體重1.5-2.0克。然而，蛋白質攝取也顯著減少了放線菌門、雙歧桿菌屬和厚壁菌門的數量，並增加了擬桿菌門。結論指出，蛋白質攝取有助於改善運動活躍個體的腸道菌群多樣性，但可能導致有益菌群（特別是雙歧桿菌屬）的顯著減少。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇研究透過系統性回顧與統合分析，整合並澄清了蛋白質攝取對運動活躍個體腸道菌群影響的不一致結果，填補了該領域缺乏全面性量化證據的空白。然而，未來的研究應優先考慮個體化、基於微生物組的策略，評估長期安全性，並釐清不同蛋白質來源的生態影響，以優化運動營養干預措施。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Effects of Protein Intake on Gut Microbiota in Physically Active Individuals: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.
**Abstract:** Protein (PRO) intake supports exercise adaptation and recovery, but its effects on gut microbiota (GM) in physically active individuals remain inconsistent across doses, PRO sources, and exercise conditions. To systematically evaluate and meta-analyze the effects of PRO intake on GM in physically active individuals. The PubMed, EBSCOhost, the Cochrane Library, and Web of Science databases were searched through June 2026. Reference lists of relevant studies and reviews were also examined. Two reviewers independently screened records and extracted study, participant, intervention, and outcome data. Eligible studies quantified PRO intake, included an exercise or physical-activity component, and reported extractable GM outcomes. Risk of bias and evidence certainty were assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2.0 tool and Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation approach, respectively. Random-effects meta-analyses using Hedges' g, subgroup analyses, and meta-regression were performed. Eighteen studies (n = 819 participants) were included. Protein intake significantly increased the Shannon and Chao1 indices but not the Simpson index or observed operational taxonomic units (OTUs). A significant U-shaped association was observed between PRO dose and the α-diversity effect size, with the fitted curve reaching its lowest point at 1.5-2.0 g kg-1 d-1. Protein intake significantly decreased populations of Actinobacteria, Bifidobacterium, and Firmicutes, and the Firmicutes to Bacteroidetes ratio, and increased Bacteroidetes numbers. Subgroup findings suggested that age, body mass index, intervention duration, PRO dose, and exercise modality and intensity moderated the effects on Shannon diversity and Bifidobacterium abundance. Protein intake appears to improve gut microbial diversity in physically active individuals but may also induce notable shifts in beneficial taxa, particularly reductions in Bifidobacterium. Future studies should prioritize individualized microbiome-informed strategies, evaluate long-term safety, and clarify the ecological impact of different PRO sources to optimize exercise nutrition interventions. PROSPERO registration No. CRD420261288197.
