### 重點摘要
鑑於中國緊張的醫患關係，本研究旨在探討中國醫師如何透過隱喻建構其對醫師、患者及醫患關係的理解。研究採用誘發隱喻分析 (EMA) 方法，調查了222名前線醫師，共產生666個陳述，並將其編碼為人本/合作、服務/倦怠及機械化三種主要隱喻範式。結果顯示人本/合作隱喻佔主導地位 (61.1%)，但中年醫師的服務/倦怠隱喻更為顯著；近三成最初採人本/合作隱喻的醫師在描述醫患關係時轉向服務/倦怠範式。這表明醫師對不同情境的隱喻概念化存在跨領域差異，研究建議應結合同理心、溝通技巧及組織支持，特別是針對中年醫師，以改善醫患溝通。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究透過創新的誘發隱喻分析方法，深入探討了中國醫師對其角色、患者及醫患關係的深層概念化，填補了單純問卷調查難以捕捉的、醫師主觀認知動態變化的研究空白。特別是揭示了預期全能與實際限制之間的張力，以及醫師在不同情境下隱喻思維的跨領域轉變，為改善醫患溝通提供了更精準的介入點。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The Monkey King's dilemma: Metaphorical constructions of physicians' identity and doctor-patient communication in China.
**Abstract:** In the context of tense doctor-patient relationships, physicians' understandings of their own roles and relationships may be relevant to clinical communication. This study examines how Chinese physicians construct their understanding of doctors, patients, and doctor-patient relationships through metaphors, and analyses cross domain shifts in metaphorical paradigms. This study used Elicited Metaphor Analysis (EMA) and surveyed 222 frontline physicians in China. The respondents completed three metaphorical prompts about doctors, patients, and doctor-patient relationships, generating a total of 666 statements. The responses were coded into three macro paradigms: Humanistic/Cooperative, Service/Burnout, and Mechanistic. Chi-square tests examined differences by career stage and hospital tier, while Sankey flow mapping visualized within participant transitions across the three elicitation domains (M1-M3). Humanistic/cooperative metaphors dominated, accounting for 61.1% (407/666); Service/burnout metaphors accounted for 23.3% (155/666); mechanistic metaphors accounted for 15.6% (104/666). Sun Wukong (the Monkey King, a powerful yet constrained Chinese mythic hero) and beasts of burden metaphors embody the tension between expected clinical omnipotence and limited physician autonomy. There were differences in baseline self-metaphors among different career stages, with service/burnout metaphors being more prominent among mid-career doctors; there was no significant difference across hospital tiers. Flow analysis showed that 95 doctors (42.8%) maintained the same paradigm across the three nodes; among 148 doctors who used humanistic/cooperative self-metaphors, 44 (29.7%) shifted to the service/burnout paradigm when describing doctor-patient relationships. Chinese physicians' elicited metaphors were predominantly Humanistic/Cooperative, while some showed a shift toward Service/Burnout framing in the relationship domain, highlighting cross domain variation in physicians' metaphorical conceptualizations. Doctor patient communication support could combine empathy and communication skills training with organizational support, particularly for mid-career physicians; reflective support approaches such as Balint groups and clearer medical dispute resolution and occupational safety support may also be considered.
