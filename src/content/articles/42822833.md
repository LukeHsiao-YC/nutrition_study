### 重點摘要
「Food is Medicine」(FIM) 介入措施，如醫學調整雜貨 (MTG)，正被研究以改善低收入族群的慢性病管理。本研究透過一項包含一般食物銀行服務、僅MTG、以及MTG加食物資源指導的三組隨機對照試驗，探討了參與者在FIM計畫中的依從性模式及所選食物的營養品質變化。結果顯示，西班牙語使用者有較高的入組率，且該FIM介入在低收入且慢性病負擔重的患者群體中實現了高水平的入組、留存與參與度。此外，所有研究組在四個月內所選食物的營養品質均有所提升。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究證明了在服務低收入且慢性病高負擔族群的醫療系統中，「Food is Medicine」介入措施能夠達成高水平的入組、留存與參與度，填補了關於此類計畫在真實世界情境中實用性和患者接受度的研究空白。未來的研究可以進一步探討不同文化或語言背景對計畫參與的影響機制，以及長期介入對臨床健康結果的具體效益。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Adherence Patterns in a Randomized Controlled Trial of a Client-Choice Pantry-Based Food is Medicine Program.
**Abstract:** Food is Medicine (FIM) interventions, including medically tailored groceries (MTG), are increasingly being investigated as an approach to improving chronic disease management in populations with lower income. To deliver on the potential benefits of FIM for ameliorating disparities in chronic disease outcomes, interventions need to be effective, pragmatic and desirable to patients. We sought to examine socio-economic and demographic correlates of enrollment, retention, adherence, and participation. Additionally, we assessed how the nutritional quality of food selected at a client choice food pantry varied across the duration of the study and by study arm. We implemented a 3-arm pilot randomized controlled trial (RCT) including 3 groups: usual pantry services, MTG only, and MTG + food resource coaching. Chi-squared tests, t-tests and ANOVA were used to compare participants versus eligible non-participants, completers versus those who did not, and among participants across study arms. Differences in the nutritional quality of foods selected between each study group were assessed using multiple variable regression models. Among 586 individuals screened, 330 completed the screener and met eligibility criteria; 210 (64% of eligible participants) enrolled. Individuals meeting inclusion criteria whose preferred language was Spanish were more likely to enroll in the study (60% of Spanish speakers enrolled versus 44% of English speakers, p=0.004). Among 210 participants, there were no statistically significant differences in characteristics between completers (81.4%) versus non-completers (18.6%). Over 4 months, food selections for all groups became more nutritious. This FIM intervention achieved a high level of enrollment, retention, adherence, and participation among safety-net healthcare system patients that typically experience a high burden of chronic disease and poorer chronic disease outcomes. The randomized controlled trial from which the cost estimates are reported is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov, ID number NCT06242808: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06242808. Registration date: 02/05/2024.
