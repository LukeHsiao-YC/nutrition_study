### 重點摘要
傳統上，由於電磁干擾的潛在風險，心臟植入式電子裝置（CIEDs）患者不建議進行生物電阻抗分析（BIA）來評估身體組成。本系統性回顧依循PRISMA指南，搜尋了多個資料庫以評估BIA在CIEDs患者中的安全性，並納入報告CIED功能結果的人體研究。研究結果顯示，納入的8項研究（共866次BIA測量，頻率範圍5-500 kHz）均未報告任何電磁干擾或影響CIED功能的不良事件。這些發現支持重新考慮對此類患者不建議使用BIA的臨床建議，表明BIA在這些患者中是安全的。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇系統性回顧填補了心臟植入式電子裝置（CIEDs）患者進行生物電阻抗分析（BIA）安全性的證據空白，挑戰了長期以來因電磁干擾疑慮而不建議使用BIA的臨床指引。未來研究可進一步探討更廣泛的BIA設備類型及測量條件下的安全性，以支持臨床指南的全面更新。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Safety of bioelectrical impedance analysis in patients with cardiac implantable electronic devices: A systematic review.
**Abstract:** Bioelectrical impedance analysis is widely used in clinical settings to assess body composition, contributes to the management of various health conditions. However, for patients with cardiac implantable electronic devices, such as pacemakers and implantable cardioverter-defibrillators, concerns about electromagnetic interference have led to longstanding recommendations against using bioelectrical impedance analysis. In this systematic review, we aimed to assess the available evidence regarding the safety of cardiac implantable electronic devices (CIEDs) during bioelectrical impedance analysis procedures. Following PRISMA guidelines, we searched PubMed, IEEE Xplore, Embase, and CINAHL for studies reporting on bioelectrical impedance analysis safety in patients with cardiac implantable electronic devices. Inclusion criteria focused on human studies reporting CIED function outcomes during BIA measurements. Data on BIA frequencies, types of CIEDs and BIA, and observed electromagnetic interference effects were extracted. The quality and bias of the studies were assessed using the Oxford Levels of Evidence and ROBINS-I tools. Out of 2003 records screened, 8 studies were included, encompassing 866 BIA measurements across frequencies ranging from 5 to 500 kHz. No studies reported electromagnetic interference or adverse events affecting CIED functionality. No evidence of electromagnetic interference or adverse effects on CIED function was identified in the available studies evaluating BIA in patients with CIEDs. These findings support reconsideration of clinical recommendations that discourage BIA use in this population.
