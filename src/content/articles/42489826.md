### 重點摘要
心血管疾病是主要的死因，飲食是關鍵的可變風險因素，而飲食問卷（FFQs）在評估飲食與疾病關聯時存在測量誤差。本元研究旨在評估近期心血管疾病研究中，FFQ驗證證據是否充分支持所分析的特定飲食暴露因子。研究系統性地分析了2019至2024年間發表的298項隊列研究，發現儘管88.3%的研究聲稱使用了經過驗證的FFQs，但在可接受的相關性閾值（r > 0.3）下，高達80.6%的研究在其關聯分析中使用的飲食變數缺乏其引用的FFQ驗證論文的直接支持。這項研究揭示了飲食與心血管疾病關聯研究中FFQ驗證存在顯著的不足，未來應更明確地報告驗證指標並確保特定飲食暴露因子得到直接驗證。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了既往文獻中未明確指出飲食與心血管疾病關聯研究裡，飲食頻率問卷(FFQ)所測量的特定飲食暴露因子與其驗證研究之間存在落差的研究空白。它量化了絕大多數研究中分析的飲食變數缺乏直接驗證的問題，指出儘管FFQ本身可能經過驗證，但其用於分析的具體飲食指標卻常未被充分驗證。未來的研究限制在於需要改進FFQ的驗證報告方式，並確保研究中使用的特定飲食暴露因子和飲食模式得到直接且明確的驗證，以提高研究結果的可靠性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Discrepancies between validated and selected dietary exposures for association analysis: a meta-research study of cohorts examining diet-cardiovascular disease associations.
**Abstract:** Cardiovascular diseases (CVD) remain a leading cause of death, with diet as a key modifiable risk factor. While prospective cohort studies using food frequency questionnaires (FFQs) underpin dietary guidelines, FFQs are prone to measurement errors that typically attenuate true associations. Characterizing the degree of error through validation is essential to properly interpret and adjust observed diet-disease relationships. It remains unclear whether FFQ validation evidence adequately supports the specific dietary exposures analyzed in recent CVD research. Therefore, this meta-research study aimed to evaluate this gap by examining discrepancies between validated dietary factors and those used in association analyses. A systematic search of multiple databases identified cohort studies published between 2019 and 2024 using keywords related to cohort studies, CVDs, and FFQs (PROSPERO: CRD42024589050). The proportion of cohorts with discrepancies was examined, defined as cases in which the dietary variables examined in association analyses lacked direct validation in the referenced FFQ validation paper. A total of 298 cohort studies from over 50 countries were included, with 88.3% using validated FFQs (against reference methods such as 24-hour dietary recalls, dietary records or biomarkers) for dietary assessment. A total of 93 unique validation papers were referenced across all included studies. However, at the acceptable correlation threshold (r > 0.3), discrepancies were identified in 80.6% of studies, indicating that the dietary variables examined in association analyses lacked direct validation in the referenced FFQ validation paper. Correlation analysis was the most commonly used validation metric (92.5% of validation studies), with 41.9% relying solely on correlation analyses. This research highlighted a significant FFQ validation gap in diet-CVD association research. Future research should clearly report FFQ validation metrics, especially de-attenuated correlation coefficients, and ensure that the dietary exposures of interest are covered in the referenced validation study. For dietary pattern studies, whenever feasible, researchers should directly validate dietary pattern scores against reference methods, so that these assessments are supported by direct evidence of validity. PROSPERO registry number: CRD42024589050.
