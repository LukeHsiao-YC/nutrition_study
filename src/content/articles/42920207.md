### 重點摘要
本研究旨在評估眶額葉皮質經顱直流電刺激 (OFC tDCS) 對肥胖者認知控制的影響。這項單盲、隨機、假性對照試驗招募 60 名肥胖成人，比較單次主動刺激與假性刺激的效果。研究結果顯示，單次 OFC tDCS 整體而言未能改善肥胖者的認知控制，與在瘦體重個體中的發現不同。然而，調節分析表明其認知效果取決於個體的神經認知特徵，例如基線食物成癮症狀或衝動性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了過去在非肥胖者中觀察到的 OFC tDCS 改善認知衝動控制的效應，在肥胖者中是否成立的知識空白。結果發現，單次 OFC tDCS 並不足以修改肥胖者的認知控制，這與瘦體重個體的報告不同。未來的研究應考慮多次刺激療程，並深入探討個體神經認知特徵在治療反應中的作用。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Effects of Orbitofrontal Cortex tDCS on Cognitive Control in Individuals With Obesity: A Randomized Sham-Controlled Trial.
**Abstract:** Neurocognitive traits can drive obesity-related eating behaviors. The orbitofrontal cortex (OFC) integrates reward and sensory information to guide food-related decision-making. Here, we build on prior data in individuals without obesity demonstrating that transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) of the OFC improves cognitive impulse control to test the effect of OFC tDCS in individuals with obesity. We use a single-blind, randomized, sham-controlled study among 60 adults with obesity to test whether either active (n = 30) or sham (n = 30) anodal tDCS targeting the left OFC specifically improves cognitive control using the Food and Word-Color Stroop tasks, the Iowa Gambling Task, and the Stop Signal Task. Across all cognitive outcomes, active stimulation did not differ from sham. However, moderation analyses revealed that higher baseline food addiction symptoms were associated with poorer performance with tDCS on the high-calorie Food Stroop condition, whereas lower impulsivity, lower emotional eating, and higher cognitive restraint were associated with improved post-stimulation performance on the low-calorie Food Stroop condition. Unlike what has been reported in lean individuals, a single session of OFC-targeted tDCS is insufficient to modify cognitive control in individuals with obesity. However, the cognitive effects of a single session of OFC stimulation depend on individual neurocognitive characteristics in obesity. ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04218383.
