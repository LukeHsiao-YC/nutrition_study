### 重點摘要
這項研究探討了在肯亞奈洛比非正式住區中，家庭內部對動物性食物 (ASF) 的優先順序與分配決策，如何影響母親和兒童的營養狀況。透過對304對母子進行橫斷面調查，研究發現母親通常會優先將更多ASF分配給兒童，但ASF優先順序與較低的母親及兒童血紅素水平相關，而實際的ASF分配則與兒童前一日ASFs攝取量呈正相關。研究強調了女性的決策權對於公平的資源分配和家庭成員獲取健康飲食的重要性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了在低收入非正式住區中，家庭內部對動物性食物的優先順序與實際分配如何影響母子營養狀況的知識空白，特別強調了女性在食物分配決策中的關鍵作用，為了解弱勢群體家庭內部的營養策略提供了深入見解。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Family Food Choice of Animal-Source Foods: Role of the Intra-Household Allocation and Prioritization With Maternal and Child Nutrition in Informal Settlements in Nairobi, Kenya.
**Abstract:** Animal-source foods (ASFs) are nutrient-dense and can improve nutrition. Women and children's consumption of ASF may depend on decisions related to how different foods are prioritized for and allocated to different household members. This study examined associations between ASF prioritization and allocation and maternal and child nutrition in low-income informal settlements in Nairobi, Kenya. A cross-sectional survey was conducted in September-October 2019 with a random sample of 304 mother-child (aged 6 to 59 months) dyads across seven wards with high livestock production. Mothers self-reported breastfeeding, complementary feeding, and ASF prioritization for household members when resources were scarce. Specific allocation to household members was assessed using a clay model to represent meat and a water model for milk. Anthropometry, dietary recalls, and hemoglobin levels were collected. Regression and causal mediation analyses assessed associations between prioritization, allocation, and nutrition. Households averaged 4.3 members and spent 38% of their income on food. Most mothers (88%) and children (92%) consumed ASFs the previous day, primarily dairy. Children were introduced to animal milk at 6 months, whereas poultry, red meat, and eggs were introduced at 8 to 9 months. Most mothers (97%) managed food distribution, prioritizing and allocating more ASFs to children than parents, adjusted for energy requirements. ASF prioritization was associated with lower hemoglobin in mothers and children, while allocation was associated with higher odds that ASF were consumed by children in the previous day. Overall, prioritization related to nutritional status, whereas allocation showed positive associations with children's diets. Women's decision-making authority is important for equitable resource distribution and individual access to healthy diets.
