### 重點摘要
過度螢幕使用與兒童過重是重要的公共衛生議題，但缺乏處理變時性混淆因子和重複暴露的因果推論研究。本研究利用新加坡世代數據，分析1030名1-10歲兒童的螢幕時間與BMI，採用g-estimation因果推論方法評估其因果效應和敏感期。結果顯示，在男孩中，任一歲數螢幕時間增加1個標準差會導致後續BMI增加0.048個標準差，但未發現敏感期；女孩則無此關聯。研究證實了男孩螢幕時間對後續BMI的平均因果效應，並強調研究時需考慮變時性混淆因子和鏈式暴露。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了過去因果推論研究中，處理變時性混淆因子和重複暴露對兒童身體質量發展影響的空白，並首次透過因果推論方法探討螢幕時間的敏感期。未來的研究應進一步探究女孩族群中缺乏關聯的原因，並持續關注變時性混淆因子和鏈式暴露在敏感期效應中的重要性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Screen device use and body mass development in children: a causal inference approach to sensitive periods.
**Abstract:** Excessive screen time and childhood overweight are major public health concerns. Although observational studies suggest a bidirectional association, causal inference studies addressing time-varying confounding and repeated exposures across childhood are scarce. This limits our ability to identify when screen time may be most consequential. While 'sensitive periods' are a central concept in life-course epidemiology, they are rarely examined by using causal methods. We evaluated the causal effect of screen time on body mass index (BMI) across childhood and examined sensitive periods. We analysed cohort data in Singapore with 10 repeated exposure and outcome assessments from ages 1-10 years (1030 children, 546 boys). Parents reported children's screen time. Children's BMI was derived from anthropometric measures. We contrasted mixed modelling with g-estimation of structural nested mean modelling, accounting for feedback between chained exposures and time-varying confounders. Using g-estimation, a 1-SD increase in screen time at any given age was associated with a 0.048-SD increase in subsequent BMI in boys (95% confidence interval: 0.005; 0.091), with no evidence for sensitive periods. In contrast, mixed modelling suggested that more screen time at age 2 years only was associated with higher BMI in boys aged 2-10 years. No associations were found in girls. Counterfactual outcomes did not differ between stringent early-life vs. moderate sustained interventions. We identified an average causal effect of screen time on subsequent BMI in boys, but no evidence for sensitive periods using causal inference. Time-varying confounders and chained exposure must be considered when studying sensitive-period effects.
