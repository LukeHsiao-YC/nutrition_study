### 重點摘要
全球微量營養素缺乏問題嚴重，且超加工食品（UPFs）攝取量高與飲食品質不佳及疾病發展相關。本系統性文獻回顧旨在探討UPFs攝取對不同生命階段微量營養素狀態的影響，透過多個資料庫篩選出僅四篇橫斷面研究。研究發現，UPFs攝取量較高與類胡蘿蔔素、維生素A、維生素E、鉬和硒的水平較低相關，但與UPFs中常見的γ-生育酚、鉀和鈉的濃度較高有關。儘管合格研究數量稀少且設計限制，但鑑於UPFs日益普及及其對健康的重大影響，未來仍需更多研究深入探討兩者間的關係。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究的限制在於合格研究數量稀少（僅四篇橫斷面研究）且研究設計不足，這限制了對超加工食品與微量營養素狀態之間關係的全面理解。未來的研究需要更多元且嚴謹的設計，以充分探討此日益重要的健康議題。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Ultra-Processed Food Consumption and Micronutrient Status Throughout the Life Course: A Systematic Review.
**Abstract:** Micronutrient deficiencies remain a significant global malnutrition issue, affecting morbidity and mortality, particularly among children and women of reproductive age. Similarly, the consumption of ultra-processed foods (UPFs) is prevalent in the diets of various populations and is associated with poor diet quality and the development of certain diseases. Determining whether dietary patterns involving UPFs contribute to this health burden is crucial for understanding how to promote prevention and achieve complete nutritional recovery. To identify the influence of UPFs on micronutrients status at different stages of life. Studies with information on biomarkers of vitamins, minerals, and/or trace elements and UPFs consumption were searched in the PubMed/MEDLINE, LILACS/SciELO, Embase, Scopus, and Web of Science databases on August 24, 2024, and repeated in July 2025. Three researchers participated in the screening and data extraction, 2 other verified the accuracy of data extraction from eligible studies. The methodological quality of the studies was assessed with the JBI critical appraisal checklist. The search strategy resulted in 377 studies and only 4 cross-sectional studies were eligible, conducted in children, pregnant women, and lactating women with different vitamins and minerals. Among the findings, greater inclusion of UPFs in the diet was associated with lower levels of carotenoids, vitamin A, vitamin E, molybdenum, and selenium, and higher concentrations of γ-tocopherol, potassium, and sodium-common micronutrients in UPFs. In children, findings on the association between UPFs consumption and anemia have been conflicting. Despite the scarcity and limitation of study designs, further research is essential to investigate and comprehend the relationship of UPFs on micronutrient deficiency due to the increasing prevalence of UPFs in diets and its significant health implications. PROSPERO registration No. CRD42023488818.
