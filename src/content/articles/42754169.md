### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討花生攝取對循環脂質體及心血管疾病 (CVD) 和第二型糖尿病 (T2D) 風險的影響。研究整合了 ARISTOTLE 臨床試驗的資料（63 名健康年輕人隨機分組攝取花生或花生製品六個月）與 EPIC-Potsdam 前瞻性世代研究（包含 T2D 和 CVD 病例），以識別花生特有的脂質體特徵 (PeLS)。結果顯示，花生攝取能改變循環脂質體，主要涉及超長鏈飽和脂肪酸 (VLCSFA) 脂質，且此 PeLS 與較低的第二型糖尿病風險顯著相關 (HR=0.89)，但與心血管疾病風險無顯著關聯。此關聯性有高達 89% 由身體質量指數 (BMI) 所中介，表明花生可能透過影響 BMI 來降低第二型糖尿病風險。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究釐清了花生獨特的脂肪酸組成如何影響人體循環脂質體，以及這些變化如何與第二型糖尿病風險相關聯，特別是發現了身體質量指數 (BMI) 在此關聯中扮演的潛在中介角色，為膳食脂肪品質對代謝健康的影響提供了新的見解。研究的潛在限制包括 ARISTOTLE 試驗的受試者數較少且主要為健康年輕成人，以及 EPIC-Potsdam 屬於觀察性研究，未來研究可擴大受試者範圍並深入探討 BMI 的確切作用機制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Peanuts-derived plasma lipids and lower risk of type-2 diabetes: Association mediated by body mass index. An intervention and case-cohort study.
**Abstract:** Cardiometabolic risk can be modified by dietary factors, including fat quality. Peanuts have a distinctive fatty acid (FA) composition rich in very long-chain saturated fatty acids (VLCSFA), with potentially favorable effects on cardiometabolic health. To characterize the effects of peanut consumption on the circulating lipidome and examine associations of the resulting changes with cardiovascular disease (CVD) and type-2 diabetes (T2D). We integrated data from the clinical trial ARISTOTLE and the prospective, population-based European Prospective Investigation into Cancer and Nutrition (EPIC)-Potsdam cohort. ARISTOTLE randomized 63 healthy young adults to 25g/d skin-roasted peanuts, 32g/d peanut butter, or 32g/d control butter with peanut oil for 6 months. EPIC-Potsdam included case-cohort samples for T2D (774 cases) and CVD (545 cases). Plasma lipids were measured pre- and post-intervention in ARISTOTLE and at baseline in EPIC-Potsdam. Linear mixed models identified within-class FA sums responsive to peanut fat ‒ pooling all participants ‒ in ARISTOTLE, which were combined to derive a peanut lipidomic signature (PeLS) in EPIC-Potsdam. Multivariable Cox models were used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) for PeLS and individual lipids with incident T2D and CVD. Hypothesis-generating mediation by body mass index (BMI) was assessed. The ARISTOTLE intervention modified 51 within-class FA sums, predominantly VLCSFA-containing lipids, particularly sphingolipids. The resulting PeLS was modestly associated with lower risk of T2D (HR=0.89 per 1-SD increase in PeLS, 95%CI: 0.80, 0.99), but not CVD (HR=1.01 per 1-SD increase in PeLS, 95%CI: 0.90, 1.15), in EPIC-Potsdam, with BMI identified as a potential mediator of this association (proportion explainable of 89%, 95%CI: 57%, 100%). Peanut consumption induces changes in the circulating lipidome, largely involving VLCSFA-containing lipids, that are associated with lower T2D risk partly through BMI. These results highlight peanuts as a source of dietary fats that may be linked to lower risk of T2D. The ARISTOTLE study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, approved by the Ethics Committee of Clinical Investigation of the University of Barcelona (Barcelona, Spain, on 1 October 2019), and registered at https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04324749 (accessed on 2 April 2026).
