### 重點摘要
GLP-1受體促效劑漸被用於治療兒童肥胖、糖尿病前期及第二型糖尿病，本研究旨在探討兒科GLP-1RA使用者中營養缺乏的發生率及營養治療/諮詢的利用現況。研究分析2017-2022年超過1億名患者的行政索賠數據，篩選出2,031名10-17歲GLP-1RA使用者，並追蹤一年內的營養缺乏診斷及營養諮詢情況。結果顯示，16.88%的患者在一年內被診斷出至少一種營養缺乏，最常見為維生素D缺乏；然而，僅有23.3%的患者在180天內接受了營養諮詢。這表明在兒科GLP-1RA治療中，營養缺乏是一種被低估的風險，提示需要將營養治療/諮詢整合至常規照護中，以支持患者的健康成長與長期預後。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇研究填補了在兒科GLP-1RA使用者中，營養缺乏發生率及營養治療/諮詢（NT/C）利用情況的知識空白，並明確指出目前照護模式在主動預防性營養支持方面存在缺失。未來的研究可進一步探討營養介入的具體成效，並評估不同GLP-1RA藥物對營養狀態的差異影響。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Nutritional Deficiencies, Complications, and Nutrition Therapy/Counseling in Pediatric Patients Using GLP-1 Receptor Agonists.
**Abstract:** Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) are increasingly used to treat childhood obesity, prediabetes, and type 2 diabetes. We examined nutritional deficiency incidence and nutrition therapy/counseling (NT/C) utilization among pediatric GLP-1RA users. We analyzed Inovalon administrative claims data from 2017 to 2022 covering over 100 million patients to identify 2,031 GLP-1RA users aged 10-17 years who met continuous enrollment criteria and had no prior diagnosis of nutritional deficiency. Nutritional deficiencies were identified using the International Classification of Diseases, 10th edition codes for up to 1 year following GLP-1RA initiation. NT/C visits, time to first NT/C visit, and differences in nutritional deficiency diagnoses by NT/C consultation were assessed. Results are reported as proportions and means, with group comparisons using chi-square tests. The mean age was 15 (±1.8) years, 61.5% were female, and 62.6% had obesity. Liraglutide (78.6%), dulaglutide (10.4%), and semaglutide (9.1%) were the most prescribed GLP-1RA drugs. Within 1 year, 16.88% of patients were diagnosed with at least one nutritional deficiency or deficiency-related complication, most frequently vitamin D deficiency (12.4%), followed by nutritional anemia (1.55%), and iron-deficiency anemia (1.44%). Only 23.3% had NT/C visit within 180 days (mean time to first visit: 149 days). Patients with NT/C consultations had higher rates of nutritional deficiencies and deficiency-related complications than those without (23.2% vs. 14.8%,  Nutritional deficiencies are a meaningful and under-recognized risk among pediatric patients receiving GLP-1RA therapy. Current care patterns suggest missed opportunities for proactive and preventative nutrition support. Integration of NT/C care into pediatric GLP-1RA treatment may support healthy growth and long-term outcomes.
