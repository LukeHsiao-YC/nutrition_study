### 重點摘要
本研究旨在填補發炎性腸道疾病(IBD)患者營養失調流行病學數據的空白，首次在統一框架下系統性評估多種主要營養障礙的盛行率。透過對201項觀察性研究進行系統性回顧與整合分析，發現IBD患者體重過輕盛行率為11.5%，營養不良為27.6%，體重過重為23.1%，肥胖為13.5%，肌肉量不足為48.3%，肌少症為12.6%。研究結論強調IBD的營養照護應超越單純的體重監測，並指出活躍期疾病與營養不良顯著相關。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
過去關於發炎性腸道疾病(IBD)營養失調的流行病學數據零碎，且缺乏同時評估體重過輕、超重或肥胖以及肌少症等主要營養失調的統一框架，本研究首次填補了此空白。然而，由於研究結果存在廣泛的預測區間，顯示高度異質性，且營養不良與肌少症數據多來自醫院而非社區設定，未來的研究需透過以人群為基礎、採用標準化診斷標準並涵蓋不同地區的調查來驗證這些觀察結果。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The prevalence of nutritional disorders in inflammatory bowel disease: A systematic review and meta-analysis.
**Abstract:** The epidemiology of nutritional disorders in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) remains poorly characterized. Existing evidence is fragmented across specific conditions or confined to single regions, and no study has simultaneously assessed the principal nutritional disorders-malnutrition, overweight or obesity, and sarcopenia-in a unified framework. To address this gap, we systematically searched PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and China Biology Medicine from inception to July 31, 2024 and updated on July 30, 2026. Observational studies reporting prevalence based on standard diagnostic criteria were included. Pooled estimates with 95% confidence interval (CI) and prediction interval (PI) were derived using a random-effects model. Pre-specified subgroup analyses were conducted by sex, age, disease subtype and activity, diagnostic criteria, study setting and gepgraphic variation. Study quality and publication bias were also assessed. After literature screening, 201 studies across 32 countries were included. Underweight affected 11.5% (95% CI: 9.0-14.6%, 95% PI : 1.0-61.7), whereas consensus-based malnutrition [GLIM (Global Leadership Initiative on Malnutrition), ESPEN (European Society for Clinical Nutrition and Metabolism), MNA (Mini Nutritional Assessment), SGA/PG-SGA (Subjective Global Assessment or its variant Patient-Generated SGA) affected 27.6% (95% CI: 20.5-36.1%, 95% PI : 3.6-79.7). Notably, this malnutrition estimate was derived almost exclusively from hospital-based settings (over 80%) and predominantly from a single region. Overweight and obesity affected 23.1% (95% CI : 20.6-25.9, 95%PI : 8.1-50.8) and 13.5% (95%CI : 11.7-15.6, 95%PI : 2.9-44.6), respectively. For muscle depletion, low muscle mass defined by skeletal muscle index was identified in 48.3% (95% CI: 41.8-55.0%, 95% PI : 19.7-78.1), whereas consensus-based sarcopenia [EWGSOP (European Working Group on Sarcopenia in Older People) or AWGS (Asian Working Group on Sarcopenia)] affected 12.6% (95% CI: 6.1-24.2%, 95% PI : 0.9-70.6). Of note, the majority of included studies for malnutrition and sarcopenia were hospital-based. Active disease was associated with significantly higher prevalence of malnutrition. No significant differences were observed between disease subtypes. In conclusion, our findings provide a broad overview of the prevalence of major nutritional disorders in patients with IBD, highlighting that nutritional care in IBD should extend beyond simple weight monitoring. However, given the wide prediction intervals, these pooled estimates should be viewed as averages across highly heterogeneous settings rather than a precise global figure. Future research, particularly population-based studies with standardized diagnostic criteria across diverse regions, is needed to validate our observation.
