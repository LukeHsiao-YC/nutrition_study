### 重點摘要
全球鈣攝取量評估常因忽略生物利用度而失準。本研究應用 Weaver 等人的演算法，整合全球食品供應數據，首次估算全球生物可利用鈣量，並與總鈣量及建議攝取量進行比較。結果顯示，全球平均鈣生物利用度為 30.7%，生物可利用鈣量普遍低於總鈣量，尤其在非洲和東南亞地區的供應量嚴重不足。這項研究強調了在評估鈣適足性時考慮生物利用度的重要性，並為制定區域性公共衛生策略以改善生物可利用鈣的獲取提供了依據。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了全球鈣攝取評估中未納入生物利用度的空白，提供了更精確的可吸收鈣量估計。然而，研究結果反映的是食物供應量而非實際攝取量，且未考慮到不同生命階段的膳食需求，這限制了其在個人層面或特定人口群體應用上的精確性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Global Calcium Bioavailability.
**Abstract:** Calcium intake shortfalls are common worldwide, yet calcium adequacy is typically estimated from total calcium content without accounting for differences in bioavailability. As a result, the amount of calcium available for absorption across the global food supply remains unclear. The objective of this study is to apply the Weaver et al. calcium bioavailability algorithm, which incorporates calcium load, phytate, and oxalate, to estimate bioavailable calcium in the global food supply, compare total and bioavailable calcium against recommended intakes, and identify regional differences in calcium adequacy. Data from the Food and Agriculture Organization Food Balance Sheets were integrated with nutrient composition data from FoodData Central to estimate total and bioavailable calcium availability by country and region. Food Balance Sheets provide standardized national-level estimates of per capita food availability for human consumption; therefore, results reflect food supply availability rather than actual intake and do not account for dietary needs across life stages. Calcium, phytate, and oxalate values were mapped to food composition databases, adjusted for serving size, and used to calculate bioavailable calcium for individual foods. Country-level estimates were generated by summing daily per capita bioavailable calcium across foods. The Weaver et al. calcium bioavailability algorithm was successfully applied to global food supply data, yielding an estimated average calcium bioavailability of 30.7%, consistent with the general estimate that approximately 30% of dietary calcium is absorbed. Predicted bioavailable calcium was lower than total calcium across all food categories, reflecting reduced absorption associated with calcium load and the presence of phytate and oxalate in the food supply. The greatest shortfalls in bioavailable calcium supply were observed in Africa and Southeast Asia, where predicted bioavailability was below recommended intakes for healthy adults. These findings demonstrate that accounting for bioavailability can improve assessment of calcium adequacy and helps identify regions where public health strategies are needed to improve access to bioavailable calcium.
