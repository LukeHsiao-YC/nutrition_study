### 重點摘要
儘管知情同意和共享決策對於選擇性骨科手術（如髖、膝關節置換或腰椎減壓）是必要且推薦的，但現行的對話和文件記錄仍未達標準。本研究旨在透過快速文獻回顧，從 Ovid Medline、Embase 和 Cochrane 資料庫中篩選出17篇符合條件的文章，以找出改善知情同意、共享決策及其文件記錄的障礙與促進因素。研究發現，改善的關鍵在於設定實際的術前術後預期、提供患者額外時間與資料、臨床醫師運用「教回法」、機構明確知情同意要求，並利用電子健康記錄提供更簡短的模板化表格。最終，醫院、診所、臨床醫師和政策制定者需共同努力，透過標準化材料和改善醫病溝通，確保患者獲得必要資訊，以達成符合標準的知情同意與共享決策。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究透過快速文獻回顧，系統性地梳理並總結了影響選擇性骨科手術中知情同意與共享決策的障礙與促進因素，填補了此領域文獻分散的空白，並為實務改進提供了具體方向。未來的研究或可探討如何有效實施這些建議，以及其對患者預後的實際影響。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Barriers and facilitators to improving informed consent, shared decision making, and clinical documentation in elective orthopedic care: A rapid review of the literature.
**Abstract:** Although informed consent for treatment is a medicolegal requirement and shared decision making (SDM) is recommended by clinical guidelines for patients undergoing elective orthopedic procedures for hip, knee, or lumbar spine, research consistently shows that these informed consent conversations and documentation of these processes are below the standard of care. Our goal was to identify barriers and facilitators to improving informed consent, SDM, and documentation for patients undergoing these procedures. We conducted a rapid review of the literature using Ovid Medline, Embase.com, and Cochrane CENTRAL and CDSR to address this goal. A search of relevant databases yielded 1114 articles for review. Two trained researchers screened the article abstracts and full texts. Eligible studies were qualitative or quantitative evaluations of informed consent or SDM for elective hip/knee replacement or lumbar decompression surgery. Seventeen articles were eligible for inclusion. Barriers and facilitators to informed consent and SDM overlapped, suggesting that the best way to improve both would be to ensure discussions with patients include setting realistic expectations of pre- and post-operative processes, that patients be provided with additional time and materials, that clinicians use teach-back to ensure patients are informed, and to ensure institutions have a clear set of expectations for what is required on the informed consent form and for the informed consent process. Further, hospitals can improve informed consent through use of shorter, templated forms that leverage the electronic health record. Eligible articles had to be English language, full-text available, and based in the US, UK, or Canada. The standardization of materials, improved patient-clinician conversations, and the use of additional materials to support patients and conversations can help the informed consent process reach a reasonable person standard. Hospitals, clinics, clinicians, and policy makers must work together to ensure patients are provided with the necessary verbal and written materials to ensure informed consent and SDM occur in routine care.
