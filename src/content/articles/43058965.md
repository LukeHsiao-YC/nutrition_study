### 重點摘要
這項範圍審查旨在綜合美國關於居住因素如何影響嬰幼兒餵養（IYCF）結果的現有證據。研究團隊檢索並納入了23篇文章，依據Taylor (2018) 框架將發現分為居住穩定性、可負擔性、品質與安全及社區環境四個途徑。結果顯示，在19項主要研究中，有8項發現居住因素與IYCF（尤其多為母乳餵養）之間存在至少一個統計學上顯著的關聯，儘管測量異質性與調整後效應的減弱使解釋複雜化。研究建議未來應採取多層次介入，並加強居住不安全測量與擴展餵養結果指標。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻綜合了美國關於居住因素與嬰幼兒餵養（IYCF）結果關聯的證據，填補了此前缺乏全面性統整的空白。然而，未來的研究仍需改進居住不安全感的測量方法，將IYCF結果擴展至母乳餵養以外的層面，並評估與居住相關的干預措施。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Housing Factors and Infant and Young Child Feeding Outcomes in the United States: A Scoping Review.
**Abstract:** Housing insecurity is a pervasive social determinant of health that may influence infant and young child feeding (IYCF) outcomes, yet evidence from the United States (U.S.) has not been comprehensively synthesized. This scoping review examined associations between housing factors and IYCF outcomes among U.S. families with children from birth through 2 years of age. Following Joanna Briggs Institute methodology and PRISMA-ScR guidelines, we searched PubMed, CINAHL, PsycINFO, and Embase through June 2026. Twenty-three articles (19 primary studies and four reviews) met inclusion criteria. Findings were organized using Taylor's (2018) Four Pathways Connecting Housing and Health Framework, with findings categorized into four pathways: stability, affordability, quality and safety, and neighborhood context. Breastfeeding outcomes were assessed most frequently. Among the 19 primary studies, 16 used quantitative designs testing housing-IYCF associations; eight identified at least one statistically significant association, while eight reported none. Heterogeneous measurement approaches complicated interpretation, and associations that were significant before adjustment were often attenuated afterward, which may reflect the role of broader social and structural factors along mediating pathways rather than an absence of association. Authors commonly recommended multilevel approaches, including housing screening and referral, expanded lactation support, integrated housing and social-needs support, and structural investments in housing and communities. Further research should strengthen measurement of housing insecurity, broaden IYCF outcomes beyond breastfeeding, and evaluate housing-related interventions to support recommended IYCF outcomes.
