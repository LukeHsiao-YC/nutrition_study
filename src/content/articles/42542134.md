### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討情緒調節與早期適應不良圖式在年輕女性適應不良飲食行為中的作用，並檢驗不同介入策略的長期效果。研究採四組準實驗性設計，招募18-35歲大學女性參與為期12週的情緒調節訓練、圖式引導訓練、兩者結合訓練或控制組，並進行四次評估。結果顯示，結合情緒調節與圖式引導的訓練在減少情緒性暴飲暴食、習慣性暴食及整體適應不良飲食方面，展現出最強且最持久的改善。這支持了情緒調節是適應不良飲食的關鍵可修改因素，並建議整合兩種方法能提升介入效果的持久性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
此研究填補了心理學模型（情緒調節與早期適應不良圖式）對適應不良飲食影響缺乏長期縱向證據的空白。然而，研究結果強調支持的是互補過程而非單一介入的功效，且由於採準實驗設計，未來研究需進一步探討在更廣泛人群中的隨機對照試驗效果。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Testing a Model of Emotion Regulation and Schema-Related Vulnerability in Maladaptive Eating: Evidence from a Four-Arm Longitudinal Study.
**Abstract:** Maladaptive eating behaviors such as emotional overeating, habitual overeating, and dysfunctional dietary restraint are important targets for prevention research. Psychological models suggest that difficulties in emotion regulation and early maladaptive schemas may contribute to dysregulated eating, yet longitudinal evidence remains limited. This study examined whether theoretically informed intervention contrasts affected maladaptive eating trajectories in young adult women. University women aged 18-35 years participated in a four-arm quasi-experimental study with assessments at baseline (T1), post-intervention (T2), and two follow-up assessments (T3 and T4). Participants were allocated by class groups to emotion regulation training (ER_only), schema-informed training (Schema_only), combined emotion regulation plus schema-informed training (ER_schema), or an active educational control condition. All programs consisted of twelve weekly 90-minute group sessions. Active intervention conditions showed more favorable trajectories than the control condition on primary outcomes. The ER_schema condition showed the strongest and most sustained reductions in emotional overeating, habitual overeating, dietary restraint, total maladaptive eating, and emotion dysregulation. ER_only produced rapid improvements in emotion regulation, whereas the Schema_only condition showed slower improvements. Secondary outcomes were smaller and less consistent. These findings support emotion regulation as a modifiable process associated with maladaptive eating and suggest that integrating self-regulation skills with schema-informed education may enhance intervention durability. Findings are best interpreted as supporting complementary processes rather than intervention efficacy. These results may inform scalable prevention programs for young adult women and contribute to health psychology research on cognitive-emotional vulnerability and self-regulation.
