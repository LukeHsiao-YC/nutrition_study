### 重點摘要
美國死亡率改善停滯，但過去研究常忽略死亡前的疾病史及多重共病。本研究分析1997-2018年美國成年人數據，透過潛在類別分析識別出五種疾病史模式，並評估其組成變化及模式內死亡率如何影響整體趨勢。結果顯示，健康無病人口每年減少近1%，主要被心血管代謝和高負擔多重共病模式取代，且大多數模式在2000年代中期後死亡率停滯或上升。這反映了高共病模式的普遍性增加，以及這些模式內存活率的惡化或停滯，突顯預防與疾病管理工作的成效有限。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究透過分析疾病史模式，填補了過去研究在理解美國死亡率停滯時，未能充分考量多重共病變化與死亡前疾病史的空白。它揭示了整體死亡率停滯不僅是因高共病模式人數增加，也因這些模式內患者存活率惡化或停滯。未來的研究可進一步探討具體的預防和疾病管理策略，以應對這些挑戰，並需考慮自我報告數據的潛在限制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Sicker at all ages? Two decades of changing disease histories and mortality in the United States.
**Abstract:** Stagnating cardiovascular disease mortality and rising drug- and suicide-related deaths are key drivers of stalled US mortality improvements. However, prior research has largely overlooked disease histories preceding death, despite increasing multimorbidity. We examined changes in disease-history profiles in the US population and assessed how shifts in profile composition and within-profile mortality shaped mortality trends. We analysed adults aged ≥30 years (N = 510 372) from the IPUMS-NHIS (National Health Interview Survey) 1997-2018 linked mortality files (follow-up through 2019). Latent class analysis on the pooled sample across all ages and years identified disease-history profiles based on self-reported cancer, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, mental health, internal diseases, obesity, and physical limitations. Logistic regression was used to estimate profile-specific 5-year mortality over time, and counterfactual decomposition was used to quantify the contributions of compositional change and within-profile mortality improvement to overall mortality trends. Five disease-history profiles were identified. Prevalence and 5-year death probability ranged from disease-free (62.4%; men 3.7% [95% confidence interval (CI) 3.5-3.8], women 2.4% [95% CI 2.3-2.5]) to high-burden multimorbidity (5.9%; men 17.8% [95% CI 16.8-18.8], women 12.6% [95% CI 11.9-13.3]), with substantial gradients across intermediate profiles. Profile composition varied substantially by gender, education, and race/ethnicity. The disease-free population declined by nearly 1% annually, replaced primarily by cardiometabolic and high-burden multimorbidity profiles. Most profiles experienced stagnating or increasing mortality after the mid-2000s. The decomposition shows that within-profile mortality improvement was the dominant driver of declining mortality; compositional shifts, mostly driven by the declining disease-free share, partially offset these gains. Stagnating US mortality reflects both the growing prevalence of high-morbidity profiles and worsening or stagnating survival within them, highlighting the limited reach of prevention and disease-management efforts.
