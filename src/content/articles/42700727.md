### 重點摘要
青少年的健康跨期決策影響其學習適應與成年發展潛力，然而社會經濟地位（SES）如何影響此決策的實證研究尚不足。本研究根據生命史理論，透過三項研究共招募來自中國西北部的284名青少年進行探討。儘管SES對健康跨期決策的整體主效應不顯著，但探索性分析一致顯示SES的影響可能因食物情境而異。結果指出，客觀與主觀SES較低的青少年在面對不健康食物時，表現出較高的時間折扣率，為SES與青少年健康相關跨期決策之間的關聯提供了初步證據。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
過去缺乏足夠的實證證據來闡明社會經濟地位是否會影響青少年的健康跨期決策。本研究填補了此空白，提供了社會經濟地位與青少年健康相關跨期決策關聯的初步證據，並指出未來的研究應更深入探討食物情境的差異性，以開發針對低社會經濟背景青少年的健康行為干預措施，如目標設定訓練與飲食環境優化。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The differences in adolescents' health intertemporal decision-making across different socioeconomic statuses: Evidence based on delay discount rates of healthy and unhealthy foods.
**Abstract:** Adolescents' health intertemporal decision-making not only affects current learning adaptation but also influences development potential in adulthood. However, there is a lack of sufficient empirical evidence regarding whether socioeconomic status, which is an important background factor reflecting the availability of resources and future expectations, influences adolescents' health intertemporal decision-making. Based on life history theory, our study conducted three studies to examine the differences in adolescents' health intertemporal decision-making across different socioeconomic statuses. Study 1 consisted of two sub-studies: Studies 1A and 1B recruited 90 and 84 adolescent participants, respectively, from northwestern China. Based on objective and subjective socioeconomic status scales, the participants were divided into high- and low-score groups to examine the differences in adolescents' health intertemporal decision-making across different socioeconomic statuses. Study 2 recruited 110 adolescent participants from northwest China and used an income feedback comparison paradigm to manipulate subjective socioeconomic status, thereby examining the relationship between the two through experimental manipulation. In all three studies, the main effect of socioeconomic status on health intertemporal decision-making was not significant. However, exploratory analyses consistently indicated that the impact of socioeconomic status on adolescents' health intertemporal decision-making might vary depending on the food context. The results of Study 1 showed that adolescents in the low-score groups for objective socioeconomic status and subjective socioeconomic status exhibited a higher time discount rate when faced with unhealthy food. The results of Study 2 showed that adolescents in the low subjective socioeconomic status group exhibited a higher time discount rate when faced with unhealthy food. The present findings provide preliminary evidence for the association between socioeconomic status and health-related intertemporal decision-making among adolescents. Moreover, they offer a reference for future research aimed at developing interventions to promote healthy behaviors in adolescents from lower socioeconomic backgrounds, including goal-setting training, future self-imagination, and food environment optimization.
