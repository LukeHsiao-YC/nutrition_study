### 重點摘要
這項回溯性世代研究旨在探討在真實世界情境下，低碳水化合物飲食（LCD）干預後長期體重維持的預測因素。研究追蹤了294名過重或肥胖的成年參與者平均36.7個月，發現其中52%出現體重復胖。結果顯示，肥胖家族史顯著增加體重復胖的風險，而每晚充足睡眠（6-8小時）則具有保護作用。本研究強調遺傳傾向和生活方式因素共同影響LCD後的長期體重管理。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了低碳水化合物飲食在真實世界環境下，長期體重維持狀況及其預測因素的不確定性。然而，作為一項回溯性研究，它可能存在因果關係推斷的限制以及潛在的數據回憶偏差。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Long-term weight maintenance after a low-carbohydrate diet: a real-world retrospective cohort study.
**Abstract:** Low-carbohydrate diet (LCD) promotes short-term weight loss in individuals with overweight or obesity, but long-term maintenance remains uncertain, particularly in real-world settings. This study aimed to identify predictors of weight regain following a short-term LCD intervention in a hospital-based weight management program. This retrospective cohort study included adult individuals with overweight or obesity (BMI ≥ 24 kg/m²) who underwent an LCD between July 2016 and July 2020. Baseline anthropometric and metabolic data were extracted from medical records, and follow-up weight and lifestyle information were collected via structured telephone interviews and questionnaires at least 12 months post-intervention. Weight regain was defined as regaining ≥25% of initial weight loss. Multivariable logistic regression identified independent predictors of weight regain, adjusting for age, sex, and other confounders. Among 294 participants, all subjects received an LCD intervention for an average of 6.9 ± 4.2 weeks, and the average follow-up duration was 36.7 months. Of these, 153 (52%) regained weight, while 141 (48%) maintained weight loss. Despite a higher initial BMI (30.6 ± 5.3 vs. 29.1 ± 4.4 kg/m², p = 0.008), maintainers exhibited greater long-term stability. A family history of obesity significantly increased weight regain risk (OR = 2.36-2.84, p < 0.01), whereas adequate sleep (6-8 h/night) was protective (OR = 0.15-0.16, p < 0.05). LCD duration, initial weight loss, and exercise frequency were not consistently associated with maintenance. Both genetic predisposition and lifestyle factors influence long-term weight outcomes, underscoring the interplay between immutable and modifiable determinants in weight management.
