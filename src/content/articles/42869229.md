### 重點摘要
這項英國生物銀行橫斷面研究調查了24,223名參與者，旨在探討飲食中血基質鐵、非血基質鐵攝取量以及HFE基因多型性與腦部MRI顯示的鐵沉積表型之間的關聯。研究結果顯示，膳食血基質鐵攝取量和血色素沉著症相關的HFE基因多型性均與腦部MRI的QSM強度增加和T2*強度降低有關，這代表腦部鐵水平升高。相反地，非血基質鐵攝取則與較低的腦部鐵水平相關。本研究結論指出，飲食中的血基質鐵攝取和血色素沉著症相關基因多型性皆與腦部鐵水平升高有關。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了個體基因和飲食鐵攝取如何影響腦部鐵質累積的理解空白，指出HFE基因多型性與膳食血基質鐵攝取對腦部鐵質累積具有相似的關聯性。然而，由於是橫斷面研究設計，它無法確立因果關係，也無法追蹤鐵質累積隨時間變化的動態，這是未來研究可探討的限制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Dietary heme iron intake and common hemochromatosis HFE polymorphisms have similar associations with brain MRI phenotypes: a UK Biobank cohort study.
**Abstract:** Brain iron accumulation is associated with aging and neurodegeneration, but how individual genetics and dietary iron intake affect brain iron is not well understood. Hemochromatosis is a genetic disorder arising from single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in the HFE gene, causing iron overload and brain iron accumulation, and is associated with accelerated brain aging. This cross-sectional study examined how dietary iron intake and hemochromatosis-associated single nucleotide polymorphisms are associated with brain MRI phenotypes sensitive to iron. We used a subset of the UK Biobank (N=24,223 participants) to investigate associations between dietary heme and non-heme iron intake (dietary predictors), HFE SNPs rs1799945 and rs1800562 (genetic predictors), brain quantitative susceptibility mapping (QSM) and T2* MRI phenotypes (primary outcomes) using multiple regression. We also investigated associations of HFE SNPs and dietary intake with hemoglobin, anemia risk, and MRI-derived liver iron levels as markers of systemic iron status. We compared brain QSM for rs1799945 and rs1800562 homozygotes and rs1799945/rs1800562 compound heterozygotes relative to control genotype subjects without either HFE SNP using ANCOVA and Tukey-Kramer post-hoc testing. Significance was set at false discovery rate (FDR) <0.05. Intake of dietary heme iron and both HFE SNPs were associated with increased QSM and decreased T2* MRI intensities (FDR<0.05), a pattern associated with increased brain iron levels. Non-heme iron intake was associated with a slight, but statistically significant, decrease in QSM and increase in T2* intensity, which is consistent with less brain iron. Both SNPs and heme iron intake were associated with higher hemoglobin and MRI-derived liver iron levels. Adjusted mean QSM intensity was generally highest for rs1800562 homozygotes and mostly higher in rs1799945 homozygotes and rs1799945/rs1800562 compound heterozygotes compared to control genotype. Dietary heme iron intake and SNPs linked to hemochromatosis are associated with altered brain MRI intensities consistent with elevated brain iron.
