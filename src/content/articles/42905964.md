### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討妊娠糖尿病（GDM）與生命早期母乳、乳暈皮膚及嬰兒糞便中微生物群落的長期關聯。研究追蹤了104對母嬰至產後6個月，在不同時間點採集樣本並進行16S rRNA基因定序。結果發現，GDM與所有三個微生物生態位中的母嬰微生物群落存在差異，包括乳暈皮膚多樣性降低、母乳及嬰兒糞便微生物群落的時間動態變化，以及特定菌屬的富集或減少。這些發現表明，GDM導致生命早期相互關聯的母嬰微生物生態位中存在持久且具生態位特異性的微生物差異。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
過去研究多聚焦於妊娠糖尿病對母嬰腸道菌群的影響，但對於這些關聯是否延伸至其他母嬰微生物生態位（如母乳、乳暈皮膚）以及其持久性尚不清楚。本研究透過縱向追蹤，成功填補了GDM對多個母嬰微生物生態位（母乳、乳暈皮膚、嬰兒糞便）中微生物群落長期影響的知識空白。然而，本研究主要揭示了GDM與微生物群落改變之間的關聯性，未深入探討其潛在的機制或因果關係，這將是未來研究需進一步探索的方向。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Microbial Imprints of Gestational Diabetes Mellitus: Longitudinal Shifts in Breast Milk, Areola Skin, and Infant Gut.
**Abstract:** Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) has been associated with alterations in maternal and infant gut microbiota. However, it remains unclear whether these associations extend to other maternal-infant microbial niches and persist during early infancy. This study aimed to characterise longitudinal associations between GDM and microbiota across human milk, areolar skin, and infant faecal niches during early life. We followed 104 mother-infant pairs (52 GDM and 52 controls) from delivery through 6 months postpartum. Human milk, areolar skin, and infant faecal samples were collected at 1, 4, and 6 months postpartum and profiled using 16S rRNA gene sequencing. Longitudinal associations were evaluated using mixed-effects models, permutational multivariate analysis of variance, and Spearman correlation. GDM was associated with differences in maternal and infant microbiota across all three niches. Reduced alpha diversity was observed in areolar skin, whereas significant GDM-by-time interactions were identified in human milk and infant faecal microbiota. Beta-diversity analyses revealed persistent differences in microbial community composition. At the genus level, GDM was associated with enrichment of Staphylococcus and temporal changes in Stenotrophomonas in human milk, decreases in Rothia in areolar skin, and an increase in Staphylococcus in infant faeces. Significant associations were also observed between several GDM-related maternal taxa and corresponding infant faecal taxa. In conclusion, GDM was associated with persistent and niche-specific microbial differences across interconnected maternal-infant microbial niches during early life. These findings provide new insights into maternal-infant microbiota in the context of GDM and support future mechanistic investigations.
