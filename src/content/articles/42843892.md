### 重點摘要
肥胖是全球性的公共衛生挑戰，常規管理策略效果有限，且常與腸道菌群失調有關。本敘述性回顧系統性地整合了實驗性、機制性及臨床前研究證據，探討發酵食品如何透過其代謝物（如短鏈脂肪酸、共軛亞油酸和吲哚衍生物）來調節肥胖。研究指出，這些發酵產生的代謝物透過腸腦軸、免疫代謝途徑及表觀遺傳機制，在多個器官產生生理作用，影響腸內分泌激素分泌、肝臟糖脂代謝、白色脂肪組織褐變及中樞食慾控制，最終有助於代謝恢復。這項研究為設計以微生物群為導向的體重管理營養策略提供了強化的概念框架。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻系統性且機制性地闡明了發酵食品中由微生物產生的主要代謝物，如何透過重塑腸道菌群組成和功能活性，並橫跨相互連接的器官系統（特別是腸腦軸、免疫代謝途徑和表觀遺傳機制），來對抗肥胖，進而強化了以微生物群為導向的體重調節營養策略的理論框架。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Microbial Metabolic Messengers from Fermented Foods: Multi-Organ Regulation of Energy Homeostasis and Obesity.
**Abstract:** Mechanistic evidence was systematically reviewed on how major metabolites produced during fermentation of foods modulate obesity through gut microbiota remodeling and multiorgan metabolic regulation, with emphasis on the gut-brain axis, immunometabolic pathways, and epigenetic mechanisms. Obesity remains a major global public health challenge, and conventional management strategies are frequently limited by poor long-term efficacy, low adherence, and undesirable side effects. Increasing evidence positions the gut microbiome as a central metabolic organ that profoundly influences host energy homeostasis and adiposity. Individuals with obesity commonly have a disrupted intestinal microbial ecosystem characterized by reduced microbial diversity, depletion of health-promoting taxa, and enrichment of facultative pathogens, which compromise intestinal barrier function, promote chronic low-grade inflammation, and disturb systemic metabolic regulation. Fermented foods, integral to traditional diets, provide a rich source of live microorganisms and a diverse repertoire of bioactive metabolites produced through fermentation. A narrative review was conducted by synthesizing evidence from experimental, mechanistic, and preclinical studies on fermented foods, gut microbial ecology, fermentation-derived metabolites, and obesity-related metabolic pathways. Current evidence indicates that the anti-obesity effects of fermented foods are mediated not only by transient microbial colonization but, more importantly, by fermentation-derived metabolites such as short-chain fatty acids, conjugated linoleic acid, and indole derivatives (eg, indole-3-propionic acid). These metabolites exert multiorgan physiological effects through the gut-brain axis, immunometabolic pathways, and epigenetic regulation. Collectively, they modulate key processes, including enteroendocrine hormone secretion, hepatic glucose and lipid metabolism, browning of white adipose tissue, and central appetite control, ultimately contributing to metabolic restoration. This review provides a systematic and mechanistic evaluation of how major fermentation-derived metabolites counteract obesity by reshaping gut microbiota composition and functional activity across interconnected organ systems. The insights provide a strengthened conceptual framework for designing microbiota-directed nutritional strategies to sustain body-weight regulation.
