### 重點摘要
鑒於線上健康資訊氾濫且難以評估，本研究旨在評估旨在幫助消費者批判性思考線上健康資訊的資源之可理解性、可執行性、可讀性及文化適切性。研究人員對符合特定標準（如英文、免費、針對消費者）的線上資源進行環境掃描，並使用PEMAT-P、AGREE-II、自訂量表及SMOG可讀性公式進行評估。在分析的105個資源中，僅68.3%達到可理解性門檻，24.6%具備可執行性，平均閱讀水平高達12.4年級（遠超建議的8年級），且僅少數資源提供翻譯版本或明確建議。這些線上資源在可理解性、可執行性和可讀性方面均未達推薦標準，顯示在提供有效支持，特別是對健康素養較低者方面存在顯著不足。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇研究揭示了當前旨在幫助消費者批判性評估線上健康資訊的資源，在可理解性、可執行性、可讀性以及文化適切性方面存在顯著不足。這代表針對健康素養較低，最需要這類支持的人群，目前缺乏足夠且有效的工具。未來的研究可著重於開發符合這些無障礙標準，並能有效服務多元族群的健康素養資源。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** How accessible are online resources that help people think critically about health information? An environmental scan and assessment of understandability, actionability, readability and cultural responsiveness.
**Abstract:** The proliferation of unregulated online health information presents ongoing challenges for users seeking to critically evaluate its quality and reliability. Resources designed to support consumers and community members to think critically about online health information must be both accessible and easy to use. This study assessed the understandability, actionability, readability and the inclusion of cultural and linguistic adaptions of online critical health literacy resources. We conducted an environmental scan of online resources. Resources were eligible for inclusion if they: (a) included information to build knowledge or skills in evaluating the credibility of health information (b) focused on health (c) were available in English, (d) targeted consumers, and (e) were freely available. Reviewers independently scored the understandability, actionability and clarity of presentation of each identified resource using the Patient Education Materials Assessment Tool for printable materials (PEMAT-P) and the AGREE-II tool. Author-developed single item screeners were used to assess cultural adaptations and the availability of translated versions of each resource. Discrepancies were resolved through consensus. Readability was assessed using the Simplified Measure of Gobbledygook (SMOG) readability formula within the SHeLL Health Literacy Editor. The scan generated 1137 results; n = 105 resources were included in our analysis after eligibility screening. Using a 70% threshold for the PEMAT, 68.3% of resources were considered understandable, and 24.6% were considered actionable. The average grade reading level of most resources was substantially higher (M=12.4) than the recommended eighth grade reading level. Only 22.7% of included resources included translated versions and only 13% of resources had specific and unambiguous recommendations using AGREE-II. Resources designed to support critical appraisal of online health information fall short of recommended thresholds for understandability, actionability, readability. This highlights a significant gap in provision, particularly for individuals with lower levels of health literacy who may stand to benefit most from accessible and effective critical appraisal support.
