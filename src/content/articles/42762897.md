### 重點摘要
食源性寄生蟲病在全球糧食安全和公共衛生中構成日益嚴峻的挑戰，尤其受氣候變遷與全球貿易影響。本研究以住肉吸蟲病（肝吸蟲病）和海獸胃線蟲病為例，綜述其流行病學、傳播途徑及社會經濟影響，強調這兩種透過淡水植物或海產品傳播的寄生蟲病對人畜健康和食品安全的重大威脅。此文旨在突顯「同一健康」整合方法的重要性，以應對這些食源性蠕蟲人畜共通病。結論強調迫切需要強化綜合監測、診斷能力與跨部門合作，以保障全球食品系統的韌性與永續性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻透過整合現有知識，填補了將住肉吸蟲病和海獸胃線蟲病置於「同一健康」框架下，探討其對全球糧食安全影響的綜合性研究空白。其所提出的未來研究或實施限制，則在於目前缺乏整合性監測、診斷能力不足、有效預防策略及跨部門合作，這些都是未來需要加強的領域。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Fascioliasis and anisakidosis highlight the need for integrated One Health approaches to food safety and security.
**Abstract:** Food-borne parasitic diseases pose an escalating challenge to global food security, public health, and sustainable food systems, particularly in the context of climate change, environmental disturbance, globalised food trade, and shifting dietary practices. Within a One Health framework, fascioliasis and anisakidosis represent two globally distributed and increasingly important helminth zoonoses that span terrestrial and aquatic food systems, affecting livestock, fisheries, and human populations. Fascioliasis, caused by Fasciola spp., remains a major constraint on livestock productivity and a neglected human disease linked to the consumption of contaminated freshwater plants and water, while anisakidosis, caused by anisakid nematodes, is an emerging seafood-borne zoonosis associated with raw or undercooked marine products and allergic disease. Both infections are recognized by the FAO/WHO of the United Nations and are listed among the top-ranked food-borne parasites of global public health importance, reflecting their disease burden, wide geographic distribution, and implications for food safety. This review synthesizes current knowledge on the epidemiology, transmission pathways, clinical manifestations, and socio-economic impacts of fascioliasis and anisakidosis, with particular emphasis on their relevance to global food security and Advancing One Health. By examining these two complementary case studies, this review underscores the urgent need for integrated surveillance, improved diagnostic capacity, effective prevention strategies, and cross-sectoral collaboration to mitigate the impact of food-borne helminth zoonoses and to safeguard the safety, resilience, and sustainability of global food systems.
