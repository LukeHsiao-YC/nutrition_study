### 重點摘要
過量攝取鈉是全球性的公共衛生問題，但其強制性減鈉政策對兒童影響的實證證據有限。本研究利用烏拉圭兒童膳食數據，透過模擬不同範圍的加工食品最高鈉含量政策（基於泛美衛生組織PAHO目標），評估其對6-59個月兒童鈉攝取的潛在影響。結果顯示，全面實施PAHO建議的政策可使總體鈉攝取量顯著減少166毫克/天，這是唯一能在所有年齡子組中實現顯著減少的政策。研究強調，強制性減鈉政策具有降低兒童鈉攝取量的潛力，且政策設計應與人群飲食模式緊密結合。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
儘管全球實施了減少加工食品鈉含量的政策，但這些政策對兒童鈉攝取量潛在影響的實證證據仍相當有限。本研究透過模擬方式填補了此一證據空白，為政策制定提供了參考依據；然而，由於是模擬研究，未來的研究限制在於需要評估這些政策在實際推行後的長期與真實影響。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Impact of mandatory maximum sodium content policies for processed and ultra-processed foods on children's sodium intake: a simulation study comparing policy scenarios in Uruguay.
**Abstract:** Excessive sodium consumption is a major public health concern worldwide. In response, a few countries worldwide have implemented mandatory policies to reduce the sodium content of processed and ultra-processed foods. However, evidence on their potential impact on sodium intake remains limited, particularly among children. The aim of this study was to estimate the potential impact of mandatory maximum sodium content policies, varying in scope, on sodium intake among young children, with a specific focus on sodium derived from processed and ultra-processed foods. A two-step modelling approach was applied using dietary data from the 2018 National Survey on Child Development and Health. First, alternative policy scenarios were defined based on the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) regional sodium targets, varying in their scope. Second, the potential impact of these scenarios on sodium intake was simulated among children aged 6-59 months. Baseline sodium intake from processed and ultra-processed products included in the PAHO targets averaged 673 mg/d and increased with age. Implementation of a comprehensive policy aligned with PAHO targets resulted in a statistically significant reduction in population-level sodium intake (-166 mg/d). Less stringent policies, prioritising subcategories that contributed the most to sodium intake, also led to statistically significant reductions (-148 mg/d and -137 mg/d for policies including subcategories contributing at least 1 and 3 % of children's sodium intake, respectively). However, statistically significant reductions across all age subgroups were observed only when all PAHO subcategories were included. These findings highlight the potential of mandatory maximum sodium content policies to reduce sodium intake among children and underscore the importance of designing interventions that are closely aligned with population dietary patterns.
