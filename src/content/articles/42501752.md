### 重點摘要
研究背景指出，選擇性參與導致研究推論的偏差和普適性不足。本研究利用挪威全國人口登記資料，預測挪威母嬰隊列研究的基線與持續參與情況，並透過反向機率權重量化和調整選擇偏差。結果顯示，基線參與權重能顯著減少因選擇性參與造成的偏差（平均值降低93%，關聯性降低75%），但流失權重對此效果較差。研究結論強調，未來研究應優先開發適用於參與和流失的抽樣權重，以確保研究結果的代表性。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻填補了在缺乏非參與者數據情況下，量化和調整研究中選擇性參與偏差的研究空白。它展示了利用全國性登記資料來構建反向機率權重以有效減少偏差的方法。研究也揭示了流失權重在減少基線選擇偏差方面的限制，並強調未來研究需同時考慮參與和流失的抽樣權重，以提高研究結果的普適性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Quantifying and adjusting for selection biases in the Norwegian Mother, Father and Child Cohort Study using population-wide individual-level registry information.
**Abstract:** Selective participation in research studies hampers researchers' ability to draw valid and generalizable inferences from analyses. Quantifying and adjusting for selective participation are desirable but can be challenging given the paucity of data for non-participants. We used individual-level information from population registers to predict baseline and continued participation in the Norwegian Mother, Father and Child Cohort Study (MoBa). Inverse probability weights were computed from logistic regression models with elastic-net regularization. We predicted selective participation and attrition in 296 987 mothers, of whom 29% returned the first MoBa questionnaire and 12.5% returned a follow-up questionnaire 8 years later. To quantify bias, we computed sample characteristics and exposure-outcome associations in the target population and stratified samples. To compare approaches for adjusting for bias, we computed weighted sample estimates by using three sets of weights. Unweighted sample estimates were systematically different from target-population values, indicating bias due to selective participation. Baseline participation weights substantially reduced the impact of selection bias on mean values by 93% and associations by 75%. Attrition weights reduced attrition bias on mean values by 78% and associations by 50% but were less effective for reducing participation bias (22% and 14% respectively). In this sample, characteristics and effect estimates are substantially different from target-population values. Participation weights were relatively effective at reducing bias due to selective participation but attrition weights were not. Estimates from studies that use attrition weights may still contain non-negligible selection bias-particularly if the baseline sample is not representative of the target population. Future studies should prioritize opportunities for deriving sampling weights for participation as well as attrition.
