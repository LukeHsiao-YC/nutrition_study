### 重點摘要
雖然含糖飲料稅的影響已被廣泛評估，但針對不健康食品徵稅的效益仍有待探討。本研究透過中斷時間序列分析，評估東加王國針對高脂肪、鹽和糖食品的九次加稅措施，對零售價格和進口量的影響，並與校正後的反事實趨勢進行比較。結果顯示，在稅收實施後的第一年，十種被課稅食品的零售價格平均上漲13%，其中多項食品漲幅顯著；同時，八種被課稅食品的進口量平均下降41%，香腸和冰淇淋的降幅尤為顯著。本研究證實，東加對不健康食品徵收消費稅與零售價格上漲及進口量減少有關，進一步充實了不健康食品稅有效提高價格和減少供應的有限證據基礎。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了關於針對不健康食品（而非僅限含糖飲料）徵收消費稅對價格和供應影響的有限證據空白。然而，本研究主要聚焦於零售價格和進口量變化，未直接評估這些稅收對實際食品消費量或長期健康結果的影響，這將是未來研究的重要方向。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Impact of taxes in Tonga targeting foods high in fat, salt and sugar: interrupted time series analyses.
**Abstract:** Sugar-sweetened beverage taxes have been widely evaluated; however, less is known about the effects of taxes on unhealthy foods. This study examines the effects of Tonga's food taxes on food prices and import volumes. Interrupted time series analysis using segmented linear regression was used to evaluate tax impacts on both retail prices and import volumes of taxed foods. Observed post-tax trends were compared with a counterfactual. The counterfactual was the projected pre-tax trends, with adjustment for autocorrelation and changes in GDP, tourism, season, exchange rates and oil prices over time. Random effects meta-analysis was used to summarise outcomes across food types. Nine Tongan food tax increases were examined, varying from US$0·17/kg to US$1·08/kg, and averaging 28 % of import value. In the first-year post-tax, retail prices increased for ten of eleven taxed foods, with an average increase of 13 % (95 %CI: 5 % to 21 %). Price increases for chicken pieces (46 % and 39 %), mutton flaps (20 %), corned beef (20 %), ice cream (11 %), salted beef (6 %) and mayonnaise (3 %) were statistically significant. Import volumes decreased for eight of nine taxed foods with a mean decrease of 41 % (95 %CI: 17 % to 58 %). Import decreases for sausages (68 %) and ice cream (72 %) were significant. Consistent with economic theory, excise taxes on unhealthy foods in Tonga were associated with increases in retail prices and decreased imports of taxed foods. These findings add to the limited evidence base that excise taxes on unhealthy foods can effectively raise prices and reduce supply.
