### 重點摘要
這項範圍性回顧旨在綜合以人本方法處理癌症相關疼痛共享決策的證據，並探討溝通如何影響疼痛認知、意義及管理。研究綜合27項文獻，發現人本方法強調支持性、同情心和個體視角，並提出疼痛是協商性、關係性和溝通性現象的五個主題。結果強調癌症疼痛溝通是影響疼痛認可、合法化及有效管理的關係與道德過程。研究提出「WORDS」助記詞（驗證、開放對話、關係存在、量身定制教育、共享決策）作為臨床指導，以改善疼痛溝通、減少污名並提升疼痛結果。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻透過綜合現有證據，填補了以人本方法處理癌症相關疼痛共享決策的知識空白。然而，研究指出目前的文獻雖然強調同情和以病人為中心的實踐，但對於影響溝通的反思性和結構性層面關注不足，這為未來研究指出了方向。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Words matter when talking about pain: A scoping review of humanistic approaches to SDM in cancer-related pain care.
**Abstract:** To synthesize evidence on humanistic approach to SDM in cancer-related pain care and examine how communication shapes pain recognition, meaning, and management. We conducted a scoping review following Joanna Briggs Institute methodology and PRISMA-ScR guidelines searching six databases and additional sources from inception to January 2026. We used an integrated mixed-methods synthesis, combining inductive thematic analysis with a deductive classification of humanistic communication characteristics informed by established frameworks. We included 27 studies, predominantly qualitative. Humanistic approaches to SDM most frequently emphasized a supportive orientation, compassion, and attention to individual perspectives. We identified five themes presenting pain as a negotiated, relational, and communicative phenomenon rather than a purely biomedical symptom: (1) pain as a site of contestation and negotiation; (2) pain education as a missing element of care; (3) pain treatment as influenced by biases; (4) pain as emotionally unseen; and (5) pain as best supported through empathetic and collaborative care. Cancer pain communication is a relational and moral process that shapes whether and how pain is recognized, legitimized, and effectively managed. While the literature emphasizes compassionate and patient-centered practices, less attention is given to reflexive and structural dimensions influencing communication. We propose a mnemonic (WORDS) to support clinically competent cancer pain communication and provide actionable clinical guidance for validation, open dialogue, relational presence, tailored education, and shared decision-making. Integrating these practices into clinician training, patient education, and intervention designs may enhance engagement among people living with pain, reduce stigma, and improve pain outcomes.
