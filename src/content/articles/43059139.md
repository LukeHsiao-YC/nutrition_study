### 重點摘要
本研究旨在評估非洲五個國家（迦納、肯亞、奈及利亞、坦尚尼亞、南非）正在考慮的五種營養成分模型（NPM）的可行性、監管覆蓋範圍及一致性。研究分析了28,609筆包裝食品數據，發現重要數據（如添加糖、纖維）常缺失，影響了模型的適用性，其中Nutri-Score的可用數據比例最低。結果顯示，南非FOPL和泛美衛生組織（PAHO）模型具有高可行性和低實施負擔，但豁免了較多產品；而世衛組織非洲區域（AFRO）和肯亞模型豁免產品少，導致較高不合規率。研究結論指出，在非洲實施NPM需平衡實證與可行性，南非FOPL和PAHO模型在當前數據和資源限制下更具可執行性，模型選擇應考量多方因素。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了非洲多個國家針對不同營養成分模型進行跨國比較評估的空白，特別強調了在現有數據與資源限制下，模型可行性與實證之間的權衡。它揭示了「最佳證據」模型（如Nutri-Score）在非洲環境中因數據缺失而面臨的實施挑戰，並提供了更具操作性的模型選擇建議。未來的研究可進一步探討如何改進非洲地區的營養數據基礎設施，以及不同NPM在實際政策實施後的長期健康影響與成本效益分析。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** A comparative assessment of nutrient profile models being considered in Africa.
**Abstract:** To conduct a cross-country assessment of five nutrient profile models (NPM) under consideration in Ghana, Kenya, Nigeria, Tanzania and South Africa by evaluating their feasibility, regulatory coverage and alignment. We applied five NPM to assess data availability, exemption rates and compliance rates for each NPM and performed pairwise correlations to measure alignment. Ghana, Kenya, Nigeria, Tanzania, South Africa. 28 609 packaged food product records reflecting items available in 2020-2023. Data availability varied by nutrient. While nutrients like energy and sodium were commonly reported, critical data like added sugar and fibre were frequently missing, requiring extensive imputation, impacting feasibility. Nutri-Score, which demands the most comprehensive data, had the lowest proportion of usable records (86 %) compared to the Pan-American Health Organization model (PAHO) (98 %) and the NPM proposed in South Africa's front-of-package labelling regulation (SA FOPL) (95 %). The SA FOPL and PAHO models demonstrated high feasibility and low implementation burden, exempting a considerable percentage of products (16-18 %). Conversely, the NPM for the WHO African Region (AFRO) and Kenyan models exempted very few products (<5 %) and resulted in the highest non-compliance. Nutri-Score showed the weakest correlation with all other models. NPM are crucial for food policies, but their successful implementation in an African context requires balancing the best available evidence with feasibility. The SA FOPL and PAHO models emerge as more implementable given current data and resource constraints, while the AFRO and Kenyan models exempt few products and carry higher burdens. NPM selection should consider available evidence, specific policy goals, regulatory capacity, data infrastructure and context-specific adaptations for successful policy implementation.
