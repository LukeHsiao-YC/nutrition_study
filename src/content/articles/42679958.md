### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討2007年至2018年間美國成人攝取高飽和脂肪、鈉和添加糖（HFSS）食物與飲料的趨勢，這類食物常與不良健康結果相關但相關研究稀少。研究團隊利用NHANES的橫斷面數據，分析了27,984名美國成人的飲食資料，並以HFSS食物攝取量佔總能量的百分比作為主要結果。結果顯示，HFSS食物總攝取量維持在總能量攝取量的60%以上並未改變，但高鈉和高飽和脂肪食物的攝取量顯著增加，而高添加糖食物的攝取量則維持不變。總體而言，美國成人HFSS食物攝取量偏高，特別是高鈉和高飽和脂肪食物的攝取趨勢令人擔憂。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了美國成人HFSS食物與飲料長期攝取趨勢的數據空白，揭示了其中高鈉和高飽和脂肪食物攝取量增加的具體情況。未來的研究應著重於評估公共衛生干預措施和政策是否能有效降低這些「值得關注的營養素」含量高的食物攝取，以應對高HFSS飲食對健康的挑戰。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Trends in the Prevalence of Foods High in Saturated Fats, Sodium, and Added Sugars among U.S. adults, NHANES 2007-2018.
**Abstract:** Foods and beverages high in saturated fats, sodium, and added sugars (HFSS) are often ultra-processed and linked to poor health outcomes, but few studies have investigated their intake. To describe the trends in the intake of HFSS foods and beverages between 2007 and 2018 in a nationally representative sample of U.S. adults, by sociodemographic characteristics and What We Eat in America food groups. This is a secondary, cross-sectional analysis of the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) between 2007 and 2018. The final sample included 27,984 adults 19 years of age or older from NHANES with at least one complete dietary recall. The primary outcomes are the percentage of total energy intake from foods classified as HFSS according to the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) Nutrient Profile Model. To estimate the percentage of energy intake from foods and beverages HFSS, linear regression models with interaction terms between cycles and covariates were used. The overall intake of foods and beverages HFSS did not change, representing over 60% of the total energy intake between 2007-2010 and 2015-2018. The intake of foods and beverages high in sodium increased by 2.0 percentage points (95% CI: 0.5, 3.5) and 3.5 percentage points (95% CI: 1.1, 5.8), respectively. The intake of foods and beverages high in saturated fats increased by 6.1 percentage points (95% CI: 4.5, 7.6) and 6.1 percentage points (95% CI: 3.9, 8.2), respectively. The intake of foods and beverages high in added sugars did not change. In the U.S., intake of HFSS foods and beverages is high. Future research should focus on whether public health interventions and policies might reduce the intake of foods high in nutrients of concern.
