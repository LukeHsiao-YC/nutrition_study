### 重點摘要
這項混合方法研究旨在探討113對患有氣喘或囊狀纖維化的兒童及其照護者對於居家監測的觀點。研究發現，兒童和照護者對居家監測的採用意願普遍很高，其中氣喘組的意願顯著高於囊狀纖維化組。主要障礙包括時間、可用性及對減少與醫療人員實體接觸的擔憂，而促進因素則包含設備的便利性與清晰指導。研究建議，成功的居家監測實施需針對患者需求進行客製化、降低負擔並整合專業醫療監督。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
此研究填補了先前對於兒童肺部疾病患者的照護者與兒童本身，對於居家監測態度與看法的研究空白。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Exploring Perspectives and Barriers in Implementing Home Monitoring for Pediatric Pulmonary Diseases: Mixed Methods Study.
**Abstract:** Home monitoring has the potential to enable early detection of disease progression in pediatric lung diseases such as asthma and cystic fibrosis (CF). There is minimal evidence regarding the opinions of caregivers and children on home monitoring. This study aimed to explore children's and caregivers' perspectives on home monitoring. This multicenter, mixed methods study included children aged 6 to 18 years with asthma or CF and their caregivers. Participants were recruited from Juliana Children's Hospital and Erasmus MC Sophia Children's Hospital between September 2023 and June 2025. Participants completed a structured questionnaire assessing experiences, attitudes, and potential barriers and facilitators to home monitoring. Fourteen children and their caregivers took part in in-depth interviews. Conventional content analysis was applied to both open-text and interview data. A total of 113 caregiver-child dyads (62 [54.9%] asthma and 51 [45.1%] CF) participated in this study. Willingness to use home monitoring was high among both children (asthma: n=47, 83.9%; CF: n=30, 62.5%) and caregivers (asthma: n=54, 87.1%; CF: n=44, 86.3%). Children with asthma and their caregivers showed significantly greater willingness to use the proposed types of home monitoring than children with CF and their caregivers. Caregivers who expressed difficulty with symptom interpretation were significantly more motivated to use home monitoring. Key barriers included time demands, usability, device size, and concerns about maintaining personal contact with health care providers. Facilitators included compact, user-friendly devices, clear instructions, and improved disease understanding. High willingness to adopt home monitoring was observed among children with asthma and children with CF, but preferences and perceived barriers differ. Tailoring device choice and implementation strategies to patient needs, minimizing burden, and integrating medical oversight are important for successful uptake.
