### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討學生對氣候友善學校餐點（特別是減肉策略）的看法，因先前對此具體菜單變化的研究較少。透過對瑞典1,268名學生進行量化問卷與質性討論的混合方法研究，結果顯示學生普遍認為低碳食譜改良比全素日更容易接受。研究發現，餐點的口味、飽足感和熟悉度等因素，比單純的永續性考量更能強力影響學生對減肉餐點的接受度。因此，建議低碳食譜改良可作為輔助全素日的實用策略，以提升學生對氣候友善餐點的接受度與攝取量。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
過去研究較少關注學生對於學校餐點中具體減肉策略（如全素日或低碳食譜改良）的實際感知和解讀。本研究填補了這項空白，深入探討了學生對這些氣候友善餐點的看法，並指出味道、熟悉度及餐點常規等因素，比永續性考量本身更能影響接受度。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** "AS LONG AS IT TASTES GOOD": STUDENTS' PERCEPTIONS OF CLIMATE-FRIENDLY SCHOOL MEALS IN A MIXED-METHODS STUDY.
**Abstract:** Shifting toward sustainable diets is key to reducing the environmental impact of food systems. In school meals, this often involves meat reduction through strategies such as all-vegetarian days and lower-carbon recipe reformulation. Yet students' perceptions of such concrete menu changes have received little attention. This convergent mixed-methods study examined students' perceptions of climate-friendly school meals and their interpretations of meat reduction. Quantitative survey data from 1,268 students across six Swedish schools were analyzed using multiple regression, alongside qualitative data from student discussions analyzed using reflexive thematic analysis. Both quantitative and qualitative findings suggested that lower-carbon recipe reformulation was more acceptable to students than vegetarian-only days. Quantitative analysis showed that more positive attitudes toward climate-friendly school meals were associated with perceiving climate considerations as important in food choice, more frequent selection of vegetarian meals at school, and being female. Students also showed clear awareness of the relative carbon footprint of red meat and plant-based foods across support levels. Qualitative findings, however, highlighted that the acceptability of climate-friendly meals depended strongly on perceived taste and satiety. Meat was positioned as a normative component of a meal, while acceptance of vegetarian meals was more conditional on familiarity and palatability. These findings corroborate earlier research showing that taste, familiarity, and meal norms shape acceptance of meat reduction in school meals more strongly than sustainability concerns alone. They also indicate that lower-carbon recipe reformulation may complement vegetarian-only days as a practical strategy for supporting acceptance and intake of climate-friendly school meals.
