### 重點摘要
低收入孕婦常面臨營養挑戰及妊娠體重增加過度的風險，儘管多組分eHealth介入措施能降低妊娠體重增加，其飲食改變的貢獻此前仍不明確。「SmartMoms in WIC」隨機臨床試驗招募了351名低收入孕婦，比較了結合健康教育與烹飪支持的eHealth介入組與僅接受常規WIC服務的對照組在孕期飲食攝入的變化。結果顯示，介入組的能量攝入從孕早期到晚期保持穩定，而常規照護組的總能量、脂肪和碳水化合物攝入量顯著增加，且飲食選擇更不健康。這項整合於WIC框架內的遠端eHealth介入成功地幫助低收入孕婦維持了健康的能量攝入，並避免了不健康飲食習慣的增加。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了過去eHealth介入措施雖能降低妊娠體重增加，但其具體飲食改變貢獻不明的空白。它明確展示了此類介入措施如何透過維持能量攝取並避免不健康飲食習慣的增加，來影響低收入孕婦的飲食狀況。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** The effects of a community-based multicomponent behavioral eHealth intervention for low-income pregnant individuals on dietary intake: The "SmartMoms in WIC" randomized clinical trial.
**Abstract:** Pregnant individuals with low-income face socioeconomic barriers affecting nutrition and increasing risk for gestational weight gain (GWG) outside of recommendations. A multicomponent eHealth intervention lowered GWG, but the contribution of dietary changes remains unknown. The objective of the study was to evaluate intervention effects on dietary intake and its association with GWG. The ∼24-week, investigator- and assessor-blinded, parallel-arm randomized clinical trial "SmartMoms in WIC", conducted between July 2019 and May 2024, assessed dietary intake by 24-hour recalls and Healthy Eating Index (HEI)-2020 in early (10,0-16,6 weeks) and late (35,0-37,6 weeks) pregnancy. GWG (kg) was calculated as late minus early pregnancy weight, and weekly GWG (g/week) as total GWG divided by weeks between visits. /setting: Pregnant individuals (n=351) in Louisiana Women, Infants, and Children (WIC) program were randomized to Intervention (n=179) or Usual Care (n=172) at 15.2±1.6 (mean ± SD) weeks gestation. The intervention supported dietary intake modification through health education, cooking equipment and recipes, in addition to WIC services. Usual Care received WIC services only. Dietary intake included total energy, macro- and micronutrients, and food components, with HEI-2020 assessing alignment with the 2020-2025 Dietary Guidelines for Americans. Linear mixed models assessed intervention effects on dietary intake and linear models examined associations with GWG, reporting estimated means, 95% confidence intervals, and P values.
