### 重點摘要
本研究旨在探討懷孕期間母體自體免疫疾病與子代第一型糖尿病 (T1D) 之間的關聯，並比較母系與父系自體免疫疾病的影響。這項瑞典全國性世代追蹤研究納入超過145萬名兒童，追蹤中位數為15.3年，結果顯示母體自體免疫疾病與子代T1D風險增加有關 (aHR 1.57)，父體自體免疫疾病也有類似的關聯 (aHR 1.51)。研究發現，儘管母父影響在整體上無顯著差異，但在特定疾病類別中存在異質性，且有父母雙方罹患T1D時子代風險最高。結論指出，母體自體免疫疾病與子代T1D風險增加相關，這種關聯可能涉及共享的家族易感性，同時也可能存在子宮內機制。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了過去對於母體自體免疫疾病與子代第一型糖尿病之間關聯，以及產前與家族因素相對貢獻不明的知識空白。透過比較父母雙方的自體免疫疾病影響並進行表親分析，研究釐清了家族易感性在子代T1D風險中的重要性，同時也暗示了某些特定疾病可能存在父/母特異性效應及潛在的子宮內機制。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Maternal Autoimmune Diseases During Pregnancy and Offspring Type 1 Diabetes.
**Abstract:** Maternal autoimmune diseases during pregnancy may influence the long-term risk of immune-mediated diseases in offspring, but the relative contributions of prenatal and familial factors to offspring type 1 diabetes (T1D) remain unclear. To investigate the association between a broad spectrum of maternal autoimmune diseases during pregnancy and offspring T1D, and to compare the associations of maternal and paternal autoimmune diseases with offspring T1D. This population-based cohort study included children identified from national health registers who were born in Sweden between 2001 and 2014, and were followed up from birth until T1D diagnosis, death, emigration, or December 31, 2023. These data were analyzed from November 2025 to June 2026. Parental autoimmune diseases diagnosed before or during pregnancy, identified from national health registers. The primary outcome was incident T1D in offspring. Hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% CIs for offspring T1D were estimated using Cox proportional hazard regression models. Associations of maternal and paternal autoimmune diseases before childbirth with offspring T1D were directly compared, and cousin analyses were performed to assess the potential role of shared familial factors. During a median follow-up of 15.3 years, 10 273 (0.71%) of the 1 455 645 included individuals (51.4% male and 48.6% female; median [IQR], age 15.3 [11.9-19.0] years) developed T1D. Maternal autoimmune disease during pregnancy was associated with an increased risk of offspring T1D (adjusted HR, 1.57; 95% CI, 1.46-1.68); paternal autoimmune disease before childbirth showed a similar association (adjusted HR, 1.51; 95% CI, 1.37-1.67). No overall difference was observed in maternal-paternal comparisons (ratio of HR, 1.04; 95% CI, 0.92-1.17), but heterogeneity was observed across specific disease categories. A clear gradient in T1D risk was observed according to parental T1D status, with the highest risk among offspring with both parents affected, followed by paternal T1D only and maternal T1D only. In cousin analyses, the association with maternal autoimmune disease was attenuated but persisted (HR, 1.27; 95% CI, 1.08-1.50). In this population-based cohort study, maternal autoimmune diseases during pregnancy were associated with an increased risk of offspring T1D. Comparable associations of maternal and paternal autoimmune diseases with offspring T1D suggest a role of shared familial susceptibility, while disease-specific differences indicate that some conditions could have parental-specific effects, suggesting contributions from shared familial susceptibility and potential intrauterine mechanisms.
