### 重點摘要
過度診斷是乳癌篩檢的潛在危害，但許多女性對此概念不了解且難以理解。本研究透過對39位39-49歲女性進行7場焦點團體訪談，採用以使用者為中心的設計方法，探討她們如何看待過度診斷、偏好的溝通方式，並評估與修改相關術語。結果顯示，參與者不認為過度診斷應被稱為「危害」，而偏好「結果」等中性詞彙，並建議使用簡單、直接且能明確區分過度診斷與假陽性的語言。研究結論指出，改進乳癌篩檢教育材料，使用清晰直接的語言解釋過度診斷並區分其與假陽性，能提升大眾理解度並改善溝通效果。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了乳癌篩檢中關於「過度診斷」概念溝通策略的空白，特別是從女性使用者角度出發，探討她們對此概念的認知、情感反應，以及偏好的表達方式。它明確指出大眾常將過度診斷與假陽性混淆的誤區，並提出了具體且經過使用者驗證的語言建議，以改善現有衛教材料的溝通效果。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** How do women suggest communicating about overdiagnosis? A user-centered design study.
**Abstract:** Overdiagnosis is the detection of a cancer that would never cause harm over a person's lifetime. Overdiagnosis is recognized as a "harm" of breast cancer screening (BCS), yet many women are not informed about it and often find the concept difficult to understand. We examined whether women view overdiagnosis as a "harm" of BCS, how they prefer potential BCS outcomes to be framed, and how they suggest communicating about overdiagnosis to others. Participants were drawn from a parent longitudinal study focused on informed BCS decisions among women aged 39-49. Thirty-nine women diverse in screening preferences, educational attainment, and race were purposively sampled to participate in 7 focus groups. This brief report presents findings from a user-centered design exercise where participants evaluated and refined language used to communicate about overdiagnosis. This exercise occurred at the end of focus groups that covered broader screening-related topics. Transcripts were analyzed using a rapid qualitative analysis. Participants did not believe that overdiagnosis should be labeled as a "harm" of BCS, instead preferring more neutral terms such as "outcome." Although some women understood the concept of overdiagnosis without additional clarification, many conflated it with false positives. Participants preferred revised language explaining overdiagnosis, that was simple, direct, and clearly distinguished overdiagnosis from false positives. Women did not perceive overdiagnosis to be a "harm" of BCS and preferred this concept it to be described as an "outcome that can happen." Communication about overdiagnosis that distinguishes it from false positives in clear and direct language may improve public understanding of the concept and improve receptivity to screening communication. BCS educational materials may be modified to use simple and comprehensive language to explain overdiagnosis, distinguishing it from false positives, and framing information as supporting decision-making and helping women know what to expect from BCS.
