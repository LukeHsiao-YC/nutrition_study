### 重點摘要
這項縱向研究旨在評估從懷孕期到產後24個月期間飲食品質的變化，並探討與社區剝奪程度的關聯。研究對219名參與者進行了飲食頻率問卷調查，並使用健康飲食指數（HEI-2015）評估飲食品質，社區剝奪指數（NDI）評估社區環境。結果顯示，產後飲食品質顯著下降，且社區剝奪程度越高，產後12個月及24個月時的飲食品質越差，特別是全水果、全穀物和總水果的攝取量減少。本研究強調產後飲食品質的下降趨勢，以及社區層面的剝奪對其產生負面影響，提示未來應推動改善產後飲食並解決社區層面障礙的介入措施。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了文獻中關於社區環境因素（即社區剝奪）如何縱向影響產後不同階段飲食品質的空白，並具體指出受影響的食物類別。儘管研究建立了關聯性，但其限制在於未能深入探討社區剝奪影響飲食品質的具體機制，亦未提出或測試針對這些障礙的特定介入策略，這可作為未來研究的方向。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Impact of Neighborhood Deprivation on Postpartum Diet Quality: A Longitudinal Study.
**Abstract:** Assess changes in diet quality from pregnancy through 24 months postpartum and examine associations with neighborhood deprivation. Secondary longitudinal analysis of the Pregnancy, Lifestyle, and Environment Study-2. Kaiser Permanente Northern California, an integrated health care delivery system. A total of 219 participants who completed a food frequency questionnaire. Diet quality using the Healthy Eating Index-2015 (HEI-2015) and neighborhood deprivation, the exposure, was assessed using the Neighborhood Deprivation Index (NDI). Generalized estimating equations examined associations between NDI and HEI-2015, and time-by-NDI interactions. Mean postpartum HEI-2015 scores were lower than the pregnancy score (mean ± SD, 71.7 ± 9.8): 68.9 ± 10.1 at 6 months, 68.3 ± 10.3 at 12 months, and 68.6 ± 9.2 at ≥ 24 months (all P < 0.01). Higher NDI was associated with lower HEI-2015 scores at 12 (β = -3.5; P = 0.001) and ≥ 24 months postpartum (β = -3.6; P = 0.02). Higher NDI was also associated with lower intake of whole fruits at 12 (β = -0.8; P = 0.001) and ≥ 24 months (β = -0.34; P = 0.04); whole grains at ≥ 24 months (β = -1.9; P < 0.001); and total fruits at 6 (β = -0.8; P = 0.001), 12 (β = -1.2; P < 0.001), and ≥ 24 months postpartum (β = -0.6; P = 0.009). Diet quality declined from pregnancy through the postpartum period, with greater neighborhood deprivation associated with poorer diet quality at 12 and ≥ 24 months. These findings support interventions that improve postpartum diet and address neighborhood-level barriers.
