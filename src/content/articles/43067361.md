### 重點摘要
本系統性回顧評估了苦瓜、匙羹藤、啤酒花和仙人掌等植物萃取物及其活性化合物，對成人食慾調節、葡萄糖和脂質代謝及人體測量學的急性和慢性影響。研究共納入51篇文章，結果顯示長期攝取苦瓜可能改善血糖，匙羹藤能調節甜味感知並減少糖癮，啤酒花具有急性食慾調節作用，而仙人掌則展現長期的食慾控制潛力。然而，這些植物萃取物的研究多存在中度至高度的偏差風險及方法學異質性。整體而言，研究為未來食慾相關研究提供了方向，但仍需更高品質的證據支持其臨床應用。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究發現，現有文獻多存在中度至高度的偏差風險和方法學異質性，且大部分研究對象為健康體重者，限制了其結果應用於臨床病患的潛力。此外，仙人掌的急性作用證據不足，而啤酒花的慢性作用則未被報導。未來的研究應專注於解決這些方法學限制，並擴大研究對象範圍以提高臨床適用性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Appetite regulating and metabolic effects of bitter melon (Momordica charantia), gurmar (Gymnema sylvestre), hops (Humulus lupulus) and Caralluma (Caralluma fimbriata): A systematic review.
**Abstract:** In this systematic review, we assessed the effects of traditionally-used plant extracts, bitter melon (Momordica charantia), gurmar (Gymnema sylvestre), hops (Humulus lupulus) and Caralluma (Caralluma fimbriata) and their isolated bioactive compounds, on appetite regulation, glucose and lipid metabolism, and anthropometry in adults through acute and chronic interventions. A literature search was conducted using Ovid MEDLINE, EMBASE, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials and Scopus until August 2026, yielding 51 included articles. Risk of bias was assessed. The results show that chronic intake of M. charantia may improve glycemic markers in individuals with metabolic dysfunction, suggesting potential as an adjunct antidiabetic therapy. Acute intake of G. sylvestre and gymnemic acid consistently altered sweet taste perception and preference. Long-term consumption further reduced sugar cravings, with effects achievable through interventions such as dissolving mints or lozenges. H. lupulus demonstrated strong acute appetite-modulating effects across energy intake, subjective ratings, and hormones, with no chronic effect reported. Applicability to clinically compromised populations remains limited, as findings are mostly restricted to healthy-weight individuals. C. fimbriata demonstrated promising chronic appetite modulation via biomarkers, energy intake, and anthropometry, but acute evidence is absent. However, the effects of these traditionally-used plant extracts are from studies with moderate-to-high risk of bias and methodological heterogeneity. The information presented here should assist researchers in focusing future studies on the most relevant appetite-related outcomes.
