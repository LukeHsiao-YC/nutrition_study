### 重點摘要
鑑於對使用大麻二酚（CBD）治療智能障礙青少年嚴重行為問題的興趣濃厚但缺乏證據，本隨機臨床試驗旨在評估其療效。該研究招募了131名6至18歲患有智能障礙和嚴重行為問題的青少年，隨機分組接受CBD或安慰劑，為期8週。結果顯示，CBD組與安慰劑組在主要結果（家長報告的異常行為量表易怒分量表得分）上並無顯著差異，且不良事件發生率相似。研究結論指出，CBD對治療智能障礙青少年的嚴重行為問題無效，不應在此族群中使用。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
本研究填補了關於大麻二酚（CBD）是否能有效治療智能障礙青少年嚴重行為問題的證據空白，明確指出其在此族群中並無療效。這項結果為臨床實踐提供了明確指導，限制了未來在此特定族群和適應症上進一步探索CBD的可能性。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Cannabidiol for Severe Behavioral Problems in Youths With Intellectual Disability: A Randomized Clinical Trial.
**Abstract:** There is a high level of interest in cannabidiol (CBD) as treatment for severe behavioral problems in children with intellectual disabilities. Given the lack of evidence, clinical trials are needed to inform practice. To investigate the efficacy of CBD to reduce severe behavioral problems in children and adolescents with intellectual disability. This parallel design, double-blind placebo-controlled randomized clinical trial was conducted between May 11, 2021, and May 8, 2025. Participants were children and adolescents aged 6 to 18 years with an intellectual disability and severe behavioral problems from 3 children's hospitals in Australia: The Royal Children's Hospital in Melbourne, Monash Children's Hospital in Melbourne, and The Children's Hospital Westmead in Sydney. Participants were randomly assigned 1:1 to receive CBD isolate in oil (10 mg/kg/d) or placebo orally for 8 weeks, with a 4-week follow-up period. The primary outcome was parent-reported score on the Aberrant Behavior Checklist, Irritability (ABC-I) subscale assessed at the end of the 8-week maintenance phase. Of 131 participants randomized after exclusion of 1 participant randomized in error, 65 were in the CBD group and 66 were in the placebo group. Overall, mean (SD) age was 11.2 (3.1) years, and 98 participants (74.8%) were male. A total of 110 participants (84.0%) had comorbid autism. Primary outcome data were available for 61 participants in the CBD group and 63 in the placebo group. Mean (SD) ABC-I subscale scores at baseline were 32.2 (7.3) and 30.0 (6.3) in the CBD and placebo groups, respectively. Intention-to-treat analysis showed similar mean (SD) ABC-I subscale scores at the end of treatment: 21.7 (11.0) in the CBD group and 19.2 (10.7) in the placebo group (mean difference, 2.4; 95% CI, -1.5 to 6.3). A similar proportion of participants experienced at least 1 adverse event in the CBD group (41 [63.1%]) and the placebo group (40 [60.6%]). In this randomized clinical trial, CBD was not effective in treating severe behavioral problems in youths with intellectual disability. The results suggest that CBD should not be used to treat severe behavioral problems in this population. ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT04821856.
