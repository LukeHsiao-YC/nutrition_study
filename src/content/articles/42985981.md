### 重點摘要
本全球性範圍回顧檢視了老年人糧食不安全與社會網絡間的關係，共納入60篇文章。研究發現，高收入國家以量化設計為主，但低中收入國家的研究和專注於老年人的研究相對有限。糧食不安全常用多種工具評估，而社會網絡則多以婚姻狀況等代理指標呈現，且很少探討其作為決定因素以外的雙向或中介作用。本回顧指出，當前證據存在地域與方法學上的限制，未來研究應拓展至低中收入國家並深入探討雙向關係，以利於發展有效的糧食或網絡介入策略。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這篇文獻透過範圍回顧，識別出目前在老年人糧食不安全與社會網絡研究領域的關鍵空白。這些空白包括：在低中收入國家（LMIC）的研究有限；方法學途徑過於狹隘，多數研究僅將社會網絡視為糧食不安全的決定因素，而很少探索反向或雙向路徑、以及將網絡作為中介或結果的分析定位。填補這些空白，特別是透過更多樣化、全球化的研究方法，對於深化糧食不安全與社會網絡如何影響老齡化的流行病學理解至關重要，並能為基於糧食或網絡的介入措施提供戰略切入點。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Food insecurity and social networks among older adults: a global scoping review.
**Abstract:** To synthesise evidence on the relationship between food insecurity and social networks among older adults, focusing on conceptualisation and measurement, analytical positionality, directionality of associations and research gaps. A scoping review. Global evidence. Adults aged 60 years and older. Sixty articles met the inclusion criteria. Research activity increased markedly after 2014, with 45 % published between 2020 and 2025. The median interval between completion of data collection and publication was 4 years. Most were conducted in high-income countries and employed quantitative designs, with limited representation from low- and middle-income countries (LMIC) and few focusing exclusively on older adults. Food insecurity was assessed using diverse instruments, most commonly the Household Food Security Survey Module (HFSSM), the Household Food Insecurity Access Scale (HFIAS) and the Food Insecurity Experience Scale (FIES). Social networks were often proxied by marital status, living arrangements or perceived social support, with few studies employing validated instruments or theoretical frameworks. Most studies treated social networks as determinants of food insecurity, while reverse/bidirectional pathways, as well as more nuanced analytical positions such as networks as mediators/outcomes, were rarely explored. This review identifies critical gaps in the evidence, including limited research in LMIC, narrow methodological approaches and a lack of attention to bidirectional pathways, which could indicate strategic entry points for food- or network-based intervention. Addressing these gaps through more diverse, globally informed approaches is essential to deepening the epidemiological understanding of how food insecurity and social networks shape ageing.
