### 重點摘要
本回溯性世代研究旨在比較三種結合全球營養領導倡議 (GLIM) 框架的營養篩查工具 (MUST, MST, NRS-2002) 在癌症成人患者中診斷一致性與預後價值。研究評估了這些模型與全因死亡率的關聯，並比較了它們的診斷效能與預後判斷能力。結果顯示，所有三種GLIM模型均與全因死亡率風險增加顯著相關，且死亡風險隨營養不良嚴重程度而遞增。其中，結合MST的GLIM模型在診斷一致性上表現最佳，且識別臨床顯著營養不良的能力最強。

### 研究縫隙 (Research Gap)
這項研究填補了在癌症患者中，哪些GLIM結合的營養篩查模型在診斷營養不良及預測死亡率方面表現更優的知識空白，特別是突顯了結合MST的模型具有最佳診斷一致性及識別能力。未來的研究可進一步探討其前瞻性驗證或基於這些發現的營養干預措施。

### 原文資訊 (Original English)
**Title:** Malnutrition defined by three global leadership initiative on malnutrition-based models and the risk of all-cause mortality in adults with common cancers: A retrospective cohort study.
**Abstract:** Our study compared the diagnostic agreement and prognostic value for overall survival of three nutritional screening tools integrated with the Global Leadership Initiative on Malnutrition framework in patients with cancer. The nutritional screening tools evaluated were the Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool, Malnutrition Screening Tool, and Nutritional Risk Screening 2002. Prognostic assessment was conducted using Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and Cox proportional hazards regression, with sensitivity analyses to ensure robustness. Logistic regression analysis was employed to assess the predictive value of each Global Leadership Initiative on Malnutrition-based model for malnutrition. Harrell's concordance index compared the discriminatory power of the models for overall survival outcomes, while kappa statistics evaluated diagnostic consistency for malnutrition across the models. Each of the three models combining the Global Leadership Initiative on Malnutrition criteria with the Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool, the Malnutrition Screening Tool, or Nutritional Risk Screening 2002 was independently associated with an increased risk of all-cause mortality. The corresponding hazard ratios were 1.494, 1.517, and 1.530, respectively, with all P values less than 0.001. Across all three models, mortality risk increased progressively with the severity of malnutrition, while their discriminatory performance for overall survival was comparable. Among the three models, the model incorporating the Malnutrition Screening Tool demonstrated the strongest agreement with the Patient-Generated Subjective Global Assessment (kappa = 0.481, P < 0.001) and the greatest ability to identify malnutrition. All three models provided meaningful prognostic information. The model incorporating the Malnutrition Screening Tool showed the closest diagnostic agreement and the strongest identification of clinically significant malnutrition.
